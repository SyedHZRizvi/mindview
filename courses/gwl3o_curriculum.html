GWL3O — Designing Your Future, Grade 11 Open

Curriculum Policy Document · Ontario Ministry of Education · The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 9 to 12: Guidance and Career Education (2006, Revised) · Aligned to Growing Success (2010) and Creating Pathways to Success (2013)

Course Code: GWL3O  |  Credit Value: 0.5  |  Hours: 55  |  Type: Open  |  Prerequisite: None (recommended: GLC2O Career Studies, Grade 10)

1. Course Description

This course helps students develop the skills, strategies, and habits required to design and pursue a meaningful future after secondary school. Students learn to assess their own strengths, interests, values, and learning style; build workplace-ready communication and teamwork skills; and use career-development frameworks to research post-secondary pathways, set goals, and prepare for transitions. GWL3O is a companion to GLC2O (Grade 10 Career Studies) and builds toward GLE4O (Grade 12 Advanced Learning Strategies), the senior capstone for the Guidance and Career Education sequence at MindView. The course supports the Ontario Creating Pathways to Success framework — Who am I? · What are my opportunities? · Who do I want to become? · What is my plan for achieving my goals?

2. Overall Goals of the Course

By the end of the course, students will:

  • analyse their personal strengths, interests, values, and learning preferences and use that self-knowledge to inform future plans;
  • apply effective personal-management strategies (time, stress, organization, resilience);
  • demonstrate workplace-ready interpersonal communication and teamwork skills;
  • identify, research, and compare post-secondary pathways and the skills they require;
  • design a personal pathway plan (SMART goals, action steps, supports, contingencies) and manage the transition from secondary school.

4. Big Ideas (by Strand)

  • Personal Management (A): Self-awareness and personal-management skills are the foundation of every successful pathway; they are skills that can be learned, not fixed traits.
  • Interpersonal Communication (B): The capacity to listen, speak, write, give and receive feedback, and work in diverse teams is decisive for workplace, post-secondary, and life success.
  • Designing Your Future (C): A pathway is designed, not discovered — students who use a deliberate inquiry process plan more confidently and adapt more flexibly.

5. Strand A — Personal Management (Self-Awareness)

Overall Expectations

  • A1. Self-Knowledge: assess their own personal characteristics — strengths, interests, values, learning style, and growth areas;
  • A2. Personal Management Skills: apply effective strategies for time management, stress management, organization, and resilience;
  • A3. Reflection and Goal-Setting: use self-knowledge to set short- and longer-term personal goals.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • A1.1 use a variety of self-assessment tools (e.g., VARK, Holland Codes, multiple-intelligences, values-clarification) to identify personal strengths and growth areas;
  • A1.2 describe how a growth mindset (Dweck) supports learning, effort, and resilience;
  • A1.3 analyse how learning preferences influence study habits and workplace performance;
  • A2.1 apply time-management strategies (e.g., backwards planning, time-blocking, Pomodoro) to school and personal commitments;
  • A2.2 apply stress-management strategies (e.g., physical activity, sleep hygiene, mindfulness, social connection, professional support) and identify when to seek help;
  • A2.3 use organizational tools (digital and analog) to manage academic and personal information;
  • A2.4 demonstrate resilience strategies when facing setbacks (reframing, problem-solving, drawing on supports);
  • A3.1 set SMART goals connected to identified strengths and growth areas;
  • A3.2 monitor progress toward goals and adjust plans as needed.
Cross-curricular focus: Personal-management strategies transfer to every academic subject (note-taking, study sessions, exam prep) and to the workplace (managing deadlines, managing energy, recovering from mistakes).

6. Strand B — Interpersonal Communication and Teamwork

Overall Expectations

  • B1. Communication Skills: demonstrate effective communication in a range of academic, workplace, and personal contexts;
  • B2. Teamwork and Collaboration: contribute to and lead diverse teams;
  • B3. Conflict and Feedback: give and receive feedback constructively and resolve conflict respectfully.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • B1.1 demonstrate active-listening behaviours (e.g., paraphrasing, asking clarifying questions, non-verbal attention);
  • B1.2 use assertive (vs. passive or aggressive) communication styles appropriately;
  • B1.3 demonstrate workplace-appropriate written communication (e.g., professional email, brief memo, status update);
  • B1.4 demonstrate workplace-appropriate verbal communication (e.g., short presentation, phone call, interview);
  • B2.1 identify team roles (Belbin) and stages of team development (Tuckman: forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning);
  • B2.2 contribute productively to a team project, including planning, dividing work, monitoring progress, and integrating contributions;
  • B2.3 demonstrate inclusive collaboration practices that respect cultural, generational, and accessibility-related differences;
  • B3.1 give constructive feedback that is specific, behaviour-focused, and actionable;
  • B3.2 receive feedback without defensiveness, asking clarifying questions and identifying next steps;
  • B3.3 use a conflict-resolution framework (e.g., interest-based, Thomas-Kilmann styles) to address disagreements.
Anchor frameworks: Active listening (Rogers); assertive communication; Belbin team roles; Tuckman stages; non-violent communication (Rosenberg); Thomas-Kilmann conflict modes; restorative practices.

7. Strand C — Designing Your Future (Pathways and Goals)

Overall Expectations

  • C1. Pathways Awareness: research the post-secondary pathways available in Ontario (apprenticeship, college, university, workplace, gap, hybrid);
  • C2. Decision-Making: align self-knowledge with pathway opportunities and make informed choices;
  • C3. Pathway Planning: design, document, and prepare to enact a personal pathway plan including managing transitions.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • C1.1 describe Ontario's four post-secondary pathways (apprenticeship, college, university, workplace) and explain the credentials each leads to;
  • C1.2 use labour-market information sources (e.g., Job Bank, National Occupational Classification) to research career paths of interest;
  • C1.3 compare local and out-of-province post-secondary opportunities, including cost, length, admission requirements, and labour-market outcomes;
  • C1.4 describe financial-planning strategies for post-secondary education (e.g., RESP, OSAP, scholarships, part-time work, family contributions);
  • C2.1 use a decision-making framework (e.g., 6-step model — frame, gather, evaluate, decide, implement, review) for pathway choices;
  • C2.2 identify and account for bias and noise in personal decision-making;
  • C2.3 articulate the trade-offs of pathway options against personal goals;
  • C3.1 design a SMART, backwards-planned, multi-year pathway plan with milestones and contingencies;
  • C3.2 prepare key transition documents (cover letter, resume, post-secondary application, scholarship application, interview preparation);
  • C3.3 use a transitions framework (e.g., Schlossberg's 4-S — Situation, Self, Support, Strategies) to plan the move from secondary school to next steps;
  • C3.4 maintain a portfolio (digital or physical) documenting growth, artefacts, and evidence for future applications.
Signature task (Pathway Plan): Each student produces a personal Pathway Plan — a multi-page document covering self-assessment summary, target pathway(s), SMART goals over 1 / 3 / 5 years, a portfolio inventory, key transition documents (cover letter + resume), and a Schlossberg-based transitions reflection. The Plan synthesises all three strands.

10. Evaluation Policy (70/30)

  • Term Work — 70% of final mark, comprising unit tests, reflective journal entries, communication artefacts (email, presentation, interview practice), and ongoing Pathway Plan artefacts. Most-recent and most-consistent evidence is used.
  • Final Evaluation — 30% of final mark, comprising the final Pathway Plan submission and presentation (15%) and a Final Examination (15%).

11. Assessment Practices

  • Assessment AS Learning — student-directed self-assessment (practice quizzes, learning-style and values inventories, self-rubrics on plan drafts).
  • Assessment FOR Learning — descriptive teacher feedback that informs next steps (diagnostic on prior GLC2O knowledge, feedback on resume draft, mid-plan review).
  • Assessment OF Learning — graded summative (unit tests, final Pathway Plan, presentation, final exam).

12. Differentiated Instruction & Universal Design for Learning

The course offers multiple means of representation (videos, infographics, captioned podcasts, labour-market dashboards), multiple means of engagement (student-selected pathway focus), and multiple means of expression (essay, portfolio, podcast, presentation, video). Where appropriate, the course recognises Indigenous, Black, and equity-deserving perspectives on career, vocation, and contribution to community.

13. Learning Skills & Work Habits

Reported separately on the report card: responsibility, organization, independent work, collaboration, initiative, self-regulation. Especially important in GWL3O for sustaining the multi-week Pathway Plan and for collaborative work in communication and teamwork tasks.
