Course Overview

SCH3U focuses on the concepts and theories that form the basis of chemistry. Students explore matter and chemical bonding, chemical reactions, quantities in chemical reactions, solutions and solubility, and atmospheric chemistry, and develop the problem-solving and laboratory skills needed for further study of chemistry. The course emphasizes the importance of chemistry in everyday life and the connection between chemistry and the environment.

Prerequisite: SNC2D — Science, Grade 10, Academic.

Total hours: 110  |  Credit value: 1.0

Fundamental Concepts

  • Matter — has distinctive structures and properties that determine how it behaves.
  • Systems and Interactions — chemical systems behave in predictable ways governed by quantitative relationships.
  • Structure and Function — the structure of substances determines their physical and chemical properties.
  • Energy and Change — energy is conserved, and its transfer drives chemical change.
  • Sustainability and Stewardship — choices made today about chemical use affect environmental and human health.

Big Ideas

Matter, Chemical Trends & Bonding: Every element has predictable chemical and physical properties determined by its structure. The type of chemical bond in a compound determines the physical and chemical properties of that compound.
Chemical Reactions: Chemicals react in predictable ways. Chemical reactions and their applications have significant implications for society and the environment.
Quantities in Chemical Reactions: Relationships in chemical reactions can be described quantitatively. The efficiency of chemical reactions can be determined and optimized by applying an understanding of quantitative relationships in such reactions.
Solutions & Solubility: Properties of solutions can be described qualitatively and quantitatively, and can be predicted. Living things depend for their survival on the unique physical and chemical properties of water.
Gases & Atmospheric Chemistry: Properties of gases can be described qualitatively and quantitatively, and can be predicted. Air quality can be affected by human activities and has a significant impact on the environment.

Strands and Specific Expectations (Ontario 2008)

Strand A — Scientific Investigation Skills and Career Exploration (embedded across all units)

A1 Investigation Skills: Initiating and planning; performing and recording; analysing and interpreting; communicating findings.

A2 Career Exploration: Identify and describe a variety of careers related to chemistry (e.g., pharmacy technician, water-treatment operator, food scientist, forensic analyst, chemical engineer, environmental chemist), and the educational pathways to those careers.

Strand B — Matter, Chemical Trends, and Chemical Bonding (Unit 1)

B1 STSE: Analyse the properties of commonly used chemical substances and their effects on society and the environment, and propose ways to lessen their impact.

B2 Skills: Investigate physical and chemical properties of elements and compounds; use the periodic table to predict trends; demonstrate understanding of ionic and covalent bonding.

B3 Knowledge: Atomic theory development (Dalton, Thomson, Rutherford, Bohr); periodic trends (atomic radius, ionization energy, electron affinity, electronegativity); ionic vs covalent bonding; Lewis structures; VSEPR molecular shapes; intermolecular forces (London dispersion, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonding); polarity.

Strand C — Chemical Reactions (Unit 2)

C1 STSE: Analyse chemical reactions used in a variety of applications (e.g., combustion engines, fireworks, batteries), and assess their impact on society and the environment.

C2 Skills: Investigate, through inquiry, different types of chemical reactions; observe and record evidence of chemical change.

C3 Knowledge: Classification of chemical reactions (synthesis, decomposition, single displacement, double displacement, combustion, neutralization); balancing chemical equations; predicting products; activity series; net ionic equations; introduction to acid-base reactions.

Strand D — Quantities in Chemical Reactions (Unit 3)

D1 STSE: Analyse processes in the home, the workplace, and the environmental sector that use chemical quantities and calculations, and assess the importance of quantitative accuracy in industrial chemical processes.

D2 Skills: Investigate quantitative relationships in chemical reactions, including limiting reactant and percent yield.

D3 Knowledge: Mole concept; Avogadro's constant; molar mass; percent composition; empirical and molecular formulas; stoichiometric calculations (mass, moles, particles, gas volumes at STP); limiting reactant; theoretical, actual, and percent yield; introduction to atom economy.

Strand E — Solutions and Solubility (Unit 4)

E1 STSE: Analyse the origins and effects of water pollution, and a variety of economic, social, and environmental issues related to drinking water.

E2 Skills: Investigate qualitative and quantitative properties of solutions; investigate the concentration of an acidic or basic solution.

E3 Knowledge: Solution terminology (solute, solvent, saturated, unsaturated, supersaturated, miscible/immiscible); solubility curves; effect of temperature/pressure; solubility rules and precipitation; concentration units (mol/L, % m/v, % v/v, ppm, ppb); dilution \(C_1V_1=C_2V_2\); Arrhenius acid-base theory; pH scale; strong vs weak acids/bases; neutralization stoichiometry; titration.

Strand F — Gases and Atmospheric Chemistry (Unit 5)

F1 STSE: Analyse the cumulative effects of human activities and technologies on air quality, and describe some Canadian initiatives to reduce air pollution, including ways to reduce their own carbon footprint.

F2 Skills: Investigate gas laws (Boyle's, Charles's, Gay-Lussac's, combined, and ideal gas) experimentally and quantitatively.

F3 Knowledge: Kinetic molecular theory of gases; the gas laws (Boyle's \(PV=k\); Charles's \(V/T=k\); Gay-Lussac's \(P/T=k\); combined \(PV/T=k\)); standard temperature and pressure (STP, SATP); molar volume at STP (22.4 L/mol); ideal gas law \(PV=nRT\); stoichiometry involving gases; air quality, greenhouse effect, ozone layer chemistry, acid precipitation.

70/30 Policy

The final mark consists of two components: 70% term mark (based on assessment OF learning throughout the course, with emphasis on the most consistent or most recent evidence) and 30% summative (a final examination, performance task, or combination thereof administered near the end of the course). Both components reflect all four achievement-chart categories with equal weighting (K/U 25%, T 25%, C 25%, A 25%).

Scientific Investigation Skills (Strand A)

  • Initiating & Planning: formulate testable questions and predictions; design controlled experiments; select equipment.
  • Performing & Recording: follow safe lab procedures (WHMIS, SDS); record qualitative and quantitative observations.
  • Analysing & Interpreting: analyse data using appropriate mathematical and statistical methods (significant figures, percent error/yield, graphing).
  • Communicating: use scientific terminology, conventions, and SI units; produce lab reports and formal presentations.

STSE Connections

  • Properties of consumer chemicals (cleaning agents, cosmetics, food additives) and safe handling under WHMIS.
  • Chemical reactions in industry: combustion of fossil fuels, neutralization of industrial waste, batteries.
  • Quantitative chemistry in pharmaceuticals, agriculture (fertilizer composition), and analytical labs.
  • Drinking water treatment, water-quality monitoring, and Canadian Drinking Water Guidelines (ppm scales).
  • Atmospheric science: stratospheric ozone (Montreal Protocol), tropospheric smog, greenhouse gases, acid precipitation.
