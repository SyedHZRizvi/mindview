This course enables students to extend their knowledge of functions. Students will investigate and apply the properties of polynomial, exponential, and trigonometric functions; continue to represent functions numerically, graphically, and algebraically; develop facility in simplifying expressions and solving equations; and solve problems that address applications of algebra, trigonometry, vectors, and geometry. Students will reason mathematically and communicate their thinking as they solve multi-step problems. This course prepares students for a variety of college technology programs.

2. Strands & Expectations

Strand A — Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

Overall Expectations: solve problems involving exponential equations graphically, including problems arising from real-world applications; solve problems involving exponential equations algebraically, including those that require the manipulation of expressions; and demonstrate an understanding of the relationships between exponential expressions and logarithmic expressions.

Key Specific Expectations

  • A1.1 Determine, through investigation, the value of a power with a rational exponent (e.g., \( a^{m/n} = (\sqrt[n]{a})^{m} \)).
  • A1.2 Simplify algebraic expressions containing integer and rational exponents using the laws of exponents.
  • A1.3 Solve simple exponential equations numerically and graphically using a graphing tool, and identify the meaning of the solution in context.
  • A2.1 Recognize the logarithm of a number to a base as the exponent to which the base must be raised to produce the number; evaluate simple logarithmic expressions.
  • A2.2 Determine, with technology, the approximate value of the logarithm of a number to base 10 or to base \( e \).
  • A2.3 Make connections between related logarithmic and exponential equations (\( y = b^{x} \Leftrightarrow x = \log_{b} y \)).
  • A3.1 Solve exponential equations by rewriting them with a common base.
  • A3.2 Solve exponential equations using logarithms (e.g., \( 5^{x} = 30 \Rightarrow x = \log 30 / \log 5 \)).
  • A3.4 Pose and solve problems involving exponential growth and decay (compound interest, half-life, depreciation).

Strand B — Polynomial Functions, Equations, and Inequalities

Overall Expectations: solve problems involving polynomial equations arising from real-world applications, and demonstrate an understanding of the remainder and factor theorems; solve polynomial inequalities both algebraically and graphically; and demonstrate an understanding of operations on polynomial expressions.

Key Specific Expectations

  • B1.1 Expand and simplify polynomial expressions involving one variable.
  • B1.2 Factor polynomial expressions involving one variable using common factoring, grouping, trinomial factoring, and difference of squares/cubes.
  • B1.4 Determine, through investigation, the remainder when a polynomial \( f(x) \) is divided by \( x - a \), and verify the remainder theorem (\( f(a) = R \)).
  • B2.1 Factor polynomial expressions of degree three or four using the factor theorem and the rational zero theorem.
  • B2.2 Determine, by factoring, the real roots of polynomial equations of degree no higher than four.
  • B2.3 Determine the family of polynomial equations whose graphs share given zeros.
  • B3.1 Solve linear and polynomial inequalities of degree no higher than three by graphing or using sign analysis.
  • B3.2 Determine, through investigation, the intervals over which a polynomial function is positive or negative, using a graph or a sign chart.

Strand C — Trigonometric Functions

Overall Expectations: determine the values of the trigonometric ratios for angles less than 360°, and solve problems using the primary trigonometric ratios, the sine law, and the cosine law; make connections between the numeric, graphical, and algebraic representations of sinusoidal functions; and identify and represent sinusoidal functions, and solve problems involving sinusoidal functions, including those arising from real-world applications.

Key Specific Expectations

  • C1.1 Determine the exact values of the sine, cosine, and tangent ratios of the special angles 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, and their multiples less than 360°.
  • C1.2 Determine the values of the trigonometric ratios for any angle between 0° and 360° using the unit circle and CAST rule.
  • C1.4 Solve problems involving oblique triangles in two and three dimensions using the sine law (including the ambiguous case) and the cosine law.
  • C2.1 Make connections between the sine ratio and the sine function and between the cosine ratio and the cosine function by graphing \( y=\sin x \) and \( y=\cos x \).
  • C2.2 Sketch the graphs of \( f(x)=a\sin(k(x-d))+c \) and \( f(x)=a\cos(k(x-d))+c \); identify and explain the role of each parameter.
  • C2.5 Determine the amplitude, period, phase shift, equation of the axis, domain, and range of a sinusoidal function.
  • C3.1 Collect data from periodic real-world phenomena (ferris wheels, tides, daylight) and represent them by sinusoidal functions.
  • C3.4 Pose and solve sinusoidal modelling problems graphically and algebraically.

Strand D — Applications of Geometry

Overall Expectations: represent vectors, add and subtract vectors, and solve problems using vector models, including those arising from real-world applications; solve problems involving two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional figures and arising from real-world applications; and determine circle properties and solve related problems, including those arising from real-world applications.

Key Specific Expectations

  • D1.1 Recognize the difference between scalar and vector quantities, and represent vectors as directed line segments in two dimensions.
  • D1.2 Resolve a vector into its horizontal and vertical components using trigonometry.
  • D1.4 Add and subtract two-dimensional vectors using parallelogram and triangle methods, and solve problems involving force and velocity.
  • D2.1 Determine the surface area and volume of pyramids, cones, spheres, and composite figures, using formulas and modelling software.
  • D2.2 Solve problems involving the optimization of perimeter, area, surface area, and volume in real-world contexts (manufacturing, packaging).
  • D3.1 Recognize the major properties of circles (chord-perpendicular bisector, inscribed angle, tangent-radius, cyclic quadrilateral) and apply them in solving problems.
  • D3.3 Solve problems involving the properties of circles and arcs in real-world contexts (gears, pulleys, architectural design).

3. Mathematical Processes

The seven mathematical processes are integrated into every strand. Students engage in:

Problem SolvingDevelop, select, apply, compare, and adapt strategies to investigate situations and solve problems.
Reasoning & ProvingDevelop and apply reasoning skills to make and investigate conjectures and to construct and defend arguments.
ReflectingDemonstrate that they are reflecting on and monitoring their thinking to clarify understanding.
Selecting Tools & StrategiesUse a variety of concrete, visual, and electronic tools (Desmos, GeoGebra, CAD software, scientific calculators) and computational strategies.
ConnectingMake connections among mathematical concepts and procedures, and relate ideas across strands and to technology applications.
RepresentingCreate a variety of representations (numeric, geometric, algebraic, graphical) and translate between them.
CommunicatingCommunicate mathematical thinking orally, visually, and in writing using appropriate vocabulary, notation, and conventions of technical practice.

4. Achievement Chart

Per Growing Success (2010), each of the four categories carries equal weight (25%) within both term work and the final evaluation.

Knowledge & Understanding
25%

Subject-specific content acquired (knowledge) and the comprehension of its meaning and significance (understanding). Includes definitions, formulas, computations, and identifying types of functions or geometric figures.

Thinking & Inquiry
25%

Use of critical and creative thinking skills and processes — planning, processing, making connections, justifying conclusions in novel and multi-step technical problems.

Communication
25%

Conveying meaning through various forms — clarity of expression, use of mathematical conventions, technical vocabulary, terminology, and labelled diagrams, sketches, or scale drawings.

Application
25%

Use of knowledge and skills to make connections within and between contexts — real-world technology modelling, transfer of concepts to manufacturing, electronics, construction, and design.

Levels of achievement: Level 4 (80–100%, exceeds standard) · Level 3 (70–79%, provincial standard) · Level 2 (60–69%, approaching) · Level 1 (50–59%, limited) · R (below 50%, insufficient).
