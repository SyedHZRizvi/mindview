HFN3M — Nutrition and Health, Grade 11 University/College Preparation

Curriculum Policy Document · Ontario Ministry of Education · The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 9–12: Social Sciences and Humanities (2013, Revised) · Aligned to Growing Success (2010)

Course Code: HFN3M  |  Credit Value: 1.0  |  Hours: 110  |  Prerequisite: None

1. Course Description

This course examines the role of nutrition in promoting health and preventing disease throughout the life cycle. Students will evaluate the relationship between nutrition, food choices, body composition, and overall well-being; investigate the influence of social, economic, cultural, and scientific factors on Canadian food choices; and develop research and inquiry skills required to access, evaluate, and apply credible nutrition information. The course emphasises Canada's Food Guide (2019), the Canadian Nutrient File, and food-safety practices recommended by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency and Health Canada.

3. Fundamental Concepts

ConceptApplication in HFN3M
Personal & Societal Well-BeingNutrition affects individual health (energy, growth, disease risk) and population-level outcomes (chronic disease rates, food security).
Diversity, Interdependence, & Global ConnectionsFood choices reflect culture, religion, geography, and economics; Canada's food system is part of a global supply chain.
Power & GovernanceHealth Canada, CFIA, and provincial agencies regulate food safety, labelling, and advertising; consumers exercise agency through informed choice.
Continuity & ChangeDietary guidelines and food culture evolve (e.g., 1992 vs 2007 vs 2019 Food Guide; rise of plant-based eating); biology of digestion and metabolism remains constant.

4. Big Ideas (by Strand)

  • Research & Inquiry (A): Reliable nutrition knowledge comes from sound research methods, peer-reviewed evidence, and critical evaluation of sources.
  • Nutrition & Health (B): Nutrients have specific physiological roles; deficiencies and excesses produce predictable, measurable health consequences.
  • Food Habits & Sources (C): Personal food choices are shaped by physiology, culture, economy, environment, and the food industry; food can be safely produced, prepared, and stored using known principles.
  • Trends, Issues & Wellness (D): Contemporary food trends and issues (food insecurity, sustainability, dietary patterns, marketing, eating disorders) profoundly affect individual and community wellness.

5. Strand A — Research & Inquiry Skills (embedded across all units)

A1. Exploring

  • A1.1 explore a variety of nutrition topics and develop research questions; A1.2 identify key concepts.

A2. Investigating

  • A2.1–A2.4 plan inquiries; create research plans; locate primary (interviews, surveys, food-frequency questionnaires, lab analysis) and secondary sources (Health Canada, Statistics Canada, peer-reviewed journals); record information.

A3. Processing Information

  • A3.1–A3.5 analyse and synthesise information; evaluate credibility, bias, and conflicts of interest; recognise pseudoscience and food myths; draw evidence-based conclusions.

A4. Communicating & Reflecting

  • A4.1–A4.5 communicate using accurate nutrition terminology; use a variety of formats (lab reports, infographics, presentations, podcasts); cite sources; reflect on learning.

Career Connections

Registered dietitian, nutritionist, food scientist, public-health nurse, food-service manager, chef, food-policy analyst, agricultural researcher, food journalist, nutrition labelling specialist, sport-nutrition consultant, community-health worker.

6. Strand B — Nutrition & Health (Chapters 1–2)

Overall Expectations

  • B1. demonstrate an understanding of the function and importance of the various nutrients required for personal health;
  • B2. demonstrate an understanding of the processes involved in digestion, absorption, and metabolism;
  • B3. demonstrate an understanding of the relationship between nutrition and physical, mental, social, and emotional well-being throughout the life cycle.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • B1.1 identify the six classes of nutrients (carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, vitamins, minerals, water) and explain their primary functions.
  • B1.2 distinguish among monosaccharides, disaccharides, and polysaccharides; describe sources, glycemic effect, and the role of fibre.
  • B1.3 distinguish among saturated, monounsaturated, polyunsaturated, and trans fats; describe essential fatty acids (omega-3, omega-6).
  • B1.4 explain the role of complete vs incomplete proteins; identify essential and non-essential amino acids; describe protein-complementation.
  • B1.5 describe fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) and water-soluble (B-complex, C) vitamins, and major and trace minerals (Ca, Fe, Na, K, Zn, I).
  • B2.1 trace the path of food through the GI tract; identify mechanical and chemical digestion at each stage.
  • B2.2 describe absorption and transport (villi, microvilli, lymph, hepatic portal vein); explain ATP production via cellular respiration.
  • B2.3 explain basal metabolic rate (BMR), the thermic effect of food, physical-activity energy expenditure, and total daily energy expenditure.
  • B3.1 explain nutritional needs across the lifespan (infancy, childhood, adolescence, adulthood, pregnancy, aging).
STSE focus: trans-fat regulation in Canada (2018 ban); fibre and colorectal cancer; iron-deficiency anaemia in adolescent menstruators; vitamin D and northern populations.

7. Strand C — Food Habits & Sources (Chapters 3–4)

Overall Expectations

  • C1. demonstrate an understanding of the various factors (biological, psychological, social, cultural, economic, environmental) that influence food choices;
  • C2. demonstrate an understanding of the sources of food and the means by which food gets from the source to the table;
  • C3. demonstrate an understanding of food preparation, food safety, and food preservation techniques.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • C1.1 identify factors influencing personal food choices (taste, hunger, cost, time, culture, religion, ethics, marketing).
  • C1.2 evaluate the nutritional content of meal plans and individual diets using Canada's Food Guide (2019), the Canadian Nutrient File, and the Nutrition Facts table.
  • C1.3 plan menus that meet the needs of specific groups (athletes, vegetarians, pregnant women, older adults, people with food allergies, diabetes).
  • C2.1 describe the path of selected foods (e.g., wheat, beef, apples, salmon) from the farm/fishery to the consumer.
  • C2.2 evaluate the effects of food processing (refining, fortification, additives, packaging) on nutritional quality.
  • C3.1 demonstrate appropriate food-preparation techniques (knife skills, dry-heat vs moist-heat cooking, leavening, emulsification).
  • C3.2 apply food-safety practices (the "Clean–Separate–Cook–Chill" rule; the temperature danger zone 4–60 °C; safe internal temperatures).
  • C3.3 describe methods of food preservation (canning, freezing, drying, fermentation, irradiation) and their effect on nutrients.
STSE focus: the 2008 Maple Leaf listeriosis outbreak; mandatory nutrition-facts labelling; pesticide residues and the "Dirty Dozen" list; bisphenol-A (BPA) in food packaging.

8. Strand D — Trends, Issues & Wellness (Chapter 5)

Overall Expectations

  • D1. demonstrate an understanding of current trends and issues affecting the food choices and the health of Canadians and others around the world;
  • D2. demonstrate an understanding of the relationship between nutrition, body image, and physical/mental health, including eating disorders;
  • D3. demonstrate an understanding of the role of public policy, food security, and sustainability in supporting wellness.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • D1.1 analyse current trends (plant-based eating, "clean eating", intermittent fasting, ultra-processed foods, functional foods, food fortification).
  • D1.2 evaluate diets and dietary patterns (Mediterranean, DASH, Mediterranean, vegan, vegetarian, Indigenous foodways) for nutritional adequacy.
  • D1.3 critically analyse food advertising and marketing, including its effect on children, adolescents, and body image.
  • D2.1 identify and describe disordered eating and eating disorders (anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder, ARFID).
  • D2.2 evaluate the relationship between body image, self-esteem, and the media's portrayal of "ideal" bodies.
  • D3.1 analyse food security and food insecurity in Canada and globally; describe the role of food banks, school nutrition programs, and policy.
  • D3.2 evaluate the environmental sustainability of food systems (greenhouse-gas emissions, water use, land use, biodiversity).
  • D3.3 identify the role of Indigenous foodways and food sovereignty in Canada.
STSE focus: the 2022 Canadian Community Health Survey on food insecurity (~17% of households); methane emissions from beef and the "planetary health diet"; restrictions on marketing unhealthy foods to children (Bill S-228).

11. Evaluation Policy (70/30)

  • Term Work — 70% of the final mark, comprised of chapter tests, performance tasks, labs, assignments, and inquiry projects. Most-recent and most-consistent evidence is used.
  • Final Evaluation — 30% of the final mark, comprised of a culminating performance task and/or a final examination near the end of the course.

All four Achievement Chart categories are addressed throughout, in both Term Work and Final Evaluation.

12. Assessment Practices

  • Assessment AS Learning — student-directed self-assessment (practice quizzes, reflection journals, food-intake records with self-evaluation).
  • Assessment FOR Learning — diagnostic, descriptive teacher feedback (no mark); informs instruction.
  • Assessment OF Learning — graded summative (chapter tests, performance tasks, final exam).

13. Learning Skills & Work Habits

Reported separately on the report card: responsibility, organisation, independent work, collaboration, initiative, self-regulation. Particularly relevant in food labs (responsibility for safe handling and cleanup), inquiry projects (organisation, independent work), and meal-planning collaborations.
