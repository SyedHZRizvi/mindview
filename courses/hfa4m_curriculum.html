HFA4M — Nutrition and Health Issues, Grade 12 University/College Preparation

Curriculum Policy Document · Ontario Ministry of Education · The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 9 to 12: Social Sciences and Humanities (2013, Revised) · Aligned to Growing Success (2010)

Course Code: HFA4M  |  Credit Value: 1.0  |  Hours: 110  |  Prerequisite: any university, university/college, or college preparation course in social sciences and humanities, English, or Canadian and world studies

1. Course Description

This course examines the relationships between food, energy balance, and nutritional status; the nutritional needs of individuals at different stages of life; and the role of nutrition in health and disease. Students will evaluate nutrition-related trends, conduct research on local and global food issues, and analyse the impact of marketing, food production, and food policy on the eating habits and health of Canadians. The course is intended to prepare students for postsecondary programs in food and nutrition, food science, dietetics, public health, kinesiology, and the hospitality industry.

3. Big Ideas (by Strand)

  • Research & Inquiry (A): Effective research uses appropriate methods, ethical practices, and credible sources to investigate nutrition and health issues.
  • Determinants of Nutritional Health (B): Nutrition is shaped by interacting biological, psychological, social, cultural, environmental, and economic factors.
  • Nutritional Issues Across the Lifespan (C): Nutritional needs and risks change throughout life — from pregnancy and infancy through to older adulthood.
  • Local & Global Food Issues (D): Food production, distribution, security, and sustainability are linked — local choices have global consequences and global forces shape local options.

4. Strand A — Research and Inquiry Skills (embedded across all units)

A1. Exploring

  • A1.1 explore a variety of topics related to nutrition and health to identify topics for research and inquiry
  • A1.2 identify key concepts related to chosen topics
  • A1.3 formulate effective questions to guide research

A2. Investigating

  • A2.1–A2.5 create research plans, locate and select information from a variety of primary and secondary sources, evaluate sources for credibility/bias/accuracy, record information accurately and ethically, distinguish among facts, theories, and opinions

A3. Processing Information

  • A3.1–A3.6 analyse and interpret research findings, demonstrate academic honesty and use proper documentation (e.g., APA), use quantitative and qualitative data, synthesise findings, formulate conclusions, draw connections between the personal and the larger context

A4. Communicating and Reflecting

  • A4.1–A4.4 use appropriate formats (essays, reports, multimedia presentations, debates), terminology of food, nutrition, and health, reflect on inquiry skills, identify careers in nutrition and food-related fields

Career Connections

Registered Dietitian (RD), food scientist, public-health nutritionist, food-industry researcher, chef/culinary educator, kinesiologist, epidemiologist, food-policy analyst, agricultural specialist, sustainable-food entrepreneur, nutrition journalist.

5. Strand B — Determinants of Nutritional Health (Unit 1)

Overall Expectations

  • B1. demonstrate an understanding of the factors that influence individuals' food choices and eating habits;
  • B2. demonstrate an understanding of the nutrients required for proper nutrition and good health, and of how the body uses them;
  • B3. demonstrate an understanding of Canadian nutritional guidelines and their use in maintaining good health.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • B1.1 describe biological, social, cultural, economic, and environmental factors that influence food choices;
  • B2.1 explain the structure and function of the six classes of nutrients — carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals, and water;
  • B2.2 explain how the body digests, absorbs, transports, and metabolises macronutrients;
  • B2.3 describe energy balance: kilocalories in vs out, BMR, thermic effect of food, physical-activity expenditure;
  • B3.1 describe Canada's Food Guide and its application; B3.2 interpret Dietary Reference Intakes (DRIs: RDA, AI, UL, EAR);
  • B3.4 use nutrition facts tables and ingredient lists to make informed food choices.
STSE focus: ultra-processed foods and the NOVA classification; the obesogenic environment; Health Canada's 2019 Food Guide revisions; nutritional supplements and pseudoscience.

6. Strand C — Nutritional Issues Across the Lifespan (Units 2 & 3)

Overall Expectations

  • C1. demonstrate an understanding of the changing nutritional needs of individuals at various life stages;
  • C2. demonstrate an understanding of food choices and nutritional issues from infancy through to old age;
  • C3. demonstrate an understanding of the relationships between diet and various health issues, including diet-related diseases.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • C1.1 describe the nutritional requirements of pregnancy and lactation (folate, iron, kcal, protein);
  • C1.2 describe infant nutrition (exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months, complementary feeding, allergens);
  • C1.3 describe nutritional needs of children, adolescents, adults, and older adults;
  • C2.3 explain the etiology and warning signs of eating disorders (anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating disorder, ARFID);
  • C3.1 explain the role of diet in cardiovascular disease (saturated/trans fats, sodium, fibre, omega-3);
  • C3.2 explain the role of diet in type 2 diabetes (refined carbohydrates, glycemic index, fibre, insulin resistance);
  • C3.3 explain the role of diet in cancer (fibre, processed meat, alcohol, antioxidants);
  • C3.4 describe causes and prevention of foodborne illness (HACCP, the temperature danger zone 4–60 °C).
STSE focus: infant-formula marketing and the WHO Code; childhood-obesity policy; the food industry and chronic disease; mental-health and disordered eating; older-adult sarcopenia and protein.

7. Strand D — Local and Global Food Issues (Units 4 & 5)

Overall Expectations

  • D1. demonstrate an understanding of food production and food systems in Canada and around the world;
  • D2. demonstrate an understanding of issues related to access to safe and nutritious food at the local, national, and global levels;
  • D3. demonstrate an understanding of factors affecting world hunger, food security, and food sustainability.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • D1.1 describe Canadian food-production systems (supply management, agribusiness, family farms);
  • D1.2 evaluate conventional vs organic vs regenerative agriculture, GMO use, pesticide regulation (PMRA);
  • D1.3 describe food processing, labelling, and the role of the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA);
  • D2.1 analyse household food insecurity in Canada (PROOF data, social-assistance gaps, northern food costs);
  • D2.2 explain Indigenous food sovereignty and the impact of colonisation on traditional food systems;
  • D3.1 explain the difference between hunger, undernutrition, and malnutrition (PEM: kwashiorkor, marasmus; hidden hunger: iodine, vitamin A, iron);
  • D3.2 analyse climate change's impact on agriculture and food security;
  • D3.3 evaluate UN Sustainable Development Goal 2 (Zero Hunger) and food-aid programs.
STSE focus: food deserts; food waste (Canada wastes ~58% of food produced); fair-trade certification; plant-based diets and greenhouse-gas emissions; the COVID-19 disruption to food supply chains; First Nations boil-water advisories and food sovereignty.

10. Evaluation Policy (70/30)

  • Term Work — 70% of final mark, comprised of unit tests, research projects, performance tasks (e.g., menu plans, food-label audits), assignments. Most-recent and most-consistent evidence is used.
  • Final Evaluation — 30% of final mark, comprised of a culminating performance task and/or a final examination administered near the end of the course.

All four Achievement Chart categories are addressed throughout, in both Term Work and Final Evaluation.

11. Assessment Practices

  • Assessment AS Learning — student-directed self-assessment (practice quizzes, food-diary reflection, peer review).
  • Assessment FOR Learning — diagnostic, descriptive teacher feedback (no mark); informs instruction.
  • Assessment OF Learning — graded summative (unit tests, research papers, final exam).

12. Learning Skills & Work Habits

Reported separately on the report card: responsibility, organization, independent work, collaboration, initiative, self-regulation.
