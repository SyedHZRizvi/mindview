This course provides an overview of the importance of international business and trade in the global economy and explores the factors that influence success in international markets. Students will learn about the techniques and strategies associated with marketing, distribution, and managing international business effectively. This course prepares students for postsecondary programs in business, including international business, marketing, and management.

2. Strands & Overall Expectations

Strand A — The Global Business Environment

Overall Expectations: students will analyse how the changing global business environment affects national and international business activity; analyse the impact of geographic, economic, political, cultural, and social factors on international business; assess the impact of trade agreements and trading blocs on international business.

Key Specific Expectations

  • A1.1 Describe the importance of international business to Canadians and the Canadian economy (e.g., share of GDP, jobs supported by exports/imports).
  • A1.2 Analyse the forces driving globalization (technology, transport, deregulation, multilateral agreements, foreign direct investment).
  • A1.3 Identify and describe the main forms of international business activity (exporting, importing, licensing, franchising, joint ventures, FDI, e-commerce).
  • A1.4 Evaluate the impact of multinational corporations (MNEs) on host countries and on the home economy.
  • A2.1 Analyse how geography (location, climate, natural resources, infrastructure, time zones) affects international business activity.
  • A2.2 Compare political and legal systems in selected countries and assess how they affect risk and opportunity (political risk, rule of law, intellectual-property protection).
  • A2.3 Compare economic systems (market, command, mixed, traditional) and economic indicators (GDP, GNI per capita, HDI, inflation, unemployment).
  • A2.4 Analyse the impact of socio-cultural factors (language, religion, customs, values, demographics) on international business decisions.
  • A3.1 Describe the role of major trade organizations (WTO, IMF, World Bank) and trade agreements (CUSMA/USMCA, CETA, CPTPP).
  • A3.2 Compare trading blocs (EU, ASEAN, Mercosur) and assess their effects on member and non-member firms.

Strand B — International Marketing

Overall Expectations: students will analyse considerations in conducting market research in international markets; demonstrate an understanding of the elements of the marketing mix and explain how marketing strategies are influenced by international issues; analyse the role of branding and product/promotion adaptation in international markets.

Key Specific Expectations

  • B1.1 Explain the importance of international market research; compare primary and secondary research methods used to assess foreign markets.
  • B1.2 Describe sources of secondary international data (Statistics Canada, Trade Commissioner Service, UN Comtrade, World Bank, country-specific agencies).
  • B1.3 Analyse the steps and challenges of cross-border market segmentation and targeting (demographic, psychographic, geographic, behavioural).
  • B2.1 Analyse how the four Ps of the marketing mix (product, price, place, promotion) must be adapted for international markets.
  • B2.2 Compare standardization and adaptation (the global/local debate) and explain when each is preferred.
  • B2.3 Describe pricing strategies for international markets (cost-plus, market-based, penetration, skimming) and factors that affect price (duties, FX, distribution).
  • B2.4 Analyse promotion strategies in foreign markets, including advertising, sales promotion, public relations, and digital marketing — and the influence of culture on messaging.
  • B3.1 Analyse how branding decisions (brand name, packaging, labelling) must respond to language, regulation, and culture.
  • B3.2 Explain the steps and ethical considerations in adapting a product for international markets (translation, units, safety standards, religious sensitivity).

Strand C — Sales, Production & Distribution

Overall Expectations: students will compare modes of entry into foreign markets and assess the factors that influence the choice; analyse the international distribution system and the role of intermediaries; analyse the methods used to transport goods to and from international markets.

Key Specific Expectations

  • C1.1 Compare modes of market entry (direct exporting, indirect exporting via agents/brokers, licensing, franchising, contract manufacturing, joint venture, FDI).
  • C1.2 Analyse the factors affecting the choice of entry mode (control, capital, risk, speed, knowledge of market).
  • C1.3 Explain the role of trade missions, export promotion agencies, and bilateral chambers of commerce in opening foreign markets.
  • C2.1 Describe the structure of a typical international distribution channel and the role of importers, agents, wholesalers, and retailers.
  • C2.2 Analyse global supply-chain decisions: sourcing, production location, lean vs resilient supply chains.
  • C2.3 Compare modes of international transport (ocean, air, rail, road, multimodal, intermodal containers) and the cost/time/risk trade-offs.
  • C2.4 Describe the role of freight forwarders, customs brokers, and 3PL providers.
  • C3.1 Explain Incoterms 2020 (EXW, FCA, FOB, CIF, DDP, etc.) and how they allocate cost, risk, and responsibility.
  • C3.2 Analyse the steps in fulfilling an international order from quotation to delivery.

Strand D — Trade Documentation & Finance

Overall Expectations: students will demonstrate an understanding of the documents required in international transactions; describe methods of payment and the role of trade finance in international business; describe the impact of foreign exchange and risk management on international transactions.

Key Specific Expectations

  • D1.1 Describe the purpose and content of core export documents: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, export declaration.
  • D1.2 Explain the role and content of an import permit, customs declaration, and Harmonized System (HS) classification.
  • D1.3 Describe the function of a letter of credit (L/C) and the parties involved (applicant, beneficiary, issuing bank, advising bank, confirming bank).
  • D2.1 Compare the main methods of international payment (cash in advance, letter of credit, documentary collection, open account, consignment) and their risk/cost trade-offs.
  • D2.2 Describe Canadian export-financing supports (EDC, BDC, Export Guarantee Program).
  • D2.3 Analyse the impact of tariffs, duties, taxes, and non-tariff barriers (quotas, embargoes, standards) on cost and competitiveness.
  • D3.1 Explain how foreign-exchange (FX) markets operate, including spot vs forward rates, direct vs indirect quotes, and bid/ask spreads.
  • D3.2 Calculate currency conversions and use forward contracts or natural hedging to manage FX risk.
  • D3.3 Analyse non-financial risks (political, transport, credit, force-majeure) and the role of trade-credit insurance and incoterm choice in mitigating them.

Strand E — Working in International Business

Overall Expectations: students will identify the skills, knowledge, and characteristics required to work in international business; demonstrate an understanding of the ethical and cross-cultural responsibilities of working in international business; design and present a culminating country/market profile that integrates the strands of the course.

Key Specific Expectations

  • E1.1 Identify the skills, knowledge, and personal characteristics (languages, cross-cultural sensitivity, adaptability, negotiation, digital fluency) required for careers in international business.
  • E1.2 Describe career paths in international business (export sales manager, trade commissioner, logistics analyst, FX trader, international marketer, customs broker, supply-chain planner).
  • E1.3 Describe the role of professional designations and certifications (CITP, FITT, CCS).
  • E2.1 Apply intercultural-communication frameworks (Hofstede dimensions, high vs low context, monochronic vs polychronic time) to business situations.
  • E2.2 Analyse the impact of cultural differences on negotiation styles, gift-giving, business etiquette, and dress.
  • E3.1 Describe ethical issues in international business — labour standards, environment, bribery (FCPA, CFPOA), human rights, intellectual-property piracy.
  • E3.2 Analyse corporate social responsibility (CSR), the triple bottom line (people, planet, profit), and the UN Global Compact / Sustainable Development Goals.
  • E4.1 Design and present a culminating country/market profile that integrates research, marketing strategy, entry-mode recommendation, documentation/logistics plan, and ethical analysis.

3. Business-Studies Processes

The Ontario business-studies framework integrates the following processes across all strands:

Inquiry & ResearchIdentify business problems, pose research questions, gather and evaluate primary and secondary data.
Analysing & Decision MakingCompare alternatives, weigh costs and benefits, apply criteria, and recommend a course of action.
CommunicationUse business vocabulary, conventions, and formats (memos, reports, presentations, infographics, country profiles).
Applying & ConnectingRelate concepts to real Canadian and global firms, current events, and personal career interests.
Ethical ReasoningIdentify stakeholders, name competing values, and propose ethically-defensible options.
Cultural AwarenessRecognize the influence of culture on business behaviour and adopt culturally-sensitive practice.
Digital & Quantitative LiteracyUse spreadsheets, trade databases (UN Comtrade, Trade Data Online), and currency converters; interpret graphs and indicators.

4. Achievement Chart

Per Growing Success (2010), each of the four categories carries equal weight (25%) within both term work and the final evaluation.

Knowledge & Understanding
25%

Subject-specific content (vocabulary, frameworks, documents, formulas) and comprehension of meaning — globalization, comparative advantage, marketing mix, Incoterms, FX, HS codes, CSR.

Thinking & Inquiry
25%

Critical and creative thinking — choosing an entry mode, weighing standardization vs adaptation, assessing political risk, designing the culminating country profile.

Communication
25%

Conveying meaning through written reports, oral presentations, infographics, memos, and country profiles; correct business terminology and conventions.

Application
25%

Application to real-world scenarios — interpreting current trade data, completing a sample export document set, currency conversion, recommending a marketing mix.

Levels of achievement: Level 4 (80–100%, exceeds standard) · Level 3 (70–79%, provincial standard) · Level 2 (60–69%, approaching) · Level 1 (50–59%, limited) · R (below 50%, insufficient).

5. Evaluation Policy

Final mark = 70% Term Work + 30% Final Evaluation, per Growing Success (2010).

ComponentWeightDescription
Unit Tests (Ch 1–5, Assessment OF Learning)40%Five chapter unit tests covering all four achievement categories.
Quizzes & Diagnostics (Assessment FOR/AS Learning)10%Formative checks; lowest score dropped.
Case Studies & Country Mini-Profiles10%Real-world cases (Tim Hortons in India, IKEA in China, FX hedging memos).
Pre-Culminating Performance Task10%Trade-documentation simulation: full export-package paperwork for a chosen product.
Subtotal — Term Work70%Reported on the Provincial Report Card
Culminating Country/Market Profile15%Independent inquiry — chosen target country, environmental analysis, marketing mix, entry mode, documentation/logistics plan, ethics, oral defence.
Final Exam (Ch 1–5 comprehensive, 3 hours, 100 marks)15%Equally weighted across K/U, Thinking, Communication, Application (25 marks each).
Final Course Mark100%One credit toward the OSSD; eligible postsecondary business prerequisite.
Note on the Culminating Project: The Culminating Country/Market Profile (Strand E) contributes 15% of the 30% Final Evaluation. The remaining 15% is the comprehensive written final exam covering Chapters 1–5. The profile demonstrates synthesis across Strands A–D and is mandatory for the granting of a credit.
