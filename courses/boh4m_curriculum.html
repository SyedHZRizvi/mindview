This course focuses on the development of leadership skills used in managing a successful business. Students will analyse the role of a leader in business, with a focus on decision making, management of group dynamics, workplace stress and conflict, motivation of employees, and planning. Effective business communication skills, ethics, and social responsibility are also emphasized. Students will learn about the role of a leader through participation in a culminating Leadership Case Study Investigation that integrates management theory with practical application.

3. Strands & Expectations

Strand A — Foundations of Management

Overall Expectations: students will analyse the role and importance of the manager in business; describe the historical development of management thought and contemporary management theories; analyse the functions of management and the skills required of an effective manager.

Key Specific Expectations

  • A1.1 Describe the role of management and the functions performed by managers (planning, organizing, leading, controlling) in organizations.
  • A1.2 Identify and describe the levels of management (top, middle, first-line) and the kinds of tasks each level performs.
  • A1.3 Identify and describe the skills required of an effective manager (Katz: technical, human, conceptual).
  • A1.4 Describe Mintzberg's ten managerial roles (interpersonal, informational, decisional) and provide examples of each in modern workplaces.
  • A2.1 Describe the historical development of management thought (scientific management, administrative principles, human-relations movement, behavioural science, systems theory, contingency theory).
  • A2.2 Compare and contrast major contemporary management approaches (Total Quality Management, learning organizations, lean management, evidence-based management).
  • A2.3 Analyse the impact of internal (mission, culture, resources) and external (economic, technological, socio-cultural, political–legal) factors on management decisions.
  • A2.4 Describe the key features of organizational culture and explain how it influences management practice.

Strand B — Leading

Overall Expectations: students will analyse different leadership styles and theories; demonstrate an understanding of group dynamics, team development, and motivation in the workplace; analyse the impact of issues related to leading and motivating employees in changing workplaces.

Key Specific Expectations

  • B1.1 Distinguish between leadership and management, and explain why both are essential in modern organizations.
  • B1.2 Compare leadership theories: trait, behavioural (Ohio State, University of Michigan, the Managerial Grid), contingency (Fiedler, situational leadership of Hersey & Blanchard), and transformational/transactional models (Burns, Bass).
  • B1.3 Analyse Lewin's leadership styles (autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire) and identify situations in which each is most effective.
  • B1.4 Describe the stages of group/team development (Tuckman: forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning).
  • B2.1 Compare content theories of motivation (Maslow's hierarchy of needs, Alderfer's ERG, McClelland's acquired needs, Herzberg's two-factor) and process theories (equity, expectancy, goal-setting).
  • B2.2 Analyse McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y assumptions about workers and the implications for management style.
  • B2.3 Identify and describe non-monetary rewards, recognition programs, job design (job rotation, enlargement, enrichment), and their impact on motivation.
  • B2.4 Analyse the effect of workplace diversity, generational differences, and Indigenous perspectives on leadership and team functioning.

Strand C — Management Challenges

Overall Expectations: students will demonstrate an understanding of effective communication and decision-making processes in the workplace; analyse common workplace conflicts and the strategies used to resolve them; analyse the management of stress and change in organizations.

Key Specific Expectations

  • C1.1 Describe the communication process (sender, encoding, channel, receiver, decoding, feedback, noise) and identify common barriers to effective communication.
  • C1.2 Compare formal and informal communication channels (upward, downward, horizontal, diagonal, the grapevine) in organizations.
  • C1.3 Demonstrate the skills of active listening, constructive feedback, and clear written and oral business communication.
  • C1.4 Analyse the impact of digital communication tools (email, instant messaging, videoconferencing, social media) on workplace communication.
  • C2.1 Describe the steps of the rational decision-making model (identify problem, gather data, generate alternatives, evaluate, choose, implement, follow up).
  • C2.2 Compare individual and group decision-making, including the benefits and pitfalls of brainstorming, Delphi technique, nominal-group technique, and the risk of groupthink.
  • C2.3 Identify common sources of workplace conflict (task, relationship, process, value) and apply Thomas–Kilmann conflict-handling styles (competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating).
  • C3.1 Describe the causes, signs, and consequences of workplace stress and identify strategies organizations use to support employee well-being and mental health.
  • C3.2 Apply change-management models (Lewin's unfreeze–change–refreeze; Kotter's eight steps) to a real-world change scenario and explain why employees resist change.

Strand D — Planning and Controlling

Overall Expectations: students will demonstrate an understanding of planning, organizing, staffing, and controlling functions in organizations; analyse the impact of business ethics and corporate social responsibility on management decisions; communicate the findings of a culminating leadership case-study investigation.

Key Specific Expectations

  • D1.1 Describe types of plans (strategic, tactical, operational, contingency, single-use, standing) and the time horizons of each.
  • D1.2 Apply the SWOT framework and SMART-goal criteria (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound) to a business scenario.
  • D1.3 Compare organizational structures (functional, divisional, matrix, network, team-based) and explain trade-offs in centralization, span of control, and chain of command.
  • D1.4 Describe the recruitment-and-selection process (job analysis, posting, screening, interviewing, testing, reference checks, offer) and the principles of fair hiring under the Ontario Human Rights Code.
  • D1.5 Describe orientation, training methods (on-the-job, classroom, e-learning, mentoring, coaching), and performance-appraisal techniques (rating scales, MBO, 360-degree).
  • D2.1 Describe the four steps of the control process (establish standards, measure performance, compare to standard, take corrective action).
  • D2.2 Compare financial controls (budgets, ratio analysis), operational controls (TQM, Six Sigma, ISO 9000), and strategic controls (balanced scorecard).
  • D3.1 Distinguish between business ethics, legal compliance, and corporate social responsibility (CSR); apply a recognized ethical framework (utilitarian, rights, justice, virtue) to a business dilemma.
  • D3.2 Analyse the triple-bottom-line (people, planet, profit) and stakeholder theory; evaluate a company's CSR report or sustainability initiative.
  • D4.1 Design and carry out a culminating leadership case-study investigation that integrates strands A–D and communicate the findings in a written report and an oral presentation.

4. Mathematical & Inquiry Processes

Although BOH4M is not a mathematics course, students engage in a parallel set of inquiry processes drawn from the Business Studies curriculum:

Inquiry & ResearchPose questions, plan research, gather and document primary and secondary data from credible sources.
Critical AnalysisCompare theories and frameworks; identify assumptions, biases, and limitations in management literature.
Decision MakingApply structured models (rational, bounded, intuitive) to choose among alternatives in case studies.
Selecting Tools & StrategiesUse SWOT, PEST, balanced scorecard, organizational charts, force-field analysis, and digital collaboration tools.
ConnectingMake connections among management functions and between this course and other disciplines (accounting, economics, marketing, psychology).
CommunicatingCommunicate ideas in writing, orally, and visually using appropriate business vocabulary and formats (memo, report, presentation, e-mail).
ReflectingReflect on personal leadership style, group dynamics, and the learning process across the course.

5. Achievement Chart

Per Growing Success (2010), each of the four categories carries equal weight (25%) within both term work and the final evaluation.

Knowledge & Understanding
25%

Subject-specific content (definitions, theories, models — Mintzberg, Maslow, Tuckman, Lewin, Herzberg, Kotter) and comprehension of their meaning and significance.

Thinking & Inquiry
25%

Use of critical and creative thinking — analysing case studies, evaluating leadership decisions, identifying assumptions, planning the culminating investigation.

Communication
25%

Conveying meaning through various forms — business memos, reports, oral presentations, professional vocabulary, clearly organized writing, audience-appropriate tone.

Application
25%

Use of knowledge and skills to make connections within and between contexts — real-world organizations, transfer of management theory to authentic dilemmas, ethical reasoning.

Levels of achievement: Level 4 (80–100%, exceeds standard) · Level 3 (70–79%, provincial standard) · Level 2 (60–69%, approaching) · Level 1 (50–59%, limited) · R (below 50%, insufficient).

Note on the Culminating Investigation: The Culminating Leadership Case Study Investigation (Strand D) contributes 15% of the 30% Final Evaluation. The remaining 15% is the comprehensive written final exam covering Chapters 1–5. The Investigation is mandatory for the granting of a credit and demonstrates synthesis across all four strands — Foundations, Leading, Management Challenges, and Planning & Controlling.
