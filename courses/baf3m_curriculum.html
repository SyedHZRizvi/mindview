This course introduces students to the fundamental principles and procedures of accounting. Students will develop financial-analysis and decision-making skills that will assist them in future studies and/or career opportunities in business. Students will acquire an understanding of accounting for a service and a merchandising business, computerized accounting, financial analysis, ethics, and current issues in accounting. (Adapted from The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 11 and 12: Business Studies, 2006, Ministry of Education.)

2. Strands & Expectations

Strand A — Fundamentals of Accounting

Overall Expectations: students will demonstrate an understanding of the purpose of accounting, the role of accounting in society, generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), the accounting equation, and the rules of debit and credit.

Key Specific Expectations

  • A1.1 Describe the purpose of accounting and explain the role accounting plays in business and society.
  • A1.2 Explain key generally accepted accounting principles (business entity, going concern, cost, time period, objectivity, revenue recognition, matching).
  • A1.3 Identify and describe the categories of accounts (assets, liabilities, owner's equity, revenue, expenses, drawings) and their characteristics.
  • A1.4 Use the fundamental accounting equation \( \text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Owner's Equity} \) to analyse the effect of transactions on the equation.
  • A1.5 Apply the rules of debit and credit to record transactions in T-accounts; identify the normal balance of each account category.
  • A1.6 Distinguish between service and merchandising businesses and describe the legal forms of business organisation (sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation).

Strand B — The Accounting Practices of Service Businesses

Overall Expectations: students will describe the steps in the accounting cycle, record and post transactions for a service business, prepare a trial balance, complete the worksheet including adjustments, and produce closing entries.

Key Specific Expectations

  • B1.1 Identify common source documents (invoices, cheques, cash-register receipts, memos, bank statements) and describe their purpose.
  • B1.2 Record routine business transactions in the two-column general journal using correct debit/credit entries and a clear explanation.
  • B1.3 Post journal entries to the general ledger; use account numbers and cross-reference posting references between books of original and final entry.
  • B1.4 Prepare a trial balance and use it to locate common posting and transcription errors (transposition, slide, omission).
  • B2.1 Complete an eight-column worksheet, including adjusting entries for supplies used, prepaid expenses, depreciation (straight-line), and accrued items.
  • B2.2 Journalise and post adjusting and closing entries; describe the function of a post-closing trial balance.
  • B2.3 Describe the use of subsidiary ledgers (Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable) and the role of control accounts.

Strand C — Accounting for a Merchandising Business & Financial Statements

Overall Expectations: students will prepare and analyse financial statements (income statement, statement of owner's equity, classified balance sheet) for both service and merchandising businesses, and use simple financial ratios to evaluate performance and position.

Key Specific Expectations

  • C1.1 Prepare an income statement (single-step and classified) showing revenue, expenses, and net income (loss); apply the matching principle.
  • C1.2 Prepare a statement of owner's equity that reconciles beginning capital, additional investments, drawings, and net income/loss to ending capital.
  • C1.3 Prepare a classified balance sheet that distinguishes current vs non-current (long-term) assets and current vs non-current liabilities; verify Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity.
  • C1.4 Calculate working capital \( \text{Working Capital} = \text{Current Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities} \) and the current ratio \( \dfrac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} \); interpret what each tells the user.
  • C1.5 Describe the additional accounts that distinguish merchandising from service businesses (Merchandise Inventory, Sales, Sales Returns, Purchases, Freight-In, Purchase Discounts, Cost of Goods Sold).
  • C1.6 Compute Cost of Goods Sold \( \text{COGS} = \text{Beg Inv} + \text{Net Purchases} - \text{End Inv} \) and Gross Profit \( = \text{Net Sales} - \text{COGS} \).
  • C1.7 Describe the steps of the accounting cycle (analyse, journalise, post, trial balance, worksheet, adjust, statements, close, post-closing trial balance).

Strand D — Internal Control & Ethical Decision Making

Overall Expectations: students will explain the principles of internal control, prepare a bank reconciliation, account for petty cash, describe ethical issues in accounting practice, and apply a model for ethical decision making.

Key Specific Expectations

  • D1.1 Describe the principles of internal control (segregation of duties, physical safeguards, authorisation, documentation, independent verification, bonding) and identify control weaknesses in a given scenario.
  • D1.2 Prepare a bank reconciliation, identifying outstanding cheques, deposits in transit, bank charges, NSF cheques, and interest revenue; journalise the resulting entries.
  • D1.3 Establish, replenish, and increase/decrease a petty-cash fund using the imprest system; journalise petty-cash transactions.
  • D2.1 Describe ethical issues and dilemmas that may arise in accounting (fraudulent reporting, misappropriation of assets, conflict of interest, confidentiality).
  • D2.2 Describe the role of a professional code of conduct (CPA Canada Code of Professional Conduct) and explain how it guides ethical behaviour.
  • D2.3 Apply a model for ethical decision making to a workplace scenario, identifying stakeholders, consequences, and alternative courses of action.

Strand E — The Use of Information Technology in Accounting

Overall Expectations: students will describe how information and communication technology is used in accounting, compare computerized and manual accounting systems, and identify users of accounting information and their decisions.

Key Specific Expectations

  • E1.1 Identify and describe the internal and external users of accounting information (owners, managers, employees, lenders, investors, customers, governments) and the decisions each user makes from the financial statements.
  • E1.2 Compare manual and computerized accounting systems, identifying the advantages, limitations, and the role each plays today.
  • E1.3 Describe how spreadsheet and accounting software (e.g., QuickBooks, Sage, Excel) automate journalising, posting, and statement preparation; identify the resulting changes to internal controls.
  • E1.4 Describe current issues and trends in accounting (IFRS adoption, sustainability reporting, the role of artificial intelligence and data analytics) and their potential impact on professional practice.

3. Business Studies Processes

The Ontario Business Studies framework emphasizes the following thinking and inquiry processes that are integrated through every strand:

Inquiry & ResearchFormulate questions, gather and organize information from a variety of sources (financial statements, news articles, professional bodies), and critically evaluate it.
Analysis & InterpretationAnalyse financial data using the accounting equation, ratios, and trends; interpret results in business contexts.
Decision Making & Problem SolvingApply ethical frameworks and accounting principles to make and justify business decisions.
CommunicationCommunicate financial information clearly using proper accounting terminology, formats, and electronic presentations.
Application of TechnologyUse spreadsheets and accounting software to record, summarize, and present financial data.
ConnectingMake connections among accounting, mathematics, economics, law, technology, and current events in business.
ReflectingReflect on personal learning, the ethical implications of business decisions, and one's responsibilities as a future financial professional.

4. Achievement Chart

Per Growing Success (2010), each of the four achievement-chart categories carries equal weight (25%) within both term work and the final evaluation.

Knowledge & Understanding
25%

Subject-specific content (definitions, GAAP, account categories, formulas, statement formats) and comprehension of accounting concepts and procedures.

Thinking & Inquiry
25%

Critical and creative thinking — analysing transactions, choosing appropriate adjustments, identifying control weaknesses, evaluating ethical dilemmas.

Communication
25%

Communicating financial information using proper terminology, statement formats, and explanations — written reports, narrative for journal entries, oral presentations.

Application
25%

Use of knowledge and skills to record real transactions, complete the accounting cycle, prepare statements for a sample business, and apply controls in workplace scenarios.

Levels of achievement: Level 4 (80–100%, exceeds standard) · Level 3 (70–79%, provincial standard) · Level 2 (60–69%, approaching) · Level 1 (50–59%, limited) · R (below 50%, insufficient).
