GLC2O — Career Studies, Grade 10 Open

Curriculum Policy Document · Ontario Ministry of Education · The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 9 and 10: Guidance and Career Education (2006, Revised) · Aligned to Creating Pathways to Success (2013) and Growing Success (2010)

Course Code: GLC2O  |  Credit Value: 0.5  |  Hours: 55  |  Prerequisite: None  |  Compulsory: Yes — required for the OSSD

1. Course Description

This course teaches students how to develop and achieve personal goals for future learning, work, and community involvement. Students will assess their interests, skills, and characteristics and investigate current economic and workplace trends, work opportunities, and ways to search for work. The course explores postsecondary learning and career options, prepares students for managing work and life transitions, and helps students focus on their goals through the development of a career plan. This compulsory half-credit is normally taken in Grade 10 and supports the Ontario Student Pathways Plan as outlined in Creating Pathways to Success.

2. Fundamental Concepts

ConceptApplication in GLC2O
Self-KnowledgeHonest, evidence-based self-assessment of interests, skills, values, personality, and learning preferences using inventories, reflection, and feedback.
Opportunity AwarenessRecognising the breadth of postsecondary and work options — apprenticeship, college, university, workplace, OYAP, entrepreneurship — and the labour-market trends shaping them.
Decision MakingApplying structured decision-making models (e.g., the IDEAS or PrOACT framework) rather than impulse to choose courses, programs, and careers.
Transition ManagementDesigning and revising a personal Individual Pathways Plan (IPP); managing change with résumé, cover letter, interview, and financial-literacy skills.

3. Big Ideas (by Strand)

  • Knowing Yourself — Strand A: Self-knowledge — interests, skills, values, personality, character, learning preferences — is the foundation on which a satisfying education and career are built.
  • Exploring Opportunities — Strand B: The world of work, postsecondary education, and community involvement is wider, more changeable, and more accessible than most students initially believe; informed exploration narrows it without closing options.
  • Making Decisions & Setting Goals — Strand C: Good decisions follow a process — gathering information, weighing options, anticipating consequences, choosing, and reflecting. Goals turn decisions into action.
  • Achieving Goals & Making Transitions — Strand D: Achieving goals requires concrete plans, transferable employability skills, financial literacy, and a willingness to revise the plan as life and the labour market change.

4. Strand A — Knowing Yourself

Overall Expectations

  • A1. use a self-assessment process to develop a personal profile that can be used in career development planning;
  • A2. evaluate and demonstrate the personal-management skills and characteristics needed for success in life, school, the workplace, and the community;
  • A3. demonstrate an understanding of how personal initiative, hard work, and motivation contribute to success in school, work, and life.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • A1.1 use a variety of self-assessment tools and processes to identify their personal characteristics (e.g., interests, abilities, learning styles, work values, personality traits);
  • A1.2 analyse the results of their self-assessment to identify factors they need to consider in planning career pathways;
  • A1.3 demonstrate an understanding of how their personal characteristics may affect their success in school, work, and the community;
  • A2.1 identify their strengths and areas for improvement in essential employability skills (e.g., communication, teamwork, problem solving, digital skills);
  • A2.2 describe character traits and habits of mind that are associated with success (e.g., responsibility, integrity, perseverance, growth mindset);
  • A3.1 demonstrate an understanding of how personal initiative and effort contribute to success in school, work, and life.
Self-assessment tools used: Holland's RIASEC interest inventory; Multiple Intelligences inventory; learning-style survey (VARK); Big Five / Myers-Briggs-style personality reflection; Ontario Skills Passport categories.

5. Strand B — Exploring Opportunities

Overall Expectations

  • B1. use a research process to locate and select relevant career information from a variety of sources for inclusion in a portfolio;
  • B2. identify a broad range of options for present and future learning, work, and community involvement;
  • B3. demonstrate an understanding of, and apply, the essential skills and work habits required in occupations that interest them.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • B1.1 demonstrate an understanding of how to use a research process for career exploration (e.g., identifying a focus, planning the research, gathering information from multiple sources, evaluating and synthesising findings);
  • B1.2 identify and use a range of resources for career exploration (e.g., Ontario Skills Passport, Job Bank, Statistics Canada, professional association websites);
  • B2.1 describe a variety of postsecondary learning destinations (apprenticeship, college, community living, university, workplace) and the requirements and entry points for each;
  • B2.2 describe a variety of options for community involvement and the requirements of the Ontario Student Diploma 40-hour community-involvement requirement;
  • B2.3 identify current trends in the local and global labour market and explain their potential implications for career planning;
  • B3.1 identify the essential employability skills required in occupations that interest them (using Ontario Skills Passport categories);
  • B3.2 describe the demands and rewards of selected occupations they have researched.
Pathways focus: Specialist High Skills Major (SHSM) programs; Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program (OYAP); Dual Credit; cooperative education; Ontario Tuition Grant and OSAP basics.

6. Strand C — Making Decisions and Setting Goals

Overall Expectations

  • C1. use a decision-making process to make informed decisions about future education, training, and work;
  • C2. use a goal-setting process to set and achieve goals for school, work, and other aspects of life;
  • C3. demonstrate an understanding of how decision-making and goal-setting skills can help them respond effectively to change.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • C1.1 identify factors that may influence decisions about future education, training, and work (e.g., values, interests, financial considerations, family expectations, labour-market trends);
  • C1.2 use a decision-making process to evaluate possible options for future education and work;
  • C1.3 explain how short-term and long-term decisions are connected;
  • C2.1 identify short-term and long-term goals related to school, work, and personal life (using SMART criteria);
  • C2.2 develop an action plan to achieve short-term and long-term goals;
  • C2.3 demonstrate an understanding of the difference between values-based goals and externally imposed goals;
  • C3.1 describe how flexibility and adaptability help in responding to changing circumstances.
Frameworks used: SMART goal criteria; IDEAS decision model (Identify, Discover, Evaluate, Act, Self-reflect); cost-benefit analysis; growth-mindset reflection (Dweck).

7. Strand D — Achieving Goals and Making Transitions

Overall Expectations

  • D1. demonstrate an understanding of and apply the skills, strategies, and habits needed to successfully manage transitions;
  • D2. produce a career and life-planning portfolio that includes a personal profile, postsecondary plans, and an action plan;
  • D3. demonstrate an understanding of financial-management skills required for postsecondary plans and life after school.

Specific Expectations (selected)

  • D1.1 identify strategies for managing transitions (e.g., from Grade 10 to senior grades, from secondary to postsecondary, into the workplace);
  • D1.2 produce a résumé and cover letter appropriate for a part-time, summer, or co-op position;
  • D1.3 demonstrate effective interview behaviours (preparation, professional communication, follow-up);
  • D2.1 develop and present a career portfolio (Individual Pathways Plan) that includes self-assessment results, postsecondary research, and a goals-and-actions section;
  • D2.2 use the Individual Pathways Plan (IPP) format consistent with Creating Pathways to Success;
  • D3.1 demonstrate an understanding of basic financial-management skills (budgeting, banking, credit, taxes, the true cost of postsecondary education);
  • D3.2 identify sources of student financial aid (OSAP, scholarships, bursaries, employer tuition support).
Capstone task: the Individual Pathways Plan portfolio — self-profile + opportunity-research summary + SMART goals + résumé/cover letter + budget — counts as the major Strand D summative.

10. Evaluation Policy (70/30)

  • Term Work — 70% of final mark, comprised of self-assessment reports, research tasks, unit tests, in-class participation, and the working drafts of the Individual Pathways Plan.
  • Final Evaluation — 30% of final mark, comprised of the Individual Pathways Plan portfolio (15%) and the Final Examination (15%).

All four Achievement Chart categories are addressed throughout, in both Term Work and Final Evaluation.

11. Assessment Practices

  • Assessment AS Learning — student self-checks: career-quiz reflections, peer feedback on mock interview, mid-unit self-rating against rubric.
  • Assessment FOR Learning — pre-unit diagnostic to surface what students already know about careers, postsecondary destinations, and financial literacy; descriptive feedback (no mark) used to focus instruction.
  • Assessment OF Learning — unit tests, the Individual Pathways Plan portfolio, the final examination — all graded against the Achievement Chart.

12. Differentiated Instruction & Universal Design for Learning

The course offers multiple means of representation (video case studies, infographics on labour-market data, guest speakers), multiple means of engagement (student-selected career-research focus, choice of portfolio format — print, digital, video), and multiple means of expression (written report, presentation, recorded interview, infographic). These accommodations support the principles of Equity and Inclusive Education in Ontario Schools and the Universal Design for Learning framework.

13. Learning Skills & Work Habits

Reported separately on the report card: responsibility, organization, independent work, collaboration, initiative, self-regulation. Career Studies is the natural setting to surface and develop these skills explicitly, because every Learning Skill maps to an employability skill on the Ontario Skills Passport. Students are asked to self-assess Learning Skills weekly and to use that evidence in the IPP.
