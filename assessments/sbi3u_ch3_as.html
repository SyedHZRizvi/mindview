Topic 3.1 — Meiosis
Question 1
Meiosis differs from mitosis because:
Solution: Meiosis: one round of DNA replication followed by two divisions, producing four haploid, genetically unique daughter cells (gametes).
Question 2
A human cell entering meiosis has 46 chromosomes. How many chromosomes does each daughter cell have at the end of meiosis II?
Solution: Meiosis halves chromosome number: 46 → 23 in each of the four haploid gametes.
Question 3
Crossing over occurs during which phase of meiosis?
Solution: Crossing over occurs in prophase I, when homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis) and exchange DNA segments at chiasmata.
Topic 3.2–3.3 — Mendelian & Dihybrid Crosses
Question 4
In pea plants, purple flower (P) is dominant to white (p). A heterozygous purple plant (Pp) is crossed with a white plant (pp). What fraction of offspring are white?
Solution: Pp × pp: gametes from Pp are 1/2 P and 1/2 p; pp gives only p. Offspring: 1/2 Pp (purple) and 1/2 pp (white). White fraction = 0.5 (1:1 ratio — signature of a test cross).
Question 5
In a dihybrid cross AaBb × AaBb (independent assortment), what is the phenotypic ratio in the offspring?
Solution: AaBb × AaBb gives 9:3:3:1 in the F₂. Derived by the product rule: (3:1)(3:1) = 9 dom-dom : 3 dom-rec : 3 rec-dom : 1 rec-rec.
Question 6
Two heterozygotes (RrYy × RrYy) are crossed. What fraction of offspring are expected to be wrinkled (rr) AND green (yy)?
Solution: Probability rr = 1/4 and probability yy = 1/4. Product = (1/4)(1/4) = 1/16 = 0.0625. This is the lone double-recessive box in the 16-square Punnett.
Topic 3.4 — Non-Mendelian Inheritance
Question 7
In snapdragons, red (RR) × white (rr) produces pink (Rr) offspring. This is an example of:
Solution: Incomplete dominance: heterozygote shows a blended intermediate phenotype (pink, between red and white). In codominance, both alleles would be fully expressed (e.g., red-and-white spotted, never pink).
Question 8
A man with type A blood (IAi) and a woman with type B blood (IBi) have a child. Which blood types are possible for the child?
Solution: IAi × IBi gives IAIB (AB), IAi (A), IBi (B), ii (O) — all four blood types are possible, each with 1/4 probability. Famous in paternity questions.
Question 9
Human skin colour is a continuous trait controlled by many genes. This pattern of inheritance is called:
Solution: Polygenic inheritance (many genes contributing small effects) produces continuous, bell-shaped variation — skin colour, height, birth weight. Pleiotropy is the opposite: one gene affecting many traits.
Topic 3.5–3.6 — Sex Linkage, Pedigrees & Biotech
Question 10
An unaffected father (XHY) and a carrier mother (XHXh) have children. What fraction of sons are expected to be affected by the X-linked recessive disorder?
Solution: Sons inherit Y from dad and one X from mom; mom has 1/2 XH and 1/2 Xh. So 1/2 of sons are XhY (affected) and 1/2 are XHY (normal). Affected fraction = 0.5.
Question 11
Down syndrome (trisomy 21) results from:
Solution: Non-disjunction in meiosis produces a gamete with two copies of chromosome 21; fertilisation with a normal gamete yields a zygote with three copies (trisomy 21). Risk rises with maternal age.
Question 12
Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) is used to:
Solution: PCR copies a specific DNA region defined by primer sequences, producing billions of copies in a few hours using heat-stable Taq polymerase. The basis of forensics, paternity testing, and COVID-19 PCR tests.
