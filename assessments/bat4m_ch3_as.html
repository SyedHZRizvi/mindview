Topic 3.1 — Horizontal & Vertical Analysis
Question 1
Sales went from \$ 400,000 to \$ 460,000. The percentage change (horizontal analysis) is:
Solution:
\( (460{,}000 - 400{,}000) / 400{,}000 = 60{,}000 / 400{,}000 = 0.15 = 15\% \).
Question 2
On a vertical (common-size) income statement, every line is expressed as a percentage of:
Solution:
On the income statement, the common-size base is Net Sales. On the balance sheet, the base is Total Assets.
Topic 3.2 — Liquidity Ratios
Question 3
Current assets \$ 240,000; current liabilities \$ 120,000. The current ratio is:
Solution:
\( 240{,}000 / 120{,}000 = 2.0 \). Healthy liquidity (most industries).
Question 4
Net credit sales \$ 600,000; average AR \$ 75,000. Days' sales in AR is approximately:
Solution:
Receivables turnover \( = 600{,}000/75{,}000 = 8.0 \) times. Days' AR \( = 365/8.0 = 45.6 \) days.
Topic 3.3 — Solvency Ratios
Question 5
Total liabilities \$ 480,000; total assets \$ 800,000. The debt-to-assets ratio is:
Solution:
\( 480{,}000 / 800{,}000 = 0.60 = 60\% \). Creditors finance 60% of the firm's assets.
Question 6
EBIT \$ 240,000; interest expense \$ 40,000. The times-interest-earned ratio is:
Solution:
\( 240{,}000 / 40{,}000 = 6 \) times. The company comfortably covers interest 6× from operations.
Topic 3.4 — Profitability Ratios
Question 7
Net income \$ 60,000; net sales \$ 800,000. Profit margin is:
Solution:
\( 60{,}000 / 800{,}000 = 0.075 = 7.5\% \).
Question 8
Net income \$ 84,000; preferred dividends \$ 4,000; average common equity \$ 400,000. ROE is:
Solution:
ROE \( = (84{,}000 - 4{,}000) / 400{,}000 = 80{,}000 / 400{,}000 = 20\% \).
Topic 3.5 — Market Ratios
Question 9
EPS \$ 4.00; share price \$ 60. P/E ratio is:
Solution:
\( 60 / 4.00 = 15.0 \). Investors pay \$ 15 for \$ 1 of trailing earnings.
Question 10
Annual dividend \$ 2.40; share price \$ 60. Dividend yield is:
Solution:
Yield \( = 2.40 / 60 = 0.04 = 4.0\% \).
Topic 3.6 — Limitations
Question 11
"Window dressing" refers to:
Solution:
Window dressing = timing transactions around year end (e.g., paying suppliers a day before year end) to flatter the current ratio or other metrics — legal but misleading.
