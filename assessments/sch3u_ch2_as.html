Balancing Equations
Question 1
Balance: \( \text{Fe} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \). The coefficient of Fe is:
Solution:
\(4\text{Fe} + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\). Coefficient of Fe = 4.
Question 2
Sum of coefficients in the balanced combustion of propane (\(\text{C}_3\text{H}_8\)) producing CO₂ and H₂O:
Solution:
\(\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + 5\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 3\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}\). Sum = 1+5+3+4 = 13.
Question 3
Which represents a balanced equation?
Solution:
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. H: 4=4 ✓, O: 2=2 ✓.
Classifying Reactions
Question 4
Classify: \(\text{Na} + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{NaCl}\).
Solution:
Two elements combine into one compound → synthesis.
Question 5
Classify: \(2\text{KClO}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + 3\text{O}_2\).
Solution:
One compound breaks into smaller products → decomposition.
Question 6
Predict the product of \(\text{Zn} + \text{CuSO}_4 \rightarrow ?\).
Solution:
Zn above Cu in activity series → displaces Cu²⁺. Single displacement: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu.
Question 7
Will Ag(s) react with HCl(aq)?
Solution:
Ag is below H in the activity series → cannot displace H⁺ → no reaction.
Net Ionic Equations
Question 8
In the molecular equation \(\text{AgNO}_3(\text{aq}) + \text{NaCl}(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{AgCl}(\text{s}) + \text{NaNO}_3(\text{aq})\), which are spectator ions?
Solution:
Spectator ions appear on both sides. Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ are unchanged; the actual reaction is Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl(s).
Question 9
Write the net ionic equation for the neutralization of HCl(aq) with NaOH(aq).
Solution:
Strong acid + strong base: net ionic is H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l). Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are spectator ions.
Question 10
Will a precipitate form when KNO₃(aq) and NaCl(aq) are mixed?
Solution:
Group 1 cations and NO₃⁻ are always soluble → no precipitate → no reaction.
Mixed Practice
Question 11
Classify: \(2\text{HCl}(\text{aq}) + \text{Mg}(\text{OH})_2(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2(\text{aq}) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})\).
Solution:
Acid + base → salt + water = neutralization, a special case of double displacement.
Question 12
Predict the products of incomplete combustion of methane (limited O₂):
Solution:
Incomplete combustion with limited O₂ produces CO (toxic) and/or C (soot), plus H₂O. This is why poorly vented gas heaters can cause CO poisoning.
