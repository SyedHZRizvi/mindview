Question 1
The six historical-thinking concepts identified by Peter Seixas and Tom Morton are:
Solution: Seixas-Morton's framework underpins Ontario's history curriculum. Each concept is a question the historian asks of any topic.
Question 2
A "primary source" is best defined as:
Solution: Primary = period-contemporary; secondary = later interpretation. Letters, treaties, photographs, oral testimonies are primary; textbooks and scholarly articles are typically secondary.
Question 3
"Presentism" is best defined as:
Solution: The "historical perspective" concept disciplines against presentism by demanding the historian take past worldviews seriously. This is not relativism — ethical judgement remains possible — but care about anachronism.
Question 4
"Confirmation bias" most commonly distorts historical reasoning by:
Solution: Confirmation bias affects historians as much as their sources. The discipline is to actively look for evidence that would refute one's working hypothesis.
Question 5
Eric Hobsbawm's "long 19th century" runs from:
Solution: Hobsbawm's "long 19th century" (1789–1914) and "short 20th century" (1914–1991) are common periodisations. They are historiographical choices, not natural divisions.
Question 6
A necessary cause is one that:
Solution: Necessary: without it, no event. Sufficient: alone enough to produce the event. Most historical events have several necessary causes, no single sufficient one.
Question 7
The Annales school of historiography is associated with:
Solution: The Annales (Bloch, Febvre, Braudel) emphasised long-term structures. Hobsbawm was Marxist; Said founded postcolonial criticism; Ginzburg pioneered microhistory.
Question 8
The most common citation systems in academic history are:
Solution: Chicago Notes-Bibliography is the standard in most history journals; MLA 9 is common in literature and at high school. APA is more common in social sciences. Check with your teacher.
Question 9
A strong analytical thesis statement:
Solution: Specificity and debatability are non-negotiable; that the thesis fits on one sentence is conventional but not essential.
Question 10
When using AI-generated text in research, the most important rule is:
Solution: AI-generated citations are routinely fabricated ("hallucinated"). Always verify every reference. Also check your teacher's syllabus and board policy about AI use in research essays.
