Unit 6: Inquiry Skills — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Research-essay readiness · Strand A
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Assess your readiness for the Research Essay. This is the most important diagnostic of the course. Be honest about gaps; we will fix them together before you write.
Question 1 Topic
[3 marks]
Propose a possible research-essay topic within world history since 1450. Then narrow it: rewrite the topic as a focused historical question, suitable for a 1500–2000-word essay.
Question 2 Thesis
[3 marks]
Draft a working thesis sentence answering your question from Question 1. The thesis should be debatable and specific.
Question 3 Sources
[4 marks]
For your topic, list (a) two primary sources you could use, (b) two secondary sources (a book or scholarly article — invent if you don't know specific ones, but plausible). Briefly indicate why each is useful.
Question 4 Source criticism
[3 marks]
A friend writes a 1980s memoir about an event in 1968. They have strong views about how the event should be remembered.
Identify three source-criticism concerns this memoir raises and how you would mitigate each.
Question 5 Concept application
[3 marks]
For your chosen topic, briefly identify how at least three of the six historical-thinking concepts (significance, cause/consequence, continuity/change, perspective, ethical dimension, evidence) apply.
Question 6 Citation
[2 marks]
Provide one MLA-9 Works Cited entry and one Chicago footnote for the following imaginary book: "Atlantic Sugar and the Long 18th Century" by Maria Soto, published 2019 by Toronto University Press.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Where in the research-essay process do you feel least confident: topic selection, source identification, thesis writing, paragraph structure, or citation? Be specific about what you need most help with.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback on your research-essay readiness.
