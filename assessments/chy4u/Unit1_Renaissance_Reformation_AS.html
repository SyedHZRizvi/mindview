Topic 1.1 — Italian Renaissance
Question 1
The intellectual programme at the core of the Italian Renaissance, focused on the study of Latin, Greek, history, and moral philosophy, is known as:
Solution: Humanism — the studia humanitatis (grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry, moral philosophy studied in Latin and Greek). Petrarch, Valla, and Pico della Mirandola are the major figures. Scholasticism was the medieval university tradition humanists challenged.
Question 2
"In the actions of all men, and especially of princes, where there is no court of appeal, one judges by the result." — Machiavelli, The Prince, Ch. 18 (1513)
Machiavelli's argument in The Prince is best understood as:
Solution: The Prince is empirical political advice written during the French and Spanish invasions of Italy, addressed to Lorenzo, arguing that princes must be willing to abandon conventional morality when survival requires. Reading it as pure cynicism misses the crisis it responds to.
Topic 1.2 — Protestant Reformation
Question 3
Martin Luther's "Ninety-five Theses" (1517) most directly attacked:
Solution: The Ninety-five Theses attacked Tetzel's sale of indulgences in Saxony (financing St Peter's Basilica). The deeper theological positions — sola fide, sola scriptura, sola gratia — followed over the next four years.
Question 4
The 1648 Peace of Westphalia ended the Thirty Years' War and is historically significant for:
Solution: Westphalia reaffirmed cuius regio, eius religio ("whose realm, his religion") and established the sovereign-state system that governed European international relations into the 20th century.
Topic 1.3 — Columbian Exchange
Question 5
The "Columbian Exchange" most accurately describes:
Solution: Alfred Crosby's 1972 term for the post-1492 transfer: maize, potatoes, tomatoes, cassava, chili to the Old World; wheat, rice, sugar, horses, cattle, plus smallpox and other diseases to the Americas; and the forced migration of ~12.5 million Africans across the Middle Passage.
Question 6
The demographic collapse of Indigenous populations in the Americas after 1492 was driven primarily by:
Solution: Disease accounted for the largest share of the 70-90% population collapse over 150 years. Conquest violence and the disruption of food systems compounded the catastrophe. The scale is one of the largest demographic events in recorded history.
Topic 1.4 — Atlantic Slavery
Question 7
Approximately how many enslaved Africans were embarked for transatlantic transport between 1501 and 1866?
Solution: The SlaveVoyages database records approximately 36,000 transatlantic voyages embarking ~12.5 million enslaved Africans, of whom ~10.7 million survived to be sold in the Americas. The largest forced migration in human history.
Question 8
Which 17th-century commodity drove most of the demand for enslaved African labour in the Americas?
Solution: Sugar was the first mass-market consumer commodity of the European economy. Its cultivation in Portuguese Brazil from c.1530 and English/French/Dutch Caribbean from the 1640s required vast plantation labour. Tobacco and (later) cotton were secondary.
Topic 1.5 — Asian Empires
Question 9
The Mughal Emperor Akbar (r.1556–1605) is best known for:
Solution: Akbar pursued sulh-i kull, including abolishing the jizya tax (1564), marrying Rajput princesses, integrating Hindus into senior administration, and sponsoring translations of Sanskrit epics. Compare to contemporary Philip II of Spain, who expelled Moriscos.
Question 10
Admiral Zheng He's seven Ming-dynasty voyages (1405–1433) reached as far as:
Solution: Zheng He's treasure ships reached South Asia, the Persian Gulf, and the East African coast (Malindi, Mombasa) more than half a century before Columbus. The Ming halted the voyages after 1433 — a much-debated decision attributed to cost, Confucian conservatism, and northern frontier concerns.
Topic 1.6 — Scientific Revolution
Question 11
Copernicus's De revolutionibus orbium coelestium (1543) proposed:
Solution: Heliocentrism. Kepler later replaced Copernicus's circular orbits with ellipses; Galileo's telescopic observations (Jupiter's moons, phases of Venus) supported the new model; Newton's Principia (1687) unified it with terrestrial mechanics.
Question 12
Andreas Vesalius's De humani corporis fabrica (1543) revolutionised:
Solution: Vesalius's Fabrica (same year as Copernicus's De revolutionibus) corrected hundreds of Galen's errors through dissection. Together they mark the start of the Scientific Revolution. William Harvey demonstrated blood circulation in 1628.
