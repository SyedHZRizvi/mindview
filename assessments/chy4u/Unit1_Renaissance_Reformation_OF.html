Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[2]
Define humanism as it applied to the Italian Renaissance, and name one humanist scholar.
Answer Key

Humanism was the Renaissance intellectual programme based on the studia humanitatis (grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry, moral philosophy) studied in Latin and Greek. Scholars included Petrarch, Lorenzo Valla, Pico della Mirandola, Erasmus, or Christine de Pizan. 1 for definition, 1 for example.

2
[2]
Name the three "solas" of Lutheran theology and briefly define each.
Answer Key

Sola fide (by faith alone) — salvation through faith, not works; sola scriptura (by scripture alone) — the Bible as ultimate authority, not church tradition; sola gratia (by grace alone) — salvation as God's free gift, not earned. 2 marks for all three correctly defined.

3
[2]
The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) was an agreement between which two states, and what did it do?
Answer Key

Treaty between Spain (Castile) and Portugal, mediated by Pope Alexander VI, dividing the non-European world along a meridian 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands. Lands west to Spain, east to Portugal — giving Spain most of the Americas and Portugal Brazil, Africa, and the route to India. Notably, no other powers and no Indigenous peoples consulted.

4
[2]
Approximately how many enslaved Africans were embarked across the Atlantic between 1501 and 1866?
Answer Key

C — about 12.5 million embarked, of whom roughly 10.7 million survived the Middle Passage. Source: SlaveVoyages database. The largest forced migration in human history.

5
[2]
Identify and date one major Ottoman military victory and one major Mughal political reform between 1450 and 1650.
Answer Key

Ottoman: capture of Constantinople (1453) by Mehmed II, or victories of Suleiman the Magnificent (e.g., Mohács 1526, Siege of Vienna 1529). Mughal: Akbar's abolition of jizya (1564), or Babur's victory at Panipat (1526).

6
[3]
Define each: (a) Columbian Exchange, (b) encomienda, (c) mita.
Answer Key

(a) Columbian Exchange: bidirectional transfer of plants, animals, microbes, and people across the Atlantic after 1492 (Alfred Crosby's term, 1972). (b) Encomienda: Spanish royal grant entitling colonists to Indigenous labour and tribute, ostensibly in exchange for Christianisation. (c) Mita: Andean forced-labour rotation system, especially in the silver mines of Potosí under Spanish rule. 1 mark each.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
7
[5]
"We were yet hardly able to drag along the road for hunger; but to satisfy that our masters had each man two fingers thrust into our mouths, and dry maize, which had been a long time ground, was rammed in… Of all the sins which we did, that one being made known to our masters, they cut off our noses…" — Olaudah Equiano, The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano (1789)
Analyse this primary source using the framework discussed in class. Address: (a) provenance and purpose; (b) what the source directly shows; (c) one strength and one limitation of this source for studying the Middle Passage; (d) one other source you would want to corroborate it with.
Answer Key

(a) Provenance: Olaudah Equiano (c.1745–1797), a formerly enslaved man who bought his freedom and became a leading London abolitionist. Purpose: published 1789 to advance British abolition. (b) Documents the physical brutality of the Middle Passage from a survivor's perspective. (c) Strength: rare primary source by an African voice; first-person testimony of conditions. Limitation: written ~30 years after events, with explicit abolitionist purpose; recent scholarship debates whether Equiano was born in Africa or South Carolina. (d) Could corroborate with ship captains' logs, the 1788 Brookes diagram, parliamentary testimony of slave-ship surgeons (e.g., Alexander Falconbridge). 5 marks: 1 each for the four parts, 1 for overall quality.

8
[4]
Was the Protestant Reformation caused primarily by Luther's theology, by the printing press, or by political ambition? Argue for one position while acknowledging the others. 5–7 sentences.
Answer Key

The strongest answers recognise multi-causation. Luther's theology was the content (sola fide, sola scriptura); the press was the scale (To the Christian Nobility sold 4,000 copies in 18 days); political ambition was the protection (Frederick the Wise sheltered Luther; German princes' interest in church lands). Jan Hus had similar theology and was burned in 1415 — theology alone was insufficient. A defensible answer chooses one as "primary" but acknowledges the others. 4 marks: 1 for argument, 2 for evidence, 1 for engaging counter-evidence.

9
[4]
Was the Italian Renaissance a "rebirth"? Briefly evaluate this metaphor using continuity-and-change reasoning. 4–6 sentences.
Answer Key

The "rebirth" metaphor (Burckhardt 19th-c.) overstates change at the cost of continuity. Continuities: medieval universities had taught Aristotle, scholasticism had developed sophisticated philosophy, classical learning was preserved in monasteries. Changes: deliberate self-conscious imitation of antiquity, expanded patronage by merchant elites (Medici), linear perspective in painting, vernacular literature, philological method (Valla). The Renaissance is best understood as a partial cultural transformation building on medieval foundations, not a rebirth of civilisation. 4 marks: 2 for naming both continuities and changes, 2 for the synthesis.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
10
[6]
Write one analytical paragraph (PEEL structure, 5-7 sentences) on the following claim: "The Columbian Exchange transformed both the Old World and the New World, but its costs fell unequally." Include at least one piece of specific evidence (a date, a place, or a quantity).
Answer Key

Strong responses identify mutual transformation (maize and potatoes to the Old World; horses, wheat, smallpox to the Americas; the forced migration of ~12.5 million Africans) and asymmetric cost (Indigenous American populations fell 70–90% within 150 years; West African demographic and political damage; European calorific benefit). The PEEL structure should include a clear Point (topic sentence asserting unequal transformation), Evidence (specific facts), Explanation (interpreting evidence), Link (back to thesis). 6 marks: 1 Point, 2 Evidence, 2 Explanation, 1 Link/conventions.

11
[3]
Rewrite the following weak thesis into a strong, debatable thesis statement: "The Reformation was important."
Answer Key

Sample: "The Reformation was a religious revolution made politically possible by the printing press, German princely interests, and Habsburg distraction with Ottoman wars; without these political and technological enabling conditions, Luther's theology would have shared the fate of Jan Hus's a century earlier." Look for: specific claim (1), engages multi-causation (1), debatable (1).

12
[3]
Compare and contrast religious toleration under Akbar (Mughal India) and Philip II (Spain) in a short paragraph (4 sentences).
Answer Key

Both ruled religiously diverse populations c.1556–1605. Akbar abolished the jizya (1564), incorporated Hindu Rajputs into senior command, held interfaith debates at Fatehpur Sikri, and sponsored Sanskrit-Persian translations — a policy of sulh-i kull. Philip II expelled the Moriscos, funded the Inquisition, and led Catholic armies in the Dutch Revolt and Spanish Armada (1588). The comparison challenges the assumption that religious toleration was a European invention. 3 marks: 1 each for Akbar's policy, Philip's policy, the broader analytical point.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
13
[5]
"Concerning the inhabitants of certain islands, especially those discovered by the Portuguese in our times, and the question whether they have human reason… these people, although in some sort their land seems remote from ours, are nevertheless… true men who have an immortal soul, and are not to be enslaved." — Pope Paul III, Sublimis Deus (1537)
Apply your knowledge of the period: (a) what does this papal bull reveal about debates within the Catholic Church about Indigenous Americans? (b) Why did the Spanish crown continue to permit the encomienda system in practice despite such documents? 5–7 sentences.
Answer Key

(a) Sublimis Deus reflects the Valladolid debate (1550–1551) between Bartolomé de las Casas and Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda over Indigenous rights, and the New Laws of 1542 that briefly restricted the encomienda. The Catholic Church (especially Dominicans) was divided; Las Casas's Brevisima relación (1552) documented atrocities. (b) Settler pressure for labour was enormous; the encomienda enriched powerful colonists who lobbied against reform; the practical capacity of Madrid to enforce protections in distant colonies was limited; alternative labour (African slavery) replaced where the encomienda was restricted. Marks: 2 for the Church debate, 2 for the gap between law and practice, 1 for specific reference.

14
[4]
Apply the concept of historical significance (Seixas-Morton criteria): is Luther's 95 Theses more historically significant than, say, the contents of Tetzel's typical Sunday sermon in 1517? Argue using the four criteria (importance to people at the time; depth and durability; quantity affected; reveals broader trends).
Answer Key

Yes, by all four criteria. (1) Time importance: contemporaries (esp. by 1520) recognised the Theses as a major event; Tetzel's sermons were routine. (2) Depth/durability: 95 Theses triggered a permanent European religious schism; sermons were ephemeral. (3) Quantity: tens of millions ultimately affected; Tetzel's sermons reached perhaps thousands. (4) Broader trends: the Theses illustrate print-enabled mass religious mobilisation, the cracking of papal authority, the role of accidental triggers in major change. Strong responses apply all four criteria explicitly. 4 marks: 1 per criterion well-applied.

15
[3]
STSE / Real-World Application: How does understanding the Columbian Exchange help us think about contemporary global trade and biosecurity (e.g., pandemic prevention, invasive species)? 3–4 sentences with one specific contemporary example.
Answer Key

Strong responses connect: pre-1492 biota were geographically isolated; 1492 began a single global biotic exchange whose consequences are still unfolding. Contemporary parallels: COVID-19 demonstrated continued vulnerability of populations to novel pathogens; invasive species (zebra mussels, emerald ash borer) continue to reshape ecosystems via trade; biosecurity is a permanent demand of globalised commerce. 3 marks: 1 for the historical connection, 1 for the contemporary example, 1 for communication.
