Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
Define and date: (a) Marshall Plan, (b) NATO, (c) Warsaw Pact.
Answer Key

(a) Marshall Plan (June 1947): ~$13 billion in US aid to rebuild Western Europe and resist communism. (b) NATO (April 1949): North Atlantic Treaty Organization military alliance. (c) Warsaw Pact (May 1955): Soviet-led counter-alliance of Eastern European communist states. 1 mark each.

2
[2]
Approximate the death toll of the Korean War (1950–1953).
Answer Key

B — estimates of ~2-4 million total dead, the majority Korean civilians. The war ended in armistice (not peace treaty), and the 38th-parallel border still divides the peninsula.

3
[3]
Define each: (a) perestroika, (b) glasnost, (c) Velvet Revolution.
Answer Key

(a) Perestroika: Gorbachev's economic "restructuring" from 1985. (b) Glasnost: "openness" — relaxation of censorship and political discussion. (c) Velvet Revolution: Czechoslovakia's nearly bloodless overthrow of communist rule, November 1989, with Václav Havel emerging as leader.

The Indian leader of non-violent independence struggle, assassinated in 1948, was:
Answer Key

B — Gandhi was assassinated by a Hindu nationalist on 30 January 1948.

(a) South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, or Singapore. (b) 2001.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
7
[5]
"From Stettin in the Baltic to Trieste in the Adriatic, an iron curtain has descended across the Continent. Behind that line lie all the capitals of the ancient states of Central and Eastern Europe… all these famous cities and the populations around them lie in what I must call the Soviet sphere, and all are subject in one form or another, not only to Soviet influence but to a very high and, in many cases, increasing measure of control from Moscow." — Winston Churchill, Fulton, Missouri, March 1946
Analyse this primary source. (a) When and where was it delivered, and why does that matter? (b) What political claim is Churchill making? (c) How might a Soviet historian have read this speech in 1946? 5-7 sentences.
Answer Key

(a) March 1946, Fulton, Missouri, USA, with President Truman in attendance. Churchill was out of office (Conservative defeat July 1945) but a global figure; he was issuing a warning publicly because he could not do so from within government. (b) Eastern Europe is being subjugated to Soviet control; the wartime alliance is over; the West must respond. (c) Soviet historians read it as proof of Western aggression and the start of the Cold War's anti-Soviet alliance; Stalin denounced it. 5 marks: 2 for context, 1 for claim, 2 for perspective-taking.

8
[4]
Why did Ghana's independence (1957) proceed largely peacefully while Algeria's (1962) required an eight-year war? Compare structural factors. 5-7 sentences.
Answer Key

(1) Settler population: Algeria had ~1 million pieds-noirs; Ghana had small British administrative population. (2) Legal status: Algeria was constitutionally departments of France since 1848; Ghana was a colony. (3) Economic stakes: Algerian oil/gas just being developed; Ghanaian cocoa less strategic. (4) Metropolitan politics: Fourth Republic France could not absorb the loss; Britain's Conservative government accepted decolonisation earlier. (5) Leadership style: Nkrumah pursued constitutional negotiation; the FLN saw armed struggle as necessary. 4 marks: 1 each for at least three factors, 1 for synthesis.

9
[4]
Why did China grow so rapidly after 1978? Suggest three converging causes. 5-7 sentences.
Answer Key

(1) Policy shift: Deng's market reforms (household-responsibility, SEZs). (2) Demographic dividend: large young working-age population. (3) Education investment under Mao despite Cultural Revolution disruption. (4) Globalisation: multinationals seeking low-cost manufacturing, WTO accession 2001. (5) State capacity: large infrastructure (HSR, Pudong). (6) Low starting base: 1978 per-capita GDP comparable to sub-Saharan Africa. 4 marks: 1 each for three causes, 1 for synthesis.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
10
[6]
Write a PEEL paragraph (6-8 sentences) on the claim: "The 1989-1991 collapse of communism was not the West 'winning' but the Soviet leadership choosing not to use force." Use specific evidence.
Answer Key

Strong responses cite: Gorbachev's restraint on Polish Solidarity (1988-89), refusal to send tanks to East Germany (October 1989), acceptance of German reunification (1990); contrast with 1953 (East German uprising), 1956 (Hungary), 1968 (Czechoslovakia) when Moscow did intervene. Soviet economic weakness alone did not require these political choices (Brezhnev had managed weakness with repression). Counter-evidence: Western pressure and Eastern European agency also mattered. The thesis can survive with the qualifier "Gorbachev's restraint was decisive, not sole." 6 marks: 1 Point, 2 Evidence, 2 Explanation, 1 Link/conventions.

11
[3]
Rewrite this weak thesis: "Globalisation has been good and bad."
Answer Key

Sample: "Globalisation since 1980 has reduced extreme poverty by ~70 percent worldwide, primarily through the integration of Asian economies into global trade, while simultaneously widening income inequality within most rich countries and concentrating cultural and technological power in fewer hands — a distributional pattern that explains both its defenders and its populist critics." Look for: specific claims (1), debatable (1), engages distribution (1).

12
[3]
In a short paragraph (4 sentences), compare the Indian (1947) and Algerian (1962) decolonisations as historical events.
Answer Key

Both ended European colonial rule but differed dramatically. India achieved independence through long political negotiation with mass civil disobedience; the violence was post-independence, in the partition's communal mass killings (1-2 million dead). Algeria fought an eight-year war (1954-1962) against French colonialism with ~500,000–1.5 million Algerian dead; the violence preceded independence. Both shaped European political crisis (Britain's imperial retreat; France's Fourth Republic collapse and de Gaulle's return). 3 marks: 1 each for India, Algeria, comparison.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
13
[5]
Apply the ethical-dimension concept to Canada's residential-school system (1831-1996). What evidence does the TRC's 2015 final report cite, and what 94 Calls to Action follow from it? 6-8 sentences.
Answer Key

Evidence: ~150,000 Indigenous children removed from families across the 19th-20th centuries; physical, sexual, and emotional abuse documented; thousands of deaths (the 2021 unmarked-grave confirmations at Kamloops, Marieval, Cowessess, Williams Lake added urgency); cultural destruction by forcible assimilation; intergenerational trauma. 94 Calls to Action: child welfare, education, language and culture, health, justice (especially missing and murdered Indigenous women), reconciliation. The TRC framed residential schools as "cultural genocide" — an ethical judgement grounded in evidence. Marks: 3 for evidence, 2 for Calls to Action.

14
[4]
Apply continuity-and-change reasoning to the Cold War (1947–1991). What persisted across 44 years, and what shifted? 5-7 sentences.
Answer Key

Continuities: nuclear deterrence / MAD; bipolar superpower rivalry; proxy wars in the Global South; ideological framing (capitalism vs communism); arms race. Changes: phases include First Cold War (1947-1962), Détente (1963-1979), Second Cold War (1979-1985), Endgame (1985-1991); arms-control treaties (LTBT 1963, ABM/SALT I 1972, INF 1987); Sino-Soviet split (1960s); changing technologies; the collapse itself was contingent. Marks: 2 for continuities, 2 for change.

15
[3]
STSE / Real-World Application: How does the climate crisis fit into your historical understanding of post-1945 globalisation? 3-4 sentences.
Answer Key

Climate change is the central externality of fossil-fuel industrialisation that drove globalisation. Cumulative CO₂ rose from ~280 ppm pre-industrial to ~420 ppm by 2020s; two-thirds of emissions are since 1990. The historical responsibility is concentrated (industrialised North Atlantic and now China) but the consequences fall most heavily on the Global South. The 2015 Paris Agreement reflects partial collective recognition; 1.5°C will likely be exceeded in the late 2020s. 3 marks: 1 for the linkage to globalisation, 1 for responsibility/distribution, 1 for the policy moment.
