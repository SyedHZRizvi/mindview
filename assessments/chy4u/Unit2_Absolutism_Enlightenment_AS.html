Topic 2.1 — Absolutism
Question 1
Louis XIV's relocation of the French court to Versailles from 1682 served primarily to:
Solution: Versailles institutionalised the lever, coucher, and elaborate precedence rituals that kept nobles in close royal supervision and competing for proximity to the king rather than plotting in the provinces. A core technique of absolutist rule.
Question 2
The 1685 Revocation of the Edict of Nantes by Louis XIV:
Solution: The 1598 Edict of Nantes (Henry IV) had granted Huguenots limited toleration. Louis XIV's 1685 revocation produced a major refugee flow to the Netherlands, England, Brandenburg, and even the Cape Colony, weakening French commerce.
Topic 2.2 — Constitutional Revolutions
Question 3
The 1689 English Bill of Rights established that:
Solution: The Bill required parliamentary consent for taxes, standing armies, and the suspension of statutes; guaranteed freedom of speech in Parliament; barred Catholics from the throne; and conditioned William and Mary's accession on these terms. Still partly in force in the UK today.
Question 4
John Locke's Two Treatises of Government (1689) argued that legitimate government:
Solution: Locke's natural-rights and consent-based theory was the influential answer to Hobbes's defence of absolute sovereignty (Leviathan, 1651). Locke would directly inform the American Declaration of Independence (1776).
Topic 2.3 — Enlightenment
Question 5
Montesquieu's The Spirit of the Laws (1748) most influentially developed:
Solution: Separation of powers, adopted by the US Constitution (1787). The "general will" is Rousseau (1762); labour theory of value Locke/Smith/Marx; evolution Darwin (1859).
Question 6
Mary Wollstonecraft's A Vindication of the Rights of Woman (1792) is best understood as:
Solution: Wollstonecraft extended Lockean and Painean natural-rights arguments to women's education and political rights. Her work foreshadowed 19th-century suffragism.
Topic 2.4 — Atlantic Slave Economy
Question 7
The 18th century was the period in which the Atlantic slave trade:
Solution: The 18th century saw the trade at peak volume: ~6.6 million of the ~12.5 million total embarked between 1700 and 1808. Britain, France, Portugal, and the Netherlands were the largest carriers.
Question 8
In 1789, the wealthiest plantation colony in the world was:
Solution: Saint-Domingue produced ~40% of world sugar and ~60% of world coffee with ~500,000 enslaved Africans. Its revolution (1791–1804) destroyed French plantation wealth and produced the second independent state in the Americas.
Topic 2.5 — East Asia
Question 9
The Tokugawa shogunate's policy of sakoku ("closed country") from 1635:
Solution: Sakoku restricted but did not eliminate foreign contact; Dutch traders at Dejima brought European medical and scientific texts that fed rangaku ("Dutch studies"). Total isolation is a common misconception.
Topic 2.6 — Enlightened Absolutism
Question 10
Joseph II's Toleranzpatent of 1781 granted:
Solution: Joseph II's 1781 Toleranzpatent (and parallel Edict on Jewish civil rights) was one of Europe's earliest large-scale religious-toleration laws. Joseph also abolished personal serfdom and dissolved many monasteries, though several reforms were reversed after his 1790 death.
