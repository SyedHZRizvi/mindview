Unit 2: Absolutism & Enlightenment — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand C
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps from Unit 1 and prior coursework before starting Unit 2. Be honest about what is unclear.
Question 1 Prereq · Terms
[3 marks]
Define each: (a) absolutism, (b) constitutionalism, (c) divine right of kings.
Question 2 Skills · Comparison
[3 marks]
In one paragraph, compare French and English political development between 1640 and 1715: in what direction did each move?
Question 3 Source analysis
[4 marks]
"Men being, as has been said, by Nature, all free, equal and independent, no one can be put out of this Estate, and subjected to the Political Power of another, without his own Consent." — John Locke, Second Treatise of Government, §95 (1689)
(a) Whose theory of government is Locke contesting? (b) What does this passage assume about human nature? (c) Name one historical event from the same year (1689) that contextualises this writing.
Question 4 Knowledge · Enlightenment
[4 marks]
Match each Enlightenment thinker to their major work (write the pairs): Voltaire — Montesquieu — Rousseau — Wollstonecraft. Works: Candide; The Social Contract; A Vindication of the Rights of Woman; The Spirit of the Laws.
Question 5 Skills · Cause/effect
[3 marks]
Suggest two ways the Atlantic slave trade economically benefited Britain in the 18th century. (You will refine these in Unit 2.)
Question 6 Knowledge · Geography
[2 marks]
Identify the location of (a) the wealthiest Caribbean sugar colony of the late 18th century, and (b) the capital of Tokugawa Japan.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which Unit 2 topic do you feel least confident about: Louis XIV's absolutism, the English constitutional revolution, Enlightenment ideas, 18th-century Atlantic slavery, or East Asia? Explain specifically what is unclear.
