Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
1
[3]
Distinguish among the following agricultural systems: subsistence, commercial, industrial, organic. Give one country or region example of each.
Answer Key

Subsistence: farmer eats most of harvest (sub-Saharan Africa, rural India). Commercial: farming primarily for sale (Canadian Prairies, Iowa). Industrial: mechanised high-input commercial agriculture (U.S. Midwest, Argentine pampas). Organic: no synthetic inputs; mostly commercial-scale (parts of EU, BC, California). Mark distribution: 0.5 per defined system, 0.25 per valid example.

2
[2]
The dominant cause of biodiversity loss from urbanization is:
Answer Key

B. Habitat conversion (farmland and forest to housing) and fragmentation (roads dividing populations) are the dominant proximate drivers of biodiversity loss from urbanization. The others matter but are secondary.

3
[3]
Define each: hazard, exposure, vulnerability. How does each contribute to disaster risk?
Answer Key

Hazard: the physical event (earthquake, hurricane). Exposure: people and infrastructure in the hazard's path. Vulnerability: how poorly prepared/resilient that exposure is. Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability. Reducing any of the three reduces risk; reducing vulnerability (building codes, early warning) is often the most cost-effective.

4
[2]
The Pacific Ring of Fire is best described as a:
Answer Key

B. Around the Pacific Plate margin lie subduction zones (Andean, Aleutian, Japanese, Indonesian, Tongan) and transform faults (San Andreas) that produce roughly 75% of Earth's volcanoes and 90% of its earthquakes.

5
[3]
List three different agricultural impacts on water systems and one mitigation strategy for each.
Answer Key

(1) Nitrate runoff from fertilizers — contaminates groundwater; mitigation: cover cropping, nutrient management plans. (2) Phosphorus runoff — eutrophies lakes (e.g., Erie); mitigation: buffer strips, reduced fertilizer application. (3) Pesticide drift — contaminates surface water; mitigation: integrated pest management, no-spray buffers. Other valid examples: irrigation depletion, soil erosion siltation, livestock waste.

6
[2]
Beef production is roughly how many times less efficient at converting grain to human food than chicken?
Answer Key

B. Beef requires ~7 kg grain per kg beef; chicken needs ~2 kg per kg. 7/2 = 3.5× less efficient. Plant-based protein is roughly 3× more efficient than chicken again, so an order of magnitude more efficient than beef.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
7
[4]
A 100-year flood has a 1% annual probability. Calculate the probability of experiencing at least one such flood in a 30-year mortgage. Show the formula and the arithmetic.
Answer Key

P(at least one) = 1 − P(none) = 1 − (1 − 0.01)³⁰ = 1 − 0.99³⁰ = 1 − 0.740 = 0.260 = 26%. The probability of seeing one in a 50-year career is 1 − 0.99⁵⁰ ≈ 39%. This is why floodplain housing is a meaningful long-term risk, even when the annual probability seems small.

8
[4]
Between 1976 and 2016 the GTA's population doubled (×2.1) while its developed area tripled (×3.5). (a) What was the change in per-capita land use? (b) If per-capita use had remained constant, how much smaller would the developed area be in 2016?
Answer Key

(a) Per-capita multiplier = 3.5/2.1 = 1.67, a 67% increase. (b) Constant per-capita use would have implied an area multiplier of 2.1; the actual multiplier of 3.5 is 1.67× larger; so the 2016 footprint is 1.67× greater than necessary. If the 1976 area was, say, 1,000 km², the constant-density 2016 area would be 2,100 km² but the actual is 3,500 km² — about 1,400 km² of additional land consumed for the same population growth. (Class 1 farmland equivalents.)

9
[4]
A 5-hectare meadow (runoff coefficient C = 0.20) is converted to a parking lot (C = 0.95). For a 50 mm/hr design storm, use Q = CiA to compare pre- and post-development peak runoff. Show your work.
Answer Key

A = 50,000 m²; i = 50 mm/hr = 0.05/3600 = 1.39 × 10⁻⁵ m/s. Meadow: Q = 0.20 × 1.39 × 10⁻⁵ × 50,000 = 0.139 m³/s. Asphalt: Q = 0.95 × 1.39 × 10⁻⁵ × 50,000 = 0.660 m³/s. Increase ≈ 4.75×. Strong answer notes downstream flood risk and need for stormwater management infrastructure.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
10
[4]
Explain the urban heat island in 3–5 sentences. Identify two physical causes and one mitigation strategy.
Answer Key

The urban heat island is the phenomenon of city centres being 2–10 °C warmer than surrounding rural areas. Causes: (1) Concrete, asphalt, and dark roofs absorb and store more solar heat than vegetation. (2) Waste heat from buildings (especially air conditioning) and vehicles adds to the local energy budget. (3) Reduced evapotranspiration in paved cities. Mitigations: increasing tree canopy (Toronto's 40% canopy target), green roofs, cool-coloured pavements, reducing impervious surfaces. Strong answers connect physical mechanism to climate-adaptation policy.

11
[4]
Compare the environmental impacts of conventional industrial agriculture vs. organic and agroecological alternatives. Use proper terminology (e.g., monoculture, cover crop, integrated pest management).
Answer Key

Conventional industrial agriculture: high yield per hectare; high inputs (synthetic fertilizer, pesticides, fossil-fuel mechanisation); monoculture; soil degradation; water and chemical pollution; high embodied energy. Organic/agroecological: lower per-hectare yields (typically 80–95% of conventional); cover crops, crop rotation, integrated pest management; soil-carbon building; lower input costs but higher labour; reduced pollution; higher biodiversity. Land-use trade-off: more land needed to produce the same calories, but landscape-level biodiversity benefits. Strong answers note that "best practice" combines features of both.

12
[4]
Define each: sprawl, smart growth, Greenbelt, intensification.
Answer Key

Sprawl: low-density car-dependent urban expansion into farmland or natural area. Smart growth: planning approach emphasising compact development, mixed-use, transit, walkability, infill. Greenbelt: protected ring of farmland and natural area around a metropolitan region (Ontario Greenbelt established 2005). Intensification: increasing density within existing built-up areas, often through transit-oriented development, infill, and laneway housing.

Part D: Application [11 marks]
13
[5]
The Greater Vancouver Regional District is planning hazard mitigation for a hypothetical Magnitude-9 Cascadia earthquake. As a geographer, identify two physical and two social vulnerability factors and propose two specific mitigation strategies.
Answer Key

Physical vulnerabilities: (1) Soft sediments under downtown Vancouver and the Fraser delta amplify shaking (liquefaction risk). (2) Coastal exposure to subduction-zone tsunami. (3) Older pre-1980s buildings without seismic retrofit. Social vulnerabilities: (1) Concentration of population (2.6 million) and economic activity. (2) Aging populations and renters with limited preparedness. (3) Critical infrastructure dependencies (port, airport, highways). Strategies: (1) Mandatory seismic upgrade of schools, bridges, and pre-1980 multi-family housing. (2) Tsunami evacuation routes and signage in coastal communities. (3) ShakeOut public preparedness drills (annual). (4) BC Earthquake Early Warning system (operational 2024). Strong answers integrate physical and social factors and propose specific, measurable interventions.

14
[3]
An average Canadian household emits roughly 22 t CO₂e/year. List three household-level actions that each reduce annual emissions by ≥1 t. Estimate the total reduction.
Answer Key

Examples: switch from natural-gas furnace to heat pump (~2 t); replace ICE car with EV in Ontario's clean grid (~2.5 t); avoid one transatlantic flight (~1.5 t); reduce beef consumption by half (~0.8 t); install rooftop solar (~1.2 t in Ontario). Sample total: 2.0 + 2.5 + 1.5 = 6 t, or about 27% reduction. Strong answers note that a Paris-compliant 2.5 t per person target requires additional system-level (grid, urban-form) change.

15
[3]
A Canadian retailer sources 100% of its winter strawberries from California. As climate change worsens California drought, evaluate the supply-chain vulnerability and propose two alternative sourcing strategies.
Answer Key

Vulnerability: California's Central Valley produces ~90% of U.S. strawberries; the multi-year megadrought has reduced agricultural water allocations and raised fallowing rates. Single-source supply chains are vulnerable to climate-amplified drought, wildfire (heat damage to ripening fruit), labour-shortage strikes, and price spikes. Alternatives: (1) Diversify sources geographically (Mexico, Florida, Peru). (2) Invest in local greenhouse production (Ontario tender-fruit greenhouse pilot programs). (3) Shift seasonal availability messaging — celebrate Ontario in-season berries June–July, accept lower variety in winter. (4) Vertical-farm pilot for high-value berries in urban Ontario. Strong answers note costs of each alternative and resilience trade-offs.
