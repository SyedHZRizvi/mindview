Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[3]B · Spheres
Name the four physical spheres of Earth and explain one interaction between any two of them.
Answer Key

Lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere. Interactions: mountain ranges (lith) deflect prevailing winds (atm); ocean evaporation (hyd) supplies atmospheric water; photosynthesis (bio) generates atmospheric O₂; cellular respiration (bio) releases CO₂ to (atm).

2
[3]B · Tectonics
Identify each plate boundary type and one Canadian or world example: convergent (subduction), divergent, transform.
Answer Key

Convergent (subduction): Cascadia subduction zone off BC; Nazca-South America (Andes). Divergent: Mid-Atlantic Ridge; East African Rift. Transform: San Andreas Fault (California); Queen Charlotte Fault (off BC).

3
[3]C · Biomes
Match each biome to a region: tropical rainforest, boreal forest, temperate grassland, desert.
Answer Key

Tropical rainforest: Amazon, Congo, Indonesia. Boreal forest: Canadian Shield, Russia (taiga), Scandinavia. Temperate grassland: Canadian Prairies, Eurasian steppe, U.S. Great Plains. Desert: Sahara, Arabian, Atacama, Australian outback (subtropical 30° belt).

4
[2]C · 10% rule
Lindeman's 10% rule (1942) means:
Answer Key

B. The energy-transfer efficiency between trophic levels averages about 10%; the rest is lost as heat, locomotion, or undigested matter. This is why ecosystems typically sustain only 4–5 trophic levels.

5
[3]C · Human-Phys
Define each: urban heat island, sprawl, Greenbelt (Ontario), 100-year flood.
Answer Key

Urban heat island: city centre 2–10 °C warmer than rural surroundings. Sprawl: low-density car-dependent urban expansion. Greenbelt: Ontario's 2005 protected farmland and natural ring around the GTA. 100-year flood: a flood with 1% annual probability of occurrence (not an interval).

6
[3]D · Sustainability
State the Brundtland (1987) definition of sustainable development. What are the three pillars of the "triple bottom line"?
Answer Key

"Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs." Three pillars: environmental, economic, social. Sometimes a fourth (cultural/Indigenous) is added.

7
[2]D · Energy
Approximately what share of Canada's electricity is non-emitting (hydro + nuclear + renewables)?
Answer Key

C. ~84% of Canadian electricity is non-emitting (60% hydro + 13% nuclear + 11% wind/solar/biomass). Mix varies by province; some provinces are still gas/coal-heavy and transitioning.

8
[3]D · Indigenous
Define each: Seven Generations principle, Two-Eyed Seeing (Etuaptmumk), IPCA, FPIC.
Answer Key

Seven Generations: Haudenosaunee/Anishinaabe principle of considering effects seven generations ahead. Two-Eyed Seeing: Mi'kmaw concept (Albert and Murdena Marshall) for integrating Indigenous knowledge and Western science. IPCA: Indigenous Protected and Conserved Area; Indigenous-led conservation. FPIC: Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (UNDRIP principle for projects affecting Indigenous lands).

9
[3]E · Climate
State the Paris Agreement temperature goal and approximate current trajectory based on stated policies.
Answer Key

Paris (2015): "well below 2 °C, pursuing 1.5 °C" of warming above pre-industrial. Current trajectories on stated policies: ~2.7 °C by 2100. Pledges if fully implemented: ~2.4 °C. The gap between pledged and required reductions is the central policy problem.

Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
10
[4]B · Hotspot velocity
Kauai is 540 km NW of the active Hawaiian hotspot and dated at 5.1 million years. Calculate the Pacific Plate motion in cm/yr. Show work.
Answer Key

Rate = 540 km / 5.1 × 10⁶ yr = 1.06 × 10⁻⁴ km/yr = 10.6 cm/year. Matches independent GPS measurements.

11
[4]B · Residence time
Lake Superior: V = 12,100 km³; outflow Q = 66 km³/yr. Lake Erie: V = 484 km³; Q = 175 km³/yr. Calculate residence time of each. Which lake will flush a pollutant faster, and why does this matter for policy?
Answer Key

Superior: τ = 12,100/66 ≈ 183 yr. Erie: τ = 484/175 ≈ 2.8 yr. Erie flushes ~66× faster. Policy: Erie's 1960s eutrophication recovered relatively quickly after phosphorus controls; Superior's contaminants persist for centuries, so precaution is essential.

12
[4]C · Simpson's D
A Carolinian forest plot contains 25 sugar maples, 15 black walnuts, 7 tulip trees, 3 sassafras. Calculate Simpson's diversity index D = 1 − Σ(pᵢ)². Show work.
Answer Key

Total = 50. p = 0.5, 0.3, 0.14, 0.06. Σ(p²) = 0.25 + 0.09 + 0.0196 + 0.0036 = 0.3632. D = 1 − 0.3632 = 0.637. Moderate diversity; a monoculture would be 0; an even 4-species mix would be 0.75.

13
[4]C · Trophic
A meadow fixes 10,000 kJ/m²/yr at the producer level. How much energy reaches a fourth-trophic-level predator? How many hectares are needed to support a wolf needing 3.65 × 10⁶ kJ/yr?
Answer Key

Energy at T4 = 10,000 × 0.1³ = 10 kJ/m²/yr. Area = 3.65 × 10⁶ / 10 = 365,000 m² = 36.5 hectares per wolf. A pack of six needs ≥ 220 hectares; real territories are typically 100+ km² because of inefficiencies.

14
[3]D · MSY
An idealised cod population has intrinsic growth rate r = 0.2/yr and carrying capacity K = 2 × 10⁶ tonnes. Compute Maximum Sustainable Yield MSY = rK/4 and compare with 1968 catches of 800,000 tonnes/yr.
Answer Key

MSY = 0.2 × 2 × 10⁶ / 4 = 100,000 tonnes/yr. Peak 1968 catch was 8× MSY. After 1992 collapse, biologists revised K downward by 60% or more (regime shift); revised MSY would be far lower. Lesson: precautionary management below calculated MSY is more robust.

15
[3]E · Probability
A "100-year flood" has 1% annual probability. What is the probability of at least one such flood in a 30-year mortgage period? Show formula and arithmetic.
Answer Key

P(at least one) = 1 − (1 − 0.01)³⁰ = 1 − 0.99³⁰ ≈ 1 − 0.740 = 0.260 = 26%. Climate change is shifting historical 100-year events to 30-year events in many regions.

16
[3]E · NDVI
A satellite pixel of intact rainforest has NIR = 0.42, Red = 0.06. A year later (after clearing) NIR = 0.18, Red = 0.20. Compute NDVI for each.
Answer Key

Intact: NDVI = (0.42 − 0.06)/(0.42 + 0.06) = 0.36/0.48 = 0.75 (healthy forest). Cleared: NDVI = (0.18 − 0.20)/(0.18 + 0.20) = −0.02/0.38 ≈ −0.05 (bare soil). Dramatic NDVI drop is a real-time deforestation signal.

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
17
[5]B · Hadley
Using a labeled diagram (description acceptable), explain why deserts cluster around 30° latitude and rainforests near the equator. Use terms: Hadley cell, ITCZ, adiabatic cooling, descending air.
Answer Key

At the equator, intense solar heating produces rising air (ITCZ). Rising air cools adiabatically; water vapour condenses; heavy precipitation produces tropical rainforests. The cooled, dried air moves poleward in the upper troposphere and descends near 30° latitude (subtropical highs). Descending air warms adiabatically, increasing its capacity to hold water vapour → dry conditions → subtropical deserts (Sahara, Arabian, Atacama, Australian outback). Diagram should show two-cell arrows with surface highs at 30° and surface low at 0°.

18
[5]C · Boreal
Write a 4–6 sentence explanation of why the boreal forest matters to global climate. Use the terms carbon storage, permafrost, fire regime.
Answer Key

The boreal forest stores roughly 30% of all terrestrial carbon — more than tropical rainforest by total mass — because much of it is in soil organic matter and permafrost peatlands. Permafrost thaw with rising temperatures could release vast amounts of stored carbon as CO₂ and methane, an amplifying positive feedback for climate change. The natural fire regime (50–150-year return) is intensifying with warming and drying; the 2023 fire season burned 17.4 million hectares (about 5% of Canada's forested area). Whether the boreal remains a net carbon sink or flips to a net source is one of the largest uncertainties in 21st-century climate projections.

19
[5]D · Doughnut
Sketch (or describe) Kate Raworth's "doughnut" model. Identify two ecological boundaries Canada exceeds and two social-floor measures Canada generally meets.
Answer Key

Doughnut diagram has inner ring (social floor: food, water, health, education, voice) and outer ring (ecological ceiling: nine planetary boundaries). Canada exceeds: climate change (per-capita emissions ~14 t/yr vs safe ~1.6 t), biodiversity loss, nitrogen flow (agricultural runoff), land-system change. Canada generally meets: food access, education, basic health (though Indigenous outcomes lag). Increasingly failing on housing affordability and Indigenous self-determination. The policy goal: stay inside both rings.

20
[5]E · Communication
Write a 4–6 sentence newspaper-style explanation of how attribution science quantifies climate change's role in specific extreme weather events. Use the 2021 BC heat dome as an example.
Answer Key

Attribution science compares observed extreme events with climate-model simulations run both with and without anthropogenic greenhouse gases. The 2021 BC heat dome killed 619 people and was the deadliest weather disaster in Canadian history. Attribution scientists at the World Weather Attribution group concluded the event was at least 150 times more likely because of human-caused warming than it would have been pre-industrial. Climate change doesn't always create new extremes, but it sharply increases probability and intensity. The technique is now applied routinely to heatwaves, fires, floods, and storms; published assessments are typically available within weeks of an event.

21
[5]A · Definitions
Define each: residence time, NDVI, ecological footprint, just transition, free prior informed consent (FPIC).
Answer Key

Residence time: τ = V/Q, average time a substance spends in a reservoir. NDVI = (NIR − Red)/(NIR + Red), satellite-based chlorophyll proxy. Ecological footprint: bioproductive area in global hectares (gha) required to supply a person's consumption and absorb wastes. Just transition: policy framework ensuring workers and communities are not abandoned during shift away from carbon-intensive industries. FPIC: Free, Prior, and Informed Consent — UNDRIP principle for projects affecting Indigenous territories.

Part D — Application [25 marks]
22
[5]B · Stormwater
A 10-hectare meadow (runoff coefficient C = 0.20) is being developed into a parking lot (C = 0.95). For a 50 mm/hr design storm, calculate Q = CiA for both. Recommend two specific stormwater-management strategies.
Answer Key

A = 100,000 m²; i = 50 mm/hr = 1.39 × 10⁻⁵ m/s. Meadow: Q = 0.20 × 1.39 × 10⁻⁵ × 100,000 = 0.278 m³/s. Asphalt: Q = 0.95 × 1.39 × 10⁻⁵ × 100,000 = 1.32 m³/s. ~4.75× increase. Strategies: bioswales, permeable pavement, retention pond, green roofs, infiltration trenches. Strong answers note Ontario municipalities now require pre-development runoff matching for new sites.

23
[5]D · Personal action
A Toronto household (4 people, ~22 t CO₂e/yr baseline) considers: (a) switch beef to chicken half-time, (b) replace gas furnace with heat pump, (c) one ICE car → EV, (d) skip one transatlantic flight, (e) install 10 kW rooftop solar. Estimate total reduction in tonnes per year and as a percentage. What system-level changes would be needed to reach 2.5 t/person Paris-consistent target?
Answer Key

(a) ~0.8 t; (b) ~2.0 t; (c) ~2.5 t in Ontario; (d) ~1.5 t; (e) ~1.2 t. Total ~8 t/yr, ~36% of baseline. Per-person reduction from 5.5 t to ~3.5 t. Reaching 2.5 t/person requires further grid decarbonisation (Ontario 2035 zero-emission electricity target), building stock electrification at scale, transit expansion, embodied-carbon reductions in consumer goods, food-system shifts.

24
[5]E · Hazard adaptation
Greater Vancouver is preparing for a hypothetical Magnitude-9 Cascadia subduction earthquake. Identify two physical and two social vulnerability factors and propose two specific mitigation/adaptation strategies.
Answer Key

Physical: (1) Liquefiable Fraser-delta sediments amplify shaking. (2) Tsunami exposure to subduction-zone-generated wave. (3) Pre-1980s buildings without seismic retrofit. Social: (1) Concentrated population (2.6 M); critical infrastructure dependence (port, airport). (2) Aging populations, renters with limited preparedness. (3) Multilingual communication challenges in emergencies. Strategies: Mandatory seismic upgrades for pre-1980 housing and schools; tsunami evacuation routes and signage; BC Earthquake Early Warning system (operational 2024); annual ShakeOut drills; backup power and water supplies at hospitals and shelters.

25
[5]D · IPCA & consent
A First Nation is considering whether to host a proposed lithium mine on traditional treaty territory. Use FPIC and Impact and Benefit Agreement (IBA) frameworks to outline the conditions and protections the nation should require.
Answer Key

FPIC: (1) Free — no coercion. (2) Prior — consent obtained before any commitment. (3) Informed — full disclosure of impacts. (4) Consent — affirmative and revocable. IBA conditions: revenue-sharing percentages; preferential local employment; community-co-led environmental monitoring; reclamation bonds posted in advance; cultural protection; ongoing community veto if conditions deteriorate. Strong answers note UNDRIP implementation in Canadian law (2021) makes FPIC a domestic legal principle, not merely an international norm.

26
[5]E · Capstone inquiry
Design a brief capstone inquiry: "Which Toronto neighbourhoods are most exposed to summer extreme heat, and how does the pattern relate to tree-canopy cover and median household income?" Outline (1) the five inquiry steps, (2) one data source per step, and (3) one limitation of the analysis.
Answer Key

(1) Question (given): spatially explicit, tractable, significant, open. (2) Gather: Landsat thermal-IR data; City of Toronto Open Data Portal canopy layer; Statistics Canada census on income and demographics. (3) Analyse: QGIS spatial joins; compute neighbourhood-level mean surface temperature, canopy %, income; map. (4) Evaluate: correlation tests; compare with peer-reviewed Toronto UHI studies (e.g., Patterson & Pyrch 2023); examine residuals. (5) Communicate: 6-page report with maps and bivariate charts; infographic for public audience. Limitations: single-day satellite vs multi-day heat events; missing demographic controls (age, building stock); spatial correlation is not causation. Strong answers acknowledge uncertainty explicitly.
