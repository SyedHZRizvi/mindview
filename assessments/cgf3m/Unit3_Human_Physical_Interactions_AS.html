Question 1
The Green Revolution refers to:
Solution: Norman Borlaug's Green Revolution (Nobel Peace Prize 1970) tripled wheat yields in Mexico and transformed Indian agriculture. It is credited with averting roughly a billion famine deaths but introduced costs (groundwater depletion, fertilizer pollution, monoculture vulnerability).
Question 2
Producing 1 kg of beef requires roughly 7 kg of grain. If grain contains about 3,500 kcal/kg, what's the food-energy conversion efficiency of beef? (kcal output / kcal input)
Solution: Output ≈ 2,500 kcal/kg edible beef. Input = 7 × 3,500 = 24,500 kcal. Efficiency = 2,500 / 24,500 ≈ 10.2%. This is essentially the 10% rule from ecology applied to industrial agriculture.
Question 3
The "urban heat island" effect typically raises city temperatures by:
Solution: Dense urban areas with concrete, asphalt, and waste heat are typically 2–10 °C warmer than surrounding rural areas. Toronto's UHI averages about 4–5 °C in summer evenings, with neighbourhood variation tied to tree canopy and building density.
Question 4
A "100-year flood" means:
Solution: "100-year flood" describes an annual probability, not a calendar interval. There is a 1% chance per year, and a roughly 26% chance over a 30-year mortgage period. Climate change is increasing the frequency of historically "100-year" floods.
Question 5
The 2016 Fort McMurray wildfire forced about 88,000 people to evacuate. It was an example of a:
Solution: A wildland-urban interface (WUI) fire spreads from forest into developed areas. The 2016 Fort McMurray fire burned 590,000 hectares of Alberta boreal forest and 2,400 buildings; rebuilding cost about $9.9 billion, Canada's costliest natural disaster.
Question 6
Canada's per-capita ecological footprint is approximately:
Solution: Canada's footprint of ~7.7 gha is among the world's highest (only some Persian Gulf states, U.S., Australia are higher). World average is 2.7 gha; Earth's biocapacity is only 1.6 gha per person.
Question 7
A January tomato in Toronto can come from (a) a heated Leamington greenhouse or (b) field-grown Mexico. Which option typically has the LOWER carbon footprint?
Solution: The heated Ontario greenhouse uses natural gas — about 2.0 kg CO₂/kg tomato. The field-grown Mexican tomato has 0.4 kg from growing + 0.4 kg from trucking = 0.8 kg. Production method matters more than distance in most lifecycle analyses, especially in winter.
Question 8
The Athabasca oil sands account for roughly what share of Canadian greenhouse gas emissions?
Solution: Oil sands extraction and upgrading produces about 12% of Canada's domestic GHG emissions — the single largest source sector. Combustion downstream (mostly in U.S. and overseas) produces additional emissions counted in importing countries' inventories.
Question 9
Earth Overshoot Day for the world in 2024 fell on approximately:
Solution: Global Earth Overshoot Day 2024 was August 1. Canada's overshoot day was March 14 — if everyone lived like Canadians, the world would consume its annual biocapacity by mid-March.
Question 10
Of nine planetary boundaries identified by Rockström et al., how many had been crossed by humanity as of 2023?
Solution: The 2023 update of the planetary boundaries framework found that humanity has crossed six of the nine boundaries: climate change, biosphere integrity, biogeochemical flows (N and P), land-system change, freshwater change, and novel entities. Ocean acidification and aerosol loading are at risk; stratospheric ozone is recovering.
