Question 1
The Brundtland Report (1987) defined sustainable development as:
Solution: Gro Harlem Brundtland's 1987 report Our Common Future is the source of the canonical definition, ratified by every major UN sustainability process since.
Question 2
Kate Raworth's "doughnut" model identifies sustainability as the space:
Solution: The doughnut defines a safe and just operating space — above a social foundation (food, water, health, education, voice) and below the ecological ceiling defined by the nine planetary boundaries.
Question 3
The Atlantic cod moratorium was imposed in:
Solution: The 1992 Northern Cod moratorium cost 35,000 fishery jobs and remains the largest single mass layoff in Canadian history. The food-web disruption proved so severe that the stock has only partly recovered three decades later.
Question 4
From 2010 to 2024, utility-scale solar electricity cost (LCOE) fell approximately:
Solution: Utility-scale solar LCOE fell from about $380/MWh in 2010 to $33/MWh in 2024 — approximately a 91% drop. Onshore wind fell about 70%. Both are now cheaper than new fossil-fuel generation in most markets.
Question 5
If a Toronto home consumes 9,000 kWh/year and rooftop solar in Toronto produces 240 kWh/m²/yr at 20% panel efficiency (1200 kWh/m² annual irradiance), what panel area is needed?
Solution: Area = 9,000 / 240 = 37.5 m². About 22 typical 1.7-m² panels = ~8.8 kW system. Capital cost ~$22,000 at $2.50/W; simple payback ~16 years.
Question 6
Canada's electricity grid is approximately what percentage non-emitting (hydro + nuclear + renewables)?
Solution: About 84% of Canadian electricity is non-emitting (60% hydro, 13% nuclear, 11% wind/solar/biomass). But the mix varies enormously by province — Quebec/Manitoba/BC are mostly hydro; Alberta/Saskatchewan are still gas-and-coal dominant though transitioning.
Question 7
As of late 2024, Canada had approximately how many long-term drinking-water advisories on First Nations reserves?
Solution: The federal government committed in 2015 to end all long-term advisories by 2021, but progress has slipped; about 28 long-term advisories on First Nations reserves remained as of late 2024. This is both a sustainability and a human-rights issue.
Question 8
Tallurutiup Imanga (Nunavut, 2019) is Canada's largest:
Solution: Tallurutiup Imanga (Lancaster Sound), established 2019, is Canada's largest marine protected area (108,000 km², roughly the area of New Brunswick) and is co-managed with the Qikiqtani Inuit Association. It is part of Canada's 30 by 30 marine commitment.
Question 9
The R-hierarchy ranks waste strategies from most to least preferred as:
Solution: The R-hierarchy is: refuse (don't acquire) > reduce > reuse > repair > refurbish > remanufacture > repurpose > recycle > recover energy. The earlier strategies eliminate impact; the later ones merely lessen it.
Question 10
Canada's 2022 federal ban on single-use plastics covers which six items?
Solution: Canada's six banned single-use plastics: checkout bags, cutlery, foodservice ware made of problematic plastics (e.g., polystyrene), ring carriers, stir sticks, and straws. Full implementation phased through 2025.
