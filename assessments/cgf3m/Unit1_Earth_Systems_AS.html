Topic 1.1 — The Four Spheres & Energy Balance
Question 1
Earth's natural greenhouse effect is essential for life because it:
Solution: Without the greenhouse effect, Earth's effective blackbody temperature would be about −18 °C. Water vapour and CO₂ absorb outgoing longwave radiation and re-radiate some downward, warming the surface by about 33 °C to the observed +15 °C. The natural effect is essential; the anthropogenic enhancement is the modern problem.
Question 2
Which surface has the highest albedo?
Solution: Fresh snow has albedo around 0.9 (reflects 90% of incoming sunlight). Open ocean is about 0.06, forests 0.10–0.15, asphalt 0.04. This is why Arctic ice loss is a strong positive feedback for climate warming.
Question 3
If the solar constant is 1361 W/m² and Earth's albedo is 0.30, what is the average solar power absorbed per square metre of Earth's surface?
Solution: The Earth intercepts the solar constant over its cross-sectional area πr² but distributes that energy over its full surface area 4πr², so the average input is 1361/4 = 340.25 W/m². Absorbed = (1 − 0.30) × 340.25 = 238 W/m².
Topic 1.2 — Plate Tectonics
Question 4
The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is an example of a:
Solution: The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is a divergent boundary where the North American and Eurasian plates (north) and South American and African plates (south) are spreading apart, creating new oceanic crust. Iceland sits astride the ridge — one of the few places on land where you can stand between two plates.
Question 5
Volcanoes are most common at all of the following boundaries EXCEPT:
Solution: Transform boundaries (like the San Andreas Fault) involve plates sliding past each other horizontally; there is no upwelling magma and no subduction, so volcanism is typically absent. Volcanoes cluster at convergent boundaries, divergent ridges, and hotspots.
Topic 1.3 — Weathering & Glacial Landforms
Question 6
Frost wedging is a form of physical weathering driven by which property of water?
Solution: Water expands about 9% when it freezes. Water that infiltrates a crack and freezes exerts pressure on the surrounding rock, slowly levering it apart. This is one of the most important physical weathering processes in Canadian latitudes.
Question 7
A drumlin is a:
Solution: A drumlin is an elongated hill of glacial till, streamlined in the direction of ice flow (steep upstream, gentle downstream). The Peterborough drumlin field, north of Lake Ontario, contains thousands of well-preserved drumlins from the last Wisconsinan glaciation.
Topic 1.4 — Atmospheric Circulation
Question 8
The world's hot deserts cluster around 30° N and 30° S because:
Solution: Subtropical highs (30° N/S) lie under the descending limb of the Hadley cell. Descending air warms adiabatically, increasing its capacity to hold water vapour — so the descending air is dry. This produces the Sahara, Arabian, Kalahari, Atacama, Australian outback, and Mojave-Sonoran deserts.
Question 9
The Coriolis effect deflects moving air:
Solution: Earth's rotation produces an apparent deflection of free-moving objects — to the right in the Northern Hemisphere, to the left in the Southern. This is why Northern Hemisphere hurricanes rotate counterclockwise and Southern Hemisphere cyclones rotate clockwise.
Topic 1.5 — Hydrologic Cycle
Question 10
Which water reservoir holds the largest share of Earth's fresh water?
Solution: Of Earth's 2.5% freshwater, glaciers and ice caps hold about 1.74% (about 70% of total fresh water). Groundwater is the next-largest reservoir; lakes, rivers, soil moisture, and atmospheric water vapour each hold less than 1% of total fresh water.
Question 11
If Lake Erie has volume 484 km³ and outflow 175 km³/yr, what is its residence time?
Solution: Residence time τ = V/Q = 484 / 175 ≈ 2.77 years. Compare with Lake Superior (12,100 km³ / 66 km³/yr ≈ 183 years). Shorter residence times mean faster pollutant flushing.
Topic 1.6 — Soils
Question 12
The five soil-forming factors (CLORPT) include:
Solution: Hans Jenny's 1941 framework: Climate, Organisms (vegetation and fauna), Relief (slope and topography), Parent material (the underlying rock or sediment), and Time. Each factor shapes soil profile differently; together they explain the global soil map.
