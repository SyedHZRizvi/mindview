Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
1
[3]
Name the four physical spheres of Earth and give one example of an interaction between two named spheres.
Answer Key

Lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere. Example interactions include: mountain ranges (lithosphere) deflecting prevailing winds (atmosphere) creating rainshadows; ocean evaporation (hydrosphere) supplying atmospheric moisture (atmosphere); photosynthesising plants (biosphere) generating atmospheric oxygen (atmosphere). Marks: 2 for the four spheres, 1 for a valid interaction.

2
[2]
Which of the following is a true statement about the natural greenhouse effect?
Answer Key

B. The natural greenhouse effect raises Earth's mean surface temperature from ~ −18 °C (blackbody) to ~ +15 °C, a 33 °C warming essential for liquid water and life. The anthropogenic enhancement is the modern problem.

3
[3]
Match each plate boundary type to its dominant landform/feature: convergent (ocean-continent), divergent (mid-ocean), transform.
Answer Key

Convergent ocean-continent: deep-sea trench, subduction zone, andesitic volcanoes, mountain range (e.g., Andes from Nazca-South America). Divergent mid-ocean: mid-ocean ridge with new oceanic crust (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge); basalt volcanism. Transform: linear faults producing earthquakes without volcanism (e.g., San Andreas Fault).

4
[2]
Distinguish between physical and chemical weathering, giving one example of each.
Answer Key

Physical weathering breaks rock apart without changing chemical composition (e.g., frost wedging, exfoliation). Chemical weathering decomposes rock by altering minerals (e.g., carbonation dissolving limestone to form karst; oxidation of iron-rich rock).

5
[3]
Describe the three-cell model of atmospheric circulation. Name each cell and state the resulting surface climate near 0°, 30°, and 60° latitude.
Answer Key

Hadley cell (0°–30°): rising air at ITCZ → equatorial rainforests; descending dry air at 30° → subtropical deserts. Ferrel cell (30°–60°): mid-latitude westerlies; mixing zone with variable weather. Polar cell (60°–90°): descending dry air at the pole; cold high pressure; polar tundra/deserts. The polar front (~60°) hosts the polar jet stream where storm tracks concentrate.

6
[2]
Most of Earth's fresh water is found in:
Answer Key

B. Of the 2.5% of Earth's water that is fresh, glaciers and ice caps hold about 1.74% (≈ 70% of all fresh water). Groundwater holds most of the rest of accessible fresh water; lakes and rivers hold only 0.013% of total water.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
7
[4]
The Pacific Plate moves NW over a stationary Hawaiian hotspot. Kauai is 540 km from the active vent at the Big Island and is dated at 5.1 million years. Calculate the plate motion rate in cm/year. Show your work.
Answer Key

Rate = 540 km / 5.1 × 10⁶ yr = 1.06 × 10⁻⁴ km/yr = 10.6 cm/year. This matches independent GPS measurements of Pacific Plate velocity. Marks: 1 unit conversion, 2 arithmetic, 1 reasonable interpretation.

8
[4]
Lake Superior has volume 12,100 km³ and mean outflow 66 km³/yr. Lake Erie has volume 484 km³ and mean outflow 175 km³/yr. (a) Calculate residence time for each. (b) Which lake will more quickly flush an introduced pollutant? Why?
Answer Key

(a) Superior: τ = 12,100 / 66 ≈ 183 yr. Erie: τ = 484 / 175 ≈ 2.77 yr. (b) Lake Erie will flush much faster — its residence time is ~66× shorter. This is why Erie's 1960s eutrophication recovered relatively quickly once phosphorus was controlled, while pollutants in Lake Superior persist for nearly two centuries.

9
[4]
Niagara Falls has retreated about 11 km from its original post-glacial position over the last 12,500 years. (a) Calculate the long-term average retreat rate. (b) Why has retreat slowed in the 20th century? (c) Estimate how long until the falls reach Lake Erie, ~40 km upstream.
Answer Key

(a) Average rate = 11,000 m / 12,500 yr = 0.88 m/yr. (b) Hydroelectric diversion reduced flow over the falls, slowing erosion to roughly 0.3 m/yr in modern conditions. (c) At 0.3 m/yr: 40,000 m / 0.3 ≈ 133,000 years. Reasonable answers within a factor of 2 should receive full credit. Time to upstream Lake Erie illustrates that geological processes operate on a different scale from human history.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
10
[4]
Using a labeled diagram (description acceptable), explain why deserts cluster near 30° latitude and rainforests near the equator. Use terms: Hadley cell, ITCZ, adiabatic cooling, descending air.
Answer Key

At the equator, intense solar heating causes air to rise (ITCZ). Rising air cools adiabatically; water vapour condenses; heavy precipitation produces rainforests. The cooled, dried air moves poleward in the upper troposphere and descends near 30°. Descending air warms adiabatically, which increases its capacity to hold water vapour, so it is dry — producing subtropical deserts. Diagram should show two-cell flow with arrows and labels.

11
[4]
Sketch (or describe) a typical Canadian soil profile under boreal forest. Identify the horizons and one diagnostic feature of each.
Answer Key

O horizon: thick acidic litter of conifer needles. A horizon: thin, dark mineral horizon with organic matter. E horizon: pale leached layer (eluvial — distinctive in Podzols). B horizon: orange-brown subsoil with accumulated iron and aluminum oxides leached from above (illuvial). C horizon: weathered glacial parent material (till). R horizon: bedrock (often Canadian Shield granite/gneiss). The whole profile is a Podzol — acidic and infertile.

12
[4]
Define each of the following geographic concepts in one or two sentences: lithosphere, asthenosphere, residence time, watershed, urban heat island.
Answer Key

Lithosphere: rigid outer rock layer (crust + uppermost mantle), broken into plates. Asthenosphere: ductile zone of mantle below lithosphere that plates move over. Residence time: average time a substance spends in a reservoir before moving on (τ = V/Q). Watershed: area of land draining to a common outlet; basic unit of water management. Urban heat island: city centre warmer than surrounding rural area due to concrete heat storage and waste heat.

Part D: Application [11 marks]
13
[5]
Imagine you are advising a municipality in southern Ontario about flood-risk mapping for new development. Use Unit 1 concepts (hydrologic cycle, glacial topography, watershed boundaries, urban runoff) to identify three considerations and recommend at least one specific design strategy.
Answer Key

Considerations: (1) Glacial topography means low-lying drained-lake clays often have poor infiltration. (2) Watershed boundaries cross municipal lines — flooding upstream affects downstream. (3) Urban runoff increases stormwater volume 3–5× over natural surfaces. Strategy examples: bioswales, permeable pavement, retention ponds sized for ≥100-year storm, green roofs, restored riparian buffers, mandatory pre-development hydrology study. A strong answer integrates spatial reasoning with quantitative awareness.

14
[3]
A new community is planned at the foot of a mountain range that receives strong prevailing onshore winds. Describe what climate features (precipitation, temperature, vegetation) the community can expect on the windward side vs the leeward side. Use the term orographic precipitation.
Answer Key

Windward (upslope) side: air rises, cools adiabatically, water vapour condenses → orographic precipitation → high rainfall, lush vegetation (e.g., temperate rainforest on BC coast). Leeward (downslope) side: descending air warms adiabatically, increasing water-vapour capacity → rainshadow → dry, often desert or semi-arid (e.g., interior BC, eastern Rockies). Temperature: slightly warmer in lee at the same elevation because of compression heating.

15
[3]
Climate change is expected to thaw permafrost across northern Canada. Identify two physical and one human consequence of permafrost thaw in the Northwest Territories.
Answer Key

Physical: (1) Ground subsidence and slumping as ice-rich soil collapses; landscape rearrangement. (2) Release of stored organic carbon (CO₂ and methane) — a positive climate feedback. Human: damaged building foundations and pipelines (Inuvik, Tuktoyaktuk); disrupted winter ice roads; impacts on Indigenous traditional travel and harvesting. Strong answers connect physical processes to human geography.
