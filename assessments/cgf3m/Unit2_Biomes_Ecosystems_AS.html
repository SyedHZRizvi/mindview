Topic 2.1–2.2 — Biomes & Energy Flow
Question 1
Which two variables most strongly determine the distribution of terrestrial biomes?
Solution: Robert Whittaker's classic 1975 climate-vegetation diagram shows biomes occupying predictable envelopes of mean annual temperature and mean annual precipitation. Soils, elevation, and seasonality are important modifiers but temperature + precipitation are the master variables.
Question 2
The boreal forest (taiga) is dominated by which type of trees?
Solution: The boreal forest, covering most of Canada and Russia, is dominated by needle-leaved evergreen conifers — black spruce, white spruce, jack pine, balsam fir — plus the deciduous conifer tamarack. Deciduous broadleaves (aspen, birch, poplar) are pioneer species after fire.
Question 3
If a meadow ecosystem fixes 10,000 kJ/m²/yr at the producer level, how much energy is available at the fourth trophic level (apex predator)?
Solution: Apply the 10% rule three times: 10,000 × 0.1 × 0.1 × 0.1 = 10 kJ/m²/yr. This is why apex predators are rare and require vast home ranges.
Question 4
Net primary productivity (NPP) is:
Solution: NPP = GPP − plant respiration. NPP is the energy plants make available to herbivores (and indirectly to higher trophic levels). Tropical rainforests have the highest NPP among terrestrial biomes (~2200 g C/m²/yr); deserts and tundra are lowest.
Topic 2.3 — Nutrient Cycles
Question 5
What is the Haber-Bosch process?
Solution: Haber-Bosch (1909) industrially fixes atmospheric N₂ to ammonia under high temperature and pressure. It now consumes ~1% of global energy and is responsible for roughly half the nitrogen in modern food production. Approximately half the protein in an average human's body contains nitrogen that passed through a Haber-Bosch reactor.
Question 6
Atmospheric CO₂ has risen from approximately 280 ppm before 1750 to over 420 ppm today. What is the percentage increase?
Solution: (420 − 280) / 280 × 100% = 140/280 = 50%. This 50% increase is the largest CO₂ shift in at least 800,000 years (the limit of the Antarctic ice-core record).
Topic 2.4–2.5 — Biodiversity & Succession
Question 7
A keystone species is one that:
Solution: Robert Paine (1966) coined the term for species like the predatory starfish Pisaster ochraceus whose removal caused a 15-species intertidal community to collapse to 8 species. Atlantic cod, wolves, and sea otters are well-known keystone species.
Question 8
Calculate Simpson's diversity index D = 1 − Σ(pᵢ)² for a community of 4 species with abundances 25, 15, 7, 3 (total 50):
Solution: p = 0.5, 0.3, 0.14, 0.06. Σ(p²) = 0.25 + 0.09 + 0.0196 + 0.0036 = 0.3632. D = 1 − 0.3632 = 0.637. Higher values indicate more diversity (max ~1).
Question 9
Which is an example of primary succession?
Solution: Primary succession occurs on substrates that lack soil — bare rock, fresh lava, post-glacial terrain. It is slow (centuries) because soil must form first. Secondary succession (a, c, d) occurs on disturbed substrates where soil and seed banks survive.
Topic 2.6 — Canadian Ecozones
Question 10
Canada's largest terrestrial ecozone is the:
Solution: The Boreal Shield covers about 1.8 million km² (about 18% of Canada's land area), stretching from Labrador to northern Saskatchewan. The Mixedwood Plains is only about 175,000 km² but is home to half of Canada's population.
