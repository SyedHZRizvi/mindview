Mole & Molar Mass
Question 1
Molar mass of CO₂ (M(C)=12.01, M(O)=16.00):
Solution:
12.01 + 2(16.00) = 44.01 g/mol.
Question 2
How many moles in 88.0 g of CO₂?
Solution:
n = m/M = 88.0/44.01 = 2.00 mol.
Question 3
How many H atoms in 1.00 mol of H₂O?
Solution:
1 mol H₂O has 2 mol H, so \(2 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 1.204 \times 10^{24}\) H atoms.
Percent Composition & Formulas
Question 4
Percent of oxygen by mass in water:
Solution:
%O = 16.00/18.02 × 100 = 88.8%.
Question 5
A compound contains 40.0% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O. Empirical formula:
Solution:
In 100 g: C 3.33 mol, H 6.63 mol, O 3.33 mol → ratio 1:2:1 → empirical formula CH₂O.
Question 6
If a compound has empirical formula CH₂O and molar mass 180 g/mol, its molecular formula is:
Solution:
Empirical mass = 30 g/mol. 180/30 = 6 → multiply subscripts by 6 → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose).
Stoichiometry
Question 7
For \(2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}\), how many mol of H₂O form from 5.0 mol H₂ (excess O₂)?
Solution:
Mole ratio H₂:H₂O = 2:2 = 1:1 → 5.0 mol H₂ → 5.0 mol H₂O.
Question 8
Mass of CO₂ produced when 2.0 mol of C is burned in excess O₂: \( \text{C} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 \).
Solution:
1:1 mole ratio. n(CO₂) = 2.0 mol. m = 2.0 × 44.01 = 88 g (88.02 g, rounded).
Question 9
In \(2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}\): 4 mol H₂ reacts with 1 mol O₂. The limiting reactant is:
Solution:
Stoichiometric ratio requires 4 mol H₂ : 2 mol O₂. But only 1 mol O₂ given → O₂ runs out first. O₂ is limiting.
Question 10
Theoretical yield is 25.0 g; actual yield is 20.0 g. Percent yield = ?
Solution:
20.0/25.0 × 100 = 80.0%.
Application
Question 11
A student reports a percent yield of 105%. What is the most likely explanation?
Solution:
A yield over 100% almost always means the product still contained moisture or other impurities, inflating its mass.
Question 12
How many atoms total are in 2.0 mol of NH₃?
Solution:
2.0 mol × 4 atoms per molecule × \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\) = 4.82 × 10²⁴ atoms.
