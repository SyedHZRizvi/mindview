Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
Question 1 [2 marks]
Which scientist contributed the concept of quantized electron energy levels?
Solution:
Bohr (1913) proposed quantized electron orbits to explain hydrogen's line spectrum.
Question 2 [3 marks]
Arrange in order of increasing electronegativity: Cs, C, F, Na.
Question 3 [3 marks]
For each pair, identify the bond type (ionic, polar covalent, or non-polar covalent): (a) K–Br; (b) C–O; (c) H–H.
Question 4 [3 marks]
Draw the Lewis structure of CCl₄ and state the VSEPR shape.
Question 5 [2 marks]
Which intermolecular force is responsible for water's unusually high boiling point?
Solution:
O is electronegative and has lone pairs → strong H-bonds between water molecules.
Question 6 [2 marks]
Write the chemical formula for the ionic compound formed between calcium and nitrogen.
Solution:
Ca²⁺ and N³⁻ → crossover gives Ca₃N₂ (calcium nitride).
Part B: Thinking & Inquiry [15 marks]
Question 7 [4 marks]
Predict and explain the order of first ionization energies for Mg, Al, and Si. (Hint: the trend across the period is not perfectly smooth.)
Question 8 [4 marks]
For SO₂: (a) draw the Lewis structure; (b) determine the molecular shape using VSEPR; (c) state whether the molecule is polar. Justify each answer.
Question 9 [3 marks]
Pentane (C₅H₁₂, b.p. 36 °C) and 2,2-dimethylpropane (also C₅H₁₂, b.p. 10 °C) have the same molecular formula but different boiling points. Explain in terms of molecular shape and IMFs.
Question 10 [4 marks]
Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) and dimethyl ether (CH₃OCH₃) share formula C₂H₆O. Predict which has the higher boiling point and explain in terms of IMFs.
Part C: Communication [15 marks]
Question 11 [4 marks]
Define the following terms in your own words: (a) electronegativity; (b) ionization energy; (c) atomic radius; (d) polyatomic ion.
Question 12 [4 marks]
Describe the difference between intramolecular bonds and intermolecular forces. Give one example of each, and explain which one is broken when ice melts to liquid water.
Question 13 [3 marks]
Explain in your own words why noble gases have very high first ionization energies but very low (essentially zero) electron affinities.
Question 14 [4 marks]
Describe the experimental evidence that supported the rejection of Thomson's plum-pudding model and adoption of Rutherford's nuclear model.
Part D: Application [15 marks]
Question 15 [4 marks]
DNA double helices are held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs. Identify the chemical groups on adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine that participate in H-bonding, and explain why this interaction is critical for biological function. (You may consult biology references.)
Question 16 [4 marks]
Why does sodium chloride dissolve readily in water but not in hexane? Explain in terms of bonding and "like dissolves like".
Question 17 [4 marks]
A consumer product label claims "all-natural, water-soluble vitamin C". Vitamin C (ascorbic acid, C₆H₈O₆) is in fact water-soluble. (a) Identify the IMFs between vitamin C and water. (b) Predict whether it would also dissolve in hexane and explain why.
Question 18 [3 marks]
A student tests four unknown white solids and records the results. Solid W melts at 801 °C and conducts electricity when dissolved in water. Solid X melts at 0 °C and is a non-conductor. Identify the most likely bond type in each. Justify.

Complete Answer Key

Q1. (d) Bohr.

Q2. Cs < Na < C < F. (EN rises across, falls down.)

Q3. (a) K–Br: ΔEN ≈ 2.1 → ionic. (b) C–O: ΔEN ≈ 0.9 → polar covalent. (c) H–H: ΔEN = 0 → non-polar covalent.

Q4. C central, 4 C–Cl single bonds, each Cl has 3 lone pairs. AX₄ → tetrahedral, 109.5°.

Q5. (c) Hydrogen bonding.

Q6. Ca₃N₂.

Q7. Expected smooth: Mg < Al < Si. Actual: Al < Mg < Si — because removing an electron from Al's 3p subshell (above filled 3s²) is easier than removing one from Mg's filled 3s² subshell. The dip arises from the energy difference between subshells.

Q8. SO₂: S central with 1 lone pair and 2 S=O double bonds (resonance). AX₂E → bent (~119°). Bond dipoles do not cancel → polar molecule.

Q9. 2,2-dimethylpropane is more spherical and has less surface area than the elongated n-pentane → smaller LDFs → lower b.p.

Q10. Ethanol (b.p. 78 °C) > dimethyl ether (b.p. −24 °C). Ethanol's O–H forms H-bonds with itself; ether has only weaker dipole-dipole because no O–H.

Q11. (a) Tendency of an atom to attract bonded electrons. (b) Energy to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom. (c) Distance from nucleus to outermost electron. (d) Covalently bonded group of atoms carrying a net charge (e.g., NO₃⁻).

Q12. Intramolecular bonds = within a molecule (covalent/ionic); intermolecular forces = between molecules (LDF, dipole-dipole, H-bond). Example: covalent O–H bonds vs. H-bonds between water molecules. When ice melts, H-bonds between water molecules weaken/rearrange — covalent bonds are not broken.

Q13. Noble gases already have a complete octet (filled valence shell). Removing an electron is very difficult (high IE); they have no driving force to gain another (essentially zero electron affinity).

Q14. Rutherford's gold-foil experiment: most α-particles passed straight through, but a small fraction were deflected at large angles. This was inconsistent with Thomson's "diffuse positive sphere with embedded electrons"; it required a small, dense, positive nucleus that occasionally repelled α-particles strongly.

Q15. A–T form 2 H-bonds; G–C form 3 H-bonds. The N–H groups serve as donors; carbonyl O and ring N as acceptors. These H-bonds are strong enough to hold the helix together but weak enough to be unzipped by polymerases during replication and transcription.

Q16. NaCl is ionic (highly polar Na⁺ + Cl⁻). Water is polar — ion–dipole interactions hydrate the ions, breaking the lattice. Hexane is non-polar — cannot stabilize ions → does not dissolve.

Q17. (a) H-bonding via –OH groups + dipole-dipole. (b) Hexane is non-polar and cannot H-bond → vitamin C would not dissolve well.

Q18. W: ionic (high m.p., conducts in solution). X: molecular non-polar (very low m.p., non-conductor).
