Topic 1.1 — Cash Controls & Bank Reconciliation
Question 1
A bank statement shows a balance of \$ 5,200. There is an outstanding cheque of \$ 400 and a deposit in transit of \$ 600. What is the adjusted bank balance?
Solution:
Adjusted bank balance \( = 5{,}200 - 400 + 600 = \$ 5{,}400 \).
Question 2
An NSF cheque returned by the bank should be:
Solution:
The bank knows about the NSF (it has already reversed the deposit). The book balance does not — so the company must subtract the NSF amount from the book balance and journalize it.
Topic 1.2 — Accounts Receivable & Bad Debts
Question 3
Net credit sales \$ 500,000. Bad-debt rate (percentage-of-sales method) is 1.5%. What is the bad-debt expense for the year?
Solution:
Bad-debt expense \( = 500{,}000 \times 0.015 = \$ 7{,}500 \). The existing allowance balance is ignored under the percentage-of-sales method.
Question 4
Aging schedule gives a target Allowance for Doubtful Accounts of \$ 9,000. The unadjusted balance is a credit of \$ 2,400. The year-end adjusting entry is:
Solution:
Under aging, the entry brings the Allowance balance to the target. Required \( = 9{,}000 - 2{,}400 = \$ 6{,}600 \). Debit Bad Debt Expense and credit Allowance for \$ 6,600.
Topic 1.3 — Short-Term Investments
Question 5
A trading security carried at \$ 12,000 has a year-end fair value of \$ 13,500. The journal entry is:
Solution:
FVTPL securities are revalued each period. Debit Investment for the \$ 1,500 increase; credit Unrealized Gain (an income-statement account).
Topic 1.4 — Inventory Cost Flow
Question 6
Under FIFO during rising prices, ending inventory is valued at:
Solution:
FIFO sends the oldest costs to COGS, leaving the most recent (and during rising prices, highest) costs in ending inventory.
Question 7
Beginning inventory 20 units @ \$ 10. Purchase 30 units @ \$ 12. Sale 25 units. Using weighted-average (periodic), what is the cost per unit?
Solution:
Total cost \( = 20(10) + 30(12) = 200 + 360 = \$ 560 \). Total units = 50. Cost/unit \( = 560/50 = \$ 11.20 \).
Question 8
Inventory cost is \$ 8,000; net realizable value is \$ 7,200. Under LCNRV, what value appears on the balance sheet?
Solution:
Lower of cost (\$ 8,000) and NRV (\$ 7,200) = \$ 7,200. A \$ 800 write-down is recorded.
Topic 1.5 — Capital Assets & Amortization
Question 9
Equipment cost \$ 50,000, residual \$ 5,000, useful life 5 years. Annual straight-line amortization is:
Solution:
\( (50{,}000 - 5{,}000)/5 = 45{,}000/5 = \$ 9{,}000 \) per year.
Question 10
Equipment cost \$ 60,000, 10-year life. Year-1 double-declining-balance amortization is:
Solution:
Rate \( = 2/10 = 20\% \). Year-1 expense \( = 60{,}000 \times 0.20 = \$ 12{,}000 \). DDB is applied to the full cost, ignoring residual until the final year.
Topic 1.6 — Disposal of Capital Assets
Question 11
A machine cost \$ 30,000 with accumulated amortization of \$ 25,000 is sold for \$ 8,000. The gain or loss on disposal is:
Solution:
Carrying value \( = 30{,}000 - 25{,}000 = \$ 5{,}000 \). Proceeds \$ 8,000. Gain \( = 8{,}000 - 5{,}000 = \$ 3{,}000 \).
Question 12
A truck (cost \$ 40,000, accumulated amortization \$ 35,000) is scrapped for zero proceeds. The journal entry includes a loss of:
Solution:
Carrying value \( = 40{,}000 - 35{,}000 = \$ 5{,}000 \). With zero proceeds, the entire carrying value is a loss.
