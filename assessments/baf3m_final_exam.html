Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
Question 1 [1 mark]
The accounting equation is:
Solution:
\( A = L + OE \) is the fundamental identity.
Question 2 [1 mark]
Which is a debit-normal account?
Solution:
Drawings: debit normal. Capital, A/P, Revenue: credit normal.
Question 3 [1 mark]
The principle that requires expenses to be recorded in the same period as the related revenue is:
Solution:
Matching principle.
Question 4 [1 mark]
Accumulated Depreciation is a:
Solution:
Contra-asset: credit balance, deducted from related asset to produce net book value.
Question 5 [1 mark]
"Posting" refers to:
Solution:
Posting moves data from journal to ledger.
Question 6 [2 marks]
If Assets = \$42 000 and Owner's Equity = \$29 500, then Liabilities =
Solution:
\(L = A - OE = 42\,000 - 29\,500 = 12\,500\).
Question 7 [2 marks]
Equipment cost \$18 000, residual \$3 000, useful life 5 years (straight-line). Annual depreciation =
Solution:
\( \dfrac{18\,000 - 3\,000}{5} = 3\,000 \).
Question 8 [2 marks]
A trial balance is out of balance by \$135. Which test most likely applies?
Solution:
\(135 \div 9 = 15\) → divisible by 9. Likely a transposition.
Question 9 [2 marks]
Net Sales \$50 000, COGS \$32 000. Gross Profit =
Solution:
\(GP = 50\,000 - 32\,000 = 18\,000\).
Question 10 [2 marks]
Current Assets \$36 000, Current Liabilities \$12 000. Current Ratio (to 2 decimals) =
Solution:
\(36\,000/12\,000 = 3.00\).
Question 11 [2 marks]
Petty-cash fund \$200. Cash on hand \$30, vouchers \$172. Cash over/short (positive = overage, negative = shortage):
Solution:
Cash + vouchers = \(30 + 172 = 202\); imprest = 200; overage \$2.
Question 12 [2 marks]
Which is a typical external user of financial statements?
Solution:
CRA is outside the business; all others are internal.

Question 14 [2 marks]
A 12-month insurance policy costing \$2 400 is paid in advance on 1 March. By 31 May, how much remains in Prepaid Insurance?
Solution:
Per month = \(2\,400/12 = 200\). Three months expired = \$600. Remaining = \(2\,400 - 600 = 1\,800\).
Question 15 [2 marks]
Beginning Capital \$24 000, Net Income \$5 200, Additional Investment \$3 000, Drawings \$1 800. Ending Capital =
Solution:
\(24\,000 + 5\,200 + 3\,000 - 1\,800 = 30\,400\).

Q18

(i) COGS = \(15\,000 + 130\,000 - 22\,000 = 123\,000\); (ii) GP = \(200\,000 - 123\,000 = 77\,000\); (iii) NI = \(77\,000 - 40\,000 = 37\,000\); (iv) GP margin = \(77\,000 / 200\,000 = 38.5\%\) — within healthy range for the industry.

Q19

Stakeholders: shareholders, lender, employees, supervisor, accountant herself. Code values: integrity, objectivity, due care. Steps (1) recognise the issue; (2) gather facts; (3) generate alternatives (refuse + escalate, agree, resign); (4) evaluate against Code, law, and consequences; (5) act — refuse, escalate to senior management or audit committee, document in writing.

Q20

Firm X: tight liquidity (1.10) but modest leverage and the best gross-profit margin; works hard on top-line but may struggle if a payable spikes. Firm Y: very high liquidity (cash hoard?), almost no debt, but thin Net-Income margin — possibly under-investing in growth. External concern for X: short-term cash crunch risk. For Y: idle capital, weak returns.

Q21

Matching = pair expenses with the period's revenues. Deferral example: depreciation allocates the cost of equipment (paid years earlier) to current period. Accrual example: salaries earned by employees in the last week of December but paid in January are recognised in December via Dr. Salaries Expense; Cr. Salaries Payable.

Q22

Income statement produces Net Income, which flows into the Statement of Owner's Equity. The Statement of Owner's Equity produces the ending Capital balance, which appears on the Balance Sheet under Owner's Equity. The Balance Sheet must satisfy Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity using this ending Capital figure.

Q23

Audit trail: ability to trace any number on a financial statement back to its underlying source and forward from any source document to its statement impact. Source documents = evidence; journal P.R. = "this line was posted to account #X"; ledger P.R. = "this number came from journal page #J"; subsidiary reconciliation = control-account total equals sum of individual customers/suppliers; bank reconciliation = independent verification of the cash balance.

Q24

Refusal — recording unshipped revenue violates revenue-recognition (revenue must be earned by delivery). CPA Code values violated: integrity, objectivity, due care. Consequences: misleading lenders/investors, possible fraud charges, professional discipline (suspension/expulsion), erosion of trust. Suggested action: refuse, explain why, escalate to senior management or the audit committee, document the conversation.

Q25

Cloud accounting eliminates arithmetic errors, accelerates statement preparation, and provides real-time financial visibility. It introduces new risks: unauthorised access, data breaches, vendor outage, loss of paper audit trail. New controls required: role-based user permissions, strong passwords/2-factor authentication, encryption, backup discipline, regular review of system audit logs by an independent reviewer.
