Q22 Strand C / STSE [5]

Q24 Strand E / STSE [5]

Q25 Strand F / STSE [5]
Carbon footprint. A typical Canadian household burns 2500 m³ of natural gas (CH₄, ~95%) per year for heating, at STP. (a) Calculate the moles of CH₄ used. (b) Mass of CO₂ produced (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O), in kg. (c) Suggest two concrete actions a homeowner could take to reduce this footprint.
Complete Worked Answer Key

Q1. (a) Cs–Cl: ΔEN ≈ 2.4 → ionic; (b) N–O: ΔEN ≈ 0.4–0.5 → polar covalent; (c) Cl–Cl: ΔEN = 0 → non-polar covalent.

Q2. Lewis: O central with 2 lone pairs + 2 O–H bonds. AX₂E₂ → bent (~104.5°). Polar — bond dipoles do not cancel.

Q3. (a) 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂; (b) 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O.

Q4. Net ionic: Pb²⁺(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → PbI₂(s).

Q5. 2(14.01) + 8(1.01) + 32.07 + 4(16.00) = 28.02 + 8.08 + 32.07 + 64.00 = 132.17 g/mol.

Q6. n(C) = 3.33, n(H) = 6.63, n(O) = 3.33 → ratio 1:2:1 → CH₂O.

Q7. n = 4.00/40.00 = 0.100 mol; c = 0.100/0.250 = 0.400 mol/L.

Q8. (a) 25 + 273 = 298 K; (b) 200/101.325 = 1.97 atm.

Q9. (a) 22.4 L/mol; (b) P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (Boyle's law).

Q10. Na < Mg < Cl < F. Going right increases Z_eff with no new shell → tighter electrons → higher IE; going down adds shells/shielding → easier removal. F (smallest of these) has highest IE.

Q11. (a) Mg above Cu — reacts: Mg + Cu(NO₃)₂ → Mg(NO₃)₂ + Cu. (b) Ag below Cu — no reaction. (c) Zn above H — reacts: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂.

Q12. n(Mg) = 10.0/24.31 = 0.4114; n(HCl) = 0.0250 × 1.50 = 0.0375. Mg needs 2× HCl = 0.823 mol — only 0.0375 available → HCl limiting. n(MgCl₂) = 0.0375/2 = 0.01875 mol → m(MgCl₂) theoretical = 0.01875 × 95.21 = 1.79 g. Percent yield = 1.20/1.79 × 100 = 67.2%.

Q13. (a) n(NaOH) = 0.1500 × 0.02240 = 3.36×10⁻³ mol = n(HCl); c(HCl) = 3.36×10⁻³/0.02500 = 0.1344 mol/L. (b) pH = −log(0.1344) = 0.872.

Q14. n(CaCO₃) = 25.0/100.09 = 0.2498 mol → n(CO₂) = 0.2498 mol → V at STP = 0.2498 × 22.4 = 5.60 L.

Q15. Combined: V₂ = V₁(P₁/P₂)(T₂/T₁) = 2.50 × (100/50) × (400/300) = 2.50 × 2.000 × 1.333 = 6.67 L.

Q16. Bond polarity (ΔEN) determines whether individual bonds are polar; geometry determines whether bond dipoles cancel. CO₂ linear → dipoles cancel → non-polar; CCl₄ tetrahedral → dipoles cancel → non-polar; H₂O bent → dipoles add → polar.

Q17. (a) Synthesis A+B→AB (e.g., 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O); decomposition AB→A+B (e.g., 2KClO₃→2KCl+3O₂). (b) Single disp.: A+BC→AC+B (Zn+CuSO₄→ZnSO₄+Cu); Double disp.: AB+CD→AD+CB (AgNO₃+NaCl→AgCl+NaNO₃). (c) Complete: CO₂+H₂O only (excess O₂); incomplete: CO and/or C+H₂O (limited O₂).

Q18. Atoms of different elements have different molar masses → 1 g of A has different moles than 1 g of B. The coefficients in a balanced equation refer to ratios of moles (or molecules). Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. By mass: 4 g H₂ + 32 g O₂ → 36 g H₂O. Mass ratio is not 2:1. By moles: 2 mol H₂ + 1 mol O₂ → 2 mol H₂O. The 2:1:2 mole ratio matches coefficients.

Q19. Mass = 0.250 × 0.100 × 40.00 = 1.00 g. Wear goggles, gloves, lab coat. Weigh NaOH (it absorbs CO₂ and moisture — work quickly). Transfer to 250-mL volumetric flask containing ~150 mL distilled water. Dissolve completely (exothermic). Cool to room T. Dilute to mark; invert to mix. NaOH is highly caustic — never let pellets contact skin; dispose properly.

Q20. (a) Greenhouse effect: CO₂ (and CH₄, N₂O, H₂O) absorb IR → traps heat → warming. Policies: carbon pricing, renewable energy, energy efficiency. (b) Ozone depletion: CFCs release stratospheric Cl → catalytically destroys O₃. Policy: Montreal Protocol (1987). (c) Acid rain: SO₂ + NO_x → H₂SO₄ + HNO₃. Policies: scrubbers, low-S fuels, catalytic converters.

Q21. O is electronegative enough to form strong H-bonds; S is not. H₂O molecules form extensive H-bond network requiring much energy to disrupt → high b.p. Water's H-bonding gives it high heat capacity (moderates climate), high heat of vaporization (cools through sweat), strong cohesion (allows capillary action in plants), and the ability to dissolve ionic and polar biomolecules.

Q22. CaCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g). Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + 2H₂O(l) (no gas). CaCO₃ releases CO₂ → causes burping; Mg(OH)₂ avoids this but has laxative side effect ("milk of magnesia").

Q23. n(N₂) = 1.00×10⁶/28.02 = 3.569×10⁴ mol → n(NH₃) theoretical = 2 × 3.569×10⁴ = 7.137×10⁴ mol → m theoretical = 7.137×10⁴ × 17.03 = 1.215×10⁶ g. With 35% yield: m actual = 0.35 × 1.215×10⁶ = 4.25×10⁵ g ≈ 425 kg.

Q24. (a) 0.020 ppm = 0.020 mg/L. mol/L = 0.020×10⁻³ g/L ÷ 207.2 g/mol = 9.65×10⁻⁸ mol/L. (b) 0.020/0.005 = 4× the MAC. (c) Replace lead-containing service line; install certified Pb-removal filter (e.g., reverse osmosis or carbon block certified to NSF/ANSI 53 for Pb); flush taps before drinking.

Q25. (a) n(CH₄) = 2500×10³ L ÷ 22.4 L/mol = 1.116×10⁵ mol. (b) n(CO₂) = n(CH₄) = 1.116×10⁵ mol → m(CO₂) = 1.116×10⁵ × 44.01 = 4.91×10⁶ g = 4910 kg ≈ 4.91 tonnes. (c) Improve insulation, install a heat pump, lower thermostat 1–2 °C, switch to electricity from low-carbon grid, replace older furnace with high-efficiency model.
