Topic 5.1–5.2 — Tissues, Organs & Anatomy
Question 1
Which plant tissue conducts water and dissolved minerals from roots to shoots?
Solution: Xylem conducts water and dissolved minerals upward. Cells are dead at maturity (tracheids, vessel elements). Phloem moves sugars (sucrose) bidirectionally.
Question 2
Vascular bundles arranged in a ring near the perimeter of a stem indicate which plant group?
Solution: Eudicot stems show a ring of vascular bundles. Monocots have scattered bundles. Other clues: eudicots — netted veins, flower parts in 4s/5s, two cotyledons.
Question 3
Root hairs primarily function to:
Solution: Root hairs are single-celled extensions of the root epidermis that vastly increase absorptive surface area for water and dissolved ions. Anchoring is mainly the job of larger roots.
Question 4
An annual ring in a tree trunk represents:
Solution: Each annual ring = one year of secondary xylem (wood) produced by the vascular cambium. Spring vessels are wide; summer vessels are narrow — the contrast lets you count years.
Topic 5.3 — Transport (Water & Sugars)
Question 5
The cohesion-tension theory explains water transport in:
Solution: Water moves up the xylem as a continuous column under tension. Transpiration pulls water out at the leaves; cohesion (water-water H-bonds) holds the column together; adhesion (water-wall) keeps it attached to the conduit walls.
Question 6
A plant moves sugars from a leaf (source) to a fruit (sink) through:
Solution: Phloem (sieve-tube elements + companion cells) moves sucrose by the pressure-flow model — active loading at the source raises pressure; unloading at the sink lowers it; sap flows down the gradient.
Question 7
A plant on a windy, dry day will likely transpire:
Solution: Low humidity creates a steeper water-vapour gradient; wind removes the boundary layer of saturated air next to the leaf. Both increase transpiration rate. If transpiration exceeds water uptake, the plant wilts.
Topic 5.4–5.6 — Reproduction, Hormones & Sustainability
Question 8
In an angiosperm flower, the female reproductive part containing the ovules is called the:
Solution: The carpel (often called the pistil when carpels fuse) consists of stigma, style, and ovary. Ovules — and thus the female gametophyte with the egg — are housed inside the ovary.
Question 9
In double fertilisation, one sperm forms the embryo. The other sperm fuses with two polar nuclei to form:
Solution: The triploid (3n) endosperm nourishes the developing embryo. Double fertilisation is unique to angiosperms; coconut "meat", corn kernels, and rice grains are largely endosperm.
Question 10
A seedling growing on a windowsill bends toward the light. The hormone primarily responsible is:
Solution: Auxin redistributes to the shaded side of the stem and elongates those cells, causing the shoot to bend toward light (phototropism).
Question 11
A ripe banana ripens nearby green tomatoes because it emits:
Solution: Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone that triggers fruit ripening. Storing fruit in a closed paper bag concentrates ethylene and accelerates ripening of green fruit.
Question 12
A monoculture system (single crop over a large area) is most vulnerable to:
Solution: Genetic uniformity in a monoculture means a single new pest or pathogen can spread rapidly through the entire field (Irish potato famine, banana fungal disease). Crop rotation, polyculture, and resistant varieties reduce risk.
