Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
Question 1 [2 marks]
Approximately what fraction of Canadian jobs is directly linked to international trade?
Solution:
Roughly one in five Canadian jobs is directly tied to trade — through manufacturing for export, transport, customs, retail of imports, and tourism.
Question 2 [2 marks]
Which entry mode involves shared equity ownership between the foreign firm and a local partner?
Solution:
A joint venture is a separate company co-owned by two or more partners (often one foreign, one local), sharing equity, profits, and risk.
Question 3 [2 marks]
A country has total GDP of \(\text{US}\$500\) billion and a population of 50 million. Its GDP per capita (in US\$) is:
Solution:
\( \dfrac{500{,}000{,}000{,}000}{50{,}000{,}000} = \text{US}\$10{,}000 \).
Question 4 [2 marks]
The legal system that builds rules from case precedent (rather than from a codified statute book) is called:
Solution:
Common law (UK, Canada except Quebec, US, Australia) uses judicial precedent. Civil law (France, Germany, Japan, Quebec) uses written codes.
Question 5 [2 marks]
Which trade agreement removes tariffs on roughly 98% of trade between Canada and the European Union?
Solution:
CETA (Canada-EU Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement) provisionally took effect in 2017 and eliminates duties on the large majority of bilateral trade.
Question 6 [3 marks]
In one hour, Country A can make 10 units of food OR 5 units of clothing. Country B can make 6 units of food OR 6 units of clothing. Country A has the comparative advantage in:
Solution:
Opportunity cost of one food unit: A gives up \(5/10 = 0.5\) clothing; B gives up \(6/6 = 1.0\) clothing. A has lower opportunity cost in food → comparative advantage in food. Conversely, B has comparative advantage in clothing.
