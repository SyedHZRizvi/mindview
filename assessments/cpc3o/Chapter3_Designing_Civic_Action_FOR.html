Chapter 3: Designing and Evaluating Civic Action — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strands A & C
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps in your prior project-planning, communication, and reflection skills before designing your own civic-action initiative in Chapter 3. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Skills · Goal Setting
[4 marks]
A friend says: "I want to make a difference at my school." Rewrite this as a SMART goal (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) for one school semester. Underline or label which part of your goal addresses each SMART element.
Sample Answer & Marking Rubric

Sample: "By 31 March 2027 (Time-bound), deliver a written proposal to my school's principal (Specific) for a student-led peer-support mental-health program (Specific), supported by a survey of at least 50 student responses showing demand (Measurable) and three letters of support from school staff (Measurable), in time for input into the School Effectiveness Plan (Relevant — connected to the real decision-making document). Achievable: the survey is feasible in one month, staff letters in two weeks, principal engagement in three meetings." Rubric: 1 each for Specific, Measurable, Achievable+Relevant, Time-bound.

Question 2 Skills · Audience
[3 marks]
Imagine you are running a campaign to encourage your city to add protected bike lanes. List three different audiences you would need to communicate with, and briefly note how the message to each would differ in tone or form.
Sample Answer & Marking Rubric

Sample: (1) City Council — formal deputation; technical, evidence-based, references staff reports; respectful tone; specific ask. (2) Local journalists — op-ed (700–900 words) or letter to editor; vivid opening, clear ask, anchored in lived experience and data. (3) Other students / general public — Instagram or TikTok post; visual, short, emotionally engaging, with a call to action (sign petition, attend rally). Each audience has different conventions — a single message sent everywhere usually misses every audience. Rubric: 1 each — audience + appropriate form/tone.

Question 3 Knowledge · Ethics
[3 marks]
A campaign on housing for unhoused people decides to interview unhoused residents and post their video stories online. Name three ethical questions the campaign should ask before posting. (You do not need to provide answers; just questions.)
Sample Answer & Marking Rubric

Acceptable: (1) Have participants given informed consent — in a form that is genuinely free (not coerced by access to services, for example)? (2) Are they at risk of retaliation (from police, landlords, employers) if their identity is publicly visible? Should faces and details be anonymised? (3) Do participants have any control over how their stories are framed, or have we taken their words and repackaged them in our message? (4) Will participants benefit from the campaign — materially, by inclusion in design, or by accountability for what the campaign promised them? (5) What is our follow-up plan after release — abandonment is a real harm. Rubric: 1 each — any three substantive ethical questions.

Question 4 Skills · Measurement
[3 marks]
You ran a one-semester campaign for stronger mental-health programming at your school. You collected 312 student signatures, made 12 deputations to your school council, and the council passed a motion directing staff to study the proposal. Identify which of these are "outputs" and which are "outcomes," and briefly explain.
Sample Answer & Marking Rubric

Sample: Outputs — the 312 signatures and the 12 deputations are activity counts (things you produced). Outcomes — the council motion is a change in the world that resulted from your activity (a decision was made that would not have been made without the campaign). The deeper impact — actual mental-health support reaching students — has not yet been demonstrated; that will require sustained follow-through into the budget cycle and the program implementation. Honest measurement names which is which and does not call outputs "impact." Rubric: 1 outputs correctly identified; 1 outcomes correctly identified; 1 honest distinction between them.

Question 5 Knowledge · Case Studies
[3 marks]
Have you heard of any of these Canadian civic-action campaigns: Idle No More, the Right to Read Inquiry, the youth climate strikes? In 2–3 sentences, write what you already know about ONE of them — even if it is not much. (No penalty for limited knowledge — this question maps what you bring.)
Sample Answer & Marking Rubric

Sample: Acceptable knowledge for each: Idle No More — started in 2012 in Saskatoon by four Indigenous women; responded to federal omnibus Bill C-45; used round dances, hunger strike, social media; broadened to challenge Indigenous-Crown relations. Right to Read Inquiry — Ontario Human Rights Commission inquiry 2020–2022; found discrimination in reading instruction for students with disabilities; led to 2023 curriculum revision mandating structured literacy. Youth climate strikes — started 2018 with Greta Thunberg; September 2019 Montreal march drew 500,000+; pushed for climate-emergency declarations. Rubric: 1 for movement named; 1 for basic factual accuracy; 1 for what the student felt was significant about it.

Question 6 Reflection · Personal
[3 marks]
Looking ahead to the civic-action initiative you will design in Chapter 3, name one issue you might focus on and one concern you already have about the design or implementation (e.g., "I worry I won't know which decision-maker to target"; "I'm not sure how to recruit participants"; "I'm worried about offending people").
Sample Answer & Marking Rubric

Sample: Issue — "I want to advocate for mental-health programming at my school." Concern — "I'm not sure how to engage students who themselves have mental-health challenges without exposing them publicly, or how to talk to the principal without seeming to attack the school." Strong reflections name a specific issue and a specific concern that the chapter can directly address (Chapter 3 covers audience-and-message design, ethics, and inclusion). Rubric: 1 issue named; 1 concern named substantively; 1 for the connection to Chapter 3 content.

Question 7 Reflection · Sustaining
[ungraded]
After CPC3O ends, do you imagine yourself continuing civic engagement? In 2 or 3 sentences, name one form of engagement you can realistically see yourself maintaining (voting, joining a student association, following an issue on social media, volunteering, running for student government, etc.) and one obstacle that might get in the way.
