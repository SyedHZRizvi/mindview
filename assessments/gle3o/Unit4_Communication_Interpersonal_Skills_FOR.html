Unit 4: Communication & Interpersonal Skills — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Pre-unit check · Strand C — C1, C2, C3, C4
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Surface your current communication patterns, group-work experiences, and digital-etiquette habits. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Honest answers help the unit target the right gaps.
Question 1 Communication Style
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe your default communication style when you disagree with someone (a friend, a parent, a teacher, a group-mate). Do you tend to (a) avoid the conversation, (b) push your view strongly, (c) give in to keep the peace, (d) say one thing but feel another? Give one example.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This diagnostic surfaces students' default styles. Most Grade 11 students lean passive (especially female-coded students in some cultural contexts) or aggressive-when-pushed. The unit will introduce assertive communication as a learnable alternative. Honest descriptions are the goal; do not grade for "ideal" style. Marks: full marks for honest self-description with example.

Question 2 I-Statement Practice
[3 marks]
Scenario: Two of your four group-project teammates have missed two meetings in a row and haven't responded to the shared document.
Write the message you would actually send (or have sent in a similar situation). Don't pre-edit for the "right" answer — write what your default would be.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnostic for current communication patterns. Students typically write one of three patterns: aggressive ("You guys are ruining this..."), passive-aggressive ("I guess we're all just doing our own thing now :)"), or passive ("No worries, hope you're okay!"). Few write assertive I-statements at this point. Use the responses to motivate the I-statement structure in 4.1. Marks: full marks for an authentic message.

Question 3 Active Listening
[3 marks]
When you're having a disagreement, do you tend to (a) listen fully before responding, (b) formulate your reply while the other person is still speaking, (c) interrupt, (d) shut down and stop processing? Be honest. In 2–3 sentences, give an example.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most students at this point report (b) — formulating reply while listening — which the active-listening lesson explicitly targets. The honest description is more valuable than the "right" answer. Marks: full for honest self-description.

Question 4 Feedback Receipt
[3 marks]
Think of a time you received critical feedback (from a teacher, coach, parent, employer, friend). What was your immediate reaction? Did you defend, paraphrase, ask for examples, thank the giver? In 3–4 sentences.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This question surfaces feedback-receiving habits. Most students default to the "yes-but" reflex. The unit will introduce pause-paraphrase-ask-thank as a learnable alternative. Marks: full for honest description with example.

Question 5 Group Work
[3 marks]
Describe a recent group project (school or otherwise). Did your group have clear roles? A shared document? Regular check-ins? What worked? What didn't? In 4 sentences.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This question surfaces common group-work failure modes — usually all three structural elements (roles, shared surface, check-ins) are missing. Use the responses to motivate the three-element model in 4.4. Marks: full for honest description.

Question 6 Conflict Style
[2 marks]
From the Thomas-Kilmann five styles (avoiding, accommodating, competing, compromising, collaborating), which best describes your default in a conflict? In 2 sentences, give one example.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most students at this stage cluster around avoiding or accommodating. The unit will introduce style flexibility — choosing the style that fits the situation rather than defaulting. Use the responses to motivate this. Marks: full for honest description with example.

Question 7 Digital Etiquette
[2 marks]
Do you write the same way in a text to a friend, a message to a teammate, and an email to a teacher? In 2–3 sentences, describe any conscious differences you make in tone, length, formality, and channel.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnostic for register-awareness. Strong answers will identify clear differences (greeting/sign-off in emails, more careful punctuation, less slang). Weaker answers report no conscious difference. Use this to motivate the channel-and-register section of 4.6. Marks: full for honest description.

📋 Diagnostic Rubric — Overall

Purpose: surface students' current communication defaults so the unit can be targeted. Many students hold beliefs like "I'm just a quiet person" or "I'm just hot-tempered" that the unit will reframe as learnable habits. The diagnostic lets you identify which students need which support.

Differentiation: students who consistently report passive-aggressive defaults benefit from explicit I-statement practice; students who report feedback-defensiveness benefit from the receive-pause-paraphrase protocol; students whose group experiences are catastrophic benefit from heavy 4.4 structural work.

Feedback to give: validate honest self-description; identify two specific learnable skills the unit will introduce; emphasise that defaults are habits, not identity.
