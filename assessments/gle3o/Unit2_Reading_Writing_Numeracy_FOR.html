Unit 2: Reading, Writing & Numeracy — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Pre-unit check · Strand B — B1, B2, B3
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Surface your current habits around reading, writing, and numeracy so the unit can be targeted at the gaps. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Reading Habits
[3 marks]
Describe in 3–4 sentences how you currently read a textbook chapter for a course. Do you highlight? Take notes? Just read through? What proportion of the content do you typically retain a week later?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This question surfaces existing reading strategy. Most students at the start of Unit 2 report some combination of "read through" + "highlight" with little post-reading retrieval — exactly the recognition-mode learning that the unit will replace. Use the responses to motivate SQ3R, annotation, and Cornell notes in 2.1. Marks: full marks for honest self-description.

Question 2 Vocabulary
[3 marks]
"The phenotypic expression of the gene depends on environmental interactions, demonstrating epigenetic regulation that complicates a simple Mendelian inheritance model."
If you encountered the word "phenotypic" in the sentence above, what strategy would you use to figure out its meaning? Identify the strategy and apply it in 2–3 sentences.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Strategies: (1) root analysis — "pheno-" (to appear/show) + "-typic" (relating to type) suggests "appearing as a type / observable characteristics"; (2) context inference — the sentence contrasts phenotypic with "Mendelian inheritance" and links it to environmental interaction, so it likely refers to the observable expression; (3) glossary check. Strong answers will use at least one of these strategies. The Grade 11 vocabulary diagnostic: students who only know "ask for help" or "skip the word" need the most coverage in 2.2. Marks: 1 strategy named, 1 strategy applied, 1 plausible meaning inferred.

Question 3 Thesis Statement
[3 marks]
Prompt: "Was the Treaty of Versailles too harsh on Germany?"
Write a one-sentence thesis statement responding to this prompt. (You don't need to know the history in depth — focus on the structure of a thesis.)
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnose: does the student write (a) a topic ("This essay is about Versailles"), (b) a fact ("Versailles was signed in 1919"), (c) a question, or (d) a debatable claim? Strong answers — even without history depth — produce a debatable, "because"-supported, evidence-forecasting thesis (e.g., "The Treaty of Versailles was too harsh on Germany because it destabilised the Weimar Republic through reparations, war-guilt clauses, and territorial losses"). Use this diagnostic to differentiate Unit 2.3 instruction: students writing topics/facts need the basic thesis lesson; students writing debatable claims need the three-part-thesis upgrade. Marks: 1 debatable claim, 1 "because" structure, 1 evidence forecast.

Question 4 Revision
[2 marks]
In 2 sentences: when you "revise" a piece of writing, what do you actually do? Do you revise once or multiple times? At what level (typos, sentences, structure, content)?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnostic for current revision habits. Most students report "I read it once and fix typos" — proofreading-only revision. Use this to motivate the multi-stage revision protocol in 2.4. Strong answers will mention multiple levels; the rare exceptional student already does content + structural editing. Marks: full for honest description.

Question 5 Numeracy · Estimation
[3 marks]
Without using a calculator, estimate \(48 \times 72\) using rounding. Show your estimation reasoning. Then explain in 2 sentences why estimation is a valuable habit when using a calculator.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Estimation: round 48 to 50 and 72 to 70; \(50 \times 70 = 3500\); the actual answer is 3,456 — close to estimate. Value of estimation: catches decimal-point errors and order-of-magnitude mistakes on the calculator (e.g., if calculator returned 34,560 the estimate would flag a 10× error). Marks: 1 reasonable estimate, 1 method shown, 1 explanation of value. Students who cannot estimate need extra coverage in 2.5.

Question 6 Graph Literacy
[3 marks]
A graph titled "Canadian Unemployment Rate, 2018–2023" shows a y-axis from 4% to 14%, and the line peaks at 13.7% in mid-2020 before declining to 5.2% by 2023.
In 3 sentences, describe what you would do before reading the data points off this graph. What would you look at first, and why?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

The Frame step: check title (what is the graph about?), axes (units, scale — y-axis 4%-14% is truncated, not starting at 0), source (who produced it, when), legend if any, and time-window (2018–2023, which captures the COVID-19 peak). The truncated y-axis can visually exaggerate change. Students who go straight to "13.7% in 2020" without framing have skipped the most important step. Marks: 1 for naming axis/units checks, 1 for noticing the source/credibility check, 1 for any mention of the time-window or axis-truncation issue.
