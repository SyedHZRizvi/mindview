Topics 5.1–5.2 — SMART Goals & Time Management
Question 1
SMART goals stand for:
Solution: SMART goals have all five characteristics. "Be better at math" is a wish; "Raise Functions mark from 68% to 80% by Dec 12 through three 30-min retrieval-practice sessions per week" is a SMART goal.
Question 2
Backwards planning means:
Solution: Backwards planning is the bridge between a goal and an actual calendar. Without it, students underestimate how much time is required and end up cramming the last week.
Question 3
The Pomodoro Technique (Cirillo) is:
Solution: 25 minutes of focused work, 5-minute break, repeat — after four "pomodoros" take a longer break. The interval is long enough for real concentration and short enough not to feel daunting. Phone in another room for full effect.
Question 4
Ward et al. (2017) found that the mere presence of a smartphone — even silenced on the desk — reduces cognitive performance.
What is the implication for Pomodoro and time-blocked study?
Solution: The "proximity effect" is real and measurable. The fix is structural — phone in another room — not motivational.
Topics 5.3–5.4 — OSSD & Postsecondary Pathways
Question 5
The OSSD requires:
Solution: The Ontario Secondary School Diploma requirements: 30 credits (18 compulsory, 12 optional), 40 hours community involvement, OSSLT (or OSSLC), and one online learning course for cohorts since 2020. Auditing early prevents Grade-12 scrambles.
Question 6
The four Ontario postsecondary pathways are:
Solution: Ontario treats all four pathways as equal. Apprenticeship (OYAP, Red Seal), college (OCAS — diplomas, applied degrees), university (OUAC — bachelor's, professional), and direct workplace are all real pathways with real career outcomes.
Question 7
OYAP is the Ontario:
Solution: Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program — leads to Red Seal certification and apprenticeships starting from secondary school. Median journeyperson income often exceeds $70,000.
Topics 5.5–5.6 — Résumés, Interviews & Portfolios
Question 8
Which is the strongest résumé bullet point?
Solution: Action verbs (organised) + quantification (4 events, 200+ participants, $450) signal credibility. "Was responsible for" reads as passive. Weak verbs and vague language waste résumé bullets.
Question 9
The STAR method for behavioural interview questions stands for:
Solution: STAR structures behavioural-interview answers — Situation, Task, Action, Result. It turns vague "tell me about a time" prompts into structured, evidence-based stories.
Question 10
A digital portfolio is most useful when it is:
Solution: Without reflection, a portfolio is just a folder. With reflection, it becomes evidence of metacognition (Unit 1.5) and a usable artefact for both applications and future self.
Question 11
When the interviewer asks "Do you have any questions for us?", the best answer is:
Solution: Interviews where the candidate has no questions read as low interest. Prepared questions about the role, team, training, or growth signal genuine engagement. Save salary questions for later in the process.
Question 12
Short answer: In 2–3 sentences, explain why a "wish" ("I want to do better in math") is not a goal and how SMART criteria transform it.
Sample: A wish is vague — it cannot be planned, tracked, or adjusted because it has no specific outcome, no deadline, and no measurable success criterion. SMART transforms it: "Raise Functions mark from 68% (after Unit 1 test) to 80% (after Unit 3 test) by Dec 12 through three 30-minute retrieval-practice sessions per week and one weekly teacher-consultation question." Now it is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound — and you can backwards-plan from December 12 to today's next action.
