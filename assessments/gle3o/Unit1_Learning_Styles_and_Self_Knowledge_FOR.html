Unit 1: Learning Styles & Self-Knowledge — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Pre-unit check · Strand A — A1.1, A1.2, A2.1, A2.2, A3.1, A3.2
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify what you already know — and what you assume — about how you learn, your motivation, and how mindset affects effort. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Be honest about what is unclear.
Question 1 Self-Knowledge · Inventory
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what you currently believe about how you learn best. What modes (visual, audio, reading/writing, hands-on) feel most natural to you? Give one concrete example from any course you have taken.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Look for: (1) at least one named mode or strategy with specific language; (2) a concrete example, not just a label; (3) some self-reflection beyond "I learn best by doing." Strong answers might say: "I find I retain things best when I write them in my own words — I think I'm a read/write learner. For example, after my chemistry class, the diagrams in the textbook don't stick until I sit down and write the steps of the reaction in sentences. When I just listen to the lecture, the material feels familiar in class but I lose most of it by the next day." Weaker answers stop at the label without a concrete example.

Question 2 Mindset
[3 marks]
Two students get back a math test with 65%. Student A says, "I'm just not a math person." Student B says, "I haven't yet learned how to do quadratic word problems reliably."
In 3–4 sentences: which student is showing a fixed mindset and which a growth mindset? What is the practical difference in what each student is likely to do next?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Student A shows fixed mindset (ability framed as identity); Student B shows growth mindset (ability framed as current state). Next-action difference: Student A is likely to disengage, give up, or avoid math; Student B is more likely to do error analysis, identify the specific weak topic (quadratic word problems), and design a targeted practice plan. The single most important verbal difference is the word "yet." Marks: 1 for correctly labelling each, 1 for the next-action contrast, 1 for accurately naming "yet" or its equivalent.

Question 3 Motivation · SDT
[4 marks]
Name a class or subject where you have felt highly motivated, and one where you have felt unmotivated. In 4–5 sentences, what made the difference? (You don't need formal SDT vocabulary yet — just describe what was happening.)
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This is a diagnostic to surface students' intuitive grasp of motivation factors. Strong answers will name elements that map onto SDT — choice (autonomy), feeling like they were getting better (competence), good relationships with the teacher or classmates (relatedness) — even without using the technical vocabulary. They may also surface "external reward" patterns: highly motivated because parents promised something, or because the mark mattered for university. Use this to introduce SDT vocabulary in the next lesson and to validate the students' own observations. Marks: 1 named example each (2), 2 for the comparison and the implicit identification of conditions.

Question 4 Metacognition
[3 marks]
In 2–3 sentences, describe what you usually do before a study session begins. Do you plan? Do you pick a strategy? Or do you just open the textbook?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This question diagnoses whether students currently use any planning step (metacognition phase 1). Most Grade 11 students at the start of the course report opening the textbook with no plan; a smaller group reports planning. Acknowledge whatever they say without judgment, then use the answers to motivate the planning-monitoring-evaluating model in Unit 1.5. Marks: full marks for honest description; the diagnostic value is in the responses, not the score.

Question 5 Self-Regulated Learning
[3 marks]
When a strategy you're using isn't working (e.g., re-reading a chapter and not retaining it), what do you typically do? (Stop, continue, switch strategies, ask for help?) Give one specific example from a real course experience.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This question diagnoses monitoring (phase 2 of metacognition). Many students continue with an ineffective strategy because they don't realise it's failing, or they don't know what else to try. Strong answers will demonstrate at least some monitoring awareness ("I usually just keep re-reading even though I know it's not working"). Use this to motivate the monitoring concept in 1.5 and the alternative strategies in Units 2–3. Marks: 1 for accurate self-description, 1 for the example, 1 for any self-awareness about whether the response is working.

Question 6 Multiple Intelligences
[3 marks]
Gardner proposed at least eight intelligences (linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, bodily-kinesthetic, musical, interpersonal, intrapersonal, naturalistic). Identify which two you suspect are your strongest, and give one example of how that has shown up in school, sport, work, or personal life.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Look for: two specific intelligences named (not just "smart"); one concrete example of each, not just a label. Strong answers move beyond school subjects to include music, sport, family, or hobbies. Use this to introduce the multiple-intelligences framework in 1.3 and to validate that intelligence has many forms. Marks: 1 per intelligence named with example (2), 1 for the explicit connection to lived experience.

📋 Diagnostic Rubric — Overall

Purpose of this diagnostic: not to assign a mark, but to surface the student's current beliefs, vocabulary, and habits around self-knowledge and motivation. Use student responses to differentiate instruction in Unit 1.

Use the data to:

  • Identify students whose current model is "fixed-mindset" — design intentional growth-mindset reframes in feedback.
  • Identify students with no current planning-monitoring-evaluating habit — invest in the metacognition lesson.
  • Identify students who can already articulate motivation conditions — accelerate their SDT vocabulary.
  • Spot students with naive learning-styles "matching" beliefs — introduce the translation-strategy alternative.

Feedback to give: descriptive only. Identify two strengths visible in the responses (e.g., "you already think reflectively about why you do well in some courses"), and two next steps (e.g., "in our first lesson on metacognition, you'll learn how to add a 5-minute planning step before each study session").
