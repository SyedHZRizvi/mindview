Unit 3: Memory, Study & Test-Taking — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Pre-unit check · Strand B — B4, B5
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Surface your current study and test-taking habits so the unit can be targeted at the gaps. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Study Habits
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe how you typically prepare for a unit test. How many days before? How many sessions? What do you actually do during each session?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most Grade 11 students at this point describe one cram session the night before, mostly rereading notes and flashcards in look-mode (recognition). A small group already spaces and uses retrieval. Use the responses to introduce spacing (3.2) and retrieval practice (3.3) as a deliberate replacement for cramming. Marks: full for honest self-description.

Question 2 Memory · Spacing
[3 marks]
Imagine you have 4 hours total to prepare for a unit test in 2 weeks. Which schedule do you think would produce the best retention: (A) 4 hours in one block the night before, or (B) 4 sessions of 1 hour spread across the 2 weeks at expanding intervals? Justify your prediction in 2–3 sentences.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Correct answer (B) — the spacing effect. Many students at this stage will pick (A) based on intuition; the diagnostic surfaces the intuition so the unit can correct it. Marks: 1 for choice, 2 for any reasoning; full marks even for choice (A) if the reasoning is articulate — the goal is to surface beliefs, not to score them.

Question 3 Retrieval Practice
[3 marks]
Have you ever closed your textbook and tried to write down or explain everything you remember about a topic, before checking your notes? If so, describe what happened. If not, explain why not.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This question surfaces the existence (or absence) of retrieval-practice habits. Most students answer "no, I just re-read." A smaller group has tried it informally. Use the responses to motivate the brain-dump and Feynman techniques in 3.3. Marks: full for honest description.

Question 4 Mnemonics & Maps
[3 marks]
Name one mnemonic or memory aid you have used (e.g., BEDMAS, SOH-CAH-TOA, MAIN, ROY G BIV). Then in 2 sentences, describe a topic from one of your other courses where a concept map (showing relationships) would be more useful than a mnemonic.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnostic for whether students distinguish item-recall (mnemonics) from relational understanding (concept maps). Strong answers will name a relational topic — cellular respiration's input-output flow, the causes of the French Revolution, the layers of the Canadian Charter. Use this to introduce the "mnemonics for items, concept maps for relationships" rule in 3.4. Marks: 1 mnemonic named, 2 for the concept-map example with a real relational topic.

Question 5 Test Format Awareness
[3 marks]
Do you prepare differently for multiple-choice, short-answer, and essay tests? In 3 sentences, describe what (if anything) you do differently for each format.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most Grade 11 students at this stage report little or no format differentiation — they "study the content" without thinking about how the format will test it. Strong answers will mention practising MC patterns, drafting structures for essays, etc. Use this to introduce format-specific preparation in 3.5. Marks: 1 per format with any specific strategy named; bonus for the broader insight that format matters.

Question 6 Test Anxiety
[3 marks]
On a scale of 1 (no anxiety) to 5 (severe), how anxious do you typically feel before a test? When the anxiety hits, what (if anything) do you currently do to manage it?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

This is a sensitive diagnostic. The aim is not to "score" anxiety but to identify students who would benefit from the explicit interventions in 3.6 (reappraisal, expressive writing, breathing). Strong descriptions surface what students currently do (positive or unhelpful) so the lesson can build on it. Marks: full for honest description; no penalty for high anxiety.

Question 7 Returned Tests
[2 marks]
When you get back a test, what do you typically do? Look at the mark and file it? Look at specific errors? Categorise what kind of error each one was?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most students at this stage report "look at the mark and file it." Use the responses to motivate the error-analysis protocol in 3.6 (careless / concept / gap / format). Marks: full for honest self-description.

📋 Diagnostic Rubric — Overall

Purpose: surface existing habits and beliefs about studying and testing. Many Grade 11 students hold intuitions (cram works; rereading is studying; "I'm just not a test person") that the unit will challenge with evidence. The diagnostic lets you spot which intuitions are widespread in your class.

Differentiation: students reporting high test anxiety should receive specific 3.6 support early; students with no spaced/retrieval habits should be the audience for 3.2–3.3 demonstrations.

Feedback to give: validate students for honest self-description; identify two specific habits the unit will offer to replace; do not grade harshly.
