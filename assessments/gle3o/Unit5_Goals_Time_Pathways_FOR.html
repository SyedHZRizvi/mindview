Unit 5: Goal-Setting, Time Management & Pathways — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Pre-unit check · Strand D — D1, D2, D3, D4
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Surface your current planning habits, OSSD audit status, and pathway thinking. The most useful unit in the course depends on you actually doing this work — and being honest about where you are now.
Question 1 Goal Setting
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, write the most important academic or personal goal you currently have. Be specific. Then ask: does it have a deadline? A measurable success criterion? A "because" connecting it to a deeper purpose?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnostic for current goal-setting habits. Most students will write a "wish" (vague aspiration like "do well in school" or "get into university") without SMART criteria. Use the responses to introduce SMART transformation in 5.1. Marks: full for honest description; the diagnostic value is in seeing what's missing.

Question 2 Time Management
[3 marks]
In 3 sentences: how do you currently plan your week? Do you use a calendar / planner / time-blocking? Or do you work from a mental to-do list?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Surfaces current planning approach. Many students at this stage report no formal planning, working from mental lists, or only writing down due dates. A small group already time-blocks or uses planner apps. Use responses to motivate 5.2's specific tools. Marks: full for honest description.

Question 3 Pomodoro / Focus
[3 marks]
Where is your phone right now while you do schoolwork? On the desk? In your pocket? In another room? In 2–3 sentences, describe your typical phone position during study and whether you think it affects your focus.
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Diagnostic for understanding of the smartphone proximity effect (Ward et al. 2017). Most students at this stage have the phone on the desk, often face-up, and have not connected this to focus quality. Use responses to motivate the "phone in another room" rule in 5.2. Marks: full for honest description; bonus if the student already articulates the proximity-effect insight.

Question 4 OSSD Audit
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences: how many credits have you completed so far? How many community-involvement hours? Have you passed the OSSLT? Are you on track for the OSSD?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Many Grade 11 students cannot answer these questions specifically. The unit will introduce the OSSD audit process in 5.3. Students with major gaps should be referred to guidance immediately, not in March of Grade 12. Marks: full for honest description; flag students whose responses suggest unrecognised gaps.

Question 5 Pathways
[3 marks]
Of the four Ontario postsecondary pathways (apprenticeship, college, university, direct workplace), which are you currently considering? Which have you actually investigated (visited a program, attended an info session, talked to someone in the field)?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most Grade 11 students at the start of GLE3O have considered only university or only college, often without serious investigation. The unit will introduce all four pathways with equal weight in 5.4. Watch for students whose pathway thinking is shaped by family pressure or peer norms; these students benefit most from informational-interview assignments. Marks: full for honest description.

Question 6 Résumé / Portfolio
[3 marks]
Do you have a current résumé? A digital portfolio (MyBlueprint, Google Drive, personal site)? If not, what's stopping you?
📖 Sample Answer / Marking Note

Most Grade 11 students do not have either a current résumé or a meaningfully-organised portfolio. The unit will lead them through building both (5.5 and 5.6). Students with résumés already should review for STAR-style descriptions and quantification. Marks: full for honest self-description.

📋 Diagnostic Rubric — Overall

Purpose: surface students' current planning habits and pathway thinking. Many Grade 11 students approach this unit without having done any of the practical OSSD-audit, pathway-research, or résumé-building work it asks for. Honest responses on this diagnostic are essential — they tell you which students need targeted guidance-department referrals (e.g., students unaware they are missing compulsory credits) and which students are ready for accelerated portfolio and pathway work.

Differentiation: Students unaware of OSSD requirements → mandatory 5.3 deep dive + guidance department referral. Students with no résumé / portfolio → mandatory 5.5 and 5.6 work. Students with university-only thinking → mandatory 5.4 four-pathway exposure with at least one informational interview.

Feedback to give: identify two specific concrete next-actions the unit will produce (e.g., "by the end of Unit 5 you will have a current résumé, an OSSD audit, and a backwards-planned Grade 12 course-selection sheet").
