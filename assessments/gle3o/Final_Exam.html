[2]A · Self-Knowledge
VARK (Fleming) sorts learners by:
Answer Key

2
[2]A · SDT
In Self-Determination Theory, the three psychological needs are:
Answer Key

B. Deci & Ryan's SDT identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

3
[3]A · Metacognition
Define each: (a) metacognition (3 phases); (b) growth mindset (Dweck); (c) intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation.
Answer Key

(a) Metacognition — thinking deliberately about one's own thinking; three phases: planning, monitoring, evaluating. (b) Growth mindset — the belief that ability is developed through effort, strategy, and feedback. (c) Intrinsic motivation — engagement driven by interest, meaning, or alignment with values; extrinsic motivation — engagement driven by external reward or to avoid punishment.

4
[2]B · Reading
SQ3R (Robinson) is:
Answer Key

A. Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. The Recite (close-book retrieval) step is where most of the learning happens.

5
[3]B · Writing
Identify: (a) the three-part thesis structure; (b) the four passes of multi-stage revision; (c) the distinction between expository and persuasive writing.
Answer Key

(a) Three-part thesis — claim + "because" + forecast of evidence. (b) Multi-stage revision — Content edit → Structural edit → Sentence-level edit → Proofread; done over multiple sittings, not one. (c) Expository writing's job is to explain or synthesise; persuasive writing's job is to argue a debatable claim. Misreading which form a prompt calls for is the most common essay-writing mistake.

6
[2]B · Numeracy
When a student calculates \(347 \times 28\) and gets 97,160, the best diagnostic is:
Answer Key

B. Estimation catches order-of-magnitude errors. True answer is 9,716; 97,160 is ~10× too large — a decimal slip.

7
[3]B · Memory
Define each: (a) the spacing effect; (b) retrieval practice / the testing effect; (c) the fluency illusion.
Answer Key

(a) Spacing effect — reviews spread across days at expanding intervals produce dramatically better long-term retention than the same total time in a single block (Cepeda et al. 2006). (b) Retrieval practice / testing effect — the act of pulling information from memory strengthens the memory trace more than equivalent rereading (Roediger & Karpicke 2006). (c) Fluency illusion — the smooth feeling of recognition during rereading produces unwarranted confidence about retrieval — "easy now, hard on the test."

8
[2]C · Communication Styles
The four communication styles are:
Answer Key

B. Assertive is associated with the best long-run outcomes and is learnable.

9
[3]C · Listening & Feedback
Define each: (a) the "I-statement" three-part structure; (b) active listening — paraphrasing technique; (c) the SBI feedback structure.
Answer Key

(a) I-statement: "I feel [feeling] when [behaviour] because [impact]." (b) Paraphrasing: restating the other's point in your own words to verify understanding ("So what I'm hearing is..."). (c) SBI (Center for Creative Leadership): Situation – Behaviour – Impact; keeps feedback specific and observable.

Part B — Thinking [25 marks]
15
[6]A + B · Multi-Concept Scenario
Ella, Grade 11, gets back a 58% on a math test. Her first thought: "I'm just not a math person." She catches the thought and re-writes: "I haven't yet learned how to do quadratic word problems reliably." She does error analysis: 4 careless arithmetic, 5 word-problem set-up errors, 3 conceptual factoring gaps.
Analyse Ella's response. In 6–8 sentences, identify: (i) the mindset shift she made; (ii) the specific Unit 3 strategy this opens up; (iii) which Unit 1 metacognitive phase her error analysis maps onto; (iv) one specific action she should take this week.
Answer Key

(i) Fixed mindset → growth mindset, marked by the "yet" reframe. (ii) Error analysis (3.6) → categorised next-actions: careless → estimate-and-check habit; concept → re-watch lesson + retrieval; gap → spaced retrieval rotation. (iii) Maps onto the evaluating phase of metacognition (Unit 1.5). (iv) Specific action: book 30 min on quadratic-word-problem retrieval practice this week; verify the factoring lesson is in her Anki rotation. The cycle is: mindset reframe → error diagnosis → strategy choice → action. Marks: 1 mindset, 2 strategy, 2 metacognition, 1 action.

16
[6]B · Major Application
Lucas's Grade 11 French course introduces ~40 new vocabulary items per week. He currently studies the night before each Friday quiz; retains for a week; forgets most before the exam.
Design a spaced + retrieval study plan. In 6–8 sentences: (i) specify a daily cadence in Anki or equivalent; (ii) describe what "retrieval" looks like as opposed to "rereading"; (iii) predict the impact on his cumulative final-exam retention; (iv) justify with reference to the spacing effect and the fluency illusion.
Answer Key

(i) Add ~20 new cards Mon and Wed (40/week); open Anki daily for 10–15 min for due cards; well-known cards expand intervals to 30+ days, weak cards return sooner. (ii) Retrieval: cover the answer side, produce the translation from memory, then verify — not "look at both sides." (iii) Cumulative exam retention rises from ~40% (cram pattern) to ~85–90% (consistent daily spaced retrieval). (iv) The spacing effect (Cepeda 2006) puts retrieval acts at moments of partial forgetting, which is when long-term memory strengthens most; the fluency illusion explains why rereading feels productive but isn't — closing the book and producing the answer is the only test of actual retention. Marks: 2 cadence, 2 retrieval-vs-reread, 1 prediction, 1 justification.

17
[6]C · Conflict Application
Two students sharing a study space at home: one wants total quiet; the other wants music while studying. Stated positions: "no music" vs "music on." The relationship is good and both want to keep it that way.
Apply Fisher & Ury's interest-vs-position distinction and the collaborative-resolution process. In 6–8 sentences: (i) excavate each side's underlying interests; (ii) brainstorm two options that could serve both interests; (iii) propose a check-back protocol.
Answer Key

(i) Quiet-wanter: focus / retention ("lyrics break my concentration; I need to retain"); Music-listener: reduced anxiety / familiar background ("silence makes me anxious"). (ii) Options: (a) headphones for the music listener (preserves both); (b) lyric-free background (instrumental playlists agreed by both); (c) time-share the workspace (mornings quiet, afternoons music). (iii) Two-week check-back: do both interests still feel met? Are there new edge cases (e.g., focus sessions before a major test)? Marks: 2 interest excavation, 2 brainstormed options, 1 evaluation criterion, 1 check-back specificity.

Hassan has interests in physical/mechanical work, helping people, and biology. He is currently considering only university programs. Apply the four-pathway investigation framework. In 7–8 sentences: (i) describe one specific program in each of the four pathways that could fit him; (ii) identify the Grade 12 prerequisites for each; (iii) recommend one informational interview he should do this semester; (iv) explain why "university only" thinking misdirects him.
Answer Key

(i) Apprenticeship: Automotive Service Technician (OYAP) — Grade 12 college-level math. College: 2-year Paramedic diploma — Grade 12 English (C/U), Biology (C/U), Math (C/U). University: BSc Kinesiology — Grade 12 English U, Biology U, Chemistry U, Math U. Direct workplace: healthcare aide while completing community-college business administration part-time. (ii) Prerequisites named above. (iii) Informational interview with a working paramedic (high-information, low-cost). (iv) "University only" thinking diverts him from pathways that likely fit better — paramedic college has direct employment and matches his interests; trades earn high incomes with high demand; the prestige assumption is harmful when it doesn't match the person. Marks: 1 per pathway program (4) + 1 informational interview + 2 misdirection argument.

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
19
[15]A + B + C + D · Major Synthesis Essay
Major Task — Write a synthesis essay (450–600 words) responding to ONE of the following prompts:

Option 1: "The most consequential thing GLE3O has changed about how I learn." Pick one specific strategy from the course (e.g., retrieval practice, I-statements, time-blocking, the SMART goal protocol), trace it through your actual practice across this semester, and argue why it matters most.
Option 2: "How self-knowledge (Strand A) feeds every other strand." Pick one self-knowledge tool (mindset reframe, SDT diagnosis, multiple-intelligences, learning-style preference) and show how it shapes your academic strategy (Strand B), your communication (Strand C), and your pathway choices (Strand D).
Option 3: "The strategy in this course I most resisted — and what changed." Pick the strategy you found least intuitive (e.g., closed-book retrieval, multi-stage revision, daily spaced repetition, I-statements, OSSD audits). Describe the resistance, what shifted, and what the experience taught you about your own learning.
Option 4: "Why my Grade 11 → Grade 12 transition plan is different now." Describe how the GLE3O work (your learner profile, OSSD audit, pathway research, time-management habits) has changed the plan you would have made at the start of the course.

Include: introduction with thesis, three body paragraphs (each evidence-supported with specific examples from your own coursework), conclusion that synthesises. Embed at least three specific references to course concepts with their proper vocabulary.
Marking Rubric

K/U (3): accurate use of relevant course concepts and vocabulary. Thinking (4): quality and depth of argument; specific personal evidence; consideration of counter-cases or alternative strategies. Communication (5): clear thesis-driven introduction, well-organised body paragraphs (topic sentence + evidence + analyse), embedded specific references with concepts named, conclusion that synthesises rather than restates. Application (3): transfer of course concepts to authentic personal experience and forward-looking decisions. Sample first sentences for Option 1: "Of all the strategies introduced in GLE3O, the single one that has most measurably changed my learning is retrieval practice — the deliberate move from rereading to closed-book retrieval has lifted my chemistry mark by 14% in a single unit while also closing what I have come to recognise as the 'fluency illusion' that fooled me throughout Grade 10." A Level-4 essay grounds the chosen strategy in specific personal evidence with dates, marks, and named subjects.

21
[5]C · Feedback & I-Statement
Scenario: in a real group conversation last week, a teammate consistently arrived late to meetings. You defaulted to "avoiding" (Thomas-Kilmann). The issue is now bigger.
Write the I-statement message you would actually send to the teammate (using the "I feel ... when ... because ..." structure), followed by your proposed collaborative-resolution check-in process. (5–7 sentences total.)
Answer Key

I-statement: "I feel rushed and stressed when meetings start late, because we have limited time to make decisions and I sometimes can't finish my pieces. Could we agree on a specific start time, and a quick text if you're delayed?" Collaborative-resolution: propose a 15-minute conversation; each person states underlying interests (yours: meeting effectiveness, your own time pressure; theirs: possible scheduling conflicts, transit, family demands); brainstorm options (earlier start? shorter meetings? asynchronous status updates?); agree on action; set check-back for 2 weeks. Marks: 1 I-statement (3-part), 1 invite-collab, 1 interest-elicitation, 1 brainstorm, 1 check-back.

📖 Complete Final Exam Answer Key & Marking Notes

This cumulative final examination covers all four GLE3O strands at all four Achievement Chart levels and contributes 15% of the 30% Final Evaluation (the other 15% is the Personal Strategy Portfolio).

Marking philosophy: reward specificity, evidence, and genuine application to the student's own coursework. Level-4 answers integrate concepts across strands and ground claims in specific personal evidence (named courses, named marks, named upcoming situations). Generic "study harder, plan better" answers score Level 2 even if technically correct.

Common Level-4 patterns: (1) explicit mindset reframe paired with strategy diagnosis; (2) spaced retrieval applied to authentic content with quantified retention prediction; (3) I-statement with all three components plus collaborative-resolution check-back; (4) four-pathway analysis with specific programs and prerequisites; (5) cross-strand synthesis where the self-knowledge informs the strategy informs the communication informs the pathway.

Common Level-2 patterns: (1) restating the prompt without applying concepts; (2) listing vocabulary without specificity; (3) confusing related concepts (e.g., conflating mindset with motivation); (4) generic claims without personal evidence; (5) skipping the synthesis move on cross-strand questions.

Rubric for the major synthesis essay (Q19, 15 marks): see embedded marking note. Use the four Achievement Chart categories (K/U 3, Thinking 4, Communication 5, Application 3). Sample first sentences are provided for each option.
