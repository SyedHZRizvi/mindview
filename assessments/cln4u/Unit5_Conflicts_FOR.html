Chapter 5: Conflicts & Resolution Between Nations — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand E
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps in your understanding of war, peace, and international institutions before starting Chapter 5.
Question 1 Prereq · War
[3 marks]
In your own words, when (if ever) is it morally acceptable for one country to use military force against another? Give two reasons for and two reasons against your view.
Question 2 Prereq · UN
[3 marks]
Without looking it up, list any three things you think the United Nations is supposed to do about international conflicts. Do you think it does these well? Why or why not?
Question 3 Prereq · War Crimes
[3 marks]
What do you understand by a "war crime"? Name any example you have heard of — historical or recent — and explain why it counted as a war crime in your view.
Question 4 Skills · Reasoning
[3 marks]
Suppose State A launches a small-scale operation across the border, hitting only a military installation in State B and killing only soldiers. State B says it has been "attacked"; State A says it was a "limited operation."
Without legal vocabulary, identify two factors you would weigh in deciding whether this is properly called an "attack." Should State B be allowed to use military force to respond?
Question 5 Skills · Peacekeeping
[3 marks]
What do UN peacekeepers do? Have you heard of any Canadian role in peacekeeping? Should Canada do more or less peacekeeping today? Why?
Question 6 Knowledge · Courts
[2 marks]
Have you heard of the International Criminal Court (ICC) or the International Court of Justice (ICJ)? Briefly say what you know about either — even if it is just one or two sentences.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which Chapter 5 topic do you feel least prepared for: the law on use of force, self-defence, Chapter VII, the Geneva Conventions, the ICC, or peaceful dispute settlement? Explain what is unclear.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
