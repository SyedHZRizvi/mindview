Topic 1.1 — What is Law?
Question 1
Which of the following best states the principle of the rule of law, as articulated by the Supreme Court in Reference re Secession of Quebec [1998]?
Solution: The rule of law means everyone — including government — is subject to law applied predictably and equally. It does not guarantee laws are just; it requires that they are general and binding on the makers. The Supreme Court in the Secession Reference identified the rule of law as one of four unwritten constitutional principles.
Question 2
Anna drives drunk, runs a red light, strikes Ben's car, and breaks his arm.
This single incident can trigger which of the following?
Solution: A single physical event can spawn criminal, quasi-criminal/regulatory, and civil proceedings independently. The standards of proof differ (beyond reasonable doubt vs balance of probabilities), and one outcome does not bind the other.
Question 3
A speeding ticket is best classified as:
Solution: Public law governs state vs individual relationships. A traffic offence is prosecuted by the Crown (provincial offences court) and is therefore public/quasi-criminal, not private.
Topic 1.2 — Natural Law vs Positivism
Question 4
According to legal positivism, the validity of a law depends on:
Solution: Positivists (Austin, Hart, Bentham) hold that law's validity is a question of its source — whether it was enacted by the recognised institutions in the recognised way. Moral evaluation is a separate question. An unjust law remains law.
Question 5
The Latin maxim lex iniusta non est lex ("an unjust law is not a law") is associated with which school of jurisprudence?
Solution: Natural-law theorists, classically Thomas Aquinas, hold that human law derives its authority from a higher moral order. A statute that violates the natural law fails to bind in conscience and, in a strong sense, "is not law" at all.
Question 6
In H.L.A. Hart's The Concept of Law (1961), the "rule of recognition" is:
Solution: Hart distinguished primary rules (rules of conduct) from secondary rules (rules about rules). The rule of recognition is the master secondary rule that lets officials identify the law of a system — the complex social fact that makes a legal system function.
Topic 1.4 — Sources of Canadian Law
Question 7
Section 52(1) of the Constitution Act, 1982 makes which document the supreme law of Canada?
Solution: Section 52(1) states: "The Constitution of Canada is the supreme law of Canada, and any law that is inconsistent with the provisions of the Constitution is, to the extent of the inconsistency, of no force or effect." This includes both the Constitution Acts of 1867 and 1982 and various other documents in the schedule.
Question 8
In Quebec, private-law matters are governed primarily by:
Solution: Quebec is a bijural province. Public-law matters (criminal, constitutional) follow common-law method; private-law matters (contract, property, family) are governed by the codified Civil Code of Quebec, which descends from the Napoleonic civil-law tradition.
Question 9
Which of the following is recognised as a source of Canadian law in addition to the Constitution, statute, common law, and royal prerogative?
Solution: Indigenous legal traditions are increasingly recognised as a source of Canadian law, not merely a subject for it. Sparrow, Tsilhqot'in Nation, and the federal UNDRIP Act (2021) treat Indigenous legal orders as independent sources whose principles must be given legal effect.
Topic 1.5 & 1.6 — Comparative Systems & IRAC
Question 10
In a common-law system, the binding part of a precedent is called:
Solution: The ratio decidendi (Latin: "the reason for deciding") is the legal rule the case stands for; it binds lower courts. Obiter dicta are remarks beyond what was needed to decide; they are persuasive but not binding.
Question 11
The IRAC method of legal reasoning stands for:
Solution: IRAC = Issue (the legal question), Rule (the law that governs), Application (the rule applied to the facts), Conclusion (the answer to the issue). It is the standard structure for case briefs, factum paragraphs, and exam answers.
Question 12
Mira photographs a classmate's wallet on a desk but does not move it. Police charge her with theft under s. 322 of the Criminal Code, which requires "taking" or "converting" property.
Applying IRAC, what is the strongest analytical move?
Solution: IRAC requires Rule (s. 322 actus reus elements) and Application (whether Mira's conduct satisfies "taking" or "converting"). Photography neither takes nor converts the physical object. Actus reus fails; mens rea is then irrelevant. The conclusion follows from a careful step-by-step application.
