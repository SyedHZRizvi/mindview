Chapter 4: Human Rights & the UN — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand D
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps in your understanding of human rights, the UN, and refugees before starting Chapter 4. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Prereq · Rights
[3 marks]
Name any three human rights you believe people are entitled to. Explain briefly why you think each is fundamental.
Question 2 Prereq · History
[3 marks]
What major historical event in the 1940s motivated the creation of the modern international human-rights regime? In your own words, why might states want such a regime?
Question 3 Prereq · Refugees
[3 marks]
What does the word "refugee" mean to you? How does a refugee differ from an immigrant or a tourist?
Question 4 Skills · Reasoning
[3 marks]
Some commentators argue that economic, social, and cultural rights (such as the right to health or education) are different in kind from civil and political rights (such as freedom of speech).
Without technical legal vocabulary, give arguments for and against this view. Which side do you tend to agree with? Why?
Question 5 Skills · Definitions
[3 marks]
Define each in your own words: (a) genocide; (b) ethnic cleansing; (c) crimes against humanity. If you cannot distinguish them, say so honestly — Chapter 4 will sharpen the distinctions.
Question 6 Knowledge · Indigenous Rights
[2 marks]
What do you know about UNDRIP (the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples)? If you have heard of it, briefly describe what it does; if not, name any one Indigenous-rights issue in Canada that you have read about.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which Chapter 4 topic do you feel least confident about: the UDHR, the international covenants, the specialised treaties, refugee law, regional systems, or UNDRIP and R2P? Explain what is unclear.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
