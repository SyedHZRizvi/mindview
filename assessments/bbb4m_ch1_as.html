Topic 1.1 — Importance of Trade to Canada
Question 1
What is the largest single trading partner of Canada (by value of merchandise exports)?
Solution:
The United States is, by a wide margin, Canada's largest single trading partner. The next-largest partners (China, the UK, Japan, Germany) are individually a fraction of US trade volume.
Question 2
If Canada's GDP is \(C\$2{,}800\) billion, total exports are \(C\$880\) billion, and total imports are \(C\$900\) billion, what is the trade-to-GDP ratio (as a percent, rounded to one decimal)?
Solution:
Trade-to-GDP \( = \dfrac{\text{Exports} + \text{Imports}}{\text{GDP}} = \dfrac{880 + 900}{2{,}800} = \dfrac{1{,}780}{2{,}800} \approx 0.6357 \approx 63.6\% \).
Topic 1.2 — Forms of International Business
Question 3
A Canadian fast-food chain grants a Singaporean entrepreneur the right to use its full business model — brand, recipes, training, and operating manual — in exchange for an upfront fee and ongoing royalties. This is BEST described as:
Solution:
Franchising bundles the entire business format (brand + operations + training). Licensing is narrower (typically just IP). JV requires shared equity. FDI requires the firm itself to invest.
Question 4
Which of the following is the LOWEST-commitment, fastest-to-start form of going international?
Solution:
Indirect exporting requires no capital investment abroad; the broker handles the foreign side. FDI is the highest-commitment option.
Topic 1.3 — Comparative Advantage
Question 5
In one hour of labour, Country X can produce 4 barrels of syrup OR 2 laptops. Country Y can produce 1 barrel of syrup OR 2 laptops. Which country has the comparative advantage in laptops?
Solution:
Opportunity cost of one laptop in X = \(4/2 = 2\) barrels; in Y = \(1/2 = 0.5\) barrels. Y gives up less syrup per laptop, so Y has the comparative advantage in laptops. (X has the comparative advantage in syrup.)
Question 6
"Absolute advantage" and "comparative advantage" are:
Solution:
Absolute advantage = producing with fewer inputs per unit. Comparative advantage = producing with lower opportunity cost. A country can have absolute advantage in everything yet still benefit from specialization based on comparative advantage.
Topic 1.4 — Political/Legal/Economic Systems
Question 7
Which Canadian province uses civil law rather than common law for matters of contract and property?
Solution:
Quebec uses civil law (Civil Code of Québec), descended from French Napoleonic tradition. The other Canadian provinces and the federal system use common law.
Question 8
A country has total GDP of \(\text{US}\$300\) billion and a population of 75 million. What is GDP per capita (in US\$)?
Solution:
GDP per capita \( = \dfrac{\text{GDP}}{\text{population}} = \dfrac{300{,}000{,}000{,}000}{75{,}000{,}000} = \text{US}\$4{,}000 \).
Topic 1.5 — Culture & Hofstede
Question 9
Hofstede's "Power Distance Index" (PDI) measures:
Solution:
PDI captures the degree to which less-powerful members of a society accept that power is distributed unequally. High in China, Mexico, Saudi Arabia; low in Canada, Denmark, Israel.
Question 10
A "high-context" culture (per Edward T. Hall) is one where:
Solution:
High-context cultures (Japan, China, Arab world) leave much unsaid; meaning is in the context. Low-context cultures (Germany, Netherlands, Canada) spell things out explicitly.
Topic 1.6 — Trade Agreements & Trading Blocs
Question 11
Which trade agreement replaced NAFTA in 2020 and governs Canada's trade with the United States and Mexico?
Solution:
The Canada-United-States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA in Canada, USMCA in the US, T-MEC in Mexico) replaced NAFTA on 1 July 2020.
Question 12
A vehicle qualifies for the CUSMA preferential tariff if its North American content is at least:
Solution:
CUSMA raised the regional value content threshold for passenger vehicles to 75% (up from 62.5% under NAFTA), phased in over several years.
