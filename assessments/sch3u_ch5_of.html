Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
Question 1 [2 marks]
List the five postulates of kinetic molecular theory of gases.
Question 2 [3 marks]
A sample of helium has V = 6.0 L at 27 °C. Calculate the volume at 127 °C, at constant P.
Solution:
T₁=300 K, T₂=400 K. V₂ = 6.0 × 400/300 = 8.0 L.
Question 3 [3 marks]
A 2.50 L container of N₂ at 101 kPa and 20 °C is compressed to 1.00 L while heated to 100 °C. Find the new pressure.
Question 4 [3 marks]
Use \(PV = nRT\) to find the volume of 0.500 mol O₂ at 30.0 °C and 95.0 kPa.
Question 5 [2 marks]
Stratospheric ozone is depleted by:
Solution:
(b) — CFC-derived Cl atoms catalytically destroy ozone.
Question 6 [2 marks]
Define: (a) STP; (b) SATP; (c) molar volume.
Part B: Thinking & Inquiry [15 marks]
Question 7 [5 marks]
Carbonate decomposition: \(\text{CaCO}_3(\text{s}) \rightarrow \text{CaO}(\text{s}) + \text{CO}_2(\text{g})\). What volume of CO₂ at STP is produced from 10.0 g of CaCO₃ (M=100.09)?
Question 8 [5 marks]
A 0.500 g sample of magnesium is reacted with excess HCl. The H₂ gas evolved is collected over water at 22 °C; total pressure 99.5 kPa. Vapour pressure of water at 22 °C is 2.6 kPa. Find the volume of dry H₂ collected. (Eq: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂.)
Question 9 [5 marks]
A car-engine cylinder has volume 0.500 L at 27 °C and 101 kPa during intake. Compression reduces volume to 0.050 L; combustion raises T to 1727 °C and the cylinder is sealed. Find the new pressure.
Part C: Communication [15 marks]
Question 10 [4 marks]
Explain in your own words how kinetic molecular theory accounts for: (a) Boyle's law; (b) Charles's law. Give a microscopic-level reason for each.
Question 11 [4 marks]
Describe two ways real gases deviate from ideal-gas behaviour, and the conditions under which the deviation is largest. Explain in terms of KMT assumptions.
Question 12 [4 marks]
Distinguish between the "ozone layer" (stratospheric) and "ground-level ozone" (tropospheric). Which is beneficial? Which is harmful? Explain.
Question 13 [3 marks]
Describe one Canadian initiative or technological measure that has reduced acid-precipitation, ozone-depleting, or greenhouse-gas emissions.
Part D: Application [15 marks]
Question 14 [5 marks]
Methane (CH₄) burns according to \(\text{CH}_4 + 2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}\). What mass of CO₂ is released for every 1000 L of CH₄ burned at SATP (24.8 L/mol)?
Question 15 [4 marks]
A scuba tank contains 12.0 L of air at 200 atm and 22 °C. (a) What volume of air does this represent at the surface (1 atm, 22 °C)? (b) Explain why the diver must surface slowly, in terms of gas behaviour in body tissues.
Question 16 [3 marks]
Atmospheric CO₂ today is approximately 420 ppm; pre-industrial was 280 ppm. (a) Express the increase as a percentage of the pre-industrial value. (b) If the entire increase came from fossil-fuel CO₂ (a simplification), what mass of CO₂ has been added to a 5.0×10¹⁸ kg atmosphere? (CO₂ molar mass 44.01; air molar mass 28.97.)
Question 17 [3 marks]
Use the ideal gas law to compute the density (g/L) of CO₂ at STP. Compare with the density of N₂ at the same conditions. Why is dry ice (solid CO₂) so good at displacing air in a beaker?

Complete Answer Key

Q1. Particles are tiny and far apart; constant random motion; elastic collisions; no intermolecular forces; KE ∝ T(K).

Q2. 8.0 L.

Q3. Combined gas law: P₂ = P₁(V₁/V₂)(T₂/T₁) = 101(2.50/1.00)(373/293) = 101 × 2.500 × 1.273 = 321 kPa.

Q4. V = nRT/P = (0.500)(8.314)(303.15)/95.0 = 1260/95.0 = 13.3 L.

Q5. (b).

Q6. STP = 0 °C, 101.325 kPa. SATP = 25 °C, 100 kPa. Molar volume = volume of 1 mol gas (22.4 L at STP, 24.8 L at SATP).

Q7. n(CaCO₃) = 10.0/100.09 = 0.0999 mol → n(CO₂) = 0.0999 mol → V at STP = 0.0999 × 22.4 = 2.24 L.

Q8. P(H₂) = 99.5 − 2.6 = 96.9 kPa. n(Mg) = 0.500/24.31 = 0.02057 mol → n(H₂) = 0.02057 mol. V = nRT/P = (0.02057)(8.314)(295.15)/96.9 = 50.5/96.9 = 0.521 L.

Q9. Combined gas law: P₂ = P₁(V₁/V₂)(T₂/T₁) = 101 × (0.500/0.050) × (2000/300) = 101 × 10 × 6.67 ≈ 6730 kPa ≈ 66 atm.

Q10. (a) Compressing volume increases collision frequency per unit area → P rises. (b) Heating increases KE → faster, more energetic collisions per unit area → V must expand (at const P) so per-wall force stays constant.

Q11. (1) Particles do have volume — at high P, the "free" volume is less than container volume. (2) Particles do attract — at low T, attractions become significant. Worst deviation: low T, high P. KMT assumes negligible volume and zero attraction.

Q12. Stratospheric O₃ (15–35 km) absorbs UV-B, protects life — beneficial. Tropospheric O₃ (ground level) is a respiratory irritant and component of smog — harmful.

Q13. Acceptable answers: Eastern Canada Acid Rain Program (SO₂ scrubbers, low-S fuels); the Montreal Protocol commitment (CFC phase-out); federal carbon pricing (greenhouse gases); catalytic converters for NOₓ control.

Q14. n(CH₄) = 1000/24.8 = 40.32 mol → n(CO₂) = 40.32 mol → m(CO₂) = 40.32 × 44.01 = 1.77 × 10³ g ≈ 1.77 kg.

Q15. (a) V₂ = V₁P₁/P₂ = 12.0 × 200/1 = 2400 L. (b) Under pressure, more N₂/O₂ dissolves in body tissues (Henry's law); rapid surfacing → gas bubbles form in blood/joints (decompression sickness / "the bends"). Slow ascent allows controlled outgassing.

Q16. (a) (420 − 280)/280 × 100% = 50% increase. (b) Moles of air ≈ 5.0×10²¹ g / 28.97 = 1.7×10²⁰ mol. ΔCO₂ as mol fraction = 140×10⁻⁶ × 1.7×10²⁰ = 2.4×10¹⁶ mol; mass CO₂ added ≈ 2.4×10¹⁶ × 44 = 1.0 × 10¹⁸ g = 1.0 × 10¹⁵ kg ≈ 1000 Gt.

Q17. At STP, 1 mol CO₂ occupies 22.4 L; density = 44.01/22.4 = 1.96 g/L. N₂: 28.02/22.4 = 1.25 g/L. CO₂ is ~57% denser than air → sinks and displaces it from below. Dry ice sublimes to CO₂ gas that fills containers from the bottom.
