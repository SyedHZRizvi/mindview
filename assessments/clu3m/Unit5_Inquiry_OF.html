Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
List and briefly define the four steps of the IRAC framework.
Answer Key

Issue: the legal question, phrased precisely. Rule: the controlling legal principle with its source (statute section, leading case). Application: apply the rule's elements to the specific facts; weigh competing arguments. Conclusion: the answer to the issue that follows from the application.

2
[3]
List the standard parts of a Canadian case brief in order.
Answer Key

(1) Citation; (2) parties & procedural history; (3) material facts; (4) issue(s); (5) holding / judgment; (6) ratio decidendi; (7) obiter dicta; (8) reasoning; (9) significance / commentary. Marks: 1 per group with full list earning 3.

3
[3]
State Driedger's "modern principle" of statutory interpretation and identify its three components.
Answer Key

"The words of an Act are to be read in their entire context and in their grammatical and ordinary sense harmoniously with the scheme of the Act, the object of the Act, and the intention of Parliament" — quoted in Rizzo & Rizzo Shoes [1998]. Three components: (1) text — ordinary meaning of words; (2) context — relationship to surrounding sections, definitions, scheme; (3) purpose — what Parliament was trying to achieve. All three read together.

4
[2]
Define ratio decidendi and obiter dicta and identify which is binding on lower courts.
Answer Key

Ratio decidendi = the binding legal principle of a case (the rule the court actually applied). Obiter dicta = remarks made in passing; persuasive only. Only the ratio binds lower courts via stare decisis. Distinguishing the two is one of the most useful and difficult skills in legal analysis.

5
[2]
Match each canon of statutory interpretation with its Latin meaning: (a) ejusdem generis; (b) noscitur a sociis.
Answer Key

(a) ejusdem generis = "of the same kind" — a general word following a list is read in light of the class of the listed examples ("cars, motorcycles, and other vehicles" — likely means motorised conveyances). (b) noscitur a sociis = "known by its associates" — words take on meaning from surrounding words.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[6]
Constable Cho stops Marcus walking in a high-crime neighbourhood at 11 pm. There is no specific tip or suspicious behaviour. The officer pats Marcus down and finds a bag of cannabis.
Apply IRAC to this scenario. Identify the issue(s), the rule (with sections and a leading case), application, and conclusion.
Answer Key

Issue: Did the detention and pat-down of Marcus violate sections 8 and 9 of the Charter? Rule: Section 9 prohibits arbitrary detention; R v Mann [2004] 3 SCR 59 requires reasonable suspicion of connection to a particular crime under investigation. Section 8 prohibits unreasonable search; Mann further requires reasonable safety concerns to justify a pat-down during investigative detention. R v Le [2019] confirms that mere presence in a high-crime area is insufficient. Application: Constable Cho lacks specific suspicion (no tip, no observed behaviour); detention is arbitrary; pat-down has no safety basis (no observation of weapon or threat). Both ss. 9 and 8 breached. Conclusion: Charter breaches established; cannabis likely excluded under s. 24(2) Grant framework (seriousness of breach high; impact significant; offence minor). Marks: 1 issue, 2 rule (sections + Mann), 2 application, 1 conclusion.

7
[4]
A municipal regulation reads: "It is an offence to bring a 'pet' onto public transit, except for a service animal." Three people are charged: (a) someone with a small dog in a carrier; (b) someone carrying a snake; (c) someone with a goldfish in a sealed bag of water.
Apply Driedger's modern principle of statutory interpretation. For each of (a), (b), and (c), is the person guilty? Why?
Answer Key

Text: "pet" — ordinarily means a tame animal kept for companionship. Dictionaries support a broad reading. Context: the regulation has an exception for "service animal," suggesting "pet" is meant in a broad sense covering all non-working animals on transit. Purpose: likely safety, hygiene, comfort of other passengers. Application: (a) small dog in carrier — clearly a pet; the carrier mitigates safety but does not change the classification; likely guilty (no service-animal exception applies). (b) snake — falls within "pet" (a kept animal for companionship); may be guilty depending on transit's specific safety rules. (c) goldfish in sealed bag — arguably a "pet" by text but raises no safety/hygiene concern (purpose); a court might read down to exclude. The case shows how text, context, and purpose interact. Marks: 1 framework set up, 1 each application (a)(b)(c).

8
[3]
A trial-court judge in 2024 is bound by a 2018 Court of Appeal decision on a Charter-section 9 issue. Apply stare decisis. Could the trial judge depart from the 2018 decision? Under what circumstances?
Answer Key

Stare decisis requires the trial court to follow the Court of Appeal on the same point of law. The trial judge can: (1) distinguish — show that the present case differs materially in fact from the 2018 case; (2) note that the Supreme Court of Canada (which sits above the Court of Appeal) has subsequently overruled or modified the 2018 reasoning, in which case the SCC ruling controls; (3) follow under protest while noting disagreement (sometimes done to invite appellate reconsideration). The trial judge cannot simply ignore or reject the 2018 ruling on the same point. Marks: 1 binding statement, 1 distinguishing, 1 SCC overruling or subsequent change.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[8]
Write a complete case brief (300–500 words) of R v Oakes [1986] 1 SCR 103. Include all standard sections: citation, facts, issue, holding, ratio, significance.
Answer Key

Citation: R v Oakes, [1986] 1 SCR 103. Facts: David Oakes was charged under s. 4 of the Narcotic Control Act with possession of 8 vials of hash oil. Section 8 of the NCA reverse-onus: once Crown proves possession, accused must prove not for the purpose of trafficking. Issue: Is the reverse-onus provision a violation of s. 11(d) presumption of innocence; if so, is it justified under s. 1? Holding: Yes, s. 8 NCA violates s. 11(d); not justified under s. 1; struck down. Ratio: Section 1 limits are justified only where (1) prescribed by law, (2) pursue a pressing and substantial objective, (3) means rationally connected to objective, (4) minimal impairment, (5) salutary effects outweigh deleterious. Reverse-onus provisions presumptively violate s. 11(d) and must clear full Oakes analysis. Significance: the foundational s. 1 case; the test has been applied in nearly every Charter case since 1986; influenced proportionality doctrine in other common-law countries. Marks: 1 citation, 1 facts, 1 issue, 1 holding, 3 ratio (Oakes steps), 1 significance.

10
[4]
Write the opening 4–5 sentences of an oral moot submission for the Crown in an appeal where the lower court excluded evidence under s. 24(2) Charter following a search-warrant breach. Include a roadmap of two arguments.
Marking Rubric

Look for: (1) appellate register — "My Lords," "in my respectful submission"; (2) decline to recapitulate facts the bench has read; (3) statement of the precise legal issue (exclusion of evidence; standard of review); (4) explicit roadmap ("I will address two arguments today: first … second …"); (5) citation of leading authority (Grant, Vu, Hunter v Southam). Marks: 1 register, 1 roadmap, 1 issue, 1 authority.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
11
[6]
Yusra, a recent immigrant, works as a personal-support worker earning $34,000. She is wrongfully dismissed without notice or pay. She speaks limited English.
Apply access-to-justice options. List at least four pathways or institutions she could use, with a brief description of how each would help.
Answer Key

(1) Ontario Ministry of Labour — Employment Standards complaint for statutory notice and severance under the ESA (no-cost; 2-year limitation). (2) Community legal clinic — many specialise in employment matters for low-income clients; clinics often handle the case at no cost or refer to clinic with employment expertise. (3) Legal Aid Ontario — limited eligibility for employment matters but some certificates issued. (4) Small Claims Court (up to $35,000) — no lawyer required, simplified procedure; can hire paralegal for $1,000–$3,000. (5) Pro Bono Ontario — volunteer-lawyer advice and sometimes representation. (6) Interpretation services — most clinics and courts arrange interpretation. Marks: 4 pathways × 1.5 each = 6.

[6]
STSE / Real-World Application: Choose ONE current Canadian legal issue (e.g., bail reform, Indigenous-rights litigation, judicial appointments, court delays, AI in legal services, access to justice). In 6–8 sentences, identify the issue, frame two specific legal questions you would investigate using the legal-studies inquiry process, and identify one primary source and one secondary source you would consult.
Marking Rubric

Strong responses: (1) chosen issue is specific and current; (2) two legal questions are framed precisely (not "is this fair?" but e.g., "Does the 2019 bail-reform legislation comply with section 11(e) of the Charter?"); (3) one primary source identified by name (a statute, regulation, or case); (4) one secondary source identified (a textbook, journal article, government report, or reputable news investigation); (5) the concepts of legal thinking (significance/continuity/cause/perspective) implicitly inform the framing. Marks: 1 issue identification, 2 legal questions, 1 primary source, 1 secondary source, 1 communication.
