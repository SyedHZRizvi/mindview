Chapter 1: Heritage & Sources of Canadian Law — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand B
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify what you already know about Canada's legal heritage and where you need help before starting Chapter 1. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Be honest about what is unclear.
Question 1 Prereq · Concepts
[3 marks]
In your own words, define the "rule of law." Give one example of a situation in which the rule of law would be threatened.
Question 2 Prereq · Civics
[3 marks]
Canada has both a federal Parliament and provincial legislatures. Briefly explain why both levels of government can pass laws, and give one example of a matter handled by each.
Question 3 Prereq · Knowledge
[2 marks]
Distinguish "public law" from "private law" and give one example of each.
Question 4 Skills · Source
[3 marks]
"Glory be to God for dappled things — / For skies of couple-colour as a brinded cow." — (this is a poem, not a legal source)
Identify whether each of the following is a primary or secondary legal source: (a) the Criminal Code of Canada; (b) a Wikipedia article on the Charter; (c) a Supreme Court of Canada decision in R v Oakes.
Question 5 Reasoning
[3 marks]
If you were arrested by police, list three things you believe you have the right to do or be told. (We will verify which are accurate in Chapter 2.)
Question 6 Knowledge
[2 marks]
Name one Canadian Supreme Court case you have heard of (any), and in one sentence say what it was about.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which Chapter 1 topic do you feel least confident about: the rule of law, ancient legal traditions, Indigenous legal traditions, the Constitution Acts, common-law vs statute law, or court structure? Explain what specifically is unclear.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
