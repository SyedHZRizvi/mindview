Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
Define the two essential elements of a crime — actus reus and mens rea — and identify the four common forms of mens rea.
Answer Key

Actus reus = the guilty act; the prohibited physical conduct, including conduct, circumstances, and consequences; must be voluntary. Mens rea = the guilty mind; the mental element at the time of the act. Four common forms: intention (purpose to bring about the consequence); knowledge (awareness of a circumstance); recklessness (awareness of a risk + proceeding); wilful blindness (deliberate failure to inquire); (also acceptable: criminal negligence — marked departure from the standard of a reasonable person).

2
[3]
Distinguish (a) summary-conviction offences, (b) indictable offences, and (c) hybrid offences. Include limitation period and trial options.
Answer Key

(a) Summary-conviction: least serious; OCJ judge alone; max $5,000/2 years less a day (default); 12-month limitation. (b) Indictable: most serious; no limitation period; possible jury trial under s. 11(f) Charter where 5-year max or more; some are s. 469 SCJ-exclusive (murder), some s. 553 OCJ-exclusive, most middle category with accused's election (s. 536(2)). (c) Hybrid (dual-procedure): Crown elects summary or indictable; ~85% of Criminal Code charges; deemed indictable until election for some procedural purposes.

3
[3]
Identify three purposes of sentencing under section 718 of the Criminal Code and define each.
Answer Key

Any three of: denunciation (society's condemnation of the conduct); deterrence (general — others; specific — this offender); rehabilitation (correction of the offender); separation from society (incapacitation through imprisonment); reparation (restitution to victims and community); promotion of responsibility (offender acknowledging harm).

4
[2]
Section 10(b) of the Charter, applied in the police context, requires that on arrest or detention the police must:
Answer Key

B. The informational and implementational duties from Manninen [1987] and Bartle [1994] — inform, give a reasonable opportunity, and cease questioning until the detainee has had that opportunity.

5
[2]
The Youth Criminal Justice Act applies to persons who allegedly committed an offence at age:
Answer Key

B. Ages 12–17 inclusive. Children under 12 are not criminally responsible under s. 13 Criminal Code. Adults aged 18+ are subject to ordinary procedure.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]
Hassan, age 22, picks up a baseball bat in anger during an argument and strikes Lin, who later dies of the head injury. The Crown charges Hassan with murder under s. 229(a).
Analyse what the Crown must prove for each branch of s. 229(a) (paragraphs (i) and (ii)) and explain how a finding could reduce the charge to manslaughter.
Answer Key

Section 229(a)(i): Crown must prove Hassan meant to cause Lin's death — subjective intent to kill. Section 229(a)(ii): if subjective intent to kill cannot be proved, the Crown can show Hassan meant to cause bodily harm that he knew was likely to cause death and was reckless whether death ensued. Both branches require subjective foresight (R v Vaillancourt [1987]). If the Crown proves neither — for example, Hassan only intended a non-fatal injury — the conviction reduces to manslaughter (s. 234), still a culpable homicide but without the subjective foresight murder requires. Marks: 1 actus reus, 2 mens rea branches (i)/(ii), 1 manslaughter reduction, 1 mention of constitutional subjective-intent rule.

7
[5]
Lia, 24, has no prior record. She is convicted of break-and-enter into a dwelling (s. 348 Criminal Code). She has begun voluntary opioid-addiction treatment. The victim was not home but reports continuing anxiety. The Crown seeks 18 months of imprisonment.
Identify the sentencing principles the judge must apply and analyse what sentence is most consistent with s. 718, 718.1, and 718.2 of the Criminal Code.
Answer Key

s. 718 purposes: denunciation (yes — home invasion); general deterrence (yes); specific deterrence (somewhat addressed by treatment); rehabilitation (strongly weighted — young, no record, voluntary treatment); reparation (restitution possible). s. 718.1 proportionality: gravity is high (dwelling-house entry); blameworthiness is moderated by addiction context and treatment efforts. s. 718.2: aggravating — dwelling, anxiety; mitigating — no record, treatment, age. s. 718.2(d)(e) restraint: consider non-imprisonment options. Likely sentence: conditional sentence under s. 742.1 (where statutorily eligible) of 12–18 months with strict conditions (curfew, continued treatment, no-contact, community service) + 2 years probation + restitution. The 18-month custodial proposal would not respect restraint and the rehabilitation purpose. Marks: 2 principles identified, 2 application, 1 specific outcome with reasoning.

8
[3]
A 15-year-old is alleged to have stolen a $300 phone. Police can use which of the following first, before laying a charge?
Answer Key

Under YCJA ss. 6–10, police must consider extrajudicial measures first for non-violent offences. Options include: no further action; informal warning; police caution; referral to a community program. If a charge is later laid, the Crown can offer extrajudicial sanctions (s. 10) before going to court — a more formal program (community service, apology, workshop) that resolves the matter without a finding of guilt. Marks: 1 for warning/caution/referral concept, 1 for extrajudicial sanctions concept, 1 for the principle that custody is presumptively unavailable for first-time non-violent property offences (s. 39).

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[6]
Write a brief case note (200–300 words) on R v Jordan [2016] 1 SCR 631, including: citation, facts, issue, ratio, and significance.
Answer Key

Citation: R v Jordan, 2016 SCC 27, [2016] 1 SCR 631. Facts: Mr. Jordan was charged with drug-trafficking offences; his trial took over 49 months to conclude — the delay was attributed largely to Crown and institutional delay. Issue: when does delay between charge and trial violate section 11(b) of the Charter (right to be tried within a reasonable time)? Holding/Ratio: The Court created presumptive ceilings — 18 months from charge to end of trial in provincial court, 30 months in superior court. Time attributed to defence delay is subtracted. Cases exceeding the ceiling are presumptively unreasonable; the Crown can justify by showing exceptional circumstances. Significance: Jordan transformed Canadian criminal-court administration. It triggered the dismissal of dozens of cases for delay, prompted Crown and police to triage caseloads, and motivated significant federal/provincial reform investment in court resources. Marks: 1 citation, 1 facts, 1 issue, 2 ratio, 1 significance.

10
[6]
In plain language (200 words) for a friend who has never studied law, explain the difference between "guilty" and "not criminally responsible on account of mental disorder" (NCR).
Answer Key

Look for: (1) "guilty" = the Crown proved every element beyond a reasonable doubt and the defence did not succeed; conviction follows + sentence. (2) "NCR" under s. 16 = the accused was suffering from a "disease of the mind" at the time of the offence and was incapable of (a) appreciating the nature/quality of the act, OR (b) knowing it was wrong. (3) NCR is NOT an acquittal in the ordinary sense — the accused is referred to a Review Board under Part XX.1 that decides on assessment, conditional discharge, or detention in a forensic facility. (4) The aim is treatment and public safety, not punishment. (5) "Not guilty" (without NCR) means the Crown failed; the accused is released without restriction. Marks: 1 guilty definition, 2 NCR test (s. 16), 2 NCR consequences (Review Board, treatment focus), 1 plain-language clarity.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
11
[6]
Officer Cho stops Marcus at 11 pm in a high-crime neighbourhood. There is no specific tip about a crime in progress. The officer pats Marcus down and finds a bag of cannabis. Marcus is charged.
Apply the Charter using IRAC. Issue, rule (sections 8 and 9 + Mann [2004]), application, conclusion. What remedy is likely under s. 24(2) Grant?
Answer Key

Issue: Did the detention and search of Marcus violate sections 9 and 8 of the Charter? Rule: Section 9 prohibits arbitrary detention; R v Mann [2004] 3 SCR 59 requires reasonable suspicion of connection to a particular crime. Section 8 prohibits unreasonable search; Mann further requires reasonable safety concerns to justify a pat-down during investigative detention. Application: mere presence in a high-crime area is insufficient for reasonable suspicion (R v Le [2019]). No specific suspicion of Marcus + no safety threat = both ss. 9 and 8 breached. s. 24(2) Grant: seriousness of breach (high — no basis at all), impact on accused (significant — pat-down is intrusive), public interest (modest — minor offence). Likely exclusion. Conclusion: Charter breaches established, cannabis excluded, Crown's case likely collapses. Marks: 1 issue, 1 rule, 2 application, 1 s. 24(2), 1 conclusion.

12
[6]
STSE / Real-World Application: The Office of the Correctional Investigator reports that Indigenous people make up roughly 5% of Canada's adult population but 32%+ of the federal-inmate population. In 5–7 sentences, explain how R v Gladue [1999] and R v Ipeelee [2012] respond to this fact. Identify one criticism of these decisions.
Answer Key

Strong responses: Gladue interpreted s. 718.2(e) Criminal Code — sentencing judges must consider (a) unique systemic/background factors affecting Indigenous offenders (residential schools, intergenerational trauma, dislocation), and (b) sanctions consistent with the offender's heritage/community. Ipeelee [2012] reaffirmed and strengthened these principles, holding that Gladue factors apply at every sentencing, including serious offences, and that "Gladue reports" should inform the analysis. Criticisms: (a) Gladue has not significantly reduced the over-incarceration rate; (b) implementation is uneven across courts and judges; (c) reports are under-funded; (d) the principles can be undermined by mandatory-minimum sentences that strip judicial discretion. Marks: 2 Gladue mechanism, 2 Ipeelee reaffirmation, 1 criticism, 1 communication.
