Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
List the four elements of a negligence claim and define each.
Answer Key

(1) Duty of care — a legally recognised relationship that obliges reasonable care (Anns/Cooper framework: recognised category OR foreseeability + proximity − policy concerns). (2) Breach of standard of care — defendant fell short of what a reasonable person in the circumstances would have done. (3) Causation — but for the breach, the harm would not have occurred (Clements). (4) Damages not too remote — actual compensable loss that was foreseeable (Wagon Mound).

2
[3]
Define each contract element: offer, acceptance, consideration.
Answer Key

Offer = a clear proposal capable of acceptance — a definite indication of willingness to be bound on stated terms (distinct from an invitation to treat). Acceptance = unconditional agreement to the terms of the offer, communicated to the offeror; a counter-offer destroys the original offer (Hyde v Wrench). Consideration = what each party gives — the price of the promise; can be modest ("peppercorn") but must have some value; bare promises are not enforceable absent consideration (with limited equitable exceptions).

3
[3]
Compare and contrast three forms of Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR): negotiation, mediation, arbitration. Address: third party? binding outcome?
Answer Key

Negotiation: direct or lawyer-assisted bargaining; no third party; parties retain full control. Mediation: neutral mediator facilitates discussion; does not decide; outcome is voluntary; agreement is binding once reached. Arbitration: neutral arbitrator hears evidence and issues a binding award; private; reviewable only on narrow grounds (procedural fairness, jurisdiction).

4
[2]
Section 43 of Ontario's Consumer Protection Act, 2002 provides what right for "direct agreements"?
Answer Key

B. Section 43 provides a 10-day cooling-off period for direct agreements (consumer contracts entered outside the supplier's place of business — typically door-to-door). The consumer can cancel without reason within that period; any deposit or payment is refunded within 15 days (s. 96).

5
[2]
Under the Ontario Family Law Act, the calculation of "net family property" excludes which of the following from a spouse's NFP?
Answer Key

B. Pre-marriage assets can generally be deducted, EXCEPT the matrimonial home. The matrimonial home is uniquely treated — its full value at separation enters the NFP regardless of pre-marriage ownership. Other excluded property includes gifts and inheritances received during marriage (if traceable), certain insurance proceeds.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]
On a snowy December afternoon, Priya enters a grocery store. The entrance mat is saturated and water has pooled. She slips, falls, and fractures her wrist. The store has not put up warning signs.
Apply the negligence framework. Identify the duty source, the standard of care, the cause-and-effect analysis, the damages, and any defence the store might raise.
Answer Key

Duty: Ontario Occupiers' Liability Act, s. 3(1) — occupiers owe a duty to take such care as is reasonable in the circumstances to ensure persons entering are reasonably safe. Standard of care: what a reasonable grocery-store occupier would do on a snowy day — replace mats, post warning signs, monitor pooling. The store has not done these. Causation: but for the saturated mat and pooled water, would Priya have fallen? On a balance of probabilities, no. Damages: medical costs, lost wages, pain and suffering — all foreseeable from a slip. Defences: contributory negligence under the Negligence Act (was Priya distracted, wearing inappropriate footwear?) reduces but does not eliminate recovery. Marks: 1 duty source, 1 standard, 1 causation, 1 damages, 1 defence.

7
[5]
Maya posts a used bike for sale on Facebook Marketplace: "Trek hybrid, $400 OBO." Sam messages "I'll take it for $400, pick up tomorrow." Maya replies "Great, see you at 2." Sam arrives, but Maya has sold the bike to Jordan for $450.
Was there a contract? Apply the five elements. What remedy does Sam have?
Answer Key

Was there a contract? (1) Offer: Maya's post is an invitation to treat. Sam's "I'll take it for $400" is the offer. (2) Acceptance: Maya's "Great, see you at 2" is acceptance. (3) Consideration: $400 vs the bike — both sides give value. (4) Capacity: both adults. (5) Intention: commercial-like context, presumption of intention. (6) Form: no writing requirement for a chattel sale; messages constitute a written record. A contract was formed. Remedy: Sam can sue for damages — the difference between the contract price and what he had to pay for a comparable substitute. Specific performance is unavailable for non-unique chattels. He would file in Small Claims Court. Marks: 5 elements × 1 each = 5.

8
[3]
Aleksey's net family property at separation is $410,000. Petra's is $350,000. Calculate the equalisation payment under the Ontario Family Law Act. Show the calculation.
Answer Key

Difference: $410,000 − $350,000 = $60,000. Equalisation payment: Aleksey pays Petra half the difference = $30,000. After payment, each spouse holds $380,000 of marital wealth — the FLA principle that the increase in wealth during marriage is shared. Marks: 1 calculation, 1 correct amount, 1 explanation.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[6]
Write a brief plain-language guide (250–400 words) for a young person being wrongfully dismissed from a job. Cover: their ESA rights, common-law reasonable notice, the duty to mitigate, and one practical step they should take.
Marking Rubric

Look for: ESA rights — minimum notice (1 week per year up to 8 weeks under s. 57) + possible severance (large employer + 5+ years under s. 64). Common-law reasonable notice — Bardal factors (age, length of service, character of employment, availability of similar work); typically larger than ESA minimum. Duty to mitigate — must seek comparable work; earnings reduce damages. Practical step — write down employment history, save the termination letter, consult an employment lawyer (many do free initial consults) or a community legal clinic. Marks: 1 each of ESA, common-law, mitigation, practical step + 2 conventions and audience-appropriate register.

10
[6]
Write a one-paragraph (5–8 sentences) explanation of Donoghue v Stevenson [1932], including the facts, the issue, the holding, and why the case still matters in Canadian tort law.
Marking Rubric

Look for: (1) facts — Mrs. Donoghue's friend bought a ginger beer in Paisley; she drank some; the remains of a decomposed snail emerged; no contract between her and the manufacturer. (2) Issue — does the manufacturer owe a duty of care to the ultimate consumer in the absence of a contractual relationship? (3) Holding — yes; Lord Atkin's "neighbour principle." (4) Significance — foundation of modern negligence law; adopted into Canadian common law; remains the starting point for product-liability and personal-injury cases. Marks: 1 facts, 1 issue, 2 holding/neighbour principle, 2 significance and writing.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
11
[6]
A door-to-door salesperson signs Tomas, age 22, to a 36-month rental contract on a water-heater system. The monthly payment is $89; the cancellation fee is "the balance of payments." Three days later, Tomas realises a comparable unit could be bought outright for $300.
Apply Ontario consumer-protection law. Identify the type of agreement, the applicable cooling-off period, the procedure for cancelling, and the refund treatment.
Answer Key

Type: direct agreement under s. 20 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2002 (entered at Tomas's apartment, not the supplier's place of business). Cooling-off: s. 43 — 10 days from receipt of the written copy. Tomas at day 3 is well within. Procedure: s. 92 — written notice delivered in any reasonable way (email, letter). Effect: contract terminated; s. 96 — any deposit/payment refunded within 15 days. Tomas owes nothing. If past 10 days: would need to argue unfair practices (s. 14 — misleading/unconscionable representations) or unconscionability — much higher burden. Marks: 1 type, 1 cooling-off, 1 procedure, 1 refund, 1 alternative-route if past period, 1 statutory citation accuracy.

12
[6]
STSE / Real-World Application: In 6–8 sentences, explain why most civil cases settle without going to trial. Identify (a) two practical reasons; (b) one risk of the settlement system from an access-to-justice perspective; (c) how mandatory mediation (e.g., Rule 76 in Toronto, Ottawa, Windsor) tries to address this.
Answer Key

Strong responses: (a) practical reasons — cost (legal fees + court costs; cost-shifting rules); time (years to trial); uncertainty (outcome at trial is unpredictable); privacy (settlements are private; trials are public); preservation of relationships (especially in family/employment contexts). (b) Access risk — power and resource imbalances may push the weaker party to settle on disadvantageous terms; private settlements do not create precedent that benefits other litigants in similar situations. (c) Mandatory mediation introduces a structured early opportunity to settle with a trained mediator, often within months of the action being commenced, before extensive legal costs accumulate; settled cases in Rule 76 mediation typically resolve in roughly half the time and cost of contested litigation. Marks: 2 reasons, 2 risk, 2 mediation mechanism.
