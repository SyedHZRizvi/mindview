Topic 3.1–3.2 — Elements & Categories
Question 1
The Latin term mens rea refers to:
Solution: Mens rea = the mental element required for the offence — intention, knowledge, recklessness, wilful blindness, or criminal negligence depending on the section. Distinguish it from motive (the reason for the act, generally not required) and actus reus (the physical act).
Question 2
A "hybrid" (dual-procedure) offence means:
Solution: A hybrid offence is "punishable by way of indictment or summary conviction" — the Crown decides which procedure to use based on seriousness, the accused's record, and other factors. ~85% of Criminal Code charges are hybrid.
Question 3
For summary-conviction offences (default), the limitation period for laying the information is:
Solution: Section 786(2) of the Criminal Code provides a 12-month limitation period from the date of the offence for laying summary-conviction information (unless otherwise specified). Indictable offences have no general limitation period.
Topic 3.3 — Trial Process
Question 4
In R v Stinchcombe [1991], the Supreme Court held that the Crown must disclose:
Solution: Stinchcombe established that the Crown has a constitutional obligation under s. 7 to disclose all relevant information — both helpful and harmful to the accused — to the defence before plea. Failure can produce a stay of proceedings.
Question 5
Under R v Antic [2017], the "ladder principle" at a bail hearing means:
Solution: The ladder principle directs justices to start with the least restrictive release (undertaking without conditions), then move up the ladder (with conditions, with surety, with deposit) only if necessary. Detention is the last rung and requires real justification under the primary, secondary, or tertiary grounds.
Topic 3.4–3.6 — Defences, Sentencing, YCJA
Question 6
A successful section 16 (mental disorder) defence results in a verdict of:
Solution: Section 16 leads to an NCR verdict (not a standard acquittal). The accused is then subject to disposition by the Review Board under Part XX.1 of the Criminal Code — assessment, treatment, and possible conditional discharge or detention in a forensic facility. NCR is a separate civil-style regime aimed at treatment and public safety.
Question 7
The fundamental principle of sentencing under s. 718.1 of the Criminal Code is:
Solution: Section 718.1 is the fundamental principle: proportionality. Sentences must reflect both the gravity of the offence (objective wrong) and the offender's degree of responsibility (moral blameworthiness). Section 718 lists multiple purposes (denunciation, deterrence, rehabilitation, etc.) that are weighed in light of the proportionality principle.
Question 8
The Youth Criminal Justice Act applies to persons who are alleged to have committed an offence when they were aged:
Solution: The YCJA applies to youth aged 12 to 17 inclusive. Children under 12 are not criminally responsible under federal law (s. 13 Criminal Code). Once a person turns 18, they are subject to adult criminal procedure.
Question 9
Sahar hears noises at her car at night. She walks outside with a baseball bat. A man steps toward her with a knife. She swings and hits his head.
Which defence is most directly engaged?
Solution: Section 34 applies: (a) reasonable belief in force being used or threatened, (b) defensive purpose, (c) reasonable in the circumstances (9-factor list under s. 34(2)). The Khill 2021 decision clarified the "role in the incident" factor — courts look at whether the accused's earlier conduct (e.g., approaching the threat) was itself reasonable.
Question 10
In R v Gladue [1999], the Supreme Court interpreted section 718.2(e) to require sentencing judges to:
Solution: Gladue (and reaffirmed in Ipeelee [2012]) requires consideration of (a) unique systemic and background factors affecting Indigenous people (residential schools, intergenerational trauma, dislocation) and (b) sanctions consistent with the offender's heritage — a corrective for systemic over-incarceration (Indigenous people are ~5% of the adult population but 32%+ of federal inmates).
