Unit 5: Historical Inquiry & Skills — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand A
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify which historical-thinking skills you have already practiced in Units 1–4 and which need explicit attention. Receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Inquiry · Questions
[3 marks]
Turn this closed yes/no question into an open historical-inquiry question: "Did Pierre Trudeau handle the FLQ Crisis correctly?" Write a defensible, open-ended version (2–3 sentences explaining your rewording).
Question 2 Sources · Classification
[3 marks]
Classify each as primary, secondary, or tertiary, and briefly justify:
(a) Sir Robert Borden's 1917 House of Commons speech announcing conscription.
(b) Pierre Berton's 1986 book Vimy.
(c) The Wikipedia article on the Halifax Explosion.
Question 3 Concepts · Six Big
[3 marks]
The 2018 federal apology for the MS St. Louis refusal of 1939.
Identify which of the six historical thinking concepts is most directly engaged by this event (e.g., when historians or policymakers think about it). Justify your choice in 2 sentences.
Question 4 Source Analysis
[3 marks]
A 1942 Wartime Information Board poster shows a woman in coveralls operating a lathe in a Canadian munitions factory. Caption: "Women — Strike a Blow for Victory!"
Apply OPVL (Origin, Purpose, Value, Limitations) to this source in 4 sentences.
Question 5 Media Literacy
[3 marks]
A friend sends you a viral TikTok claiming "Canada had secret involvement in the 2003 Iraq invasion that the government hid." Apply Caulfield's SIFT method: in 3–4 sentences, explain what you would do to evaluate the claim.
Question 6 Citation
[2 marks]
Identify what's missing from this citation: "Borden, 1917." In 2 sentences, list what a complete citation should include.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Of the six historical thinking concepts (significance, cause-and-consequence, continuity-and-change, historical perspective, evidence, ethical dimensions), which do you find most difficult to apply? Explain.

Teacher Feedback
