Unit 2: Canada 1929–1945 — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand C
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps from Unit 1 and assess prior knowledge of the Depression and WWII before starting Unit 2. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Prereq · Unit 1
[3 marks]
From Unit 1, briefly explain: (a) what was the Statute of Westminster (1931)? (b) Why does it matter for Canada's role in 1939?
Question 2 Prior Knowledge
[3 marks]
What do you already know about the Great Depression? In 2–3 sentences, describe what comes to mind. (You will compare this with what you learn in Unit 2.)
Question 3 Prior Knowledge
[3 marks]
"I should have liked to find a man of pacific manner and outlook." — Mackenzie King on Hitler, after their June 1937 meeting in Berlin.
What do you already know about Hitler, Nazism, and the rise of WWII? List 2–3 facts.
Question 4 Skills · Source Reading
[3 marks]
A Canadian Wartime Information Board poster from 1942 shows a woman working at a lathe in a munitions factory, with the caption: "We Can Do It!"
In 3 sentences: (a) what does the poster claim/show? (b) what does it want viewers to feel? (c) what historical reality is it both reflecting and obscuring?
Question 5 Prior Knowledge
[3 marks]
What do you already know about D-Day and the Holocaust? Identify 2 facts about each.
Question 6 Reflection
[2 marks]
Which of these Unit 2 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) the Depression; (b) Canada's road to war and the 1939 declaration; (c) Dieppe, Italy, D-Day, and the Netherlands liberation; (d) the home front; (e) Japanese-Canadian internment; (f) the Holocaust? Explain what specifically is unclear.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Unit 2 contains difficult content (Holocaust, internment, antisemitism, residential schools era beginning). What do you need from your teacher or classmates to engage this material respectfully and well?

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
