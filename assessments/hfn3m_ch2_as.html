Topic 2.1–2.3 — GI Tract & Digestion
Question 1
Which organ produces bile?
Solution: Bile is produced in the liver and stored/concentrated in the gallbladder. It is released into the duodenum to emulsify dietary fat.
Question 2
Where does most absorption of nutrients occur?
Solution: The small intestine — with its villi, microvilli, and ~250 m² surface area — absorbs ≈95% of all nutrients. The large intestine mostly absorbs water.
Question 3
Pepsin functions best at approximately what pH?
Solution: Pepsin is active in the highly acidic stomach environment, pH ≈ 2. It becomes inactive when chyme is neutralised by pancreatic bicarbonate in the duodenum.
Question 4
Which structure increases the absorptive surface area of the small intestine the most?
Solution: The brush border of microvilli on each villus produces the largest single multiplier — together with villi and circular folds, the small intestine reaches ~250 m².
Topic 2.4–2.5 — Colon, Microbiome, Cellular Respiration
Question 5
Approximately how many bacterial cells are in an adult human colon?
Solution: The adult colon hosts ~\(10^{14}\) bacterial cells — comparable in number to total human cells in the body.
Question 6
The colon ferments fibre into short-chain fatty acids. Which SCFA is the primary fuel for colonocytes?
Solution: Butyrate is the preferred fuel of colon epithelial cells (colonocytes). Acetate and propionate are absorbed and used by the liver and peripheral tissues.
Question 7
Approximately how many ATP are produced from the complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose molecule (modern estimate)?
Solution: Modern textbooks give ≈ 30 ATP per glucose (older texts say 36–38). The contribution: glycolysis ~2 ATP, citric-acid cycle ~2 ATP, electron transport ~26 ATP.
Question 8
Where does the citric-acid (Krebs) cycle occur?
Solution: Glycolysis is in the cytoplasm; pyruvate oxidation + Krebs cycle in the mitochondrial matrix; the electron transport chain on the inner mitochondrial membrane.
Topic 2.6 — Energy Balance & BMR
Question 9
Using Mifflin–St Jeor for a 65 kg, 170 cm, 17-year-old female, calculate BMR (kcal/day).
Solution: Female BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161 = 10(65) + 6.25(170) − 5(17) − 161 = 650 + 1062.5 − 85 − 161 ≈ 1 466.5 kcal/day ≈ 1 467.
Question 10
The thermic effect of food (TEF) is approximately what fraction of a mixed-diet energy intake?
Solution: ~10% for a mixed diet. Protein has a much higher TEF (~25%) than carbohydrate or fat (~5%).
Question 11
A 60 kg sedentary woman has BMR ≈ 1 300 kcal. Using activity factor 1.2, what is her TDEE?
Solution: TDEE = BMR × activity factor = 1 300 × 1.2 = 1 560 kcal/day. This is her maintenance level.
