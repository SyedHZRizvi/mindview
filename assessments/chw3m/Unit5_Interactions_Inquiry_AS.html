Topic 5.1 — Silk Roads & Indian Ocean
Question 1
The Silk Roads are best described as:
Solution: The term was coined by von Richthofen in 1877. "Silk Road" names a network, not a road; most caravans covered only 200–500 km before unloading at a market town, where local merchants forwarded the cargo.
Question 2
Zheng He's seven Ming-era voyages (1405–33) were significant because:
Solution: Zheng He's fleets reached Calicut, Hormuz, Aden, Mogadishu, and Kilwa; were among the largest naval forces of the era; and were then voluntarily abandoned by the Ming after 1433, creating the geopolitical vacuum the Portuguese filled from 1498.
Topic 5.2 — 1492 in Global Perspective
Question 3
In 1492, while Columbus was crossing the Atlantic, the Ming Dynasty in China was ruled by:
Solution: Hongzhi (r. 1487–1505) ruled Ming China in 1492. For most of the world's population, 1492 was an unremarkable year — the eventual significance of the Atlantic crossing was unknowable in real time.
Question 4
The "Columbian Exchange" refers to:
Solution: The Columbian Exchange transformed agriculture on both sides (maize/potatoes east; wheat/cattle west) and brought catastrophic disease to the Americas. A population of perhaps 50–60 million dropped to 5–10 million within a century.
Question 5
A rigorous Grade 11 assessment of the historical significance of 1492 would:
Solution: Historical significance is constructed by retrospective evaluation. 1492 is significant because of what followed; reading it as the date when "the medieval world ended" is a Eurocentric framing.
Topic 5.3 — Historical Inquiry
Question 6
A strong historical research question is:
Solution: Researchable + debatable + significant = a strong inquiry question. Weak questions are too broad, already settled, or unanswerable from accessible sources.
Question 7
A primary historical source is:
Solution: Primary sources are period material. Secondary sources are later analytical works. Both are essential; primary sources give evidentiary grounding, secondary sources situate the inquiry in the scholarly conversation.
Topic 5.4 — Historiography
Question 8
Source-criticism — examining who produced a source, for whom, with what knowledge and agenda — is:
Solution: No source is uncritically usable. Source-criticism asks of every document the same questions about authorship, context, audience, purpose, limits — enabling productive use despite inevitable bias.
Question 9
"Multiple perspectives" in historical writing does NOT mean:
Solution: Multiple perspectives enrich historical understanding; they do not relieve historians of empirical accountability. Holocaust denial, climate-change denial, and slavery-apologism fail not because they are unconventional but because they fail on the evidence.
Question 10
A strong culminating-inquiry thesis on a CHW3M topic should be:
Solution: A Level 4 thesis is debatable, specific, supportable, and historiographically engaged. "The Mongols were bad" is a stance, not a thesis. "Comparing Marco Polo, Rashid al-Din, and the Mongol Secret History reveals divergent contemporary assessments of Pax Mongolica's enrichment vs commerce" is a thesis.
