Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [14 marks]
1
[3]
Identify the Five Pillars of Islam, briefly describing each.
Answer Key

(1) Shahada — declaration of faith ("There is no god but God, and Muhammad is his messenger"). (2) Salat — prayer five times daily. (3) Zakat — almsgiving (c. 2.5% of wealth annually). (4) Sawm — daytime fasting during Ramadan. (5) Hajj — pilgrimage to Mecca, once in a lifetime if able. Marks: 0.5 each + 0.5 framing.

2
[2]
The word "algebra" derives from:
Answer Key

B. Al-Khwarizmi's c. 825 al-jabr book founded algebra. His Latinised name "Algorithmus" gave us "algorithm."

3
[3]
Identify three Song-era Chinese innovations and briefly explain each: (a) movable type, (b) the magnetic compass for navigation, (c) paper money.
Answer Key

(a) Movable type — invented by Bi Sheng c. 1040 CE using ceramic individual characters; four centuries before Gutenberg's independent European version. (b) Magnetic compass — used for navigation in China by c. 1100; transmitted to the wider world via Indian Ocean and Mongol routes. (c) Paper money — jiaozi, issued under government monopoly from c. 1023; world's first state-issued paper currency. Marks: 1 each.

4
[2]
The Mongol Empire at its maximum (c. 1259) covered approximately:
Answer Key

B. The largest contiguous land empire in history — c. 24 million sq km, perhaps 100 million subjects.

5
[2]
Identify Mansa Musa I and his contribution to medieval West African history.
Answer Key

Mali emperor (r. c. 1312–37), most famous for his 1324–25 pilgrimage to Mecca with 60,000 attendants and 12,000 slaves carrying gold; gave away so much gold in Cairo that he depressed the Egyptian gold market for over a decade. His reign demonstrated Mali's wealth and integration into trans-Saharan and Islamic networks; the 1375 Catalan Atlas of Europe depicted him as the wealthiest king in the world. Marks: 1 identification, 1 significance.

6
[2]
The Aztec / Mexica capital Tenochtitlán in 1519 had population:
Answer Key

B. Tenochtitlán had c. 200,000–250,000 inhabitants when Cortés arrived — larger than London or Paris of the same period.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
7
[4]
"This Negro lord is called Musse Melly, lord of the Negroes of Guinea. So abundant is the gold which is found in his country that he is the richest and most noble king in all the land." — Catalan Atlas, 1375
Apply SOURCING and SIGNIFICANCE. (a) Who produced this source, when, and for what audience? (b) What does its existence tell us about 14th-century European knowledge of West Africa? (c) What does it complicate about the later "Dark Africa" narrative?
Answer Key

(a) The atlas was drawn by Abraham Cresques in Majorca in 1375, commissioned for King Charles V of France; Cresques drew on Genoese merchant reports and North African intermediaries. (b) Fourteenth-century European elites knew about West African political and economic power — they could place Mansa Musa on a state-of-the-art map. (c) The later "Dark Africa" narrative was constructed retrospectively, partly to justify the Atlantic slave trade and colonial conquest. Medieval Europeans did NOT think of Africa as isolated or unknown — the framing of Africa as primitive came centuries later. Marks: 1 sourcing, 1 medieval awareness, 2 historiographic critique.

8
[4]
Why did Song China (c. 1100) NOT industrialise, despite having gunpowder, coal-burning iron, compass, mass printing, and large-scale water mills? Identify THREE structural reasons.
Answer Key

(1) The Mongol conquests (1234 north, 1279 south) devastated population and infrastructure; recovery under Ming never reached Song innovation density. (2) Confucian-bureaucratic culture treated trade as morally low; successful merchants converted wealth into land and degrees rather than productive investment. (3) Resource geography — Chinese coal was poorly located relative to population centres (vs British coal beneath Manchester and Leeds). (4) Wage structure — abundant labour meant mechanisation offered less return than in high-wage Britain. (5) Political fragmentation absence — China was unified; European inter-state competition forced governments to fund military / exploratory innovation. The lesson: technological capacity does not automatically translate into industrial transformation. Marks: 1 each (any 3) + 1 synthesis.

9
[4]
Why did the Spanish military victory over the Aztec (1519–21) and Inca (1532–33) happen so quickly? Identify FOUR distinct factors.
Answer Key

(1) Disease — smallpox and other Eurasian pathogens preceded and followed the conquistadors, killing perhaps 50–90% of native populations. (2) Steel weapons, crossbows, horses, gunpowder gave tactical edge in specific battles. (3) Alliance with local enemies of the central empire — Cortés allied with Tlaxcalans against the Mexica; Pizarro exploited the Inca civil war between Atahualpa and Huáscar. (4) Catastrophic timing — the Inca had been weakened by a civil war that smallpox-deaths-of-elites had partly caused. (5) Surprise — neither empire knew oceanic invaders existed before they arrived. The verdict: the conquistadors won battles; the microbes won the war. Marks: 1 each.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
10
[4]
In one well-developed paragraph (5–7 sentences), explain why the Pax Mongolica (c. 1250–1350) is best understood as both/and rather than either/or — both catastrophe and creative exchange.
Answer Key

The Mongol conquests cost millions of lives — Baghdad 1258, Khwarezm, Bukhara, Samarkand all suffered massive destruction. Yet the empire that emerged in the next generation was unusually open, religiously pluralistic, and integrated. The Pax Mongolica made overland travel from China to Europe safe for the first and only time in pre-modern history; Marco Polo's 1271–95 stay, the missions of John of Plano Carpini and William of Rubruck, and the diffusion of printing, gunpowder, and the compass westward all date to this period. The Black Death itself probably travelled west along Mongol routes from a Central Asian outbreak — accelerating both creative exchange and devastating epidemic. Strong historical writing holds the catastrophe AND the exchange in view simultaneously, refusing the binary that asks "were the Mongols good or bad?" Marks: 1 catastrophe, 2 exchange, 1 synthesis / conventions.

11
[4]
Describe the Chinese civil-service examination (keju) system under the Song. Why was it institutionally remarkable for its time, and what does its eventual influence on French and British civil services tell us about cross-cultural transmission?
Answer Key

The keju recruited officials through competitive written exams based on Confucian classics. By the late Song, c. 400,000 candidates a year sat prefectural exams; c. 200 a year passed the metropolitan exam and entered government. The system was not democratic (only the wealthy could afford preparation; women excluded) but it broke hereditary monopolies and produced a learned class loyal to the imperial state, not to clan or region — institutionally unprecedented in its era. No European state had a comparable institution before the 19th century. The 19th-century French concours and British civil-service reform (Northcote-Trevelyan Report 1854) were explicitly modelled on the Chinese system — a notable case of West borrowing from East. Marks: 1 description, 1 institutional novelty, 1 modern transmission, 1 conventions.

12
[4]
Explain the demographic collapse known as the "Great Dying" of the Americas after 1492. Aim for 5–7 sentences and address both quantitative scale and historical-thinking significance.
Answer Key

Pre-contact American populations are estimated by current consensus at 50–60 million (some estimates higher). After 1492, smallpox, influenza, measles, and typhus swept populations with no prior exposure: perhaps 30% mortality from a single smallpox wave, then 30% from the next a generation later, then more. Combined with violent conquest, forced labour, food-system disruption, and forced relocation, total population may have fallen by 80–90% in some regions by 1600 — an estimated 50 million American deaths. This is the largest demographic disaster in human history. The historical-thinking implication: any narrative of the European "discovery" of the Americas that fails to name the Great Dying is an evasion — Strand A's ethical-dimension concept requires explicit acknowledgement of who suffered, what they lost, and what we owe to that memory. Marks: 1 estimate of pre-contact, 1 mechanism, 1 scale of loss, 1 ethical / communication.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
13
[4]
Apply the historical-thinking concept of MULTIPLE PERSPECTIVES to the Mongol Empire. Construct paragraphs reflecting how Mongol expansion was experienced by FOUR different historical actors (e.g., a Venetian merchant, a Persian peasant, a Mongol khan, a Yuan-era Chinese scholar).
Answer Key

(1) Venetian merchant (c. 1280): an extraordinary opportunity — safe roads, Asian markets opened, exotic goods available; Marco Polo's account is the canonical example. (2) Persian peasant (c. 1230): catastrophe — Khwarezmian cities destroyed, agricultural infrastructure devastated, taxation harsh, plague spreading. (3) Mongol khan (c. 1260): the natural order — steppe peoples successfully exporting their political form to settled societies. (4) Yuan-era Chinese scholar (c. 1300): an awkward accommodation — Mongol political dominance but Chinese cultural and bureaucratic continuity; the Yuan dynasty was Sinicising fast. (5) Sub-Saharan African or Sahelian merchant: distant news at most, little direct effect. Multiple perspectives enrich the picture without flattening it; the empire was many things at once. Marks: 1 each perspective.

14
[4]
Apply historical-thinking to Great Zimbabwe. Why did 19th-century European archaeologists invent elaborate Phoenician or Sabaean theories to deny indigenous African construction of the site? What does this episode teach about how researcher assumptions shape interpretation?
Answer Key

European racial assumptions — that sub-Saharan Africans were incapable of building monumental stone architecture — drove 19th-century archaeologists (notably J. Theodore Bent and Richard Hall) to invent Phoenician, Sabaean, or Queen-of-Sheba origin theories despite stratigraphic evidence to the contrary. The episode illustrates: (1) Researcher assumptions can override stratigraphic evidence — bias is not eliminated by professional credentials. (2) Imperial-era archaeology was bound up with the political project of denying indigenous African capacity, which mattered for justifying colonial rule (Rhodesia, modern Zimbabwe). (3) Twentieth-century work (Caton-Thompson 1929, MacIver before her) corrected the record, but the resistance was institutional. (4) For modern historians: source-criticism applies to secondary sources too — colonial-era archaeology must itself be a primary source we read critically. Marks: 1 racial assumption, 1 political function, 1 correction, 1 methodological lesson.

15
[4]
STSE Application: The "Arabic" numerals 0–9 used worldwide are originally Indian, transmitted via Arab mathematicians. Trace this transmission with at least three named transmission stages, and explain what this story illustrates about the global origins of modern science.
Answer Key

(1) ORIGIN: Indian mathematicians develop decimal place-value notation with zero by the 7th century CE (Brahmagupta's Brahmasphutasiddhanta, 628 CE; Bakhshali manuscript). (2) ISLAMIC TRANSMISSION: 8th-century translation of Indian works at the Abbasid House of Wisdom in Baghdad; al-Khwarizmi's c. 825 On the Calculation with Hindu Numerals systematises the system in Arabic. (3) EUROPEAN TRANSMISSION: 12th-century Latin translations of al-Khwarizmi (notably Adelard of Bath); 1202 Fibonacci's Liber Abaci popularises the system in commercial Italy. (4) ADOPTION: European merchants resist the new "Arabic" numerals until 15th–16th centuries, when account-books and printed manuals displace Roman numerals. The lesson: modern arithmetic is a multi-cultural, multi-stage achievement; the Eurocentric story of "the rise of the West" elides Indian invention, Islamic preservation, and Mediterranean transmission. Marks: 3 stages, 1 lesson / synthesis.
