Topic 4.1 — Islam & the Caliphates
Question 1
The hijra of 622 CE — Muhammad's emigration from Mecca to Medina — is significant because it:
Solution: The hijra is year 1 AH (anno hegirae) of the Islamic calendar. At Medina, Muhammad organised the umma as a political community, not just a religious one.
Question 2
The English words algebra and algorithm derive from:
Solution: Al-Khwarizmi's Compendious Book on Calculation by Completion (al-jabr) and Balancing (c. 825) gave us "algebra"; his name was Latinised as "Algorithmus." Both fundamentals of modern mathematics are Islamic-era developments.
Topic 4.2 — Tang/Song China & the Mongols
Question 3
The Chinese civil-service examination (keju) system, fully developed under the Song, was significant because it:
Solution: The keju was merit-based (within wealth-permitted preparation) — exceptional for its time. By the late Song, perhaps 400,000 candidates a year sat exams. The French and British modern civil-service exams of the 19th century were explicitly modelled on it.
Question 4
Bi Sheng invented movable type in China around:
Solution: Bi Sheng's ceramic movable type appeared c. 1040, four centuries before Gutenberg's c. 1450 metal version. (Gutenberg's invention was independent — there's no evidence he knew of Bi Sheng's work.)
Question 5
A balanced historical assessment of the Mongol Empire requires recognising:
Solution: Both/and is the correct historical answer. The conquests were brutal (Baghdad 1258); the Pax Mongolica that followed was a real period of safe trans-Eurasian travel that diffused technologies (gunpowder, printing, compass) and unfortunately also the plague.
Topic 4.3 — Africa & the Americas
Question 6
The 1375 Catalan Atlas places Mansa Musa enthroned in West Africa, holding a gold orb, with the inscription: "So abundant is the gold which is found in his country that he is the richest and most noble king in all the land."
This source demonstrates that:
Solution: The Catalan Atlas proves European awareness of West African wealth two centuries before the Atlantic slave trade. The framing of Africa as isolated/dark was constructed later, partly to justify imperial subjugation.
Question 7
Great Zimbabwe (c. 1100–c. 1450) — a stone-built southern African state — was:
Solution: Colonial-era European archaeologists invented elaborate Phoenician and Sabaean theories rather than accept that indigenous Africans had built the largest pre-colonial stone structures in sub-Saharan Africa. This is a classic case of racism shaping historical interpretation.
Question 8
The Aztec Empire conquered by Cortés in 1521 was:
Solution: Tenochtitlán was founded 1325; the Mexica only formed the Triple Alliance in 1428; their empire was less than a century old at contact. The same is true of the Inca state.
Topic 4.4 — Medieval India
Question 9
The "Arabic" numerals 0–9 used worldwide today are:
Solution: The decimal place-value system (with zero) is documented in Indian texts by the 7th century CE. Transmitted to the Islamic world via Indian mathematical works at the House of Wisdom; al-Khwarizmi's 9th-c. book systematised it in Arabic; reached Europe through Latin translations after 1100.
Question 10
The Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646) at its peak housed a city — Vijayanagara (modern Hampi) — that the Portuguese visitor Domingo Paes described as:
Solution: Paes (c. 1520) found Vijayanagara comparable to Rome in scale. Its destruction in 1565 at the Battle of Talikota ended this Hindu polity-building trajectory.
