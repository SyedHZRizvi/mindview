Careers
Question 1
The Canadian professional designation specifically for international-trade competence is:
Solution:
CITP (Certified International Trade Professional), granted by FITT, is Canada's main international-trade designation. CCS is the customs-specialist credential.
Question 2
A Trade Commissioner works for:
Solution:
Trade Commissioners are Canadian federal employees posted at embassies and consulates abroad, supporting Canadian businesses entering local markets.
Cross-Cultural Communication
Question 3
In a "monochronic" culture (e.g., Germany, Switzerland), time is typically treated as:
Solution:
Monochronic cultures treat time linearly — punctuality and one-topic-at-a-time. Polychronic cultures (Latin America, Middle East) are more flexible.
Question 4
In high-context cultures, long silences during a negotiation often mean:
Solution:
Silence in high-context cultures (Japan, Korea, much of East Asia) typically signals thoughtful consideration. Filling the silence too quickly is read as impatience.
Ethics & CSR
Question 5
The "triple bottom line" measures performance on three dimensions:
Solution:
John Elkington's triple bottom line: People (social), Planet (environment), Profit (financial). Coined in 1994.
Question 6
Canada's main law against bribery of foreign public officials is the:
Solution:
CFPOA is the Canadian law. FCPA is the US law; UK Bribery Act is the UK's. All three criminalize bribing foreign officials.
Question 7
The 2013 Rana Plaza disaster, in which 1,134 garment workers died, took place in:
Solution:
Rana Plaza was an 8-story garment factory building in Savar, Bangladesh. Its collapse on 24 April 2013 led to lasting reforms in garment-supply-chain ethics.
Working Abroad & Culminating Profile
Question 8
The "U-curve" of cultural adjustment typically begins with:
Solution:
The classic U-curve: honeymoon → culture shock → adjustment → mastery → re-entry. The dip is in the middle, hence "U-curve".
Question 9
Many expatriates leave their employer within 18 months of returning home. The single most cited cause is:
Solution:
Repatriation failure is overwhelmingly about career — returning expats want a role that uses their international experience. Without that, they leave for an employer that values it.
Question 10
The BBB4M Culminating Country/Market Profile is worth approximately what percentage of the final course mark?
Solution:
The culminating profile is 15% of the final mark (half of the 30% Final Evaluation). The other half is the written final exam.
