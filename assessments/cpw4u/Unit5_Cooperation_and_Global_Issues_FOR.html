Unit 5: Cooperation and Global Issues — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand E — E1.1, E1.2, E2.1, E3.1, E3.2, E3.3
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps in your prior knowledge of international institutions, global issues, and Canadian foreign policy before starting Unit 5.
Question 1 Prereq · Vocabulary
[3 marks]
In one sentence each, define: (a) multilateralism; (b) climate change; (c) international institution. Don't worry about precision — the goal is to indicate what you currently understand.
Question 2 Prior Knowledge · UN
[3 marks]
"We the peoples of the United Nations determined to save succeeding generations from the scourge of war, which twice in our lifetime has brought untold sorrow to mankind..." — Preamble to the UN Charter, 1945
What do you already know about the UN? When was it founded, what does the Security Council do, and what is the General Assembly?
Question 3 Prior Knowledge · Climate
[3 marks]
What do you know about the Paris Agreement (2015)? What does it commit countries to do? Why is the 1.5°C threshold significant?
Question 4 Prereq · Trade
[3 marks]
Name three Canadian trade agreements or partnerships. For each, briefly describe what it covers.
Question 5 Skills · Critical Thinking
[4 marks]
"Multilateralism is dead; long live multilateralism." — adapted from contemporary commentary, 2025
In 4 sentences: (a) identify one institution or treaty that has been weakened since 2017; (b) identify one new initiative that suggests multilateral cooperation continues; (c) name one Canadian policy interest at stake; (d) take a position on whether multilateralism is in decline.
Question 6 Skills · Source Analysis
[3 marks]
"COVID-19 has been a textbook case of the limits of global cooperation in a system of competing nation-states." — adapted from WHO commentary, 2022
In 3-4 sentences, identify one example from the COVID-19 response that supports this view AND one example of effective international cooperation during the pandemic.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 5 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) the UN system; (b) ICJ, ICC, WTO; (c) climate governance; (d) global health and pandemic preparedness; (e) migration and trade; (f) the future of multilateralism? Explain what specifically is unclear.
📖 Sample Answers and Marking Rubric (Instructor View)

Q1 (3 marks, 1 each): Multilateralism — diplomatic engagement involving three or more states through institutions; opposed to bilateralism and unilateralism. Climate change — long-term shift in global climate patterns, primarily driven by anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions; key challenge for global governance. International institution — formal organisation of states for collective action (UN, NATO, WTO, WHO, etc.); creates rules, norms, and structures.

Q2 (3 marks): UN founded 1945 (Charter signed 26 June, in force 24 October); HQ New York; 193 member states. Security Council — primary responsibility for international peace and security; 15 members (5 permanent with veto, 10 elected non-permanent); can authorise sanctions and use of force under Chapter VII. General Assembly — every member has one vote; passes non-binding resolutions on most matters, binding on UN administration. Award 1 founding, 1 Security Council, 1 General Assembly.

Q3 (3 marks): Paris 2015 — international climate agreement; commits parties to submit and progressively strengthen NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions); headline target "well below 2°C" + pursue 1.5°C. 1.5°C is the threshold above which climate scientists project particularly severe impacts (sea-level rise, ecosystem collapse, food-system stress). 2024 was the first calendar year on record to exceed 1.5°C of warming. Award 1 Paris description, 1 NDC, 1 1.5°C significance.

Q4 (3 marks, 1 each): Examples: USMCA (Canada-US-Mexico, 2020, replaced NAFTA); CPTPP (Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership, 2018, 11 members); CETA (Canada-EU, provisional 2017, ratified 2024); Canada-Korea FTA 2014; Canada-Ukraine FTA 2017 (modernised 2024); Canada-Israel FTA 1997 (modernised 2019); Canada-UK Trade Continuity 2021. Award 1 per agreement (name + brief coverage).

Q5 (4 marks): (a) Weakened: WTO Appellate Body shutdown since 2017; Doha Round abandoned; tariff politics 2024-25. (b) New initiatives: CPTPP 2018, Paris Agreement 2015, Loss and Damage Fund 2022, Pandemic Accord negotiations, the 2024 Pandemic Fund, AI governance Bletchley 2023 / Seoul 2024. (c) Canadian interests: trade diversification (CETA, CPTPP); climate engagement; refugee protection; international development. (d) Position: defensible either direction. Award 1 weakened example, 1 new initiative, 1 Canadian interest, 1 position.

Q6 (3 marks): Limits example: vaccine nationalism; trade restrictions on PPE; WTO TRIPS waiver blocked; WHO IHR compliance gaps; "infodemic" of misinformation. Cooperation example: WHO PHEIC declaration coordination; COVAX (~2B doses delivered); CTAP technology pool (limited but real); scientific cooperation on sequencing and vaccines; G20 emergency funding. Award 1 limits, 1 cooperation, 1 sophistication.

Q7 (ungraded): Common gaps include distinguishing ICJ from ICC; understanding the WTO Appellate Body shutdown; the Pandemic Accord status.
