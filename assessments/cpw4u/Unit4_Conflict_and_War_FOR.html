Unit 4: Conflict and War — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand D — D1.1, D1.2, D2.1, D2.2, D3.1
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps in your prior knowledge of war causation, IHL, and Canada's military role before starting Unit 4.
Question 1 Prereq · Vocabulary
[3 marks]
In one sentence each, define: (a) just war theory; (b) genocide; (c) peacekeeping. Don't worry about precision — the goal is to indicate what you currently understand.
Question 2 Prior Knowledge
[3 marks]
What do you already know about how wars start? Identify two factors you think can lead countries to war.
Question 3 Prior Knowledge · IHL
[3 marks]
"All citizens are equal before the law... attacks against the civilian population as such... are prohibited." — adapted from Article 51, Additional Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions
What do you know about the Geneva Conventions? Why do they matter in armed conflict?
Question 4 Prereq · Canadian Military
[3 marks]
Name three Canadian military institutions or operations you know (e.g., NATO, NORAD, peacekeeping, Afghanistan). For each, briefly describe what it does.
Question 5 Skills · Critical Thinking
[4 marks]
"Some wars are just; some are not. The question is which is which, and why." — paraphrase of Michael Walzer, Just and Unjust Wars
In 4 sentences: (a) identify one war (historical or contemporary) you think was clearly just; (b) identify one war you think was clearly unjust; (c) name one principle or criterion you would use to distinguish; (d) name one difficulty in making such judgments.
Question 6 Skills · Source Analysis
[3 marks]
"Never again." — common 20th-century commemorative pledge after the Holocaust
In 3-4 sentences, identify one historical instance of mass atrocity that the international community failed to prevent or stop (despite the "never again" commitment). What does this reveal about the gap between rhetoric and political will?
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 4 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) structural causes of war; (b) just war theory; (c) international humanitarian law; (d) R2P; (e) specific conflict case studies (Rwanda, Bosnia, Ukraine); (f) Canada's military role (NATO, NORAD, peacekeeping)? Explain what specifically is unclear.
📖 Sample Answers and Marking Rubric (Instructor View)

Q1 (3 marks, 1 each): Just war theory — ethical framework for evaluating whether and how war can be morally justified (Augustine, Aquinas, Walzer). Genocide — intentional destruction in whole or part of a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group (1948 Convention). Peacekeeping — neutral monitoring or buffer operations between combatants, typically with host-state consent.

Q2 (3 marks): Examples: security dilemma; resource competition; ethnic-nationalist mobilisation; regime change / preemption; alliance entanglements; economic interdependence; misperception. Award 1 per identified factor (up to 2) + 1 for sophistication.

Q3 (3 marks): Geneva Conventions = 1949 set of treaties governing conduct in armed conflict (wounded, sea, POWs, civilians). Why matter: protect civilians and combatants no longer fighting; provide enforcement mechanism (war-crimes prosecutions); part of IHL framework. Award 1 description, 1 importance, 1 sophistication.

Q4 (3 marks, 1 each): Examples: NATO (1949, collective-defence alliance); NORAD (1958, US-Canada continental air defence); UN peacekeeping (1956–, Pearson invention; ~33 Canadian deployed 2024); Afghanistan mission (2001-14, ~40,000 rotated, 158 dead); Operation Reassurance (2017–, NATO Latvia battle group); CAF (Canadian Armed Forces — three branches: Army, Navy, Air Force).

Q5 (4 marks): (a) Examples often given: WWII against Nazi Germany; humanitarian intervention in Rwanda; Ukrainian defence against Russian invasion. (b) Examples often given: 2003 Iraq War; 19th-century colonial wars; nuclear strikes on Hiroshima/Nagasaki (contested); 1999 Kosovo (contested). (c) Principles: just cause; legitimate authority; right intention; last resort; proportionality. (d) Difficulties: each criterion is context-dependent; "just cause" is contested; great-power politics often overrides ethical judgment.

Q6 (3 marks): Examples: Rwanda 1994 (800K-1M deaths, UNAMIR prevented from acting); Cambodia 1975-79 (Khmer Rouge); Yugoslavia 1991-95 (Srebrenica); Darfur 2003-; Syria 2011-24; Myanmar Rohingya 2017-; Ukraine 2022-; Gaza 2023-. What this reveals: gap between moral commitment and political will; great-power dynamics (UNSC veto); selective humanitarianism. Award 1 example, 1 analysis, 1 sophistication.

Q7 (ungraded): Common gaps: distinguishing IHL from human-rights law; understanding R2P pillars; the specific case studies in their context.
