Unit 3: Human Rights and Welfare — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand C — C1.1, C1.2, C2.1, C3.1, C3.2
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify gaps in your prior knowledge of human rights instruments, the Canadian welfare state, and Indigenous-rights frameworks before starting Unit 3.
Question 1 Prereq · Vocabulary
[3 marks]
In one sentence each, define: (a) human rights; (b) refugee; (c) welfare state. Don't worry about precision — your goal is to indicate what you currently understand.
Question 2 Prior Knowledge
[3 marks]
"All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights." — UDHR Article 1, 1948
What do you already know about the Universal Declaration of Human Rights? Who wrote it? When was it adopted? Why does it matter?
Question 3 Prior Knowledge · TRC
[3 marks]
What do you know about the Truth and Reconciliation Commission? When did it operate, who chaired it, and what were its key findings?
Question 4 Prereq · Canadian Programs
[3 marks]
Name three Canadian social-welfare programs (e.g., Medicare, the Canada Pension Plan, the Canada Child Benefit). For each, briefly describe what it does and which level of government delivers it.
Question 5 Skills · Source Analysis
[4 marks]
"Everyone has the right to a standard of living adequate for the health and well-being of himself and of his family, including food, clothing, housing and medical care and necessary social services." — UDHR Article 25(1), 1948
In 4 sentences: (a) identify whether this is a "civil/political" or "economic/social/cultural" right; (b) explain why this distinction matters legally; (c) identify one Canadian program that addresses some of these obligations; (d) identify one ongoing Canadian gap.
Question 6 Skills · Critical Thinking
[3 marks]
A friend says: "Canada is one of the best countries in the world for human rights." In 3-4 sentences, identify one strong piece of evidence for this view AND one important challenge or qualification.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 3 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) the UDHR and international rights instruments; (b) the 1951 Refugee Convention; (c) the Canadian Charter and human-rights codes; (d) the TRC and UNDRIP; (e) the welfare state; (f) measuring human welfare? Explain what specifically is unclear.
📖 Sample Answers and Marking Rubric (Instructor View)

Q1 (3 marks, 1 each): Human rights — rights all persons hold by virtue of being human, regardless of citizenship or status. Refugee — person outside their country owing to well-founded fear of persecution on protected grounds. Welfare state — state programs guaranteeing against economic risks and providing universal services.

Q2 (3 marks): 1948 UNGA adoption; drafting committee chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt; first draft by Canadian John Humphrey; foundational moral and political document; basis for subsequent ICCPR and ICESCR (1966).

Q3 (3 marks): TRC 2008-15 (or 2009-15 hearings); chaired by Justice Murray Sinclair; documented residential-schools history; 6,750+ survivor statements; 94 Calls to Action; concluded the system constituted "cultural genocide."

Q4 (3 marks, 1 each): Examples: Medicare (provincial delivery, federal cost-sharing under CHA); CPP (federal-provincial joint, contributory pension); CCB (federal, income-tested monthly to families); Employment Insurance (federal, contributory unemployment + sickness + parental); OAS (federal, universal flat-rate seniors income); GIS (federal, income-tested top-up for low-income seniors). Award 1 per program.

Q5 (4 marks): (a) Economic/social/cultural right (covered by ICESCR 1966); (b) civil/political rights typically have stronger enforcement mechanisms (treaty bodies, courts) than economic/social/cultural rights which are framed as "progressive realisation"; (c) Canadian Medicare addresses some of these obligations; (d) gaps include homelessness, food insecurity (~17% of Canadians 2024), prescription drug coverage (until 2024 Bill C-64), dental care (2023-25 rollout), Indigenous on-reserve service gaps.

Q6 (3 marks): Evidence: Canada consistently ranks high on HDI (18th 2024); has constitutional rights protection (Charter); has welfare state with universal Medicare; has accepted ~40,000 Syrians and ~210,000 Ukrainians on humanitarian grounds; ranked 7-9th on Economist Democracy Index. Challenges: Indigenous on-reserve life expectancy ~10 years below Canadian average; MMIWG crisis; ongoing CHRT findings on First Nations child welfare (~$40 billion settlement 2024); homelessness crisis; income inequality; over 4,000 known residential-school deaths (revised upward). Award 1 evidence, 1 challenge, 1 sophistication.

Q7 (ungraded): Use to inform pedagogy. Common gaps: distinguishing civil/political from economic/social/cultural rights; understanding section 33 implications for Charter; UNDRIP's evolving legal status.
