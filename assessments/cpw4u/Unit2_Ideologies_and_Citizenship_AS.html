Topics 2.1–2.2 — Ideology, Liberalism, Conservatism, Socialism
Question 1
The Left-Right political spectrum originated in:
Solution: The Left-Right spectrum dates to the French Revolution: in the 1789 Estates-General and subsequent Assemblies, supporters of the king sat on the right of the presiding officer, supporters of revolutionary change on the left. The terminology has been used ever since.
Question 2
"If it has been said that ours is a divisive force, I reply that the dignity of man is more important than the unity of the state." — Pierre Trudeau, federal Liberal leader, 1968
Trudeau's statement is closest to which ideological tradition?
Solution: Trudeau's prioritisation of individual "dignity" over the "unity of the state" reflects classical and social-liberal commitments. The lineage runs from Locke through Mill to Rawls and contemporary Canadian liberalism.
Question 3
Edmund Burke's Reflections on the Revolution in France (1790) is the foundational text of:
Solution: Burke argued societies are organic wholes built over centuries; institutions embody accumulated wisdom; abrupt theoretical change destroys the social fabric. The text is foundational for "traditional" conservatism; 20th-century conservatism diversified into libertarian, neo-conservative, and post-liberal currents.
Question 4
The Canadian Cooperative Commonwealth Federation (CCF) was founded in 1932 and became the:
Solution: The CCF was founded in Calgary in 1932 by J.S. Woodsworth and others (a coalition of farmer, labour, and social-gospel reformers). In 1961 it merged with the Canadian Labour Congress to form the NDP. Tommy Douglas — Saskatchewan CCF premier 1944–61 — became the first federal NDP leader.
Topic 2.3 — Fascism, Populism, Indigenous Political Thought
Question 5
Cas Mudde and Cristóbal Rovira Kaltwasser (2017) define populism as:
Solution: The Mudde/Rovira Kaltwasser definition is the leading academic account. Populism is "thin-centred" because it attaches to thicker ideologies — left-populism (Chávez, Sanders, Mélenchon) and right-populism (Trump, Le Pen, Meloni, Orbán) share the people-vs-elite frame.
Question 6
Robert Paxton's nine "mobilising passions" of fascism (2004) centrally include:
Solution: Paxton's Anatomy of Fascism identifies fascism's mobilising passions as including crisis, group primacy, victimhood, decline anxiety, leader cult, beauty of violence, group superiority, and populist anti-elitism. Fascism is not synonymous with all right-wing authoritarianism — it is a specific, historically-situated combination.
Topic 2.4 — Citizenship and the Charter
Question 7
T.H. Marshall's classic three-layer account of citizenship (1950) distinguishes:
Solution: Marshall's Citizenship and Social Class (1950) distinguished civil rights (legal equality, due process), political rights (vote, office-holding), and social rights (welfare minimum) as successive historical layers — each a political achievement, not a natural condition.
Question 8
In Sauvé v. Canada (No. 2) (2002), the SCC:
Solution: The SCC (5-4, McLachlin CJ) struck down the prohibition on voting by prisoners serving 2+ years. The right to vote is the fundamental democratic right and cannot be removed except by section 33. Later, Frank v. Canada (2019) extended voting rights to long-term non-residents.
Topics 2.5–2.6 — Parties and Civil Society
Question 9
The Canadian federal first-past-the-post (FPTP) electoral system:
Solution: FPTP (single-member plurality) awards each riding to the candidate with the most votes (plurality, not majority). It favours regionally concentrated parties (Bloc Québécois, regional Liberals/Conservatives) and disadvantages diffuse national parties (Greens at ~6% national but 1-2 seats).
Question 10
Robert Putnam's Bowling Alone (2000) used the concept of "social capital" to argue that:
Solution: Putnam tracked the late-20th-century decline of voluntary associations (bowling leagues, PTAs, religious groups, community organisations) and argued this decline weakened the social capital needed for healthy democracy. The argument has been challenged and refined; Tufekci's Twitter and Tear Gas (2017) tracks the digital-era reconfiguration.
Question 11
Idle No More (November 2012–) began with:
Solution: Idle No More began in November 2012 with four Saskatchewan women organising teach-ins against Bill C-45 (which weakened environmental protection). It became continent-wide, including round-dance flash mobs, Chief Theresa Spence's hunger strike (Dec 2012-Jan 2013), and the 12 January 2013 Day of Action.
Question 12
Short answer: In 2–3 sentences, explain how political ideologies map (or fail to map) onto Canadian federal parties in 2025. Use one specific example.
Sample: Canadian federal parties do not map cleanly onto single ideologies — each is a coalition. The Liberal Party combines centrist social liberalism (the post-1971 multiculturalism, the 2015-25 redistributive shifts) with brokerage moderation; the Conservative Party (post-2003) combines traditional Burkean conservatism, libertarian/neoliberal economics, and post-2022 populist tropes; the NDP combines democratic socialism (Singh's pharmacare/dental advocacy), labour-movement social democracy, and progressive cultural politics; the Bloc combines Quebec nationalism with economic centrism. Example: the post-2022 Poilievre Conservative use of populist rhetorical strategies (anti-"gatekeepers", "common people vs. elites") shows realignment within an institutional party.
