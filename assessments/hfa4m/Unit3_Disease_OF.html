Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
Define atherosclerosis, LDL, and HDL. Which is "good" and which is "bad," and why?
Answer Key

Atherosclerosis = build-up of cholesterol-rich plaques inside artery walls, narrowing arteries and triggering clots that cause heart attack and stroke. LDL = low-density lipoprotein, delivers cholesterol to tissues; oxidised LDL contributes to plaque formation — "bad." HDL = high-density lipoprotein, returns cholesterol to liver for excretion — "good."

2
[2]
Which dietary change is most effective at lowering LDL cholesterol?
Answer Key

B. Replacing saturated/trans fats with unsaturated fats lowers LDL; soluble fibre (oats, legumes, psyllium) binds bile acids and lowers LDL.

3
[3]
Explain insulin resistance in your own words. Why does it lead to high blood glucose?
Answer Key

Insulin resistance = cells respond sluggishly to insulin. The pancreas compensates by secreting more insulin; over time β-cells fail. With reduced glucose uptake and increased hepatic glucose output, blood glucose rises. Chronic hyperglycaemia damages capillaries (eyes, kidneys, nerves) and accelerates atherosclerosis.

4
[2]
Which two foods belong to IARC's Group 1 (carcinogenic to humans)?
Answer Key

B. IARC Group 1: processed meat (since 2015) and alcoholic beverages. Red meat is 2A (probable).

5
[3]
List three dietary factors that protect bone and explain their role.
Answer Key

Calcium — primary bone mineral (1000 mg/day adults 19–50; 1200 mg/day for women >50, men >70). Vitamin D — essential for calcium absorption (RDA 600–800 IU; supplement of 400 IU/day recommended over 50 by Health Canada). Protein — supports bone matrix and muscle to prevent falls. Other: vitamin K, magnesium, potassium; limit excess sodium and caffeine; weight-bearing/resistance exercise builds and maintains bone.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
6
[4]
Sodium audit: A typical lunch contains a deli ham sandwich (1240 mg Na), a bowl of canned soup (790 mg Na), a bottled tea (15 mg Na), and a granola bar (110 mg Na). (a) Calculate total Na. (b) Compare to adolescent AI (1500 mg) and CDRR (2300 mg). (c) Suggest two swaps to cut sodium by half.
Answer Key

(a) Total = 1240 + 790 + 15 + 110 = 2155 mg Na. (b) Exceeds AI (1500 mg) and approaches CDRR (2300 mg) — in a single meal. (c) Swaps: (1) Replace canned soup with homemade soup or a small salad (cuts ~600–700 mg); (2) Use unsalted bread, fresh sliced turkey or grilled chicken (cuts ~500 mg). Better swap: build sandwich on whole-grain bread with hummus, fresh vegetables, low-sodium cheese, lean protein. Total may fall below 1100 mg.

7
[4]
Glycemic-load analysis: A breakfast option compares (A) 2 slices white toast + 2 tbsp jam + sweetened OJ vs (B) 1 cup steel-cut oats + berries + Greek yogurt + 1 hard-boiled egg. (a) Predict which produces a higher glucose spike and explain. (b) Identify two additional health-related advantages of option B.
Answer Key

(a) Option A: high-GI refined-flour bread + concentrated sugar + high-GI juice → rapid, large glucose rise, then crash. Option B: low-GI intact oats + protein (yogurt, egg) + fibre (berries, oats) → slower digestion, gentler rise. (b) Advantages of B: higher satiety (protein + fibre delay return of hunger); better micronutrient profile (calcium from yogurt, antioxidants from berries, choline/lutein from egg, B vitamins from oats); higher fibre (4+ g vs <2 g in option A). Better fit for someone with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.

8
[4]
Design an investigation to test whether a brief food-safety lesson (e.g., 15-min video on the temperature danger zone) reduces unsafe food-handling reports among Grade 12 students one week later. Identify variables, sample, and analysis plan.
Answer Key

Hypothesis: lesson reduces unsafe practices. Sample: 60 Grade 12 students randomly assigned to lesson (intervention) or wait-list control. IV: lesson exposure. DV: self-reported food-handling practices (validated 10-item questionnaire — e.g., thaw method, leftover storage time, separating raw/cooked) one week later. Controlled: identical questionnaire, identical instructions. Ethics: voluntary, anonymous, informed consent. Analysis: compare mean scores (independent t-test or non-parametric equivalent) and proportion of unsafe practices, with 95% CIs. Limitations: self-report bias, short follow-up.

Part C: Communication [13 marks]
9
[5]
Write a paragraph (~150 words) explaining the role of dietary fibre, including soluble vs insoluble fibre, and its impact on CVD and T2DM risk.
Answer Key

Look for: distinction between soluble (oats, legumes, psyllium, fruits — viscous, binds bile, lowers LDL, slows glucose absorption) and insoluble (whole-grain bran, vegetables — adds bulk, promotes regularity). Health Canada AI: 25 g/day women, 38 g/day men. Higher fibre intake associates with lower CVD events, better glycemic control, lower colorectal cancer risk. Fibre also feeds gut microbiome that produces beneficial short-chain fatty acids.

10
[4]
Build a comparison table: cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, cancer, osteoporosis. Columns: one major dietary risk, one protective dietary factor.
Answer Key

CVD — risk: saturated/trans fats, excess sodium, low fibre; protective: unsaturated fats, fibre, potassium, DASH/Mediterranean pattern. T2DM — risk: refined carbohydrate, sugar-sweetened beverages, high BMI; protective: fibre, low-GI carbohydrate, physical activity. Cancer — risk: processed meat, alcohol, excess body weight; protective: fibre, vegetables/fruit, physical activity. Osteoporosis — risk: low calcium/vitamin D, excess sodium, smoking, inactivity; protective: calcium + vitamin D + weight-bearing exercise.

11
[4]
Describe the four food-safety principles (CLEAN, SEPARATE, COOK, CHILL). For each, give one concrete kitchen example.
Answer Key

CLEAN — wash hands 20 s before/after handling food; sanitise surfaces; rinse produce. SEPARATE — use separate cutting boards for raw meat and ready-to-eat foods; store raw meat below ready-to-eat on lower fridge shelves. COOK — use thermometer to verify chicken 74 °C, ground beef 71 °C, whole cuts 63 °C + rest. CHILL — refrigerate perishables within 2 hours; thaw in fridge, not on counter; freezer ≤−18 °C.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[6]
Case study — Prediabetes: A 19-year-old college student is told he has prediabetes (A1C 6.2%). He weighs 88 kg, eats fast food 4×/week, drinks 2 sugar-sweetened beverages/day, and is sedentary. Outline a comprehensive intervention covering diet, activity, monitoring, and stigma-reducing communication. Cite the Diabetes Prevention Program evidence base.
Answer Key

Diet: shift to low-GI pattern with vegetables/half plate, intact whole grains, legumes, lean protein, healthy fats; reduce sugar-sweetened beverages (target zero) and fast food; portion guidance; fibre target. Activity: Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines — work toward 150 min/week moderate-vigorous + 2 days resistance; start with brief walks, build up. Monitoring: A1C recheck in 3 months; track weight, BP, lipids; consider continuous glucose monitor briefly to see real-time response. Communication: respectful, non-stigmatising, focus on health behaviours rather than weight as the only target; collaborative goal-setting (SMART goals); registered dietitian referral. DPP evidence: lifestyle intervention (5–7% weight loss + 150 min activity) reduces conversion from prediabetes to T2DM by 58%, outperforming metformin.

13
[6]
STSE — Foodborne Outbreak: A family barbecue: 8 hours after eating, 7 of 14 guests develop diarrhea and vomiting. They ate chicken kebabs, potato salad, fruit salad, brownies. Most ate the kebabs and potato salad. (a) List three plausible pathogens and likely sources. (b) What is the immediate response? (c) What's the system-level response (CFIA, public health)?
Answer Key

(a) Salmonella or Campylobacter from undercooked chicken (incubation 6–72 h); Staph aureus toxin from potato salad in danger zone (incubation 30 min – 6 h fits acute onset). Less likely: E. coli (longer incubation). (b) Immediate: rehydration (oral rehydration solution); seek medical care if severe (bloody stool, high fever, signs of dehydration); preserve food samples; do not prepare food for others while symptomatic. (c) Report to local public-health unit; lab samples may identify pathogen; investigate handling timeline (cross-contamination, temperature, hands); broader CFIA involvement if commercial supplier is implicated. Prevention: separate cutting boards/utensils for raw chicken; cook to 74 °C internal verified by thermometer; keep potato salad ≤4 °C until serving; discard after ≤2 hours in danger zone.
