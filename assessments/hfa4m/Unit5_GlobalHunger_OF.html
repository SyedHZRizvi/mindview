Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [12 marks]
1
[3]
Define stunting, wasting, and hidden hunger. Which most reflects chronic versus acute undernutrition?
Answer Key

Stunting — low height-for-age; chronic undernutrition during first 1000 days; affects ~148 million children under 5. Wasting — low weight-for-height; acute undernutrition; affects ~45 million children. Hidden hunger — micronutrient deficiency (iron, vitamin A, iodine, zinc) despite adequate energy; ~2 billion people globally. Stunting = chronic; wasting = acute.

2
[2]
Which food has the highest greenhouse-gas footprint per kg (Poore & Nemecek 2018)?
Answer Key

B. Beef from beef herds ~60 kg CO₂eq/kg. Eggs ~4.5; tofu ~2; lentils ~0.9. Driven by enteric methane (cattle digestion) and land use.

3
[3]
List three drivers of global hunger and explain how each contributes.
Answer Key

Conflict — single largest driver; wars displace people, destroy harvests, block supply chains (Syria, Yemen, Sudan, Ukraine, Gaza). Climate shocks — droughts, floods, cyclones reduce yields and damage infrastructure. Economic inequality — food unaffordable for poorest within and between countries. Gender inequality — women produce up to 80% of food in many low-income agricultural communities but face barriers to land, credit, education. Trade and policy rules — agricultural subsidies in high-income countries depress prices for low-income farmers.

[2]
What does SDG 2 ("Zero Hunger") aim to achieve, and by when?
Answer Key

B. SDG 2 (one of 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals adopted 2015) aims to end hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture by 2030. Progress has stalled.

5
[2]
Fair Trade certification primarily addresses:
Answer Key

C. Fair Trade sets minimum prices, community premiums, and prohibits child/forced labour for commodity supply chains (coffee, cocoa, bananas, tea, sugar).

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]
Carbon-footprint calculation: A student eats beef 4 times/week (200 g/serving). She considers swapping 2 servings for lentils (200 g cooked). Using Poore & Nemecek factors (beef 60 kg CO₂eq/kg; lentils 0.9 kg CO₂eq/kg): (a) Calculate current weekly beef emissions. (b) Calculate new plan emissions. (c) Calculate weekly and annual savings. (d) Identify two additional health benefits of the swap.
Answer Key

(a) Current = 4 × 0.2 × 60 = 48 kg CO₂eq/week. (b) New = (2 × 0.2 × 60) + (2 × 0.2 × 0.9) = 24 + 0.36 = 24.4 kg/week. (c) Weekly savings = 48 − 24.4 = 23.6 kg CO₂eq/week; annual = 23.6 × 52 ≈ 1227 kg CO₂eq/year — equivalent to ~5000 km driving a gasoline car. (d) Health benefits: increased fibre (lentils ~16 g/cup), reduced saturated fat (lower CVD risk), reduced processed-meat exposure (lower colorectal cancer risk if beef was processed), more polyphenols from plant foods.

7
[4]
A development agency is choosing between sending wheat shipments (in-kind food) and giving cash transfers to households in a region with functioning local markets but a long-running drought. Build a pros/cons comparison and recommend.
Answer Key

In-kind: pros — guaranteed delivery, visible to donors, useful when markets are broken; cons — undermines local farmers, requires complex logistics and storage, less dignified, may not match cultural foods. Cash: pros — supports local markets and producers, more dignified, allows choice, often cheaper to deliver, addresses both food and other essentials; cons — assumes markets function and access is safe, perception of misuse (research shows misuse is rare and minor). Recommendation: in this scenario (markets function but drought is structural), cash is preferred — supports local recovery while meeting acute needs. Hybrid programs (cash plus in-kind during peak shortage) are increasingly common.

8
[4]
Design an investigation in your school to measure how much food is wasted in the cafeteria during one week. Identify variables, measurement method, and analysis.
Answer Key

Variable measured: mass of food waste per day. Method: post-meal weigh-bins separated by category (plate waste, untouched food, beverages) at each lunch period for 5 school days. Control conditions: consistent measurement protocol, scales calibrated. Sample: full student body (or representative cohort). Analysis: total kg/day; average kg/student/day; breakdown by category; compare to national/global benchmarks; identify high-waste items. Ethics: anonymous (waste bin), school approval, sanitary handling. Limitations: doesn't capture pre-meal waste (kitchen prep); weather/menu effects. Output: present findings + recommendations to school administration (menu adjustments, portion sizing, education campaign, composting).

Part C: Communication [13 marks]
9
[5]
Write a paragraph (~150 words) explaining the connection between food systems and climate change. Cover both directions (food system → climate; climate → food system), at least two key statistics, and one example of an effective intervention.
Answer Key

Look for: food systems contribute 21–37% of global GHG emissions (IPCC); largest single use of land and freshwater; specific high-emission foods (beef, lamb, dairy via methane); food waste = 3rd-largest emitter if it were a country. Climate impacts on food: heat, drought, floods, shifting growing seasons, pest expansion, ocean warming/acidification cut yields and disrupt fisheries. Effective interventions: plant-forward diet shift (e.g., EAT-Lancet planetary health diet), reduced food waste, regenerative agriculture, methane-reducing feed additives, policy levers (carbon pricing, ag subsidies), reducing food deforestation.

10
[4]
Compare in a table: anorexia of the macro level — undernutrition (kwashiorkor and marasmus) versus the modern Canadian condition of obesity. Columns: cause, prevalence, treatment.
Answer Key

Kwashiorkor: severe protein deficit (often with some calories from starch); oedema, hair colour change, growth failure; mostly low-income settings, especially weaned children; treatment: gradual refeeding with balanced protein + RUTF, address underlying social/health issues. Marasmus: severe overall energy deficit; wasting, no oedema; treatment: gradual refeeding (refeeding syndrome risk). Obesity: chronic excess energy + obesogenic environment + biological/genetic factors; ~30% of Canadian adults (BMI ≥30); treatment: lifestyle, behavioural therapy, pharmacotherapy (e.g., GLP-1 agonists), surgery for severe cases; reduce weight stigma. All three reflect mismatches between food environment and human biology.

11
[4]
Describe the EAT-Lancet "planetary health diet" in a paragraph. What does it recommend? Who proposed it? How does it address both human and planetary health?
Answer Key

EAT-Lancet Commission 2019 — Walter Willett, Johan Rockström, and ~37 scientists from 16 countries. Reference diet (~2500 kcal): ~½ plate vegetables and fruits; mostly whole grains; modest plant proteins (legumes, nuts); modest fish and poultry; very limited red meat (≤14 g/day red meat); limited dairy (~250 g/day); limited added sugar (≤31 g/day). Goal: feed 10 billion people in 2050 while staying within planetary boundaries (climate change, biodiversity loss, freshwater, land use, nitrogen/phosphorus cycles). Estimated to prevent ~11 million premature deaths/year from chronic diet-related disease. Critiqued by some agriculture and meat groups; widely accepted as a directional framework.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[6]
Case study — Acute Food Emergency: A region affected by armed conflict has been classified IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Over 30% of households cannot meet basic food needs; child wasting rate is 20%. International aid is being planned. (a) Explain IPC Phase 4 vs Phase 5. (b) Outline the appropriate humanitarian response including ready-to-use therapeutic foods. (c) Identify two longer-term structural solutions once the conflict subsides.
Answer Key

(a) IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) — ≥20% of households face extreme food gap; high acute malnutrition; excess mortality; emergency strategies (selling assets, charity, aid). Phase 5 (Famine) — additionally ≥20% face extreme food lack, severe acute malnutrition ≥30%, crude mortality ≥2/10,000/day. Phase 4 is one step below Famine. (b) Response: WFP and UNICEF coordinated humanitarian food assistance; RUTFs (Plumpy'Nut paste — 500 kcal/sachet, fortified, no cooking, no water needed) for severely malnourished children; safe water (cholera risk); medical screening; primary care; protection. Negotiate humanitarian corridors with conflict parties. (c) Longer-term: support to smallholder farmers (seeds, credit, irrigation); women's empowerment and education; rebuilding markets; social safety nets; peace and good governance — without security, none of the above persists. Health systems and surveillance for early-warning IPC tracking.

13
[6]
STSE — Ethical Food Choices: You are putting together your family's grocery list. Discuss how you would weigh the following considerations for at least three items: animal welfare, fair trade, climate footprint, household budget, cultural fit. Demonstrate an ethical framework rather than a single right answer.
Answer Key

Look for: nuanced ethical reasoning. Examples — coffee: Fair Trade certified often best balance of farmer income + organic practice + modest premium; trade-off if budget is tight. Eggs: free-run/free-range certified > caged (improving welfare); cost trade-off acknowledged. Beef: highest climate footprint; consider reducing portions or frequency rather than eliminating if culturally important; pasture-raised has marginally different impact. Bananas: Fair Trade matters because of long history of worker exploitation; small premium. Family dietary preferences: respect cultural fit; nutrition + tradition + ethics need not conflict. Recognise no single food meets every ethical criterion — building habits of asking the questions matters more than perfect choices. Policy reform (carbon pricing, supply-chain transparency, labour law) does the heavy lifting that individual purchases cannot.
