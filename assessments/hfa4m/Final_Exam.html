Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[3]B · Determinants
List the six classes of nutrients and identify which provide energy (kcal/g) and which do not.
Answer Key

Carbohydrate (4 kcal/g), Protein (4 kcal/g), Fat (9 kcal/g) — energy-yielding. Vitamins, Minerals, Water — yield no energy themselves but are essential for metabolic function.

2
[3]B · Determinants
State the AMDR for adults for each energy-yielding macronutrient.
Answer Key

Carbohydrate 45–65% of daily energy. Fat 20–35%. Protein 10–35%.

3
[2]B
Which DRI describes the highest daily intake unlikely to pose adverse effects?
Answer Key

D. UL = Tolerable Upper Intake Level.

4
[2]B
Calcium RDA for adolescents (9–18 years):
Answer Key

C. 1300 mg/day — supports peak bone-mass accretion.

5
[3]C · Lifespan
Name three nutrients with increased needs during pregnancy and the role of each.
Answer Key

Folate (600 µg DFE) — prevents NTDs. Iron (27 mg/day) — supports increased blood volume and fetal stores. Iodine (220 µg/day) — fetal brain/thyroid development. Also acceptable: calcium, vitamin D, DHA, choline.

6
[2]C
When does the WHO recommend introducing complementary foods alongside breast milk?
Answer Key

C. 6 months. Exclusive BF to 6 months, complementary feeding alongside continued BF to 2 years and beyond.

7
[2]C · Disease
Which lipoprotein is called "bad cholesterol"?
Answer Key

B. LDL delivers cholesterol to tissues; oxidised LDL contributes to atherosclerotic plaque.

8
[3]C · Disease
Explain insulin resistance and its role in type 2 diabetes.
Answer Key

Insulin resistance — cells respond sluggishly to insulin signalling. Pancreas compensates by oversecretion (hyperinsulinaemia) until β-cells fail. Blood glucose rises, leading to T2DM (A1C ≥ 6.5%). Chronic hyperglycaemia damages capillaries and accelerates atherosclerosis.

9
[2]C
Which IARC group classifies processed meat as carcinogenic to humans (2015)?
Answer Key

A. Group 1. Red meat is 2A (probably). Alcohol is also Group 1.

10
[3]D · Local Food
Name the three sectors operating under Canada's supply management and outline its three core mechanisms.
Answer Key

Sectors: dairy, poultry (chicken + turkey), eggs. Mechanisms: production quotas, import controls, administered pricing.



Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
11
[5]B · Macronutrient calculation
A meal contains 60 g carbohydrate, 25 g protein, 15 g fat, and 5 g fibre (subtracted from total CHO for net CHO calculation if used). (a) Calculate total kcal. (b) Calculate % of energy from each macronutrient. (c) Comment on AMDR compliance.
Answer Key

(a) CHO: 60 × 4 = 240; protein: 25 × 4 = 100; fat: 15 × 9 = 135; total ≈ 475 kcal. (b) %CHO = 240/475 ≈ 51%; %fat = 135/475 ≈ 28%; %protein = 100/475 ≈ 21%. (c) All three within AMDR (CHO 45–65%, fat 20–35%, protein 10–35%) — well-balanced.

12
[5]B · BMR estimation
Estimate BMR and TDEE for a 17-year-old male, 72 kg, 178 cm tall, moderately active (factor 1.55). Mifflin–St Jeor (male): BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a + 5. Comment on energy needs.
Answer Key

BMR = 10(72) + 6.25(178) − 5(17) + 5 = 720 + 1112.5 − 85 + 5 = 1752.5 kcal/day. TDEE = 1752.5 × 1.55 ≈ 2716 kcal/day. Comment: appropriate range for an active 17-year-old male; adequate kcal essential for growth, sport, and academic performance. Restrictive diets below ~2200 kcal in this profile risk impairing growth, recovery, immune function.

13
[5]C · Diabetes case
A 22-year-old man has prediabetes (A1C 6.1%), BMI 31 kg/m², sedentary lifestyle, drinks 3 sugary beverages/day, eats fast food 4×/week. Design a 12-week behaviour-change plan. Cite the Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP).
Answer Key

Plan: (1) Eliminate sugar-sweetened beverages — replace with water/unsweetened tea. (2) Reduce fast food to 1×/week, plan 3 home meals (legumes, vegetables, intact whole grains). (3) Activity — start with 30 min brisk walking, 5 days/week; add resistance 2 days/week. (4) Track A1C at 12 weeks. (5) Connect with registered dietitian for individualised guidance. DPP evidence: lifestyle (5–7% weight loss + 150 min/week MVPA) reduced T2DM progression by 58%, outperforming metformin. Address stigma — focus on behaviours, not weight.

14
[5]D · Climate carbon math
A vegetarian student adds 4 servings of cheese (150 g each) per week. Cheese emits ~21 kg CO₂eq/kg. (a) Calculate weekly cheese-related emissions. (b) She swaps 2 servings/week for 200 g cooked beans (≈0.9 kg CO₂eq/kg). Calculate new weekly emissions and savings. (c) Discuss whether this is a meaningful climate action.
Answer Key

(a) Current = 4 × 0.15 × 21 = 12.6 kg CO₂eq/week. (b) New cheese = 2 × 0.15 × 21 = 6.3; new bean = 2 × 0.2 × 0.9 = 0.36; total = 6.66 kg/week. Savings = 12.6 − 6.66 ≈ 5.94 kg/week; annual ~310 kg CO₂eq. (c) Meaningful as an individual contribution (~1300 km of driving avoided) but smaller than reducing beef. Aggregate impact comes from collective shifts + policy + supply-chain changes. Cheese (~21 kg CO₂eq/kg) is among the higher-impact dairy products.

15
[5]D · Investigation design
Design an investigation to determine whether labelling fruit with school-cafeteria branding increases student selection vs. unbranded fruit. Identify hypothesis, variables, sample, and ethics.
Answer Key

Hypothesis: branded fruit increases selection. IV: branding (branded vs unbranded). DV: % of students selecting fruit during lunch. Sample: 5 weeks alternating branding, ~600 students/week. Controlled: same fruit type, location, price. Measurement: tally selections; record total students. Analysis: compare proportions with 95% CIs; chi-square. Ethics: school approval, anonymous tally, no individual data. Limitations: novelty effects, weather, menu confound. Output: recommendation to cafeteria administration.



Part C — Communication [25 marks]
16
[5]B · Canada's Food Guide
Write a paragraph (~150 words) explaining Canada's Food Guide (2019), including plate proportions, recommended beverage, and the three behaviour-based messages.
Answer Key

Look for: 2019 revision shifted from the 1992 four-group rainbow to a plate visual — ½ vegetables and fruits, ¼ whole grains, ¼ protein foods (including plant proteins like legumes, tofu, nuts, seeds, alongside fish, meat, dairy, eggs). Water recommended drink. Three behavioural messages: cook more often; eat meals with others; be mindful of eating habits. Guide also emphasises culturally appropriate eating and avoiding marketing of foods high in sugar/sodium/saturated fat.

17
[4]C · Comparison
Compare and contrast anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa in a brief table. Columns: defining feature, typical weight, key complications, key treatment focus.
Answer Key

AN: restriction + intense fear of weight gain; significantly low weight; bradycardia, osteoporosis, refeeding-syndrome risk; nutritional rehabilitation + psychotherapy + family-based therapy for adolescents. BN: recurrent binges + compensatory behaviour; often normal weight; parotid swelling, dental erosion, oesophageal tears, hypokalemia; CBT-E (enhanced CBT) + nutrition rehab + psychiatric care.

18
[5]D · Food labels
In a labelled diagram or detailed description, identify the major sections of a Canadian Nutrition Facts table and explain how a careful shopper uses it.
Answer Key

Sections: Serving size; Calories; Fat (sat + trans); Carbohydrate (sugar incl. added, fibre); Protein; Vitamins/minerals; %DV column. Shopper compares per-100 g (not just per-serving since manufacturers set serving sizes); applies 5%/15% DV rule (≤5% a little, ≥15% a lot); checks ingredient list (descending order, shorter and recognisable preferable, no sugar as first ingredient); checks priority-allergen statements. New front-of-pack symbol (mandatory Jan 2026) flags high sat fat/sodium/sugar.

19
[5]D · Food security
Write a paragraph (~150 words) explaining household food insecurity in Canada — what it is, who is most affected, why income is the primary driver, and one policy lever.
Answer Key

Look for: PROOF definition; ~18% of households in 2022 (highest since national tracking); higher rates in lone-parent female-led, Black, Indigenous, renter, and social-assistance households; northern food costs 2–3× southern Canada; nutritious diets cost more per kcal; food charity reaches only ~23% of food-insecure households; effective policy levers include indexed social-assistance rates, guaranteed minimum income, Canada Disability Benefit, National School Food Program (2024). Income transfers reduce food insecurity more reliably than food charity.

20
[6]D · Climate & Food
Write a paragraph (~200 words) explaining the bidirectional relationship between food systems and climate change. Include at least two key statistics, two specific examples of high-emission foods, and two strategies for reducing food-system emissions.
Answer Key

Look for: food systems contribute 21–37% of global anthropogenic GHG (IPCC); agriculture uses most freshwater and land; food waste = 3rd-largest emitter if it were a country. High-emission foods: beef (~60 kg CO₂eq/kg, via enteric methane), lamb (24), cheese (21). Climate impacts on food: heat waves, droughts, floods, shifting growing seasons, ocean warming reduce yields. Strategies: plant-forward dietary shift (EAT-Lancet planetary health diet), reducing food waste (UNEP 2024: 19% globally), regenerative agriculture (soil carbon, reduced fertiliser), methane-reducing feed additives (3-NOP), and policy (carbon pricing, ag-subsidy reform). Individual + systemic action together produce the biggest impact.



Part D — Application [25 marks]
21
[6]B · Menu planning case
Design a one-day menu (breakfast, lunch, dinner, two snacks) for a 16-year-old female vegan athlete. Provide approximate macronutrient totals (target ~2400 kcal). Address protein, iron, B12, calcium, vitamin D, DHA, and zinc.
Answer Key

Sample: Breakfast — oats with fortified soy milk, berries, ground flax (omega-3), pumpkin seeds. Snack — apple + 2 tbsp peanut butter. Lunch — chickpea and lentil curry, brown rice, spinach salad with tahini-lemon. Snack — fortified soy yogurt with hemp seeds. Dinner — tofu stir-fry with broccoli, edamame, tamari, brown rice; clementine. Approx totals: ~2400 kcal; ~85 g protein (1.4 g/kg if 60 kg); ample fibre. Supplements: B12 (1000 µg/week or 250 µg/day), vitamin D 1000 IU/day, algal DHA 200–500 mg/day. Iron-rich (lentils, tofu, fortified cereal) paired with vitamin C (citrus, peppers, berries) for absorption. Calcium from fortified plant milks + tahini + leafy greens; zinc from legumes, seeds, fortified cereal.

22
[6]C · CVD case
Case study: A 47-year-old man's blood test: total cholesterol 6.5 mmol/L, LDL 4.2 mmol/L, HDL 0.9, triglycerides 2.8, blood pressure 145/92, A1C 6.0%, BMI 30, waist 104 cm. Smoker. Sedentary. Design a multi-component nutrition and lifestyle intervention citing DASH and Mediterranean evidence.
Answer Key

Multiple risk factors: hyperlipidaemia, prediabetes, hypertension, central obesity, smoking. Intervention: (1) Replace saturated/trans fats with mono- and polyunsaturated (olive oil, nuts, fatty fish) — Mediterranean pattern (PREDIMED 2013 — 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events). (2) DASH-style: sodium ≤2300 mg/day, more potassium-rich vegetables, fruit, low-fat dairy → ~11 mm Hg systolic reduction in trials. (3) Soluble fibre (oats, legumes, psyllium) lowers LDL. (4) Smoking cessation referral (highest-impact single change). (5) Activity — 150 min/week MVPA + resistance 2 days. (6) Weight management (modest 5–10% loss improves all markers). (7) Recheck labs in 3 months; statin discussion with physician if persists. Reduce alcohol; track sleep and stress.

23
[6]C · Food safety case
Case study — Outbreak: 18 of 32 guests develop bloody diarrhea 24 hours after a community potluck featuring chicken kebabs, undercooked hamburger patties, and a creamy pasta salad. (a) Identify the most likely pathogen(s) and likely source. (b) Outline the household and public-health response. (c) Identify three preventive measures.
Answer Key

(a) Bloody diarrhea + ~24 h incubation suggests EHEC (E. coli O157:H7) from undercooked hamburger patties (typical incubation 1–10 days; bloody diarrhea is a hallmark). Other possibilities: Campylobacter (chicken, 12–72 h, sometimes bloody). Less likely: Staph aureus (very rapid 30 min – 6 h, vomiting more than diarrhea). (b) Household: rehydrate; seek medical care for bloody stool, dehydration, kidney involvement (HUS risk in EHEC); preserve food samples. Public health: report to local public-health unit; ID pathogen; investigate sourcing and handling; trace beef supplier. (c) Prevention: (1) Cook ground beef to 71 °C internal verified by thermometer; (2) Avoid cross-contamination (separate cutting boards, hand-washing); (3) Refrigerate perishables within 2 hours; (4) Keep creamy salads ≤4 °C until serving; (5) Hand-washing 20 s before/after handling food.

24
[7]D · STSE Global & Local
STSE — Connected Local and Global: A Toronto family is reflecting on their food choices through a sustainability and ethics lens. (a) Identify three local-food-system issues they should consider. (b) Identify three global-food-system issues. (c) Propose four practical changes that address both levels simultaneously. (d) Discuss the limits of individual choices versus the need for policy.
Answer Key

(a) Local: household food security, school food access for kids, Indigenous food sovereignty (treaty rights, country foods), support for Ontario farmers, food waste in the home. (b) Global: climate-driven yield disruption, conflict-driven hunger (Sudan, Gaza, etc.), inequitable global trade (high-income subsidies vs low-income farmers), corporate consolidation in agribusiness, ocean health and overfishing. (c) Practical changes: (1) shift toward plant-forward eating (climate + health + reduces global demand on grain-fed livestock); (2) buy Fair Trade for tropical commodities (coffee, cocoa, bananas — supports global farmer income and labour standards); (3) reduce household food waste (climate + global hunger framing + budget); (4) support National School Food Program advocacy + local food banks while pushing for income policy. (d) Limits: individual actions matter as cultural signals and modest aggregate effect; structural drivers (income, policy, trade, corporate concentration) require collective action — vote, advocate, support Indigenous-led food sovereignty work; combine personal practice with civic engagement.

[6]D · Indigenous food sovereignty
Case study: A First Nation in northern Ontario reports that traditional moose hunting has declined as climate change shifts species range, and grocery prices in their community are 2–3× southern Canada. (a) Identify the relevant historical context. (b) Distinguish food security from food sovereignty in this context. (c) Outline two community-led actions and one policy-level action that support Indigenous food sovereignty. (d) Explain how an ally can support without overstepping.
Answer Key

(a) Colonial disruption: treaties restricting hunting/fishing rights; buffalo destruction (1860s–1880s on Plains, analogous patterns elsewhere); residential schools and Sixties Scoop disrupting food knowledge; the imposition of rationed dry goods; climate change accelerating range shifts. (b) Security = access to enough safe nutritious food (often solved by southern-style assistance). Sovereignty = right to define own food systems, including traditional foods, cultural protocols, community governance. (c) Community-led: hunter-support programs that fund equipment + travel and share country food with elders + families; community freezers stocked with country foods; community-led traditional-food markets and youth education in harvesting. Policy: federal Nutrition North reform with community-led oversight; treaty-rights protection enabling harvest where currently restricted; investment in Indigenous-led food research (e.g., First Nations Food, Nutrition and Environment Study). (d) Allies: listen to and amplify Indigenous voices; learn before speaking; donate to Indigenous-led organisations; advocate for treaty rights; not appropriate to "save" or design solutions externally — community self-determination is core.
