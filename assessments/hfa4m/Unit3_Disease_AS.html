Topic 3.1 — Cardiovascular Disease
Question 1
Which lipoprotein is commonly called "bad cholesterol" because it delivers cholesterol to artery walls?
Solution: LDL = low-density lipoprotein, delivers cholesterol to tissues; oxidised LDL is the precursor of atherosclerotic plaque. HDL retrieves cholesterol from tissues.
Question 2
The DASH diet was specifically designed to:
Solution: DASH = Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. Emphasises vegetables, fruit, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein; limits sodium. Reduces systolic BP by ~11 mm Hg in trials.
Question 3
What is the chronic-disease-risk-reduction sodium intake target in Canada?
Solution: Health Canada / DRI: AI = 1500 mg/day; chronic-disease-risk-reduction value = 2300 mg/day. Canadians average ~2760 mg/day.
Topic 3.2 — Type 2 Diabetes
Question 4
The fundamental problem in type 2 diabetes is:
Solution: Type 2: insulin resistance + eventual β-cell failure. Type 1 is autoimmune destruction of β-cells requiring lifelong insulin.
Question 5
A patient's A1C is 6.5%. This indicates:
Solution: Diabetes Canada: A1C ≥6.5% is the diagnostic threshold. Prediabetes = 6.0–6.4%. A1C represents average glucose over ~2–3 months.
Topic 3.3 — Cancer & Obesity
Question 6
The IARC (2015) classified which food as a Group 1 carcinogen (causes cancer in humans)?
Solution: Processed meat is Group 1 (carcinogenic to humans), increasing colorectal cancer risk ~18% per 50 g/day. Red meat is 2A (probable). Alcohol is also Group 1.
Question 7
A 30-year-old adult is 1.70 m tall and weighs 92 kg. Calculate BMI to one decimal.
Solution: BMI = 92 / (1.70²) = 92 / 2.89 = 31.8 kg/m² — within the "obesity" range. BMI is a screening tool; clinical assessment of overall health matters more than the single number.
Question 8
Which dietary factor is MOST protective against osteoporosis?
Solution: Calcium + vitamin D (for absorption) + weight-bearing/resistance exercise build and maintain bone. Protein, vitamin K, magnesium also matter; smoking, excess alcohol, sedentary behaviour increase risk.
Topic 3.4 — Foodborne Illness
Question 9
The temperature "danger zone" for food safety is:
Solution: 4–60 °C is the range where most pathogenic bacteria multiply rapidly. Keep cold foods ≤4 °C and hot foods ≥60 °C.
Question 10
Safe minimum internal cooking temperature for poultry:
Solution: Poultry: 74 °C (165 °F) — to inactivate Salmonella and Campylobacter. Ground beef 71 °C; whole cuts 63 °C with rest; reheated leftovers 74 °C.
Question 11
HACCP stands for:
Solution: HACCP is the systematic food-safety framework that identifies biological/chemical/physical hazards and sets critical control points throughout production.
Question 12
Which is the SINGLE most effective preventive action against foodborne illness?
Solution: Hand-washing breaks the fecal-oral and cross-contamination routes responsible for most foodborne illness. Cook, chill, separate are the next steps.
