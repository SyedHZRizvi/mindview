Topic 1.1 — Determinants of Food Choice
Question 1
Which is the BEST example of an environmental determinant of food choice?
Solution: (b) is the food environment — physical proximity of unhealthy vs healthy retail. (a) biological; (c) cultural; (d) psychological. The "food swamp" pattern (high access to unhealthy options) is a well-documented environmental determinant.
Question 2
PROOF (University of Toronto) reported approximately what percentage of Canadian households experienced food insecurity in 2022?
Solution: About 18.4% of Canadian households were food-insecure in 2022 — the highest rate since national tracking began. PROOF distinguishes marginal, moderate, and severe insecurity.
Topic 1.2 — Macronutrients
Question 3
A nutrition label lists 30 g carbohydrate, 12 g protein, 8 g fat. Calculate the total kilocalories.
Solution: CHO: 30×4 = 120 kcal. Protein: 12×4 = 48 kcal. Fat: 8×9 = 72 kcal. Total = 120 + 48 + 72 = 240 kcal.
Question 4
Which fat is most consistently associated with raising LDL ("bad") cholesterol?
Solution: Saturated fats (animal fats, palm/coconut oil) and trans fats (industrially partially hydrogenated, now banned in Canada) raise LDL. Mono- and poly-unsaturated fats lower LDL. Dietary cholesterol has a smaller effect than saturated fat on most healthy adults.
Question 5
A complete protein supplies all nine essential amino acids in adequate amounts. Which combination is best described as "complementary proteins"?
Solution: Grains (rice) are limited in lysine but rich in methionine; legumes (beans) are limited in methionine but rich in lysine. Combined, they supply all nine essential amino acids — a classic complementary protein pair.
Topic 1.3 — Micronutrients
Question 6
Which vitamin is fat-soluble and produced in skin from cholesterol when exposed to UVB?
Solution: Vitamin D is the fat-soluble vitamin synthesised from 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin under UVB exposure. In Canada (north of 40° N), winter synthesis is essentially zero — supplementation is widely recommended.
Question 7
A strict vegan with no supplement use is MOST at risk for deficiency of:
Solution: Vitamin B12 is virtually absent from plant foods unless they are fortified. Untreated deficiency causes megaloblastic anaemia and neurological symptoms. Vegans must consume fortified foods or supplements.
Topic 1.4 — Digestion & Energy Balance
Question 8
Most of the absorption of nutrients takes place in the:
Solution: Villi and microvilli in the small intestine give roughly 250 m² of absorptive surface — most amino acids, monosaccharides, fatty acids, vitamins, and minerals are absorbed here.
Question 9
Estimate BMR using Mifflin–St Jeor (female): 60 kg, 165 cm, 16 years old. (BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a − 161)
Solution: BMR = 10(60) + 6.25(165) − 5(16) − 161 = 600 + 1031.25 − 80 − 161 = 1390.25 kcal/day. Multiply by an activity factor (1.2–1.9) to estimate TDEE.
Question 10
Canada's Food Guide (2019) recommends what proportion of the plate be vegetables and fruits?
Solution: Health Canada (2019) replaced the four-food-group rainbow with a plate: ½ vegetables and fruits, ¼ whole grains, ¼ protein foods (including plant proteins), with water as the recommended drink.
Question 11
Which Dietary Reference Intake (DRI) represents the highest daily intake unlikely to pose a risk of adverse effects?
Solution: UL = Tolerable Upper Intake Level. RDA meets needs of 97–98%; AI is used when an RDA cannot be set; EAR meets the needs of 50%.
Question 12
Approximate Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range (AMDR) for fat (% of daily kilocalories) for adults?
Solution: AMDR for adults: carbohydrate 45–65%, fat 20–35%, protein 10–35%. Below 20% fat risks essential-fatty-acid deficiency; above 35% may displace fibre-rich carbohydrate.
