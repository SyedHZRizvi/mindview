Topic 2.1 — Pregnancy & Infant Nutrition
Question 1
Folic acid supplementation BEFORE conception reduces the risk of which condition by about 70%?
Solution: Adequate folate around conception reduces NTDs by ~70%. The neural tube closes by ~day 28; Health Canada recommends 0.4 mg folic acid daily for anyone who could become pregnant.
Question 2
The WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first:
Solution: The WHO, Health Canada, and Canadian Paediatric Society recommend exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, with continued breastfeeding alongside complementary foods to at least 2 years.
Question 3
Why should honey NOT be given to an infant under 12 months?
Solution: Infants under 12 months have immature gut flora that cannot prevent Clostridium botulinum spores from germinating and producing toxin — risk of infant botulism. Avoid honey, including raw or pasteurised, before first birthday.
Topic 2.2 — Adolescent & Adult Nutrition
Question 4
Approximate calcium RDA during adolescence (9–18 years):
Solution: Calcium RDA peaks at 1300 mg/day in adolescents (9–18) to support peak bone-mass accretion. ~90% of peak bone mass is reached by age 18.
Question 5
A 14-year-old athlete drinks energy drinks daily. The Canadian Paediatric Society recommends an adolescent's caffeine intake stay below approximately:
Solution: The Canadian Paediatric Society advises adolescents stay under 100 mg/day — about one cup of brewed coffee or ~2.5 mg per kg body weight. Energy drinks vary widely (some exceed 200 mg per can).
Topic 2.3 — Older Adults & Eating Disorders
Question 6
Sarcopenia in older adults refers to:
Solution: Sarcopenia = age-related loss of muscle. Slowed by resistance exercise and adequate protein (1.0–1.2 g/kg/day for older adults — higher than the 0.8 g/kg RDA for younger adults).
Question 7
Why is vitamin D supplementation often recommended for older adults in Canada?
Solution: Reduced skin synthesis with age + Canada's northern latitudes + indoor lifestyle = low vitamin D status. Health Canada recommends a 400 IU/day supplement for everyone over 50.
Question 8
Which eating disorder is characterised by recurrent binges followed by compensatory behaviours like self-induced vomiting?
Solution: Bulimia nervosa = binge + compensatory behaviour. Anorexia = restriction + low weight. BED = binges without regular compensatory behaviour. ARFID = avoidance not driven by body-image concerns.
Question 9
A pregnant woman in the second trimester needs approximately how many additional kilocalories per day compared to her pre-pregnancy needs?
Solution: ~+340 kcal/day in the 2nd trimester (and +452 in the 3rd). "Eating for two" overstates the need; quality and specific nutrients (folate, iron, iodine, DHA) matter more than total calories.
Question 10
Refeeding syndrome — a potentially fatal electrolyte shift — is most associated with which intervention?
Solution: Refeeding syndrome occurs when carbohydrate is reintroduced quickly to a malnourished patient, causing dangerous shifts in phosphate, potassium, magnesium. Refeeding must start at low energy and be monitored medically.
