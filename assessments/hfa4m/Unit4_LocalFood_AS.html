Topic 4.1 — Canadian Food Production
Question 1
Which Canadian agricultural sectors operate under "supply management"?
Solution: Supply management covers dairy, poultry (chicken, turkey), and eggs — production quotas, import controls, price-setting via marketing boards. Other sectors face free-market commodity prices.
Question 2
In Canada, "Canada Organic" certification is administered by which federal agency?
Solution: CFIA administers the Canada Organic Regime — three-year transition from synthetic inputs, no GMOs, no synthetic pesticides except a short approved list.
Topic 4.2 — GMOs, Pesticides, Sustainability
Question 3
Approximately what percentage of canola, soybeans, and corn grown in Canada are genetically modified?
Solution: More than 90% of canola, soybeans, and corn grown in Canada are GM (herbicide-tolerant and/or Bt insect-resistant). Canada does not require mandatory GMO labelling.
Question 4
Which Canadian agency regulates pesticide approvals and maximum residue limits?
Solution: PMRA (under Health Canada) regulates pesticide approvals under the Pest Control Products Act. CFIA enforces compliance with maximum residue limits.
Topic 4.3 — Food Processing & Labelling
Question 5
In the NOVA classification, which category includes commercial chicken nuggets, frozen lasagne, and energy drinks?
Solution: NOVA 4 (UPF) = industrial formulations with ingredients rarely used in home kitchens (HFCS, modified starches, hydrolysed proteins, emulsifiers, artificial flavours/colours). Canadians average ~48% of energy from UPF.
Question 6
On a Canadian Nutrition Facts table, what is a useful interpretation of "%DV"?
Solution: Health Canada's guidance: 5% DV or less is "a little"; 15% DV or more is "a lot." Apply to fibre, vitamins, minerals (want a lot) and saturated fat, sodium, sugar (want a little).
Topic 4.4 — Food Security in Canada
Question 7
What is the dominant driver of household food insecurity in Canada?
Solution: Food insecurity in Canada is overwhelmingly an income problem (PROOF research). Canada produces ample food; uneven income distribution drives access gaps.
Question 8
Which 2024 federal initiative aims to expand school meals across Canada?
Solution: The National School Food Program was launched in 2024 as Canada's first federal investment in school meals — historically a gap among G7 countries.
Topic 4.5 — Indigenous Food Sovereignty
Question 9
The "Three Sisters" Indigenous polyculture system refers to:
Solution: Three Sisters = corn + beans + squash, interplanted in a traditional Haudenosaunee/Anishinaabe agriculture system. Corn provides structure; beans fix nitrogen; squash shades soil and suppresses weeds.
Question 10
What is "Indigenous food sovereignty"?
Solution: Indigenous food sovereignty = the right of Indigenous peoples to define their own food systems — what foods, how produced, by whom. Four principles: food is sacred; cultural participation in harvesting; self-determination; community-based institutions.
