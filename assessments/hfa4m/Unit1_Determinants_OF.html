Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
List the six classes of nutrients and state for each one example food source and one major function in the body.
Answer Key

Carbohydrate (grains, fruit) — primary energy source. Lipid (oils, butter, fatty fish) — energy storage, membranes, hormone precursor. Protein (meat, legumes) — structure, enzymes, transport. Vitamins (vegetables, fruit, dairy) — coenzymes, antioxidants. Minerals (dairy, leafy greens, lean meat) — bone, electrolytes, oxygen transport. Water — solvent, thermoregulation, transport.

2
[2]
Which provides 9 kcal per gram?
Answer Key

C. Fat = 9 kcal/g. Carbohydrate and protein = 4; alcohol = 7.

3
[2]
Which Dietary Reference Intake (DRI) is used to make recommendations for an individual?
Answer Key

B. RDA covers 97–98% of healthy individuals; EAR is used to assess population intake (50%); UL is the safety ceiling.

4
[3]
Describe the three main components of "energy out" and roughly what proportion each contributes to total daily energy expenditure.
Answer Key

BMR (basal metabolic rate) ~60–70%: energy for vital functions at complete rest. TEF (thermic effect of food) ~10%: energy used to digest, absorb, metabolise food (protein has highest TEF). Physical activity ~15–30%, highly variable.

5
[3]
Distinguish between essential and non-essential nutrients. Give two examples of each.
Answer Key

Essential — the body cannot synthesise enough; must come from diet (e.g., 9 essential amino acids like lysine; essential fatty acids linoleic and α-linolenic; vitamin C; vitamin B12). Non-essential — the body can make from precursors (e.g., glucose from gluconeogenesis when needed; some amino acids like alanine).

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
6
[4]
A protein bar lists: 24 g CHO, 12 g fat, 18 g protein per bar. (a) Calculate total kcal. (b) Calculate % energy from each macronutrient. (c) Comment on whether the bar fits within the AMDR for adults.
Answer Key

(a) CHO 24×4 = 96; fat 12×9 = 108; protein 18×4 = 72. Total = 276 kcal. (b) %CHO = 96/276 ≈ 35%; %fat = 108/276 ≈ 39%; %protein = 72/276 ≈ 26%. (c) AMDR: CHO 45–65%, fat 20–35%, protein 10–35%. The bar is high in fat (above 35%) and low in CHO (below 45%). Acceptable as occasional snack but not a balanced meal.

7
[4]
A 17-year-old female student is 60 kg, 168 cm tall, lightly active (factor 1.375). (a) Estimate BMR using Mifflin–St Jeor (female: BMR = 10w + 6.25h − 5a − 161). (b) Estimate TDEE. (c) Comment on whether a 1200-kcal/day weight-loss diet would be appropriate.
Answer Key

(a) BMR = 10(60) + 6.25(168) − 5(17) − 161 = 600 + 1050 − 85 − 161 = 1404 kcal/day. (b) TDEE = 1404 × 1.375 ≈ 1931 kcal/day. (c) 1200 kcal/day is below BMR — too restrictive for an adolescent. May impair growth, menstrual function, bone development. Recommend higher intake (~1700–1800 kcal/day if weight loss is goal) and emphasise food quality, physical activity, and adolescent-appropriate guidance under medical supervision.

8
[4]
Design an investigation to test whether teenagers in your school can accurately identify which food in a pair contains more sodium using the Nutrition Facts label. Identify variables and how you would measure outcome.
Answer Key

Hypothesis: students with a brief label-reading lesson identify higher-sodium item more accurately than untrained peers. IV: lesson (yes/no) — randomly assigned. DV: number of correct identifications in 10 paired comparisons. Controlled: same food pairs, time limit, no calculators, individual completion. Ethics: voluntary, anonymous. Analysis: compare mean correct in trained vs untrained group; report % accuracy + 95% CI; test for significance. Limitations: short-term effect; doesn't measure transfer to behaviour.

Answer Key

10
[4]
In a labelled diagram or detailed description, trace the digestion of a turkey sandwich on whole-grain bread with cheese, from mouth to small intestine. Name the enzymes involved.
Answer Key

Mouth: salivary amylase begins starch digestion; teeth fragment. Esophagus: bolus transit. Stomach: HCl + pepsin begin protein digestion (turkey, cheese); gastric lipase begins lipid hydrolysis. Small intestine duodenum: pancreatic amylase (finishes starch), lipase + bile (emulsifies and digests cheese fat), trypsin/chymotrypsin (proteins → peptides), brush-border peptidases → amino acids. Absorption: glucose and amino acids → capillaries → hepatic portal vein → liver. Fatty acids/monoglycerides → enterocytes → re-esterified → chylomicrons → lymph → blood. Calcium absorbed in duodenum (vitamin D-dependent).

11
[5]
Build a comparison table contrasting saturated fats, monounsaturated fats, polyunsaturated omega-3 fats, and trans fats. Columns: chemical feature, example food source, effect on LDL, recommendation.
Answer Key

Saturated: no C=C; butter, fatty meat, palm/coconut oil; raises LDL; limit. Monounsaturated: one C=C; olive oil, avocado, canola; lowers LDL; recommended. PUFA omega-3: multiple C=C with first at omega-3 position; fatty fish, flax, walnuts; lowers LDL/raises HDL/anti-inflammatory; recommended (2 servings fish/week). Trans (industrial): partially hydrogenated; banned in Canada (2018); raises LDL, lowers HDL; avoid entirely.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[6]
Case Study — Label Audit: Two breakfast cereals, per 30 g serving. Cereal A: 120 kcal, 1 g sat fat (5% DV), 130 mg Na (6% DV), 10 g sugar (incl. 9 g added), 1 g fibre (4% DV), 2 g protein. Cereal B: 110 kcal, 0.5 g sat fat (3% DV), 80 mg Na (3% DV), 5 g sugar (incl. 2 g added), 4 g fibre (14% DV), 4 g protein. (a) Apply the 5%/15% DV rule. (b) Recommend one and justify with three reasons drawn from the labels.
Answer Key

(a) Both are "a little" sat fat and sodium (≤5% DV). A is "a little" fibre (4%); B is closer to "a lot" (14%). (b) Recommend Cereal B: (1) Much less added sugar — 2 g vs 9 g; (2) Higher fibre — 14% DV vs 4% DV (better satiety, gut health); (3) More protein — 4 g vs 2 g (more satiating). Lower sodium is a bonus. Look at ingredient list: B likely starts with whole grain, A likely with sugar.

13
[6]
STSE — Food Environment: A student lives in an urban neighbourhood with 14 fast-food outlets and no grocery store within 1 km (a "food swamp"). Identify two systemic interventions (policy or community-level) and two individual strategies. Reflect on the relative impact of each level.
Answer Key

Systemic: (1) Municipal zoning that requires a minimum number of healthful retailers per neighbourhood or limits fast-food density near schools; (2) Subsidised mobile-market or community food-hub programs; living-wage and basic-income policy to address affordability. Individual: (1) Weekly menu planning + grocery trip to a larger store; (2) Meal preparation in batches and freezing portable meals. Reflection: systemic changes scale to all affected residents and address root causes; individual strategies require time, money, and skills that are themselves shaped by systemic conditions. Effective policy outperforms exhortation to "make better choices."
