Atomic Theory & Periodic Trends
Question 1
Which scientist's gold-foil experiment provided evidence for a dense, positively charged atomic nucleus?
Solution:
Ernest Rutherford's 1911 gold-foil experiment showed most α-particles passed through but a small fraction were sharply deflected, indicating a small dense positive nucleus.
Question 2
Across a period (left to right), atomic radius generally:
Solution:
Across a period, \(Z_{\text{eff}}\) increases without adding a new shell, pulling the electron cloud closer. Atomic radius decreases.
Question 3
Arrange in order of increasing first ionization energy: K, Ca, F, Cl.
Solution:
IE increases up a group and across a period. K (group 1, period 4) lowest. Ca (group 2) higher. Cl (period 3, group 17) much higher. F (period 2, group 17) highest. K < Ca < Cl < F.
Ionic & Covalent Bonding
Question 4
Predict the bond type in MgO using \(\Delta\text{EN}\): EN(Mg)=1.3, EN(O)=3.4.
Solution:
\(\Delta\text{EN} = 3.4 - 1.3 = 2.1 \gt 1.7\) → ionic (and Mg is a metal, O is a non-metal).
Question 5
Write the formula for the ionic compound formed between aluminum and oxygen.
Solution:
Al³⁺ + O²⁻ → crossover gives Al₂O₃.
Question 6
Which compound is a polar molecule?
Solution:
CO₂ (linear), CCl₄ (tetrahedral), CH₄ (tetrahedral) are symmetric — bond dipoles cancel. H₂O is bent with two polar O–H bonds; dipoles add → polar molecule.
Lewis Structures & VSEPR Shapes
Question 7
How many lone pairs are on the central atom in NH₃?
Solution:
N has 5 valence e⁻. Three N–H bonds use 6 electrons (3 from N + 3 from H). One lone pair remains on N. Answer: 1.
Question 8
VSEPR shape of CH₄:
Solution:
AX₄ (4 bonded atoms, 0 lone pairs) → tetrahedral, bond angle 109.5°.
Question 9
Approximate H–O–H bond angle in water?
Solution:
AX₂E₂ (2 bonded, 2 lone pairs). Lone pairs compress the angle below 109.5° → ~104.5°.
Intermolecular Forces
Question 10
Which substance has hydrogen bonding?
Solution:
H-bonding requires H directly bonded to N, O, or F. Only HF qualifies. H₂S has H bonded to S (not strong enough); CH₃Cl has dipole-dipole but no H-bond.
Question 11
Which has the highest boiling point?
Solution:
All non-polar — only London dispersion forces. LDFs grow with molar mass / electron count. I₂ (largest) has the highest b.p. (184 °C, a solid at room temperature).
Question 12
Why is the boiling point of H₂O (100 °C) much higher than H₂S (−60 °C), even though both are bent AX₂E₂ molecules?
Solution:
O is electronegative enough to form strong H-bonds; S is not (EN too low). Hydrogen bonding in H₂O raises the boiling point dramatically. Boiling breaks IMFs, not covalent bonds.
