Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
13
[4]B · Ch1
A snack contains 30 g carb, 6 g protein, 10 g fat. Calculate total kcal and %energy from each macronutrient.
Answer Key

Carb 30 × 4 = 120, protein 6 × 4 = 24, fat 10 × 9 = 90. Total = 234 kcal. %carb = 51%, %protein = 10%, %fat = 38%. Fat is above the 35% AMDR upper limit.

14
[4]B · Ch2
Calculate TDEE for a 70 kg, 175 cm, 17-year-old male using Mifflin–St Jeor with activity factor 1.55.
Answer Key

BMR = 10(70) + 6.25(175) − 5(17) + 5 = 700 + 1 093.75 − 85 + 5 = 1 713.75 kcal/day. TDEE = 1 713.75 × 1.55 ≈ 2 656 kcal/day.

15
[5]A · Inquiry
Design an experiment to test whether vitamin C retention differs between steamed and boiled broccoli. Identify IV, DV, three controlled variables, and the measurement method.
Answer Key

IV: cooking method (steam 5 min vs boil 10 min). DV: vitamin C concentration (mg/100 g) by DCPIP titration or HPLC. Controlled: same broccoli variety + batch, same start mass, same equipment, same kitchen, same observer. Replicate ≥3× per condition. Predict: steaming retains ~85%, boiling ~45%.

16
[4]C · Ch4
Four hours after a BBQ, two guests vomit; by 24 h six are sick. Hamburgers (well done), potato salad (3 h outside, 30 °C), spinach salad, cake, lemonade. Identify the most likely pathogen and the food vehicle.
Answer Key

Staphylococcus aureus (preformed heat-stable toxin → rapid 1–6 h onset; vomiting prominent). Food vehicle: potato salad with mayo — protein-rich, in danger zone 3 h. The well-done burgers, low-water-activity cake, and acidic lemonade are unlikely culprits.

17
[4]D · Ch5
Compare the CO₂-eq emissions of a 200 g beef sirloin (60 kg CO₂-eq/kg) vs 1 cup lentil curry (0.9 kg CO₂-eq/kg cooked, ≈0.25 kg). Calculate.
Answer Key

Beef: 0.2 × 60 = 12 kg CO₂-eq. Lentils: 0.25 × 0.9 = 0.23 kg CO₂-eq. Beef emits ~52× more CO₂-eq per serving than lentils — the single biggest dietary lever for climate footprint.

18
[4]A · Inquiry
A celebrity Instagram post claims "Alkaline water cures cancer". Apply A1.4-level critique using three checks: mechanism, evidence, source.
Answer Key

Mechanism: blood pH is buffered at 7.35–7.45; water cannot meaningfully shift it. Evidence: no RCT supports alkaline water for cancer. Source: celebrity, often selling branded water; conflict of interest. Conclusion: claim is implausible and unsupported.

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
19
[5]B · Ch1
Explain the difference between saturated, monounsaturated, polyunsaturated, and trans fats. Refer to structure, food source, and health effect.
Answer Key

Saturated — no C=C; straight tails (butter, beef); excess raises LDL. Monounsaturated — one C=C kink (olive oil); lowers LDL when replacing saturated. Polyunsaturated — multiple C=C (sunflower oil, fish oil); contains essential omega-3/6; lowers LDL. Trans — straight industrial chains; uniquely raise LDL + lower HDL; banned in Canadian food production since 17 Sept 2018.

20
[5]B · Ch2
Describe the three stages of cellular respiration. State the location and key product of each.
Answer Key

(1) Glycolysis — cytoplasm — net 2 ATP + 2 NADH + 2 pyruvate. (2) Pyruvate oxidation + Krebs — mitochondrial matrix — 6 CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂. (3) ETC + chemiosmosis — inner mitochondrial membrane — ~26 ATP; O₂ → H₂O.

21
[5]C · Ch3
Write a 1-page text-only "fact sheet" for a 14-year-old vegetarian student titled "Four Nutrients to Watch on a Plant-Based Diet". Cite RDA targets where relevant.
Answer Key

(1) Vitamin B12 — animal-only naturally; vegans need fortified plant milks, nutritional yeast, or supplement (RDA 2.4 µg/day). (2) Vitamin D — winter UV-B insufficient at Canadian latitudes; aim 600 IU/day from fortified beverages + supplement. (3) Iron — plant iron is non-heme (5–15% absorbed); RDA 15 mg/day for menstruating teens; pair with vitamin C (oranges, peppers, broccoli). (4) Calcium — RDA 1 300 mg/day at 9–18; use fortified soy milk, calcium-set tofu, kale, almonds.

22
[5]C · Ch4
Construct a 1-page kitchen poster titled "Four Cs of Food Safety". Include action steps for each.
Answer Key

CLEAN — wash hands 20 s; sanitise surfaces with diluted bleach. SEPARATE — separate cutting boards; raw meat on bottom shelf. COOK — thermometer in thickest part; ground beef 71 °C, ground poultry/leftovers 74 °C, whole cuts beef/pork 63 °C + 3 min rest. CHILL — refrigerate within 2 h (1 h if > 32 °C); fridge ≤ 4 °C, freezer ≤ −18 °C. Thaw in fridge, never on counter.

23
[5]D · Ch5
Compare the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet in a 3-row table: target health outcome, key food emphasis, level of supporting evidence.
Answer Key

Mediterranean — CVD prevention. Olive oil, fish, legumes, whole grains, nuts, vegetables, moderate wine. PREDIMED 2013: ~30% reduction in major CVD events. DASH — hypertension reduction. Vegetables, fruit, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein, sodium 1 500–2 300 mg. Appel et al. NEJM 1997: SBP ↓6–11 mm Hg in 2 weeks. Both share whole-food, plant-forward emphasis; DASH is more sodium-restrictive.

Part D — Application [25 marks]
24
[5]B · Ch1
Case Study — Lunchbox. Tariq packs daily: white-bread peanut-butter sandwich, an apple, 500 mL juice. Evaluate using the 2019 Food Guide. Suggest three improvements.
Answer Key

Strengths: PB has protein + unsaturated fat; apple provides fibre & vitamin C. Weaknesses: white bread refined; juice (500 mL) high added sugar; no vegetables; no calcium/D source; no omega-3. Improvements: (1) whole-grain bread; (2) replace half juice with water + add raw vegetables; (3) add fortified soy milk or yogurt; (4) add canned tuna or hemp seeds for omega-3.

25
[5]B · Ch2
Case Study — Energy balance. 17-year-old male, BMR 1 800 kcal, activity factor 1.7, currently eats 2 800 kcal/day. Determine deficit/surplus and predict weekly weight change (7 700 kcal ≈ 1 kg fat).
Answer Key

TDEE = 1 800 × 1.7 = 3 060 kcal/day. Intake 2 800 → deficit 260 kcal/day. Weekly deficit 260 × 7 = 1 820 kcal/week → predicted weight loss ≈ 0.24 kg/week (~240 g). For muscle gain, add ~500 kcal/day with adequate protein.

26
[5]C · Ch3
Case Study — Pregnancy meal plan. 65 kg woman, second trimester. Calculate her protein need (1.1 g/kg + 25 g) and design one balanced day of meals.
Answer Key

Need: 1.1 × 65 + 25 = ~97 g protein/day; energy ~2 200 + 340 = 2 540 kcal; folate 600 µg + 400 µg supplement; iron 27 mg; calcium 1 000+ mg. Sample day: Breakfast — oatmeal + fortified soy milk + berries + nuts. Snack — Greek yogurt + apple. Lunch — lentil & spinach salad with feta + whole-grain bread. Snack — hummus + carrots. Dinner — 100 g baked salmon + brown rice + roasted broccoli. Drinks: water + 1 cup milk at bedtime + folic-acid supplement.

27
[5]C · Ch4
Case Study — Anaphylaxis at school. A peer with known peanut allergy eats a granola bar and develops hives, lip swelling, wheezing, and dizziness within 5 minutes. Outline the emergency response with epinephrine details.
Answer Key

Recognise anaphylaxis (hives + airway + cardiovascular signs). Inject EpiPen IM into outer thigh (through clothing if needed), hold 3 s. Call 911. Lay him down (sit up if breathing dominant); legs elevated. If symptoms persist 5–15 min and a second EpiPen is available, give it. Stay with him. Ontario's Sabrina's Law (2005) mandates anaphylaxis policies, staff training, and accessible epinephrine in schools.

28
[5]D · Ch5
STSE — Food insecurity policy. Cite the 2022 Canadian household food-insecurity rate. Name two federal programs that aim to reduce it. Identify the most-evidence-supported population-level lever.
Answer Key

Rate: ~18.4% of Canadian households in 2022 (Statistics Canada). Federal programs: (1) Canada Child Benefit — demonstrably reduces child food insecurity (research by PROOF/University of Toronto). (2) Nutrition North Canada — subsidy for remote Northern food costs. (3) The 2024 National School Food Program ($1 B over 5 years). Most-evidence-supported lever: income adequacy — raising social-assistance rates above the Nutritious Food Basket cost. Food banks help in crises but do not solve the underlying income problem.
