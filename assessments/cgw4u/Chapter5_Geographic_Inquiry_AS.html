Topic 5.1 — Inquiry Process & Concepts
Question 1
The four concepts of geographic thinking in the Ontario CGW4U curriculum are spatial significance, patterns & trends, interrelationships, and:
Solution: The four concepts of geographic thinking are spatial significance, patterns & trends, interrelationships, and geographic perspective. They guide inquiry questions and structure analysis.
Question 2
A well-framed geographic inquiry question is best characterised by being:
Solution: Strong questions are focused, researchable, and open-ended enough to invite analysis. Yes/no answers and confirmation-seeking framings produce weak inquiries.
Topic 5.2 — Sources & Evidence
Question 3
The CRAAP test for evaluating sources stands for:
Solution: CRAAP = Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose. A useful checklist for evaluating any source before relying on it.
Question 4
The OCAP principles for Indigenous data sovereignty refer to:
Solution: OCAP = Ownership, Control, Access, Possession. Developed by the First Nations Information Governance Centre, these principles govern how data about First Nations communities should be handled.
Topic 5.3 — GIS & Remote Sensing
Question 5
A GIS layer storing population density as a grid of cells is in which data model?
Solution: Raster. Continuous phenomena (temperature, elevation, population density, NDVI) are well represented as grids of cells. Discrete features (cities, roads, boundaries) are typically vectors.
Question 6
Calculate NDVI: a pixel records near-infrared (NIR) reflectance 0.50 and red reflectance 0.10. Compute NDVI (to two decimals).
Solution: NDVI = (NIR − RED)/(NIR + RED) = (0.50 − 0.10)/(0.50 + 0.10) = 0.40/0.60 ≈ 0.67. NDVI > 0.6 indicates dense, healthy vegetation. Water has NDVI < 0; built surfaces near 0.
Topic 5.4 — Cartographic Communication
Question 7
A choropleth map is the most appropriate visualisation for:
Solution: Choropleth maps shade enumeration units by rate or ratio. Raw counts are best as proportional symbols (otherwise large countries look "high" just because they are large). Flows are flow maps; elevation is contours/raster.
Question 8
For a quantitative variable ranging from very low to very high with a meaningful zero, the most appropriate colour scheme is:
Solution: Sequential — single hue from light (low) to dark (high) for ordered data with one direction. Diverging is for data with above-and-below-reference values. Qualitative is for unordered categories.
Topic 5.5 — Culminating Inquiry
Question 9
In an essay or policy brief, a counter-argument should be:
Solution: Strong arguments engage the strongest version of the opposing view, then either rebut it or qualify the thesis in light of it. Ignoring counter-arguments weakens the analysis.
Question 10
Calculate: A culminating-task inquiry uses 4 peer-reviewed sources, 3 government reports, 2 NGO briefs, and 1 news article. What is the share of peer-reviewed sources?
Solution: 4/10 = 40%. Strong inquiries triangulate across source types — peer-reviewed for analytical depth, government/NGO for institutional perspective, news for currency and on-the-ground accounts.
