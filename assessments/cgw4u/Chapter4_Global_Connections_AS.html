Topic 4.1 — Globalization & Comparative Advantage
Question 1
Comparative advantage means a country should specialise in goods for which it has:
Solution: Lowest opportunity cost (Ricardo, 1817). A country can have lower productivity in both goods but still gain from trade by specialising where its relative cost is lowest.
Question 2
Calculate: Country A can produce either 10 units of wheat or 4 units of steel per worker-hour. Country B can produce 6 wheat or 3 steel. Which country has comparative advantage in steel?
Solution: Country A's opportunity cost of 1 steel = 10/4 = 2.5 wheat. Country B's = 6/3 = 2 wheat. Country B gives up less wheat per steel, so B has comparative advantage in steel. Country A has comparative advantage in wheat (0.4 vs 0.5 steel per wheat).
Topic 4.2 — Trade Agreements
Question 3
CUSMA/USMCA replaced NAFTA when?
Solution: CUSMA entered into force 1 July 2020. NAFTA was signed in 1992 and operative 1994.
Question 4
The WTO most-favoured-nation (MFN) principle requires that:
Solution: MFN requires non-discrimination — the best terms extended to one member must be extended to all WTO members. Exceptions: regional FTAs (Article XXIV) and Generalised System of Preferences for developing countries.
Topic 4.3 — Transnational Corporations
Question 5
According to UNCTAD, transnational corporations account for approximately what share of global GDP?
Solution: About one-third of global GDP and over half of world trade (much of it intra-firm). The 100 largest TNCs alone produce more than 5% of world GDP.
Question 6
The 2021 OECD agreement set a global minimum corporate tax rate of:
Solution: 15% minimum corporate tax (Pillar Two of the OECD/G20 BEPS framework), agreed by 140+ jurisdictions in October 2021. Canada legislated implementation effective 2024.
Topic 4.4 — IMF & World Bank
Question 7
The IMF's primary role is:
Solution: IMF surveils economies and lends short-term during BoP crises. Long-term development lending is the World Bank's role. Both were founded at Bretton Woods (1944).
Question 8
Calculate: A country has external debt $90 billion and GDP $200 billion. Compute debt-to-GDP and classify it (typical IMF watch threshold ~60% for emerging economies).
Solution: 90/200 = 0.45 = 45%. Below the 60% threshold — typically sustainable. The watch threshold is higher for advanced economies (Canada's federal debt-to-GDP ~42%; Japan ~250%).
Topic 4.5 — Supply Chains
Question 9
China's share of global rare-earth processing is approximately:
Solution: Over 90% of rare-earth element processing is in China. China also handles ~60% of global lithium refining and ~80% of cobalt refining. Mining is more distributed; refining is the choke point.
Question 10
The "smile curve" of value capture suggests that the highest value-added in a global value chain is captured at:
Solution: The smile curve is U-shaped: high value capture at design (left tip) and branding/services (right tip), low at middle (assembly). This is why countries pursuing development climb the curve toward design and brand ownership.
Topic 4.6 — Migration & Remittances
Question 11
Roughly what percentage of the world's population lives outside their country of birth?
Solution: About 281 million people — 3.6% of world population (UN Population Division 2024). The percentage has roughly tripled since 1970.
Question 12
Calculate: The Philippines received ~$40 billion in remittances in 2024 against a GDP of ~$465 billion. Compute remittances as a share of GDP (to one decimal).
Solution: 40/465 ≈ 0.086 = 8.6%. Among the largest remittance recipients in absolute terms. Tonga, Tajikistan, and Lebanon have remittance-to-GDP ratios exceeding 30%.
