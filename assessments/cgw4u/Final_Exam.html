Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[3]B · Quality of Life
Name the three dimensions of the Human Development Index and the indicator(s) used for each.
Answer Key

Long & healthy life — life expectancy at birth. Knowledge — mean + expected years of schooling. Decent standard of living — GNI per capita (PPP). Combined as geometric mean.

2
[3]C · Climate
State the pre-industrial and current (2024) atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and the major source of the increase.
Answer Key

Pre-industrial (~1750) ≈ 280 ppm; 2024 ≈ 422 ppm; ~50% rise. Major source: fossil-fuel combustion (~75% of emissions), with land-use change and cement contributing the rest.

3
[3]D · Cooperation
Describe the three pillars of the Responsibility to Protect (R2P) doctrine.
Answer Key

(1) State has primary responsibility to protect its population from 4 atrocity crimes. (2) International community has responsibility to assist states. (3) If state manifestly fails, international community has responsibility for timely & decisive action including last-resort UN-authorised force.

4
[3]E · Globalization
Define comparative advantage. Briefly contrast it with absolute advantage.
Answer Key

Comparative advantage: a country should specialise where its opportunity cost is lowest. Absolute advantage: producing more per input. Even with absolute advantage in everything, a country still gains by specialising where its relative cost (comparative) is lowest (Ricardo, 1817).

5
[3]A · Inquiry
Name the four concepts of geographic thinking and define each in one sentence.
Answer Key

Spatial significance — why a place or pattern matters. Patterns and trends — recurring spatial forms and temporal changes. Interrelationships — links among physical and human systems. Geographic perspective — interpretive lens drawn from disciplines and stakeholder positions.

6
[2]B
Replacement-level fertility is approximately:
Answer Key

B. 2.1 — two to replace parents, plus a margin for pre-reproductive mortality.

7
[2]C
The Paris Agreement (2015) targets warming "well below":
Answer Key

B. Well below 2 °C, with efforts toward 1.5 °C. Current NDCs put the world on track for ~2.5–2.9 °C.

8
[2]D
The UN Security Council has how many permanent members with veto power?
Answer Key

B. 5 — China, France, Russia, the UK, the US (P5).

9
[2]E
The 2021 OECD-led agreement set a global minimum corporate tax of:
Answer Key

B. 15%. Adopted by 140+ jurisdictions; Canada legislated implementation effective 2024.

10
[2]A
NDVI is calculated from satellite imagery as:
Answer Key

A. Healthy vegetation strongly reflects NIR and absorbs red. NDVI ranges from −1 (water) to +1 (dense vegetation).

Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
11
[5]B · HDI calculation
A country has life expectancy 72, expected schooling 13, mean schooling 8, GNI per capita US$10 000 (PPP). Compute HDI using the UNDP geometric-mean method (life: 20–85; expected: 0–18; mean: 0–15; income log: 100–75 000).
Answer Key

Life index = (72−20)/(85−20) = 52/65 = 0.800. Expected-schooling = 13/18 = 0.722. Mean-schooling = 8/15 = 0.533. Education index = avg = 0.628. Income = (ln 10000 − ln 100)/(ln 75000 − ln 100) = (9.210 − 4.605)/(11.225 − 4.605) = 4.605/6.620 = 0.696. HDI = ∛(0.800 × 0.628 × 0.696) = ∛0.349 ≈ 0.704. Medium tier (just above 0.700).

12
[5]C · Methane CO₂e
A region emits 300 Mt CO₂ and 12 Mt CH₄ per year. Using GWP-100 = 28 for methane, compute total CO₂e and identify which gas requires more aggressive policy attention.
Answer Key

CH₄ → CO₂e: 12 × 28 = 336 Mt. Total = 300 + 336 = 636 Mt CO₂e. CH₄ dominates here despite lower mass. Policy priority: methane (livestock, oil & gas, landfills) for near-term climate impact; long-term still requires CO₂ reductions because of cumulative atmospheric lifetime.

13
[5]D · Refugee burden
Jordan hosts ~750 000 Syrian refugees with population ~11 million. Canada hosts ~85 000 refugees with population ~40 million. Compute per-1000 burden for each and discuss implications for global responsibility sharing.
Answer Key

Jordan: 750 000/11 000 000 × 1000 ≈ 68/1000. Canada: 85 000/40 000 000 × 1000 ≈ 2.1/1000. Jordan's burden ~32× Canada's. Most refugees are hosted by neighbouring middle-income countries. Equitable burden-sharing requires expanded resettlement, financial transfers, and capacity support (UN Global Compact on Refugees, 2018).

14
[5]E · Comparative advantage
Country A produces 6 wheat or 2 cars per labour-day; Country B produces 3 wheat or 3 cars. Identify comparative advantage for each, suggest a mutually beneficial exchange rate, and show that both gain from specialisation if each has 100 labour-days.
Answer Key

A: opportunity cost of 1 car = 3 wheat. B: opportunity cost of 1 car = 1 wheat. B has comparative advantage in cars. A has comparative advantage in wheat (1 wheat costs 1/3 car vs 1 wheat costs 1 car in B). Mutually beneficial exchange: any rate strictly between 1 and 3 wheat per car (e.g., 2 wheat per car). Before specialisation, 50/50 time: A produces 300 wheat + 100 cars; B produces 150 wheat + 150 cars; total 450 wheat + 250 cars. With full specialisation: A 600 wheat + 0 cars; B 0 wheat + 300 cars; total 600 wheat + 300 cars — strictly more of both.

15
[5]A · NDVI + map type
Pixel A: NIR = 0.55, RED = 0.08. Pixel B: NIR = 0.08, RED = 0.18. (a) Compute NDVI for each and classify likely surface. (b) Which map type and projection would best show global NDVI seasonal change?
Answer Key

Pixel A: (0.55−0.08)/(0.55+0.08) = 0.47/0.63 ≈ 0.75 — dense vegetation. Pixel B: (0.08−0.18)/(0.08+0.18) = −0.10/0.26 ≈ −0.38 — water. (b) Global NDVI: raster choropleth (cell-by-cell) with sequential green palette; Equal Earth or Robinson projection; faceted by month or as animated time-series; appropriate for showing seasonal vegetation pulse in the Northern Hemisphere and shifting tropical greenness.

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
16
[5]B · Composite indicators
In 200–250 words, explain how the HDI improves on GDP per capita as a well-being measure and identify two things even the HDI misses.
Answer Key

HDI broadens the lens beyond economic output to health and education, using a geometric mean that penalises weakness in any dimension. Strong answers also note: PPP adjustment for cross-country comparison; HDI captures average outcomes, ignoring inequality (addressed partially by IHDI); environmental sustainability (HPI); gender disparities (GII); subjective well-being and political freedom not represented. Two missing elements: inequality within country; environmental cost of producing the income; cultural and Indigenous well-being; political freedom.

17
[5]C · Carbon cycle
Draw or describe a labelled diagram of the carbon cycle including atmosphere, ocean, biosphere, lithosphere, and the major fluxes (photosynthesis, respiration, ocean exchange, fossil-fuel combustion, deforestation).
Answer Key

Reservoirs (Gt C): atmosphere ~875; ocean ~38 000; terrestrial biosphere ~2050; lithosphere ~5 000 000. Fluxes (Gt C/yr): photosynthesis ~120; respiration/decomposition ~120; ocean ⇄ atmosphere ~90 each way; volcanic ~0.1; fossil-fuel combustion ~9.6; deforestation ~1.5. Roughly half of human emissions accumulate in the atmosphere; the rest split between ocean and land sinks.

18
[5]D · UN vs NATO
Construct a 2-row table comparing the UN and NATO. Columns: Founded, Membership, Primary mandate, Decision rule.
Answer Key

UN: 1945 / 193 / international peace, security, development / GA majority; SC P5 veto on substantive matters. NATO: 1949 / 32 / collective defence (Article 5) / consensus-based decision making among all members.

19
[5]E · Globalization eval
In 200–300 words, evaluate the claim "globalization has been good for the world." Address at least two strengths and two limitations, and reach a defensible conclusion.
Answer Key

Strengths: ~1 billion lifted out of extreme poverty; lower consumer prices; technology diffusion; international institutions for cooperation. Limitations: deindustrialisation in some regions; rising inequality within countries; environmental and climate costs; financial volatility; cultural homogenisation. Strong answers reach a defensible verdict — typically "net positive globally but unevenly distributed; requires adjustment and redistribution policies." Reference Branko Milanovic elephant graph or Rodrik trilemma earns extra credit.

20
[5]A · Choropleth design
Describe the design choices (projection, classification, colour, classes, elements) you would make to produce a global map of life expectancy at birth. Justify each choice.
Answer Key

Projection: Robinson or Equal Earth (preserves area). Classification: natural breaks (Jenks), 5 classes (skewed data). Colour: sequential single-hue (light → dark) since life expectancy is ordered with a meaningful direction. Required elements: title ("Life expectancy at birth, 2024"), legend with class breaks, north arrow, scale, data source ("UN DESA World Population Prospects 2024"), projection note, date of compilation. Consider a small inset for sub-Saharan Africa for legibility.

Part D — Application [25 marks]
21
[5]B · Case — Niger fertility
Case study — Niger and the demographic transition: Niger's TFR is ~6.6, the world's highest. Identify three non-coercive interventions with strong cross-country evidence for reducing fertility and improving well-being.
Answer Key

(1) Girls' secondary education — strongest single lever. (2) Maternal and child healthcare access — reduces infant mortality, which lowers demand for "insurance" births. (3) Access to modern contraception and family-planning counselling. (4) Women's economic empowerment and labour-force participation. (5) Marriage-age legislation and enforcement. (6) Conditional cash transfers for school attendance. Strong answers integrate these levers (e.g., schooling → later marriage → smaller families).

22
[5]C · Case — Athabasca oil sands
Case study — Athabasca oil sands: Canada's oil-sands sector emits ~80 Mt CO₂e/year. Evaluate two decarbonisation pathways: emissions cap vs CCS at continued production. Identify trade-offs and political feasibility.
Answer Key

Emissions cap: faster on emissions, signals declining production trajectory; politically difficult in AB/SK; risks stranded assets but aligns with 1.5 °C. CCS: maintains production while reducing emissions; requires huge capital ($16B+ Pathways proposal); cannot capture downstream combustion abroad; technology unproven at oil-sands scale; energy penalty. Trade-offs: speed vs jobs transition. Best policy mix: declining cap with CCS incentives, transition funding, and methane regulations.

23
[5]D · Case — Nile water
Case study — Nile basin: Ethiopia's GERD on the Blue Nile changes water flows for Sudan and Egypt. Identify two geographic drivers of tension and two cooperative mechanisms that could reduce conflict risk.
Answer Key

Drivers: upstream/downstream asymmetry; legacy 1959 bilateral allocation excluding upstream riparians; climate-change variability; demographic pressure. Cooperation: Nile Basin Initiative (1999); AU-mediated agreed filling schedule; data sharing; coordinated reservoir management; mutual hydropower exchange (Ethiopia exporting electricity to Sudan and Egypt); third-party mediation by Norway or UN.

24
[5]E · Case — EV batteries
Case study — Canadian EV battery supply chain: Canada is positioning itself as an integrated EV battery supplier. Evaluate three geographic advantages of Canada and two policy actions to capture more downstream value.
Answer Key

Advantages: nickel (Sudbury, Voisey's Bay); cobalt (Cobalt Camp ON); lithium (James Bay QC); graphite, copper, REEs (SK, NWT); clean electricity grid (~85% non-emitting); trade access (CUSMA qualifies for US IRA EV credits, CETA); skilled workforce. Policy actions: federal Critical Minerals Strategy ($3.8B); JV with foreign battery makers (LG-Stellantis Windsor, Volkswagen St Thomas, Northvolt Montérégie); Indigenous consultation under UNDRIP Act; recycling investments (Li-Cycle, Toronto); accelerated permitting paired with environmental conditions.

25
[5]A · Synthesis inquiry
Capstone inquiry — Climate displacement & international law: Outline a culminating inquiry plan: question, two concepts of geographic thinking engaged, three sources, one map deliverable, and an argument with a counter-argument.
Answer Key

Question: "Should climate-displaced people be recognised under international refugee law, and how would this change Canada's obligations?" Concepts: interrelationships (climate-displacement-policy link); geographic perspective (international law, Bangladesh/SIDS, Canadian foreign policy). Sources: IDMC Global Report on Internal Displacement; UNHCR legal considerations on climate; Government of Canada Climate Refugees Working Group; peer-reviewed legal scholarship (e.g., McAdam, IRL). Map: choropleth of climate-driven displacement risk index, plus point locations of SIDS at greatest risk. Argument: expand the refugee definition through a Protocol covering climate-displaced persons, with Canada leading a coalition; counter-argument: such expansion would dilute refugee protection or face backlash from major hosts; rebuttal: distinct legal category preserves the 1951 framework while addressing the gap.
