Topic 2.1 — Greenhouse Effect & Climate Science
Question 1
Atmospheric CO₂ in the pre-industrial era was approximately 280 ppm. As of 2024, it has risen to approximately:
Solution: 422 ppm — a 50% rise above pre-industrial 280. NOAA Mauna Loa data show a steady upward trend since records began in 1958 (when CO₂ was ~315 ppm).
Question 2
Methane's Global Warming Potential over 100 years (GWP-100) relative to CO₂ is approximately:
Solution: About 28 (IPCC AR6). Methane absorbs IR much more strongly per molecule than CO₂ but persists in the atmosphere only ~12 years; over 20 years GWP-20 is ~84.
Question 3
Calculate: A farm emits 200 tonnes of methane per year. Convert to CO₂-equivalent (GWP-100 = 28).
Solution: 200 × 28 = 5600 tonnes CO₂e. Capturing this methane and burning it for energy reduces radiative forcing roughly 10-fold and generates electricity — a high-leverage climate intervention.
Topic 2.2 — Carbon Cycle & Feedbacks
Question 4
Which of the following is a positive climate feedback?
Solution: Ice–albedo feedback is strongly positive. Reflective ice (albedo ~0.8) is replaced by dark ocean (albedo ~0.06), absorbing more sunlight, melting more ice. The Arctic has warmed >3× the global average.
Question 5
Calculate the airborne fraction: emissions = 10 Gt C/year; atmospheric increase = 2.4 ppm; 1 ppm ≈ 2.13 Gt C.
Solution: Atmospheric increase = 2.4 × 2.13 = 5.11 Gt C. Airborne fraction = 5.11/10 ≈ 0.51 or 51%. The remaining ~49% is absorbed by ocean and biosphere.
Topic 2.3 — Ecological Footprint
Question 6
If everyone lived like the average Canadian (footprint ~7.7 gha) and biocapacity is ~1.6 gha/person, approximately how many Earths would humanity need?
Solution: 7.7 / 1.6 ≈ 4.8 Earths. The carbon component (driven by fossil energy, transport, and goods imports) is the dominant share.
Question 7
Of the nine planetary boundaries proposed by Rockström and colleagues (updated 2023), how many have already been transgressed?
Solution: Six: climate change, biosphere integrity, biogeochemical flows (N & P), land-system change, freshwater change, and novel entities. The remaining three (ocean acidification, stratospheric ozone, atmospheric aerosols) are at risk but within bounds.
Topic 2.4 — Climate Policy
Question 8
The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to hold warming "well below 2°C" and pursue efforts toward what stretch limit?
Solution: 1.5 °C. The IPCC's 1.5 °C Special Report (2018) showed substantially fewer impacts at 1.5 °C vs 2 °C. Current NDCs put the world on track for ~2.5–2.9 °C.
Question 9
Calculate: Canada's federal carbon price reached $80/tonne in 2024. Burning 1 L of gasoline releases ~2.3 kg CO₂. What is the per-litre carbon-price cost (cents/L)?
Solution: (2.3 kg/1000 kg/t) × $80/t = $0.184/L = 18.4 cents/L. At the scheduled 2030 price of $170/t, the cost would be ~39 cents/L.
Topic 2.5 — Water & Food Security
Question 10
Approximately what share of fresh water withdrawals goes to agriculture globally?
Solution: About 70% of global fresh-water withdrawals go to agriculture, with industry ~20% and domestic use ~10%. Improving irrigation efficiency (drip, deficit irrigation) has the largest potential for water savings.
Question 11
According to the FAO, roughly what fraction of food produced globally is lost or wasted between farm and fork?
Solution: About one-third (~1.3 billion tonnes/year). In low-income countries losses concentrate at the post-harvest and storage stages; in high-income countries waste concentrates at the retail and household stages.
Question 12
Calculate per-capita renewable water: a country has 60 km³/year renewable water and a population of 30 million. Compute m³/person/year and classify using the Falkenmark scale (1700 = stress, 1000 = scarcity, 500 = absolute scarcity).
Solution: 60 × 10⁹ / 30 × 10⁶ = 2000 m³/person/year. Above the 1700 stress threshold — adequate but not abundant. Climate change and population growth could push this below 1700 within decades, triggering stress.
