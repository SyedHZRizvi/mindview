Topic 3.1 — Geographies of Conflict
Question 1
Since 1945, the vast majority of armed conflicts have been:
Solution: Intrastate. Uppsala Conflict Data Program counts ~59 active state-based conflicts in 2023, almost all internal. Interstate wars have become rarer, though Russia-Ukraine 2022 is a major exception.
Question 2
Which of the following is NOT considered a typical driver of conflict?
Solution: Conflict drivers include resource competition, ethnic divisions, governance failure, contested borders, climate stress, and the "conflict trap." Improving life expectancy is generally associated with peace and development.
Topic 3.2 — Refugees & IDPs
Question 3
A refugee, as defined by the 1951 Refugee Convention, is a person who:
Solution: The 1951 Convention defines a refugee as someone outside their country of origin with a well-founded fear of persecution on protected grounds. (A) is an IDP. (C) is an economic migrant. (D) is "climate-displaced" — not yet a recognised legal category.
Question 4
Calculate: Türkiye hosts ~3.5 million Syrian refugees and has a population of ~85 million. What is the per-1000-residents refugee burden? (Round to nearest whole number.)
Solution: 3 500 000 / 85 000 000 × 1000 ≈ 41 refugees per 1000 residents. By contrast Germany (~14/1000) and Canada (~3/1000) host far fewer per capita. Most refugees live in middle-income host states.
Question 5
The "three durable solutions" recognised by UNHCR for refugees are voluntary repatriation, local integration, and:
Solution: Resettlement to a third country (e.g., Canada). Forced return would violate non-refoulement. Less than 1% of refugees are resettled each year — the resettlement pipeline is much smaller than need.
Topic 3.3 — UN System
Question 6
The UN Security Council has how many permanent members with veto power?
Solution: 5 — China, France, Russia, the UK, the US (the "P5"). Plus 10 elected non-permanent members serving two-year terms.
Question 7
General Assembly resolutions are:
Solution: General Assembly resolutions are non-binding (except internal matters and budget). Only Security Council resolutions under Chapter VII can be legally binding (with enforcement provisions).
Topic 3.4 — NATO
Question 8
NATO's Article 5 (collective defence) has been invoked how many times in its history?
Solution: Once — after the 11 September 2001 attacks on the US, leading to the NATO ISAF mission in Afghanistan. Canada deployed troops to Kandahar; the mission ended in 2014.
Question 9
Calculate: NATO's 2014 target is 2% of GDP on defence. Canada's GDP is ~$2.1 trillion. Canada spent ~1.37% on defence in 2024. How much additional annual spending is needed to reach 2%?
Solution: Target: 0.02 × 2100 = $42 billion. Current: 0.0137 × 2100 ≈ $28.8 billion. Gap = $42 − $28.8 ≈ $13.2 billion/year. Canada has committed to reach 2% by 2032.
Topic 3.5 — R2P & Canada's Role
Question 10
The Responsibility to Protect (R2P) doctrine applies to four mass-atrocity crimes. They are:
Solution: Adopted at the 2005 UN World Summit, R2P specifically covers genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity, and ethnic cleansing. Canada led the ICISS commission that drafted the doctrine.
Question 11
Lester B. Pearson's 1956 proposal that helped launch modern UN peacekeeping responded to which crisis?
Solution: The 1956 Suez Crisis. Pearson, then Canadian External Affairs minister, proposed the first armed peacekeeping force (UNEF I) and won the 1957 Nobel Peace Prize.
Question 12
Canada's annual contribution to UN peacekeeping has changed dramatically. In 1993 Canada contributed ~3300 uniformed personnel; by 2024 the figure is approximately:
Solution: Approximately 40 uniformed personnel in UN missions as of 2024 — a steep drop from the 1990s. Canada has shifted emphasis to NATO operations and capacity-building (training).
