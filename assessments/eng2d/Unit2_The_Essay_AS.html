Topic 2.1 — The Writing Process
Question 1
Which of the following is the correct order for the five stages of the writing process?
Solution: The conventional sequence is Prewriting (generate ideas), Drafting (rough version), Revising (large-scale rethinking), Editing (sentence-level fixes), Publishing (final presentation). The process is recursive — you can return to earlier stages — but this is the standard order.
Question 2
Revision differs from editing because it focuses on:
Solution: Revision ("re-vision") tackles structural and argumentative choices. Editing addresses sentence-level grammar, mechanics, and word choice. Revise first; edit second.
Topic 2.2 — Paragraph Structure
Question 3
In a PEEL paragraph, the order of the four moves is:
Solution: P-E-E-L: Point (topic sentence), Evidence (quotation), Explain (analysis of how the evidence proves the point), Link (transition to next paragraph or back to thesis).
Question 4
In a well-built PEEL paragraph, the longest part should usually be:
Solution: The Explain step is where the analytical work happens. A paragraph where the quotation is longer than the analysis is summarising, not analysing. The longest part should be your interpretation.
Topic 2.3 — The Literary-Analysis Essay
Question 5
In a literary-analysis essay, the correct verb tense for describing what happens in the text is:
Solution: The literary present tense reflects the convention that the text is always happening, every time it is read. Use the past tense only for historical facts (Shakespeare wrote, the Globe burned).
Question 6
In a formal literary-analysis essay, which of these voices is most appropriate?
Solution: Formal literary analysis uses third-person with confident analytical verbs (argues, reveals, frames, critiques). First and second person make the writer's claim sound tentative and shift focus from the text to the writer.
Topic 2.4 — Persuasive Essay & Appeals
Question 7
An appeal based on the speaker's credibility, expertise, or trustworthiness is called:
Solution: Ethos = credibility/character. Logos = logic/evidence. Pathos = emotion. Aristotle's three classical appeals; strong persuasive writing usually combines all three.
Question 8
A persuasive essay's counterargument paragraph should:
Solution: Acknowledging the strongest opposing view shows fairness (an ethos move) and lets you refute the strongest objection rather than a strawman (a logos move). Both make your case more persuasive.
Topic 2.5 — MLA Citation
Question 9
The correct MLA-9 in-text citation for a quotation from Act 2, Scene 2, line 33 of Romeo and Juliet is:
Solution: For plays, MLA uses act.scene.line separated by periods. The author's name is not usually needed if the play has been identified in the surrounding text. For other works, the standard form is (Author lastname pagenumber).
Question 10
"Patchwriting" is best described as:
Solution: Patchwriting keeps the original author's voice and structure with only small substitutions; it is a form of plagiarism even when cited. A true paraphrase rewrites the idea in your own words and sentence structure — and still requires citation.
