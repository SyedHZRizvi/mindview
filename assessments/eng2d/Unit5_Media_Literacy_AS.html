Topic 5.1 — Media Texts & Construction
Question 1
A "media text" is best described as:
Solution: A media text is any text distributed through mass-media channels — print, broadcast, film, web, social. The key concept is that every such text is constructed, not natural.
Question 2
A film shot from a low angle, looking up at a character, typically suggests:
Solution: Low-angle shots make subjects appear powerful — the camera (and viewer) looks up at them. High-angle shots make subjects appear weak. Eye-level is neutral. This is one of cinema's most consistent visual codes.
Topic 5.2 — Advertising Techniques
Question 3
An ad that claims "Three out of four dentists recommend our toothpaste" is using primarily:
Solution: The statistic appeals to logos (evidence), and the dentist endorsement appeals to ethos (expert authority). Both depend on the reader trusting the unstated source of the survey.
Question 4
An ad uses the phrase "Premium quality. Total satisfaction." with no specific details. This is an example of:
Solution: Glittering generality — words like "premium," "ultimate," "total" — sound positive without committing to a specific claim. They evade fact-checking by saying nothing falsifiable.
Topic 5.3 — News vs Opinion
Question 5
The "inverted pyramid" structure of a news article means:
Solution: The inverted pyramid puts the most important facts first, so a reader who reads only the first paragraph still gets the key information. The structure was originally designed for telegraphic transmission.
Question 6
In Caulfield's SIFT method for evaluating online claims, the four moves are:
Solution: Mike Caulfield's SIFT: Stop before sharing; Investigate the source; Find better coverage in reputable outlets; Trace claims, quotes, and media to the original context.
Topic 5.4 — Social-Media Literacy
Question 7
A social-media "filter bubble" is best described as:
Solution: The filter bubble is the personalised feed the platform's algorithm shows you, based on your past activity. Two users on the same platform may see entirely different versions of "the world." Eli Pariser coined the term in 2011.
Question 8
"Lateral reading" means:
Solution: Lateral reading is the fact-checker's habit: open new tabs and check what other reputable sources say about a claim, rather than reading the original source top-to-bottom and trusting what it says about itself.
Topic 5.5 — Creating Media
Question 9
In planning an original media text, the first design question is:
Solution: Audience analysis is the first design decision — it controls every subsequent choice (form, register, length, platform). "Everyone" is not an audience.
Question 10
A storyboard is most useful because it:
Solution: A storyboard records design decisions in low-cost sketch form, so problems with shot choice, framing, or pacing can be fixed before expensive production. It does not need to be artistic; it needs to record decisions.
