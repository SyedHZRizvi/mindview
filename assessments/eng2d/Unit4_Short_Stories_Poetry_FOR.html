Unit 4: Short Stories & Poetry — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand B
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify what you already know about short fiction, character, and poetry before starting Unit 4. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Story Elements
[3 marks]
Name and define three of the five elements of fiction (plot, character, setting, theme, point of view). For each, give one sentence from a story you have read.
Question 2 Character
[3 marks]
What is the difference between direct and indirect characterisation? Provide one short example of each, with the same fictional character if possible.
Question 3 Theme
[3 marks]
"My father was a fox farmer. That is, he raised silver foxes, in pens; and in the fall and early winter, when their fur was prime, he killed them and skinned them and sold their pelts." — Alice Munro, "Boys and Girls" (opening)
Suggest one possible theme for a story that opens this way. Write your theme as a complete sentence (not a single word).
Question 4 Poetry · Sound
[3 marks]
"Once upon a midnight dreary, while I pondered, weak and weary," — Edgar Allan Poe, "The Raven"
Identify two sound devices in this line and briefly explain what each contributes to the line's effect.
Question 5 Poetry · Form
[3 marks]
Describe the basic structure of a sonnet (any kind). How many lines? What is the meter usually? What kinds of subjects do sonnets traditionally address?
Question 6 Comparison
[3 marks]
Name two short stories or poems you have read. What is one meaningful similarity between them, and one meaningful difference?
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which is harder for you — analysing short fiction or analysing poetry? Why?

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
