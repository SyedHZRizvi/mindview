Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [12 marks]
1
[3]
List the five stages of the writing process and explain in one sentence what happens in each.
Answer Key

Prewriting — generate, explore, and organise ideas. Drafting — write a complete (rough) version. Revising — rethink large-scale choices: thesis, argument, paragraph order, evidence. Editing — fix sentence-level grammar, mechanics, word choice. Publishing — prepare the final presentation-ready version. Award 0.5 marks per correctly named stage with brief explanation; full marks for 5/5.

2
[3]
Define each of Aristotle's three appeals and give one example: (a) ethos, (b) pathos, (c) logos.
Answer Key

(a) Ethos — appeal to credibility/character; e.g., a doctor in a white coat endorsing a medication. (b) Pathos — appeal to emotion; e.g., a charity ad showing a child reuniting with a parent. (c) Logos — appeal to logic/evidence; e.g., "Studies show 90% of teenagers …" + statistical data. Marks: 1 each for definition + example.

3
[3]
Identify each step of a PEEL paragraph and describe what it does.
Answer Key

Point — topic sentence stating the sub-claim of the paragraph. Evidence — direct quotation or specific textual reference. Explain — interpretation of how and why the evidence supports the point (the analytical work). Link — closing sentence connecting back to thesis or forward to the next paragraph.

4
[3]
Write the correct MLA-9 in-text citations for the following:
(a) A quotation from page 102 of To Kill a Mockingbird by Harper Lee.
(b) A quotation from Act 3, Scene 2, line 75 of Romeo and Juliet.
(c) A quotation from line 8 of a poem.
Answer Key

(a) (Lee 102). (b) (3.2.75). (c) (line 8) or, once you have established you are citing line numbers, (8).

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
5
[5]
"Romeo says, 'But, soft! what light through yonder window breaks? / It is the east, and Juliet is the sun' (2.2.2–3)."
Write a complete PEEL paragraph (5–7 sentences) developing the claim: "Romeo's sun metaphor in the balcony scene reorients his world around Juliet, but also forecasts the brevity of their time together."
Answer Key

Look for: (P) clear topic sentence asserting the dual function of the metaphor; (E) the embedded quotation already provided, integrated into the sentence; (E) interpretation of "east" / "sun" (cosmic centre, dawn-rising imagery) and explanation of why the dawn metaphor is also a metaphor of ending (dawn ends night); (L) connection back to thesis or forward. Marks: 1 P, 1 E, 2 Explain, 1 Link / conventions.

6
[4]
Convert the following topic into a debatable, supportable thesis: "Romeo and Juliet's love."
Answer Key

Sample: "Shakespeare presents Romeo and Juliet's love as a force powerful enough to suspend two characters' identities ('be but sworn my love, / And I'll no longer be a Capulet') but not powerful enough to overcome the institutions — feud, church, postal failure — that surround it." Look for: specific text reference (1), debatable claim (1), analytical verb (1), conventions (1).

7
[4]
Analyse the difference between a strong and a weak topic sentence. Provide one weak and one strong topic sentence for the same paragraph topic ("the Friar's role in Romeo and Juliet").
Answer Key

Weak: "The Friar plays an important role in the play." (No claim, no specificity.) Strong: "Friar Lawrence's hasty decision to marry the lovers — taken despite his own counsel of 'wisely and slow' — sets the chain of secrecy and acceleration that drives the tragedy." Look for: identification of why the weak one is weak (no claim, vague), why the strong one is strong (specific, debatable, supportable). Marks: 2 for the contrast, 2 for the examples.

Part C: Communication [13 marks]
8
[8]
Major Task — Persuasive Essay Plan

Choose ONE of the following persuasive topics and produce a complete essay plan (NOT the full essay) including:
(a) thesis statement;
(b) three body-paragraph topic sentences;
(c) at least one piece of evidence per body paragraph (real or invented);
(d) a counterargument paragraph topic sentence;
(e) one sentence for the conclusion.

Options:
1. High schools in Ontario should adopt 9:00 a.m. start times.
2. Social-media literacy should be a mandatory Grade 10 course.
3. Senior students should be allowed to leave campus during free periods.
Marking Rubric

Thesis (2): debatable, specific, supportable. Three topic sentences (3): each previews a sub-claim, not just a topic. Three pieces of evidence (1.5): each is concrete, attributable. Counterargument (1): names the strongest opposing view fairly. Conclusion (0.5): synthesises rather than restates.

9
[5]
Correct each grammatical or mechanical error in the following sentences:
(a) "Each of the students were prepared for the exam."
(b) "The novel which I read last semester it was very engaging."
(c) "Romeo, who is the protagonist of Shakespeare's play, are conflicted about avenging his father."
(d) "Hughes's poem is more better than I expected."
(e) "The students who's essays were submitted on time received feedback."
Answer Key

(a) "Each … was prepared." (Each = singular.) (b) "The novel that I read last semester was very engaging." (Remove redundant "it"; "that" preferred for restrictive clauses.) (c) "Romeo … is conflicted about avenging his cousin's death." (Singular subject. Also: in Romeo and Juliet, Romeo is not avenging a father — that is Hamlet.) (d) "better" — drop "more" (no double comparative). (e) "whose essays" (possessive — "who's" = "who is").

Part D: Application [12 marks]
10
[6]
Apply the literary-analysis essay form. Write the opening paragraph (introduction only) of a 500-word essay responding to the prompt: "How does Shakespeare's use of the sonnet form in the prologue and the lovers' first meeting (1.5) prepare the audience for the tragedy?"

Your introduction should include: a hook, brief context (author, title, situation), and a clear thesis. Aim for 5–7 sentences.
Marking Rubric

Hook (1): engaging opening, not "Throughout history." Context (2): author, title, brief situational frame. Thesis (2): specific, debatable claim about how the sonnet form prepares the audience (it sets up love-resolution conventions, then frustrates them). Voice / conventions (1): third-person, literary present, analytical diction.

11
[3]
Apply ethos / pathos / logos. Choose a real or invented advertisement and analyse in 3–4 sentences which of the three appeals it relies on most heavily. Quote or describe one specific feature that produces the appeal.
Answer Key

Look for: a specific ad (1), identification of the dominant appeal (1), one specific feature explained (1). Acceptable analyses include cosmetic ads (pathos through slow-motion / lighting), tech ads (logos through specifications), endorsement ads (ethos through celebrity).

12
[3]
STSE / Real-World Application: Explain in 3–4 sentences how the skills from this unit (writing process, paragraph structure, persuasive appeals, citation) transfer to a non-English context — university coursework, professional communication, civic participation, or personal communication on social media.
Answer Key

Strong responses identify a specific non-English transfer: lab reports use thesis-evidence-explain; professional emails use audience analysis; political speeches use ethos/pathos/logos; tweets use compression with intent; citation prevents plagiarism in any field that uses sources. Marks: 1 specific transfer, 1 connection to a unit skill, 1 example.
