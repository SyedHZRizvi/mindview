Topic 3.1–3.2 — Food Guide & Labels
Question 1
What proportion of the 2019 Canada's Food Guide plate is dedicated to vegetables and fruit?
Solution: The 2019 plate model is ½ vegetables & fruit, ¼ whole grain foods, ¼ protein foods. Water is the recommended beverage.
Question 2
According to Health Canada's 5%–15% rule, what does 5% DV or less mean?
Solution: 5% DV or less = "a little"; 15% DV or more = "a lot". For nutrients to limit (sodium, saturated fat, sugars) you want low; for nutrients to encourage (fibre, vitamin D, calcium, iron, potassium) you want high.
Question 3
Cereal A has 12% DV sodium per 30 g serving. If you eat 60 g, what % DV does that serving deliver?
Solution: Double the serving = double the %DV = 24%. Always check serving size first when comparing.
Question 4
According to the 2019 Canada's Food Guide, the recommended beverage is:
Solution: Water is the recommended beverage in the 2019 Food Guide, replacing the earlier requirement for two cups of milk per day.
Topic 3.3 — DRIs & Daily Values
Question 5
Which DRI is meant to be used as an individual target?
Solution: RDA meets the needs of 97–98% of healthy individuals. EAR is for population assessment; UL is a safety ceiling. AMDR sets ranges (45–65% C, 20–35% F, 10–35% P).
Question 6
For ages 9–18, the calcium RDA is highest. What is the value (in mg)?
Solution: 1 300 mg/day. Adult RDA falls to 1 000 mg/day at 19–50 and rises to 1 200 mg/day at 51+ (women) and 71+ (men).
Question 7
Iron RDA for an adult menstruating woman:
Solution: 18 mg/day for menstruating adult women. Men: 8 mg/day. Pregnant women: 27 mg/day. Adolescent menstruators: 15 mg/day.
Topic 3.4–3.5 — Life Stages & Special Diets
Question 8
Folate (folic acid) supplementation begins before conception. What is the recommended dose?
Solution: 400 µg/day folic-acid supplementation, started ideally 3 months pre-conception. The neural tube closes by week 4 — often before pregnancy is recognised.
Question 9
Exclusive breast-feeding is recommended by Health Canada for the first:
Solution: 6 months. Iron-rich complementary foods (meat, fortified cereal, legumes, tofu) are introduced thereafter alongside continued breast-feeding to ~2 years.
Question 10
An endurance athlete's daily carbohydrate need (g/kg body weight):
Solution: 5–10 g/kg/day to refill muscle glycogen after training; tournament/competition days may push this higher. Strength athletes typically need less carbohydrate but more protein (1.6–2.0 g/kg).
Question 11
Which is one of Health Canada's 11 priority allergens?
Solution: The 11 priority allergens that must be plainly declared on Canadian labels: peanuts, tree nuts, sesame, milk, eggs, fish, shellfish (crustaceans + molluscs), soy, wheat, sulphites, mustard.
