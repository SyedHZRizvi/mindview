Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[2]
A neutral atom of sulfur has 16 protons and 17 neutrons. Its mass number A is:
Answer Key

D. 33. Mass number = protons + neutrons = 16 + 17 = 33. The 32 isotope is more common, but this particular atom has 17 neutrons, giving \(^{33}S\).

2
[3]
Write IUPAC names for: (a) MgCl₂, (b) FeO, (c) \(Cu(NO_3)_2\), (d) \(SO_3\).
Answer Key

(a) Magnesium chloride. (b) Iron(II) oxide — Fe must be +2 to balance one O²⁻. (c) Copper(II) nitrate — each \(NO_3^-\) is −1, two give −2, so Cu must be +2. (d) Sulfur trioxide — covalent, use Greek prefix "tri" for 3 oxygens (the leading element drops "mono").

3
[3]
Write the chemical formulas for: (a) ammonium phosphate, (b) calcium hydroxide, (c) dinitrogen pentoxide.
Answer Key

(a) \((NH_4)_3PO_4\) — \(NH_4^+\) (×3) balances \(PO_4^{3-}\). (b) \(Ca(OH)_2\) — \(Ca^{2+}\) balances two \(OH^-\). (c) \(N_2O_5\) — Greek prefixes give 2 N + 5 O.

4
[3]
A solution has pH 11. Is it acidic, basic, or neutral? Estimate the \([H^+]\) concentration.
Answer Key

pH 11 > 7 → basic (about the pH of household ammonia). \([H^+] = 10^{-pH} = 10^{-11}\) M, very low concentration of \(H^+\) compared to \([OH^-] = 10^{-3}\) M.

5
[2]
Identify the type of reaction: \(2HgO \rightarrow 2Hg + O_2\).
Answer Key

B. Decomposition. One compound splits into two simpler substances; \(AB \rightarrow A + B\). This is the reaction Joseph Priestley used in 1774 to discover oxygen.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
6
[5]
Balance the following equations: (a) \(\_C_4H_{10} + \_O_2 \rightarrow \_CO_2 + \_H_2O\) (combustion of butane). (b) \(\_Fe + \_O_2 \rightarrow \_Fe_2O_3\) (rusting). (c) \(\_AgNO_3 + \_CuCl_2 \rightarrow \_AgCl + \_Cu(NO_3)_2\).
Answer Key

(a) \(2C_4H_{10} + 13O_2 \rightarrow 8CO_2 + 10H_2O\). Carbon: 8 = 8 ✓; Hydrogen: 20 = 20 ✓; Oxygen: 26 = 26 ✓. (b) \(4Fe + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2Fe_2O_3\). Fe: 4 = 4 ✓; O: 6 = 6 ✓. (c) \(2AgNO_3 + CuCl_2 \rightarrow 2AgCl + Cu(NO_3)_2\). Ag: 2 = 2 ✓; N: 2 = 2 ✓; O: 6 = 6 ✓; Cu: 1 = 1 ✓; Cl: 2 = 2 ✓.

7
[4]
Predict the products and balance: (a) \(K + Cl_2 \rightarrow ?\); (b) \(Pb(NO_3)_2 + KI \rightarrow ?\); (c) \(Mg + AgNO_3 \rightarrow ?\); (d) \(C_2H_6 + O_2 \rightarrow ?\).
Answer Key

(a) Synthesis: \(2K + Cl_2 \rightarrow 2KCl\). (b) Double displacement: \(Pb(NO_3)_2 + 2KI \rightarrow PbI_2 + 2KNO_3\). \(PbI_2\) is the famous yellow precipitate. (c) Single displacement: \(Mg + 2AgNO_3 \rightarrow Mg(NO_3)_2 + 2Ag\); Mg is more reactive than Ag. (d) Combustion: \(2C_2H_6 + 7O_2 \rightarrow 4CO_2 + 6H_2O\).

8
[3]
Design an experiment to compare the activity of zinc, iron, and copper. Identify a suitable test, expected order of reactivity, and one controlled variable.
Answer Key

Test: place equal-sized strips of each metal in equal volumes of dilute HCl; observe bubbling (\(H_2\) evolution). Most reactive metal displaces hydrogen vigorously; least reactive shows no reaction. Expected order (from activity series): Zn > Fe > Cu. Cu does not react with HCl because it is below H in the activity series. Controlled: same HCl concentration and volume, same temperature, same surface area of metal (use strips of equal mass or area), same observation time. Replicate three times. Conclusion: rank by visible reaction rate.

Part C: Communication [13 marks]
9
[5]
Draw and describe the Bohr–Rutherford diagrams for sodium (Na, Z = 11) and chlorine (Cl, Z = 17). Then describe how they combine to form NaCl.
Answer Key

Sodium: 11p in nucleus, 11 electrons in shells 2, 8, 1. Chlorine: 17p in nucleus, 17 electrons in shells 2, 8, 7. To reach octet stability, Na loses its single 3rd-shell electron, becoming \(Na^+\) with the noble-gas electron configuration of Ne. Cl gains that electron, becoming \(Cl^-\) with the noble-gas configuration of Ar. The opposite charges attract electrostatically: a 3-D ionic lattice forms, where each \(Na^+\) is surrounded by 6 \(Cl^-\) and vice versa. The result is a brittle crystalline solid with a high melting point (801 °C).

10
[4]
Compare ionic and covalent bonding in a table. Include: type of atoms involved, electron behaviour, physical state at room temperature, melting point (high/low), electrical conductivity when molten.
Answer Key

Ionic: metal + non-metal; electrons transferred; usually crystalline solid; high melting point (often >500 °C); conducts when molten or dissolved (mobile ions). Covalent: non-metal + non-metal; electrons shared; often gas or liquid or low-melting solid at room T; low melting point (often <300 °C); usually does not conduct (no mobile charges) — exceptions: graphite, some acids in water.

11
[4]
Explain the meaning of pH in your own words. Why is the scale logarithmic, and why is that significant for biological and environmental systems?
Answer Key

pH measures hydrogen-ion concentration: \(pH = -\log_{10}[H^+]\). Lower pH = more \(H^+\) = more acidic. The scale is logarithmic because biological and environmental \([H^+]\) spans many orders of magnitude — pure water \(10^{-7}\) M to stomach acid \(10^{-2}\) M is a factor of 100,000. A logarithmic scale (0–14) makes that range humanly readable. Significance: small pH numbers represent enormous concentration changes. A lake going from pH 7 to pH 5 (acid precipitation) means 100× more \(H^+\) — enough to kill fish eggs and many invertebrates. Body pH must stay near 7.4 ± 0.05; deviation of 0.4 units in either direction is medically critical.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[5]
Case Study — Antacids: A patient takes calcium carbonate (\(CaCO_3\)) tablets for heartburn. Write the balanced neutralisation equation and explain how the products give relief from indigestion.
Answer Key

Stomach acid is HCl (about pH 1.5–2). Calcium carbonate is a weak base. Balanced reaction: \(CaCO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow CaCl_2 + H_2O + CO_2\). Products: \(CaCl_2\) (calcium chloride — water-soluble salt, absorbed or passed); \(H_2O\) (water); \(CO_2\) (carbon dioxide — burped out, which is why antacids sometimes cause burping). The reaction neutralises excess stomach acid by consuming \(H^+\), raising stomach pH toward 4–6 and relieving the burning sensation. Note: this is also a double-displacement reaction overall, with an immediate decomposition of the carbonate part producing \(CO_2\).

13
[4]
STSE — Acid Precipitation: Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from coal-burning power plants form acidic compounds in the atmosphere. Identify the two acids produced, explain the impact on a freshwater lake, and describe one mitigation strategy.
Answer Key

\(SO_2\) reacts with atmospheric water to form sulfurous acid then sulfuric acid (\(H_2SO_4\)). \(NO_x\) compounds form nitric acid (\(HNO_3\)). Acidic rain falls into lakes, lowering pH. As lake pH drops below ~5: fish eggs cannot hatch; many invertebrates die; aluminium leaches from soils into the water, becoming toxic; biodiversity collapses. Famous Canadian example: lakes around Sudbury, ON, were severely acidified by smelter emissions; recovery began with the construction of the 380 m Inco Superstack (1972) — which only spread the problem farther — and accelerated with the 1991 Canada-US Air Quality Agreement requiring \(SO_2\) reductions. Mitigation strategies: install flue-gas scrubbers (calcium hydroxide reacts with \(SO_2\)) on power plants; switch to lower-sulfur fuels or renewable energy; lime acidified lakes (adds \(CaCO_3\) to neutralise) for short-term recovery; international air-quality agreements; carbon pricing to discourage coal burning generally.

14
[3]
WHMIS Application: A workplace label shows pictograms for "flame" and "exclamation mark", signal word "Danger". What hazards does the product likely have? List three safety precautions.
Answer Key

Flame pictogram: flammable liquid or solid. Exclamation mark: acute toxicity (less severe), irritant (skin, eye, respiratory). Signal word "Danger" indicates more severe end of the hazard category. Three precautions: (1) keep away from open flames, sparks, hot surfaces; store in a flammables cabinet. (2) Use PPE: chemical-resistant gloves, safety goggles, lab coat. (3) Work in a well-ventilated area or fume hood; have eyewash station and fire extinguisher accessible; consult the SDS before starting work. Dispose of properly in labelled containers.
