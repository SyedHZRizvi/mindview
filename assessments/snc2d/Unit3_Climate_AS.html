Topic 3.1 — Weather, Climate & the Greenhouse Effect
Question 1
Which best distinguishes climate from weather?
Solution: Weather = atmospheric conditions over short periods (hours/days). Climate = the multi-decadal average and range of weather over a region. "Climate is what you expect; weather is what you get."
Question 2
Which is NOT a greenhouse gas?
Solution: \(N_2\) (and \(O_2\)) are symmetric diatomic molecules and do not absorb infrared radiation appreciably, so they are not greenhouse gases — despite making up 99% of the atmosphere. \(CO_2\), \(CH_4\), \(H_2O\), and \(N_2O\) all absorb IR effectively.
Question 3
Without the natural greenhouse effect, Earth's average surface temperature would be approximately:
Solution: Without greenhouse gases, Earth's surface would average about −18 °C — frozen and uninhabitable. The natural greenhouse effect warms us by about 33 °C to the current ~15 °C average. The enhanced greenhouse effect adds further warming above this baseline.
Topic 3.2 — Carbon Cycle & Evidence
Question 4
Which process removes \(CO_2\) from the atmosphere?
Solution: Photosynthesis: \(6CO_2 + 6H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2\), removing \(CO_2\) and storing it as carbohydrate. The other three processes release \(CO_2\).
Question 5
Pre-industrial atmospheric \(CO_2\) was about 280 ppm. In 2024, it is over 420 ppm. By approximately what percentage has \(CO_2\) increased?
Solution: Increase = (420 − 280) / 280 × 100% = 140/280 × 100% = 50%. Atmospheric \(CO_2\) is now about 50% higher than pre-industrial — a level not seen in 3+ million years.
Question 6
Which is the longest continuous direct measurement of atmospheric \(CO_2\)?
Solution: Charles Keeling began direct, continuous \(CO_2\) measurements at the Mauna Loa Observatory in 1958. The resulting "Keeling Curve" shows seasonal sawtooth variations on a rising trend. Ice cores provide much longer (800,000-year) but indirect records.
Topic 3.3 — Feedbacks & Mitigation
Question 7
The ice-albedo feedback is an example of:
Solution: Warming → ice melts → exposes darker land/ocean → more sunlight absorbed → more warming. This is positive feedback. It contributes to Arctic amplification — the Arctic warming 3–4× faster than the global average.
Question 8
The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to limit global warming to:
Solution: Article 2 of the Paris Agreement: hold the increase in global average temperature to "well below 2 °C" above pre-industrial levels, and pursue efforts to limit the increase to 1.5 °C. Current policies are estimated to give about 2.5–3 °C.
Question 9
If methane has a 100-year global warming potential of 28, then 5 tonnes of methane is equivalent to how many tonnes of \(CO_2\)?
Solution: CO₂-eq = mass × GWP = 5 × 28 = 140 tonnes. This is why methane-leak reduction is one of the fastest, cheapest mitigation actions.
Question 10
Which is an example of climate-change adaptation rather than mitigation?
Solution: Mitigation cuts emissions (a, c, d). Adaptation responds to impacts already underway — building sea walls, redesigning storm drains, breeding heat-tolerant crops, relocating communities. Both are necessary.
