Topic 2.1 — Atomic Structure
Question 1
A neutral atom of magnesium (Z = 12, A = 24) has how many neutrons?
Solution: Neutrons = A − Z = 24 − 12 = 12. A neutral atom also has 12 electrons (equal to protons).
Question 2
In a Bohr–Rutherford diagram, how many electrons are shown in the third shell of a neutral chlorine atom (Z = 17)?
Solution: Fill inside out: shell 1 = 2, shell 2 = 8, shell 3 = 17 − 10 = 7. Chlorine has 7 valence electrons, which is why it gains 1 to form Cl⁻ (octet).
Question 3
Two atoms of the same element with different mass numbers are called:
Solution: Isotopes have the same number of protons (same element) but different numbers of neutrons. Example: \(^{12}C\) and \(^{14}C\). Ions differ in charge; isomers in structural arrangement; allotropes in physical form.
Topic 2.2 — Bonding & Nomenclature
Question 4
The formula for aluminium oxide is:
Solution: \(Al^{3+}\) and \(O^{2-}\) crisscross → \(Al_2O_3\). Check charges: 2 × (+3) + 3 × (−2) = 6 − 6 = 0. Neutral compound.
Question 5
The compound \(N_2O_4\) is named:
Solution: Both atoms are non-metals, so use Greek prefixes: di + nitrogen + tetra + oxide. "Tetraoxide" simplifies to "tetroxide" (drop the "a" before the "o"). Final: dinitrogen tetroxide.
Question 6
Which represents an ionic bond?
Solution: NaCl: metal + non-metal, electrons transfer from Na to Cl. The others are non-metal + non-metal, sharing electrons (covalent).
Topic 2.3 — Balancing & Reaction Types
Question 7
Balance: \(\_\,H_2 + \_\,O_2 \rightarrow \_\,H_2O\). What coefficient goes in front of \(H_2\)?
Solution: Balanced equation: \(2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O\). Coefficient in front of \(H_2\) is 2. Check: H 4 = 4, O 2 = 2.
Question 8
The reaction \(Zn + 2HCl \rightarrow ZnCl_2 + H_2\) is classified as:
Solution: An element (Zn) replaces another element (H) in a compound (HCl). This matches the pattern \(A + BC \rightarrow AC + B\), single displacement. Zn is more reactive than H, so it displaces hydrogen — releasing \(H_2\) gas.
Question 9
Balance the combustion of methane: \(CH_4 + \_\,O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + \_\,H_2O\). What coefficient goes in front of \(O_2\)?
Solution: Balanced: \(CH_4 + 2O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + 2H_2O\). Carbon 1=1, hydrogen 4=4 (so 2 \(H_2O\)), oxygen 4 on right (2 from \(CO_2\) + 2 from \(H_2O\)), so 2 \(O_2\) on left. Coefficient = 2.
Topic 2.4 — Acids, Bases & pH
Question 10
A solution has \([H^+] = 10^{-4}\) M. What is its pH?
Solution: \(pH = -\log_{10}[H^+] = -\log(10^{-4}) = \textbf{4}\). This is acidic (pH < 7) — about the acidity of orange juice or coffee.
Question 11
How many times more \(H^+\) ions are in a solution of pH 3 compared to pH 6?
Solution: Each pH unit is a 10× change. ΔpH = 6 − 3 = 3 units, so concentration differs by \(10^3 = \textbf{1000}\) times. Solution at pH 3 has 1000× more \(H^+\) than at pH 6.
Question 12
In the neutralisation \(HCl + NaOH \rightarrow \_\_\_ + H_2O\), the salt is:
Solution: Acid + Base → Salt + Water. The cation of the base (Na⁺) pairs with the anion of the acid (Cl⁻) to form NaCl. The reaction is: \(HCl + NaOH \rightarrow NaCl + H_2O\).
