Topic 1.1 — Cells & the Cell Cycle
Question 1
Which statement is the foundation of cell theory?
Solution: Cell theory has three parts: (1) all living things are made of one or more cells; (2) the cell is the basic unit of life; (3) cells come from pre-existing cells. Prokaryotes lack a nucleus, ruling out (a). Mitosis is one form of division — bacteria use binary fission. DNA exists in all cells but not always as chromosomes.
Question 2
During which phase of mitosis do sister chromatids separate?
Solution: Anaphase is when sister chromatids separate at the centromere and are pulled to opposite poles by shortening spindle fibres. Metaphase precedes this with alignment; telophase follows with nuclear membrane reformation.
Question 3
A microscope has a 10× eyepiece and a 40× objective. What is the total magnification?
Solution: Total magnification = eyepiece × objective = 10 × 40 = 400×. The image appears 400 times larger than its real size.
Question 4
Cytokinesis in plant cells differs from animal cells because:
Solution: Plant cells have rigid cell walls that cannot constrict. Instead, vesicles from the Golgi apparatus deposit cell-wall material along the equator, forming a cell plate that grows outward to fuse with the existing wall. Animal cells use a contractile ring (cleavage furrow).
Topic 1.2 — Animal & Plant Tissues
Question 5
Which tissue would you expect to find lining the small intestine where nutrients are absorbed?
Solution: Simple columnar epithelium provides a thin, single-cell layer for efficient absorption, and the microvilli on the apical surface multiply surface area roughly 20-fold. Stratified squamous is on the skin and oesophagus (protection); cardiac muscle is in the heart only; dense connective is in tendons.
Question 6
Which is true of skeletal muscle?
Solution: Skeletal muscle is voluntary (under conscious control), shows striations (organised actin/myosin), and is multinucleated. (b) describes cardiac; (c) and (d) describe smooth muscle.
Question 7
Which plant tissue carries dissolved sugars from leaves to roots and fruit?
Solution: Phloem (living sieve-tube elements with companion cells) carries sugars (mostly sucrose) from photosynthetic "source" tissues to growing "sink" tissues. Xylem carries water and minerals upward only.
Topic 1.3 — Organ Systems
Question 8
Gas exchange in the lungs occurs in the:
Solution: Alveoli are the ~300 million tiny air sacs whose single-cell-thick walls allow rapid diffusion of \(O_2\) into capillaries and \(CO_2\) out. The trachea, bronchi, and bronchioles are conducting passages; no gas exchange happens there.
Question 9
The left ventricle pumps blood into the:
Solution: The left ventricle pumps oxygen-rich blood into the aorta, which feeds the entire systemic circuit. The right ventricle pumps oxygen-poor blood into the pulmonary artery toward the lungs.
Question 10
In a sample of dividing onion-root tip cells, 90 of 100 are in interphase and 10 are dividing. If the full cell cycle is 20 h, estimate the duration of interphase.
Solution: The proportion of cells in a phase corresponds to the time spent in it. Interphase fraction = 90/100 = 0.90. Duration = 0.90 × 20 h = 18 h. Mitosis fills the remaining 2 h — a typical breakdown for actively dividing meristem tissue.
Question 11
A pluripotent stem cell can develop into:
Solution: Pluripotent cells (e.g., embryonic stem cells, induced pluripotent stem cells) can become any of the ~200 cell types of the adult body but not extra-embryonic tissues. Totipotent cells (the fertilised egg and its first few descendants) can become both. Multipotent cells are restricted to a lineage, like blood (hematopoietic stem cells).
Question 12
A malignant tumour differs from a benign tumour because it:
Solution: Malignant cancers invade nearby tissues and may metastasise (spread to distant sites via blood or lymphatic vessels). Benign tumours remain localised and are usually less life-threatening, although they can still cause harm by pressing on organs.
