Topic 5.1 — Scientific Method & Experimental Design
Question 1
A good hypothesis must be:
Solution: A scientific hypothesis must be a testable statement that could in principle be shown wrong by experiment. Claims that cannot be falsified are outside the domain of science.
Question 2
In an experiment to test the effect of fertiliser on plant growth, the dependent variable is:
Solution: The dependent variable is what is measured (the outcome). Independent variable is what is changed (fertiliser). Soil type and water amount should be controlled (kept constant).
Question 3
Replication of trials in an experiment is important because it:
Solution: Repeating trials averages out random fluctuations and allows you to estimate uncertainty (e.g., standard deviation). It does not eliminate systematic error and cannot "prove" anything definitively.
Topic 5.2 — Lab Safety & WHMIS
Question 4
The WHMIS pictogram showing a flame indicates:
Solution: The flame pictogram indicates a flammable material — keep away from heat, sparks, open flames. Corrosive uses the "corrosion" pictogram (test tube dripping on hand/surface).
Question 5
When diluting concentrated sulfuric acid, you should:
Solution: Always add acid to water, slowly and with stirring. The reaction releases heat; doing it the other way around can boil the surface water and spit acid out. Mnemonic: "Do as you oughta — add acid to water."
Topic 5.3 — Data & Error Analysis
Question 6
An experimental boiling point is 99.1 °C; the accepted value is 100.0 °C. Calculate percent error.
Solution: Percent error = |experimental − accepted| / accepted × 100% = |99.1 − 100.0|/100.0 × 100% = 0.9/100 × 100% = 0.9% — a small error.
Question 7
A thermometer with markings every 1 °C is used. The single-measurement uncertainty is approximately:
Solution: The convention is half the smallest division: ±0.5 of the marked unit. So 1 °C markings → ±0.5 °C uncertainty on a single reading.
Question 8
A systematic error in an experiment:
Solution: Systematic errors are biases (e.g., a thermometer reading 2 °C too high) that consistently shift results. Averaging more trials cannot reduce them — only fixing the design or calibration can.
Topic 5.4 — STSE & Careers
Question 9
"STSE" stands for:
Solution: STSE explores the deep connections between scientific knowledge and the broader world. It is built into Ontario's science curriculum across all strands.
Question 10
Which is a college-pathway (technician-level) science career?
Solution: Medical laboratory technologist is a typical college program (3-year diploma in Ontario). The others typically require university degrees or further graduate/professional training.
