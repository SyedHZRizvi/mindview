Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[2]B · Biology
During which phase of mitosis do sister chromatids separate?
Answer Key

Anaphase. Sister chromatids separate at the centromere and are pulled to opposite poles by spindle fibres.

2
[3]B · Tissues
List the four primary animal tissues and give one organ for each.
Answer Key

Epithelial (skin, intestinal lining); Connective (bone, blood, cartilage, tendons); Muscle (skeletal, cardiac, smooth); Nervous (brain, spinal cord).

3
[3]B · Organ Systems
State the function of: (a) alveoli, (b) villi, (c) nephrons.
Answer Key

(a) Alveoli: site of gas exchange (\(O_2/CO_2\)) in the lungs, single-cell-thick walls maximise diffusion. (b) Villi: finger-like projections lining the small intestine that increase surface area for nutrient absorption. (c) Nephrons: functional units of the kidney where blood is filtered and urine is formed.

4
[2]C · Chemistry
A neutral atom of phosphorus (P) has atomic number 15. How many valence electrons?
Answer Key

5. Group 15 → 5 valence electrons. Electron configuration: 2, 8, 5.

5
[3]C · Nomenclature
Give the IUPAC name of: (a) \(K_2SO_4\), (b) \(CO_2\), (c) \(Fe_2O_3\).
Answer Key

(a) Potassium sulfate (K⁺ and \(SO_4^{2-}\)). (b) Carbon dioxide (Greek prefix for 2 oxygens). (c) Iron(III) oxide (Roman numeral for multivalent Fe; each Fe is +3).

6
[3]C · Balancing
Balance: \(\_\,C_3H_8 + \_\,O_2 \rightarrow \_\,CO_2 + \_\,H_2O\) (combustion of propane).
Answer Key

\(C_3H_8 + 5O_2 \rightarrow 3CO_2 + 4H_2O\). C: 3=3; H: 8=8; O: 10=10.

7
[2]C · pH
A solution has pH 9. Is it:
Answer Key

Slightly basic. pH 9 is two units above neutral (7), so basic but mild (about the pH of baking-soda solution).

8
[2]D · Climate
Which is NOT a greenhouse gas?
Answer Key

Oxygen. \(O_2\) is a symmetric diatomic and does not absorb infrared. The others all absorb IR strongly.

9
[3]D · Carbon cycle
Identify three processes in the carbon cycle and label each as a source or sink of atmospheric \(CO_2\).
Answer Key

Photosynthesis: sink (\(CO_2\) → biomass). Respiration: source. Decomposition: source. Combustion (fossil fuels): source. Ocean uptake: sink (in cool surface waters). Volcanic outgassing: small source.

10
[2]E · Light
What is the speed of light in vacuum?
Answer Key

\(3.00 \times 10^8\) m/s (about 300,000 km/s — the speed at which light travels 1 m in 3.3 ns).

Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
11
[5]B · Mitotic index
A student observes 300 onion-root cells: 270 in interphase, 12 in prophase, 6 in metaphase, 6 in anaphase, 6 in telophase. If the cell cycle is 18 hours, estimate the duration of mitosis as a whole.
Answer Key

Mitosis cells = 12+6+6+6 = 30; total counted = 300. Fraction in mitosis = 30/300 = 0.10. Duration of mitosis = 0.10 × 18 h = 1.8 h ≈ 108 min. Interphase ≈ 16.2 h (90%).

12
[5]C · Reaction prediction
Predict products and balance: (a) \(Zn + 2HCl \rightarrow ?\); (b) \(NaOH + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow ?\); (c) \(KClO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} ?\); (d) \(C_2H_5OH + O_2 \rightarrow ?\); (e) \(AgNO_3 + NaCl \rightarrow ?\).
Answer Key

(a) Single displacement: \(Zn + 2HCl \rightarrow ZnCl_2 + H_2\). (b) Neutralisation: \(2NaOH + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow Na_2SO_4 + 2H_2O\). (c) Decomposition: \(2KClO_3 \rightarrow 2KCl + 3O_2\) (lab source of \(O_2\)). (d) Combustion: \(C_2H_5OH + 3O_2 \rightarrow 2CO_2 + 3H_2O\) (ethanol burning). (e) Double displacement: \(AgNO_3 + NaCl \rightarrow AgCl(s) + NaNO_3\) (white precipitate, the classic chloride test).

13
[5]D · Calculation
A power plant emits 200 tonnes of methane and 50,000 tonnes of \(CO_2\) per year. GWP-100 of methane is 28. (a) Calculate total \(CO_2\)-equivalent emissions. (b) What percentage of total is from methane?
Answer Key

(a) Methane CO₂-eq = 200 × 28 = 5600 tonnes. Total CO₂-eq = 50,000 + 5600 = 55,600 tonnes. (b) Methane share = 5600 / 55,600 × 100% ≈ 10.1%. This illustrates why methane reduction matters — a small mass leak punches above its weight on climate.

14
[5]E · Snell's law
A light ray in air strikes a glass surface (\(n = 1.50\)) at 40° from the normal. (a) Find the angle of refraction. (b) If the same ray then exits the glass into water (\(n = 1.33\)), find the angle in water.
Answer Key

(a) \(1.00 \sin 40° = 1.50 \sin\theta_2\) → \(\sin\theta_2 = 0.643/1.50 = 0.429\) → \(\theta_2 = \arcsin(0.429) = \textbf{25.4°}\). (b) Going glass → water: \(1.50 \sin 25.4° = 1.33 \sin\theta_3\) → \(\sin\theta_3 = 1.50 \times 0.429/1.33 = 0.484\) → \(\theta_3 = \arcsin(0.484) = \textbf{28.9°}\). Bends away from normal (entering less-dense medium).

15
[5]A · Lab design
Design a controlled experiment to test how surface area affects the rate at which calcium carbonate (chalk) reacts with HCl. Identify IV, DV, three controlled variables, and an expected result.
Answer Key

IV: surface area (use same mass of chalk in different forms: whole stick, broken into pieces, ground to powder). DV: rate of reaction (mass loss/min, or volume of \(CO_2\) collected/min, or time for fixed mass to dissolve). Controlled: same mass of \(CaCO_3\); same volume of HCl; same HCl concentration; same temperature; same stirring rate. Expected result: rate increases with surface area — powder reacts much faster than the stick because more particles are exposed to acid molecules. This is the rate-of-reaction principle widely applied in pharmaceutical formulation (fine powder for fast-acting medications).

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
16
[5]B · Diagram
Describe (or sketch) a labeled diagram of an animal cell showing at least five organelles. State the function of each.
Answer Key

Nucleus: control centre, contains DNA. Mitochondrion: ATP production via cellular respiration. Ribosome: protein synthesis. Endoplasmic reticulum: protein and lipid synthesis (rough = with ribosomes; smooth = no ribosomes). Golgi apparatus: packaging and modifying proteins for secretion. Lysosome: digestion of cellular waste. Cell membrane: selective barrier, regulates entry/exit. Cytoplasm: gel-like interior where reactions occur.

17
[5]C · Bohr–Rutherford
Describe the Bohr–Rutherford diagrams for magnesium (Mg, Z = 12) and oxygen (O, Z = 8). Explain how they bond to form MgO.
Answer Key

Mg: 12 p, 12 e⁻ in shells 2, 8, 2. O: 8 p, 8 e⁻ in shells 2, 6. Mg loses its 2 valence electrons to reach octet (configuration of Ne) → \(Mg^{2+}\). O gains those 2 electrons to complete its valence shell (config of Ne) → \(O^{2-}\). The opposite charges attract: ionic bond forms a 1:1 lattice of \(Mg^{2+}\) and \(O^{2-}\), giving the ionic compound MgO (a hard, white, very high-melting-point solid used in refractory bricks).

18
[5]D · Greenhouse effect
Explain the natural greenhouse effect step by step, including the role of infrared radiation. Distinguish it from the enhanced greenhouse effect.
Answer Key

(1) Visible/UV sunlight passes through atmosphere; ~30% reflected (clouds, ice), ~70% absorbed by surface. (2) Surface warms and re-emits energy as longer-wavelength infrared radiation. (3) Greenhouse gases (\(H_2O\), \(CO_2\), \(CH_4\), \(N_2O\)) absorb IR and re-emit it in all directions, including back to the surface. (4) Result: surface is ~33 °C warmer than without these gases (—18 °C → +15 °C average). The natural effect makes Earth habitable; the enhanced effect is the additional warming from human-emitted greenhouse gases since 1850 — about 1.1–1.2 °C of global warming and rising.

19
[5]E · Ray diagram
Describe the construction of a ray diagram for an object placed between the focal point and \(2f\) of a converging lens. State the image characteristics.
Answer Key

Three principal rays: (1) parallel to axis from object → refracts through far F. (2) Through optical centre → undeviated. (3) Through near F → exits parallel to axis. The rays converge beyond \(2f\) on the far side. Image is real, inverted, larger than the object. Used in slide projectors and old-school overhead projectors.

20
[5]A · Lab report
A student is writing a lab report. List the standard sections and briefly explain what goes in each.
Answer Key

Title: descriptive, specific to the experiment. Purpose/Question: what you investigated. Hypothesis: testable prediction with reasoning. Materials: list of equipment and chemicals with quantities. Procedure: numbered, reproducible steps. Observations/Data: tables, graphs, units, sig figs. Analysis: calculations, graphs, identification of patterns. Discussion: interpretation, comparison to expected results, identification of sources of error (random and systematic). Conclusion: brief statement answering the question. Sources: references in a consistent format. Safety: PPE used and WHMIS pictograms identified.

Part D — Application [25 marks]
21
[5]B · Case study
Asthma and Smoking: Explain how cigarette smoke damages the respiratory system. Include at least three specific mechanisms or effects.
Answer Key

(1) Tar and irritants damage the cilia lining airways (epithelial tissue), so mucus and particles cannot be cleared upward, increasing infection risk. (2) Inflammation thickens airway walls and narrows the lumen, making breathing harder and triggering asthma attacks. (3) Nicotine constricts blood vessels, reducing tissue oxygen. (4) Carcinogens (e.g., benzene, formaldehyde) damage DNA in lung cells, increasing risk of lung cancer (≈85% of lung-cancer cases are caused by smoking). (5) Chronic exposure destroys alveolar walls (emphysema), reducing the surface area available for gas exchange — patients become breathless from minimal exertion. (6) Smoking during pregnancy reduces fetal oxygen delivery, contributing to low birth weight and developmental issues.

22
[5]C · STSE — Antacids
Antacid Effectiveness: Two common antacids are calcium carbonate (\(CaCO_3\)) and magnesium hydroxide (\(Mg(OH)_2\)). Write neutralisation equations for each with HCl and discuss the advantage of each (consider potential side effects).
Answer Key

\(CaCO_3 + 2HCl \rightarrow CaCl_2 + H_2O + CO_2\) — fast acting, but \(CO_2\) can cause burping/bloating; long-term high doses can cause hypercalcemia. \(Mg(OH)_2 + 2HCl \rightarrow MgCl_2 + 2H_2O\) — also fast, but Mg has a laxative effect (used in "Milk of Magnesia") so can cause diarrhea. Many products combine \(Mg(OH)_2\) with aluminium hydroxide \(Al(OH)_3\) (which is constipating) to balance side effects. Patient choice depends on individual response, cost, and other health conditions (e.g., kidney issues affect Mg handling).

23
[5]D · STSE — Climate Policy
Canadian Climate Policy: Canada has committed to net-zero emissions by 2050. Identify two specific policies or technologies that contribute, one challenge for implementation, and one equity consideration.
Answer Key

Policies/technologies: federal carbon pricing (escalating to \$170/tonne by 2030); zero-emissions vehicle mandate (100% of new car sales by 2035); Clean Electricity Regulations (net-zero grid by 2035); methane reduction targets in oil and gas (75% cut by 2030); investment in nuclear small-modular reactors; building heat-pump incentives; carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS) for hard-to-decarbonise sectors. Challenges: existing fossil-fuel infrastructure and employment (oil and gas accounts for ~5% of GDP); grid expansion required to handle electrification; cost of retrofitting buildings; political polarisation; ensuring competitiveness with countries that have weaker policies. Equity: rural and remote communities (often Indigenous) face higher heating and transportation costs without comparable alternatives; just-transition funding for fossil-fuel workers in Alberta and Newfoundland; carbon rebates structured to be progressive (lower-income households receive more rebate than they pay in carbon costs); Indigenous-led conservation and renewable energy projects.

24
[5]E · Application — Corrective lenses
Vision Correction: A patient is hyperopic (far-sighted); her near point is 1.0 m. (a) What focal length lens corrects her vision so she can read at 25 cm? (b) Calculate the power in dioptres.
Answer Key

For reading at 25 cm: \(d_o = 25\) cm = 0.25 m. The lens must create a virtual image at her near point (1.0 m), so \(d_i = -100\) cm = −1.0 m. Thin-lens equation: \(1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/0.25 + 1/(-1.0) = 4 - 1 = 3\) m⁻¹ → \(f = 1/3\) m = 0.333 m = 33.3 cm. (b) Power = \(1/f\) = +3.0 dioptres (positive = converging lens, used for hyperopia). The prescription would be written as "+3.0 D".

25
[5]A · Integration
Indigenous Knowledge: Choose one SNC2D unit. Explain one way Indigenous knowledge could inform or enrich the science in that unit. Discuss one ethical principle for engagement with Indigenous communities.
Answer Key

(Example: Climate Change, Strand D.) Indigenous knowledge from Inuit communities in the Canadian Arctic — recorded for centuries through oral tradition and direct observation — provides detailed accounts of sea-ice timing, animal behaviour, and weather patterns. Inuit hunters have observed thinner ice, later freeze-up, shifting wildlife ranges, and unusual species (e.g., orcas now appearing in formerly ice-covered waters). This Indigenous Knowledge has been integrated with satellite and instrumental records to give richer pictures of Arctic climate change, including changes (ice quality, snow texture) instruments cannot capture. Ethical principle: Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC) — Indigenous communities must be meaningfully consulted before research is conducted on their lands or knowledge; they must understand the goals, agree to participate, and have authority over how data is used and shared. Recognised under the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP), now adopted as law in Canada (UNDRIP Act, 2021). Researchers should also follow the OCAP principles for First Nations data: Ownership, Control, Access, and Possession. Knowledge holders should be credited and benefits of research shared equitably.
