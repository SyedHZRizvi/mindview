Topic 1.1–1.2 — Kingdoms, Domains & Binomial Nomenclature
Question 1
Which of the following correctly lists the three domains of life?
Solution: The three domains (Carl Woese, 1977) are Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. Kingdoms (Plantae, Animalia, Fungi, Protista) sit within Eukarya.
Question 2
Which is the correct way to write the scientific name of the polar bear (Ursus maritimus)?
Solution: Binomial nomenclature: genus capitalised, species lowercase, both italicised (or underlined when handwritten). Ursus maritimus is correct.
Question 3
A single-celled organism with no nucleus and a peptidoglycan cell wall belongs to which domain?
Solution: Bacteria have peptidoglycan in their cell walls and lack a nucleus. Archaea also lack a nucleus but use different membrane lipids (ether-linked) and never have peptidoglycan walls.
Question 4
List the taxonomic hierarchy in order from broadest to narrowest. Order: Domain, _____, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, _____, Species. Type the two missing levels separated by a comma.
Solution: Full order: Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. Mnemonic: "Do Kings Play Chess On Fine Green Sand?"
Topic 1.3–1.4 — Phylogeny & Invertebrate/Vertebrate Survey
Question 5
On a cladogram, the most recent common ancestor of two taxa is the:
Solution: Each node on a cladogram represents a hypothetical common ancestor. The deeper the shared node, the more distant the relationship.
Question 6
A shared derived character that defines a clade is called:
Solution: A synapomorphy is a shared derived (inherited from a recent common ancestor) trait that defines a monophyletic group.
Question 7
All members of phylum Chordata possess, at some life stage, all of the following EXCEPT:
Solution: Chordates have notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, and a post-anal tail at some stage. A chitin exoskeleton is the hallmark of Arthropoda, not Chordata.
Question 8
Which feature is unique to mammals?
Solution: Hair and mammary glands are unique mammalian features. Endothermy is shared with birds; four-chambered hearts are shared with crocodiles and birds; the amniotic egg is shared with all amniotes (reptiles, birds, mammals).
Topic 1.5–1.6 — Biodiversity & Human Impact
Question 9
A community has 80 individuals of species A, 15 of species B, and 5 of species C. Calculate Simpson's diversity index, D = 1 − Σ(pi)². Round to two decimal places.
Solution: Proportions: 0.80, 0.15, 0.05. Σ(pi)² = 0.64 + 0.0225 + 0.0025 = 0.665. D = 1 − 0.665 ≈ 0.34. Lower D indicates a community dominated by one species (low evenness).
Question 10
A non-native species that spreads aggressively and reduces native biodiversity is called:
Solution: An invasive species is non-native AND causes ecological/economic harm. Not all non-natives are invasive. Examples in Ontario: zebra mussels, garlic mustard, emerald ash borer.
Question 11
An organism that exists only in one specific geographic region is called:
Solution: Endemic = native to and found only in a specific place (e.g., lemurs are endemic to Madagascar). Endemic species are highly vulnerable to habitat loss.
Question 12
DDT damaged populations of fish-eating birds primarily by:
Solution: DDT accumulates up the food chain (biomagnification). In top predators (bald eagle, peregrine falcon, osprey), DDT/DDE disrupted calcium deposition, producing thin eggshells that broke. Canada banned DDT in 1972; populations have since rebounded.
