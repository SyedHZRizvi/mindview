Unit 3: Workplace, Leadership & Adult Communication — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand C
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify your starting point on adult communication, feedback, conflict, and leadership skills before formal instruction. Descriptive feedback only.
Question 1 Email Register
[3 marks]
Draft a 3–4-sentence email to a teacher requesting a meeting to discuss your university references. Use what you currently believe is the right register for the audience.
Question 2 Feedback Experience
[3 marks]
Describe a time someone gave you critical feedback you initially didn't agree with. In 3–4 sentences, identify: what you felt in the moment, how you responded, and what you would do differently now.
Question 3 Emotional Awareness
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe a moment in the past week when you felt strong emotion at school or work. Name the emotion as specifically as you can (not "bad" or "stressed"). What triggered it?
Question 4 Conflict
[3 marks]
Describe a recent disagreement with a peer, family member, or co-worker. In 4–5 sentences: what was each party's position? Looking back, can you identify the underlying interest each side actually cared about?
Question 5 Cross-Cultural
[2 marks]
Have you worked or studied with someone from a different cultural or generational background? In 3–4 sentences, describe one moment where you noticed a communication style different from your own.
Question 6 Leadership
[2 marks]
Describe a time you led an effort without formal authority — at school, in a group project, in a club, on a sports team, at work. What worked? What didn't?
Question 7 Self-Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 3 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) professional email and meeting etiquette; (b) giving and receiving feedback; (c) emotional intelligence; (d) cross-cultural and intergenerational communication; (e) interest-based negotiation (Harvard method); (f) leading without authority and managing up? Explain what specifically is unclear.

📖 Complete Answer Key — Instructor Reference

Q1 (3 marks): Look for register match — neither overly casual ("hey!") nor over-formal ("Dear esteemed teacher"). Strong emails: specific subject line; ask in sentence 1; context follows; polite close. Note specific gaps — ask in paragraph 3, no subject line, register mismatch.

Q2 (3 marks): Look for retrospective awareness. Students who can identify their initial reactive defence vs the eventually-useful kernel of the feedback are ready for SBI and Heen-and-Stone instruction. Students who report only "they were wrong" need more development on the "decide what to do with it" step.

Q3 (3 marks): Look for emotional granularity. Default-level vocabulary ("stressed," "bad") is the diagnostic signal — students need vocabulary work. Students who name specific emotions (frustrated, embarrassed, anxious, resentful, hopeful) are at a stronger starting point.

Q4 (3 marks): Look for position-vs-interest awareness — even if the student can't yet name those concepts, can they retrospectively identify what each side actually cared about? This is the core Harvard-method readiness check.

Q5 (2 marks): Look for openness vs default reactivity. Students who describe trying to understand difference (not "they were weird") are starting from a better place. Students who report no cross-cultural exposure should be paired with peers who have it during instruction.

Q6 (2 marks): Look for the leadership-without-authority pattern: influence, follow-through, generous help. Students who report only formal-authority roles (captain, head, chair) need exposure to the quieter variety; students who report informal patterns (organising, supporting, connecting) are showing the durable skills.

Q7 (ungraded): Use to plan differentiation. Common gaps: register confusion in email; under-developed emotional vocabulary; conflict-avoidance; lack of exposure to negotiation frameworks.

Marking guidance: Diagnostic, not graded. Two strengths + two next steps per student.
