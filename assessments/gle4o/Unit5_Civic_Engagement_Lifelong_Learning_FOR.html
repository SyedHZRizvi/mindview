Unit 5: Civic Engagement and Lifelong Learning — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand D
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify your starting point on civic participation, volunteer engagement, lifelong-learning posture, career adaptation, networks, and "enough" thinking. Descriptive feedback only.
Question 1 Civic Knowledge
[3 marks]
Without looking it up: Name your federal MP, your provincial MPP, and your municipal councillor or ward. If you don't know any, what does that suggest about your current civic engagement?
Question 2 Voting Intent
[2 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe your current attitude toward voting. Have you ever attended an all-candidates meeting, written to an elected representative, or read your local newspaper's political coverage?
Question 3 Volunteering
[3 marks]
Describe your OSSD community-involvement experience. What did you do? What did you learn? What did you enjoy or not enjoy?
Question 4 Lifelong Learning
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what you do for learning outside school. Do you read books that aren't assigned? Listen to educational podcasts? Take online courses? What is your "learning habit"?
Question 5 Career View
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what you currently think about how AI and automation will affect the job market over the next ten years. Are you worried, indifferent, optimistic? Why?
Question 6 Networks
[2 marks]
Identify three adults outside your immediate family whom you would feel comfortable contacting for advice about a major life decision. In 3–4 sentences, describe how you came to know each and how often you are in contact.
Question 7 Self-Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 5 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) voting at three government levels and civic participation; (b) adult volunteering vs OSSD hours; (c) lifelong-learning frameworks (70-20-10, deliberate practice, cohort method); (d) portfolio careers and AI labour-market adaptation; (e) network-building and mentorship; (f) defining personal "enough" in a finite life? Explain.

📖 Complete Answer Key — Instructor Reference

Q1 (3 marks): Civic-knowledge baseline. Most students will name none or one. The five-minute fix (look up postal-code → three representatives) is the practical move for the start of the unit.

Q2 (2 marks): Voting intent. Look for engaged-but-uninformed vs disengaged-and-cynical patterns. Both are addressable; the disengaged-cynical position needs more direct counter-evidence about close ridings and turnout-differential outcomes.

Q3 (3 marks): Volunteer reflection — what did the OSSD requirement actually produce? Students who can describe what they learned about themselves are stronger; students who report "I just did the hours" need the structured-reflection move (date, organisation, role, hours, learning).

Q4 (3 marks): Learning-habit baseline. Students who report no learning habit outside school are the modal — the unit's job is to introduce specific frameworks (one book per quarter, library card use, MOOCs, cohort programs). Students with existing habits can sharpen them.

Q5 (3 marks): Career-anxiety baseline. Look for accurate-but-not-paralysing assessment. Students who are dismissive ("AI won't affect my field") need a counter-frame; students who are panicked need the "task-reshaping, not elimination" reframe.

Q6 (2 marks): Network baseline. Students who can name three adults are already developing a personal board of directors. Students who can name zero need explicit instruction on relationship-building from a small starting point.

Q7 (ungraded): Use to plan differentiation. Common gaps: civic-knowledge gaps (representatives, three-level structure); volunteer reflection skills; learning-habit installation; network-building anxiety.

Marking guidance: Diagnostic, not graded. Two strengths + two priorities per student.
