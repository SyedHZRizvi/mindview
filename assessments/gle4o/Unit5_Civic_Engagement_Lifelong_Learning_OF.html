Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
1
[3]
Identify each: (a) the strength of weak ties (Granovetter); (b) the portfolio career (Handy); (c) the hedonic treadmill (Brickman and Campbell).
Answer Key

(a) Strength of weak ties (Granovetter, 1973) — jobs and opportunities reach you most often through acquaintances rather than close friends, because acquaintances bring new information from different networks. (b) Portfolio career (Handy, 1989) — multiple simultaneous or sequential income streams from different sources; diversification of income; increasingly common in 2026 labour markets. (c) Hedonic treadmill (Brickman and Campbell, 1971) — adaptation neutralises material gains; above a moderate income threshold, additional income produces diminishing well-being returns.

2
[2]
In Canada, federal electoral turnout for voters aged 18–24 in the 2021 federal election was approximately:
Answer Key

B. Elections Canada 2021 data: 47% turnout 18–24 vs 75% turnout 65–74. The gap shapes which voters politicians prioritise.

3
[2]
In 2–3 sentences, distinguish federal, provincial, and municipal jurisdiction in Canada with one example each.
Answer Key

Federal jurisdiction includes immigration, defence, criminal law, monetary policy (e.g., national immigration levels plan, federal Criminal Code, Bank of Canada). Provincial jurisdiction includes education, health-care delivery, the Residential Tenancies Act, post-secondary tuition policy (e.g., Ontario MOHLTC running OHIP, the LTB enforcing the RTA). Municipal jurisdiction includes transit operations, by-laws, zoning, parks, libraries (e.g., TTC operations, Toronto zoning by-law). Marks: 1 each level with example.

4
[3]
Define each: (a) deliberate practice (Ericsson); (b) cohort-based learning; (c) Cialdini's reciprocity principle.
Answer Key

(a) Deliberate practice (Ericsson) — practice on the edge of current ability, with feedback; not all practice is equal; the "10,000 hours" claim is a popularised simplification of the underlying quality-of-practice argument. (b) Cohort-based learning — time-limited peer group with an expert moves through material together; empirically higher retention than self-paced solo MOOCs. (c) Cialdini's reciprocity — small favours done genuinely produce later returned favours; one of his six principles of influence.

5
[2]
The income threshold above which incremental income produces diminishing well-being returns, in Canadian research, is approximately:
Answer Key

B. Kahneman and Deaton (2010) and subsequent reanalyses converge around $90,000–$100,000 household income. The threshold has shifted upward with inflation and continues to vary by household composition and location.

6
[3]
Identify three civic-participation actions an 18-year-old can take beyond voting.
Answer Key

Examples: (1) Contacting elected representatives (handwritten letters, phone calls; constituency-office contact). (2) Attending all-candidates meetings during campaigns. (3) Joining a riding association of a political party for nomination votes and policy participation. (4) Submitting briefs to parliamentary committees. (5) Signing or initiating e-petitions on the federal or Ontario portals. (6) Volunteer-canvassing for a campaign. (7) Attending parliamentary committee hearings (ParlVU). (8) Reading Hansard via openparliament.ca. Marks: 1 each (3).

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [15 marks]
7
[5]
"By 2030, roughly 30–40% of the working-age population will need to substantially re-skill at least once." — McKinsey Global Institute Reskilling Revolution, 2024.
Analyse the implications of this claim for an 18-year-old's first career decisions. In 5–6 sentences, identify two strategic adaptations the student should make.
Answer Key

The claim implies several adaptations. (1) Invest in meta-learning — the skill of learning new skills will be applied repeatedly; 70-20-10 model, deliberate practice, and cohort-based learning are tools to develop. (2) Invest in AI-resilient parts of skill sets — judgment, integration, relationship work; AI reshapes task content within jobs faster than it eliminates jobs. (3) Build optionality — extended emergency fund (6 months), professional online presence, side income, ability to leave a role without panic. (4) Treat every job as also a learning project — what new skill am I acquiring? (5) Build a personal board of mentors — diverse perspectives across career, finance, mental health enable better re-skilling decisions later. Marks: 2 each (strong responses identify two with substantive explanation), 1 synthesis.

8
[5]
Apply the Tail End calculation (Urban, 2014). For a hypothetical 18-year-old in Toronto whose parents live in Sudbury, who will move to Vancouver for work at 22 and likely return to visit 2 weeks a year: roughly how many in-person days remain with parents over 30 years? What does the calculation imply for current behaviour?
Answer Key

Assumptions: parents 52 now, likely live to ~82. Years 18–22: roughly 30 visit-days/year × 4 years = 120 days. Years 22–52: 14 visit-days/year × 30 years = 420 days. Total: ~540 in-person days remaining over the next 34 years. If childhood-to-now was ~5,000 in-person days, the remaining time is roughly 10% of total lifetime in-person days. Implication: visits home over the next decade are not optional politeness but the majority of remaining time with these specific people. The reframing changes attitude toward holiday visits, phone calls, and parent-friend balance during young-adult years. Marks: 2 computation, 1 acknowledgment of moves and lifespans, 2 implication.

9
[5]
Critique the claim: "Networking is transactional and inauthentic, so I should focus only on doing good work and not 'play the networking game.'" In 5–6 sentences, identify what is right about the concern and what is missing.
Answer Key

What is right: the transactional approach (showing up at events to collect contacts and ask for jobs) does feel performative and is empirically less effective. Adam Grant's "givers" research finds the bounded-generous pattern outperforms the matcher and taker patterns over 5-year horizons; this is not the transactional caricature. What is missing: the alternative is not "no networking" but a relational frame — being genuinely useful to other people over time, sending small messages, making introductions, remembering details. Granovetter's "weak ties" research shows jobs and opportunities reach people through acquaintances more than through close friends, so the size and diversity of network meaningfully shapes opportunity. The disengaged-stoic position (do good work, don't network) under-performs the relational-generous-bounded position empirically. The mature framing: skip the transactional networking events; invest in the small relational moves that compound. Marks: 2 acknowledging the right concern, 2 missing piece, 1 synthesis.
