Unit 2: Strategies for Post-Secondary Academic and Career Success — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand B
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify your starting point on post-secondary readiness — study strategies, executive function, academic integrity, digital tools, and support-service awareness. Descriptive feedback only.
Question 1 Pathway
[3 marks]
Where are you most likely heading after OSSD: university, college, apprenticeship, workplace, or something else? In 3–4 sentences, describe your current best guess and what you know about that destination's demands.
Question 2 Study Methods
[3 marks]
Describe your current study strategy. What do you do when you have to read a 30-page chapter for a course? How do you take notes during a lecture? Be honest about what actually happens vs what you intend.
Question 3 Executive Function
[3 marks]
How do you currently manage your time, deadlines, and tasks? Do you use a calendar, a task list, an app, paper, or nothing? In 3–4 sentences, describe your system and how well it works.
Question 4 AI Use
[3 marks]
Describe how you currently use generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) — if at all — in your schoolwork. In 3–4 sentences, identify one task where you find AI useful and one task where you think AI use would be inappropriate.
Question 5 Support Services
[2 marks]
Without looking it up: name two academic support services you would expect to find on most Ontario university or college campuses. Have you ever used a similar service in secondary school?
Question 6 Digital Tools
[2 marks]
Which note-taking, calendar, or task-management apps do you currently use? How would you describe your "digital workflow"?
Question 7 Self-Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 2 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) the structural differences between OSSD and university/college/apprenticeship; (b) critical reading methods (SQ3R, Cornell, zettelkasten); (c) academic integrity policies and AI use rules; (d) implementation intentions and timeboxing; (e) digital productivity tools and citation managers; (f) campus support services? Explain what specifically is unclear.

📖 Complete Answer Key — Instructor Reference

Q1 (3 marks): Look for awareness of the specific demands of the chosen pathway (e.g., university hours of independent reading; college contact-hour structure; apprenticeship on-the-job hours; workplace entry expectations). Vague or "I don't know" responses are the primary diagnostic signal — these students need pathway-specific instruction.

Q2 (3 marks): Look for honesty. Students who report re-reading, highlighting, and last-minute cramming need direct instruction on active-recall and spaced-retrieval methods. Students who report any structured method (SQ3R, Cornell, flashcards, mind maps) are starting from a stronger base.

Q3 (3 marks): Look for system maturity. Students who report no system or "I keep it in my head" need executive-function scaffolding. Students who use a calendar or task app are ready for timeboxing and implementation intentions instruction.

Q4 (3 marks): Look for honest engagement. Students who deny using AI in 2026 are usually misreporting; students who report using it for drafting whole essays need direct instruction on policy and the misconduct line. Students who already distinguish brainstorming/critiquing/grammar (within policy) from generation-as-substitute (outside policy) show baseline maturity.

Q5 (2 marks): Awareness baseline. Typical right answers: writing centre, peer tutoring, office hours, library research consultation, accessibility services, academic-success workshops, counselling. If students name fewer than two, the support-service unit deserves more time.

Q6 (2 marks): Note current tool stack. Students with no system need minimum-viable setup (Notion or OneNote, Google or Outlook Calendar, Zotero, OneDrive). Students with elaborate but unused systems need consolidation guidance.

Q7 (ungraded): Use to plan differentiation. Common gaps: under-estimating reading volume; AI-policy unfamiliarity; lack of citation-manager experience; vague awareness of accessibility services and registration timelines.

Marking guidance: The instrument is diagnostic, not graded. Provide descriptive feedback with two specific strengths and two specific next-step priorities. Use responses to plan unit differentiation.
