Topic 2.1–2.2 — OSSD-to-Post-Secondary and Reading
Question 1
Compared with OSSD, a typical Ontario undergraduate course assesses students on:
Solution: Post-secondary shifts from participation-graded to production-graded. Single major essays at 30–40% weight, no draft round, are typical in Ontario first-year university courses.
Question 2
A Red Seal apprenticeship in Ontario typically requires:
Solution: Red Seal trades in Ontario combine on-the-job hours (varies by trade, 6,000–9,000 typical), in-school technical blocks, and a final provincial exam. The Red Seal endorsement is interprovincial. Skilled Trades Ontario is the 2022 regulator.
Question 3
"Highlighting predicts neither retention nor exam grade in research."
According to research on study strategies, the most effective practice for long-term retention is:
Solution: Roediger and Karpicke (2006) and decades of cognitive-science research show active recall and spaced retrieval far outperform re-reading and highlighting. SQ3R's "Recite" step operationalises active recall.
Question 4
The SQ3R reading method (Robinson, 1946) stands for:
Solution: Francis P. Robinson's SQ3R (1946) — Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review — is the most-tested study framework. Each step has a cognitive function.
Topic 2.3–2.4 — Academic Integrity and Executive Function
Question 5
As of 2026, Ontario universities have settled on the following AI-use rule:
Solution: Ontario universities settled on per-instructor permission between 2023 and 2025. The syllabus is the authoritative document; default-prohibited-unless-permitted is the assumption.
Question 6
Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer, 1999) refer to:
Solution: Gollwitzer's research showed if-then plans ("If 9 a.m. Monday, then I will be at my desk reading Chapter 4") roughly double follow-through across exercise, study, voting, and other behaviour-change domains.
Question 7
Timeboxing is the practice of:
Solution: Timeboxing assigns blocks of time to specific tasks on the calendar; the discipline forces realistic time estimation and reveals that most students under-estimate task time by 50–100%.
Question 8
Submitting an AI-generated essay as your own — without disclosure — in an Ontario university is treated as:
Solution: Every Ontario university academic-integrity policy (U of T Code, Queen's Senate Policy, McMaster Academic Integrity Policy, York Senate Policy on Academic Honesty) treats undisclosed AI use as plagiarism.
Topic 2.5–2.6 — Digital Tools and Support Services
Question 9
A citation manager (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) does which of the following:
Solution: Citation managers reduce a 1-hour bibliography task to 3 minutes; Zotero is free and recommended at most Ontario universities.
Question 10
Accessibility Services at an Ontario university:
Solution: Accessibility Services covers documented learning, ADHD, autism, mental-health, and chronic medical conditions in addition to physical impairments. Registration is private under PHIPA. Not retroactive — register week one.
Question 11
The most underused academic-support service on most Ontario campuses is:
Solution: Office hours are scheduled 1–2 hours per week by every instructor; at U of T research, the modal attendance is zero. Three to five office-hours visits per course per term produces a meaningful relationship; one visit is the minimum to be remembered.
Question 12
Short answer: In 2–3 sentences, describe the difference between an articulated AI use that is "within policy" and one that constitutes academic misconduct.
Sample: "Within policy" use means the syllabus permits AI in some role, the student uses AI as a collaborator (for brainstorming, outline critique, grammar check), the prose and argument remain the student's own work, and the AI use is disclosed in a brief statement. Academic misconduct means submitting AI-generated text as if it were the student's own thinking — without disclosure when disclosure is required, or in violation of an "AI prohibited" syllabus rule. The line is whether the submitted work reflects the student's reasoning, with AI as tool, or substitutes for that reasoning.
