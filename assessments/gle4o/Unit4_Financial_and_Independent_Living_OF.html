Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
1
[3]
Identify each: (a) the 50/30/20 rule; (b) the Residential Tenancies Act 2006; (c) the Tax-Free Savings Account.
Answer Key

(a) 50/30/20 rule (Warren and Tyagi, 2005) — 50% of after-tax income to needs, 30% to wants, 20% to savings and debt repayment beyond minimums. (b) Residential Tenancies Act 2006 (Ontario) — governs residential tenancies; tenant-favouring; standard Form 2229E lease; limits deposits to one month's rent (last-month's-rent only); no-pet clauses void; Landlord and Tenant Board is the adjudicator. (c) Tax-Free Savings Account — registered account opened at 18; 2026 contribution room $7,000; growth and withdrawals tax-free forever; unused room accumulates.

2
[2]
As of 1 October 2024, Ontario's general minimum wage is:
Answer Key

B. Ontario's minimum wage rose to $17.20/hour effective 1 October 2024. The October indexing has continued through 2025–26.

3
[2]
In 2–3 sentences, explain how a credit score (300–900 in Canada) is built. Name the largest two component weightings.
Answer Key

Canadian credit scores (Equifax and TransUnion) are built from: payment history (~35%), credit utilisation (~30%), length of history (~15%), new credit (~10%), credit mix (~10%). Payment history and utilisation are the two largest levers — pay on time every time and keep utilisation under 30% (preferably under 10%) of credit limit at statement date.

4
[3]
Identify each: (a) the federal Canada Student Loans interest rule as of April 2023; (b) the Repayment Assistance Plan; (c) the FHSA.
Answer Key

(a) Federal Canada Student Loans permanently stopped accruing interest after graduation as of April 2023. Ontario provincial student loans still accrue interest at the prime rate after the 6-month grace period. (b) RAP — federal Repayment Assistance Plan; reduces or pauses payments when income falls below threshold (roughly $40,000 single in 2026, indexed annually). (c) FHSA — First Home Savings Account; introduced April 2023 for prospective first-time home buyers; combines RRSP-like deduction and TFSA-like tax-free growth for home-down-payment savings.

5
[2]
Under Ontario's RTA 2006, which is true?
Answer Key

B. Only one month's rent is permitted as a deposit (a last-month's-rent deposit). No-pet clauses in residential tenancies are void (narrow condo exceptions). Eviction requires a valid RTA ground and an LTB hearing if contested.

6
[3]
Compute compound interest. Using \(A = P(1+r)^n\), find the final amount of: (a) $1,000 invested at 18 for 47 years at 7%; (b) $1,000 invested at 30 for 35 years at 7%; (c) the difference and what it shows about starting early.
Answer Key

(a) \(A = \$1{,}000 \times 1.07^{47} \approx \$23{,}867\). (b) \(A = \$1{,}000 \times 1.07^{35} \approx \$10{,}677\). (c) Difference is roughly $13,000; the 12-year head start more than doubles the eventual amount because the additional years are at the productive end of the curve. Marks: 1 each (a) and (b), 1 conceptual explanation.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [15 marks]
7
[5]
"Aisha, 22, works full-time at $19/hour. Net ~$32,800/year or $2,733/month. Toronto 2BR share: $1,500. Groceries $400, TTC $158, phone $50, internet $30, insurance $25, OSAP $200 = $2,363 needs (86%). Wants $200 (7%), savings $170 (6%)."
Analyse Aisha's budget. In 5–6 sentences, identify why the 50/30/20 rule fails her, and propose three specific options to bring her back into balance.
Answer Key

The 50/30/20 rule fails because Toronto rent dominates after-tax income — her needs are 86% rather than the target 50%, leaving 14% for wants and savings combined. Three structural options: (1) more roommates — 3 roommates in a 2BR drops her rent share to roughly $1,000 (saving $500/month); (2) move outside the core — a room in Etobicoke or Mississauga for ~$1,200 saves $300/month and lengthens commute; (3) raise income — negotiate a raise (entry-level Toronto office wages have shifted upward), pick up a side income, or move to a higher-paying role; (4) OSAP RAP application — if her income falls below the threshold, federal RAP can pause or reduce the $200 payment. Marks: 2 diagnosis, 3 options (1 each).

8
[5]
Apply compound-interest reasoning to the "do I own a car?" decision. Devon, 19, downtown Toronto, can buy a used 2018 Civic for $14,000 (financed). Compute approximate annual full cost of ownership vs TTC + occasional Uber. Then estimate the 10-year compounding cost at 7% real return on the difference invested.
Answer Key

Ownership: $14,000 financed at 8% over 60 months = ~$284/month. Insurance for 19-year-old downtown driver: ~$5,200/year ($433/month). Fuel + maintenance: ~$200/month. Parking near downtown apartment: ~$200/month. Total: ~$1,117/month, $13,400/year. TTC + Uber: ~$206/month, ~$2,470/year. Difference: ~$10,930/year, or $911/month. Investing $911/month at 7% real for 10 years: future value ≈ \(\sum_{n=0}^{119} 911 \cdot 1.005833^{n} \approx \$158{,}000\). The 10-year opportunity cost of owning a car downtown is roughly $158,000 of foregone investment growth. The decision should reflect actual transit access, work location, and family obligations — but the math is dramatic. Marks: 1 ownership cost, 1 transit cost, 1 difference, 2 compounding calculation.

9
[5]
Critique the claim: "An 18-year-old should max out RRSP contributions first to save tax." In 5–6 sentences, identify why the recommendation is usually backwards, and explain when the rule might be different.
Answer Key

For an 18-year-old in a 0–20% effective tax bracket, RRSP contributions save very little current tax (perhaps 15–20% of $1,000 contributed) and create taxable income at withdrawal in retirement when total income is likely higher than today. TFSA is the right home for young-adult investing: contributions are post-tax but growth and withdrawals are tax-free forever, so the tax benefit is maximised at the time you have lowest tax rate. The young-adult rule: max TFSA first, RRSP later when income has risen. Exception: if an employer matches RRSP contributions (50% match or similar), the match is free money and almost always worth taking — the RRSP-first rule applies to the matched portion only. Marks: 2 backwards-rule analysis, 2 TFSA-first reasoning, 1 employer-match exception.

11
[5]
Mira, 19, has $14,000 T4 income, $7,500 T2202 tuition, $0 RRSP contributions. Write a 5–6 sentence explanation of what her T1 General will produce, including her expected federal tax payable, refund estimate, and what she should do with any refund.
Answer Key

Total income $14,000. Federal basic personal amount $15,705 already exceeds taxable income, so federal tax owed is $0 before any credits. Adding tuition credits ($7,500), CPP and EI credits, the credits substantially exceed tax payable — Mira owes $0 federal. Provincially similar. Refund: federal and provincial taxes withheld at source from the T4 (perhaps $1,100), plus the GST/HST credit and Ontario Trillium Benefit (~$360/year for low-income singles, paid quarterly), totalling perhaps $1,400–$1,460. The recommended use of the refund: $700 to top up emergency fund, $760 to TFSA invested in a low-cost diversified portfolio. Tuition credits carry forward: ~$6,000 unused stays on file for future tax years. Marks: 1 income, 1 credits, 1 refund estimate, 1 refund use, 1 carry-forward.

12
[4]
Write 4–5 sentences explaining what compound interest is and why "every dollar saved at 18 is worth roughly 15 dollars at 58" works at typical long-term real returns.
Answer Key

Compound interest is the addition of interest to the principal so that subsequent interest is earned on the new larger sum — the growth compounds rather than adds linearly. The formula is \(A = P(1+r)^n\) where \(P\) is principal, \(r\) is the annual rate, and \(n\) is the number of years. At a 7% real long-term equity return, $1 invested for 40 years is \(\$1 \times 1.07^{40} \approx \$14.97\), so the "$1 to $15 by 58" claim is accurate at typical long-term real returns. The corollary works in reverse: high-interest debt at 20% APR compounds against you at roughly the same rate, which is why credit-card debt is the most-costly category to carry. Marks: 1 definition, 1 formula, 1 numerical example, 1 reverse-side insight.
