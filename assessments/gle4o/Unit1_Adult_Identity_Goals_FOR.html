Unit 1: Adult Identity, Self-Awareness & Goal-Setting — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand A
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Diagnostic check at the start of Unit 1. Identify your starting point on adult identity development, values clarification, goal-setting, and mental-health literacy. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Be honest about what is unclear.
Question 1 Prior Knowledge
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what "becoming an adult" means to you. What changes in the year after OSSD compared with the year before?
Question 2 Self-Awareness
[3 marks]
List the three things you think you are best at, based on your secondary school experience. For each, briefly describe one specific moment when you used the strength (4–6 sentences total).
Question 3 Values
[2 marks]
"Articulated values are what you tell yourself you care about. Revealed values are what your time and money say you care about."
In 2–3 sentences, identify two things you would say you value highly. For each, describe whether your time and money over the past month confirm or contradict the value.
Question 4 Goal-Setting
[4 marks]
Where do you imagine yourself in five years (age 23)? In 4–5 sentences, describe: what you might be doing, where you might live, what you might have accomplished, and what is most uncertain.
Question 5 Locus of Control
[3 marks]
Describe a recent moment when something didn't go as you wanted. In 4–5 sentences, identify: what was within your control, what was outside your control, and what you did with each.
Question 6 Mental-Health Literacy
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what you know about mental-health supports available to Ontario post-secondary students. Have you ever used a mental-health support — formal or informal? What helped or did not help?
Question 7 Self-Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 1 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) Erikson and emerging-adulthood theory; (b) values clarification methods; (c) strengths inventory tools (VIA, CliftonStrengths); (d) SMART and three-horizon goal-setting; (e) locus of control and resilience; (f) mental-health self-advocacy and Ontario supports? Explain what specifically is unclear.

📖 Complete Answer Key — Instructor Reference

Q1 (3 marks): Look for awareness that adulthood involves both legal markers (age 18 — voting, consent for healthcare, contracts) and practical-self-direction markers (no imposed structure, financial responsibility, self-advocacy). Mature responses note that adolescence-to-adulthood is a gradual stage (emerging adulthood) rather than a single switch.

Q2 (3 marks): Look for specific strengths with concrete evidence. A strength claim without an example is decorative; with an example it is a usable story for future scholarship/essay/interview. Encourage students who listed only academic strengths to also consider interpersonal, executive, and creative strengths.

Q3 (2 marks): Look for honest self-observation. Students will often write socially acceptable values (family, education) — note the gap between articulated value and revealed time/money allocation as an opening for discussion, not as failure.

Q4 (4 marks): Look for concreteness without overclaim. Strong responses describe a specific picture (work, location, accomplishment) while acknowledging uncertainty. Vague or pessimistic responses signal places to build agency through goal-setting work.

Q5 (3 marks): Look for accurate distinction between controllable and uncontrollable factors. Over-internal responses (everything is my fault) and over-external responses (nothing is my fault) both need calibration. Flexible explanatory-style responses are the mature target.

Q6 (3 marks): Look for awareness of Ontario-specific supports (Good2Talk, campus counselling, family doctor, ConnexOntario) and willingness to acknowledge use of supports without stigma. Lack of awareness of any supports is itself useful diagnostic data.

Q7 (ungraded): Use to target instruction. Common areas of weakness: distinguishing emerging adulthood from extended adolescence; using VIA/CliftonStrengths beyond surface labels; building 10-year vision concrete enough to picture; locus of control nuance (avoiding over-internal blame).

Marking guidance: The instrument is diagnostic, not graded. Provide descriptive feedback only. Identify two specific strengths and two specific next-step priorities per student. Use responses to plan differentiation for the unit.
