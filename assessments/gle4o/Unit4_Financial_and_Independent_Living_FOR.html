Unit 4: Financial Literacy and Independent Living — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand D
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify your starting point on financial literacy, taxes, tenant rights, banking, and independent-living competencies. Descriptive feedback only.
Question 1 Budget
[3 marks]
Describe how you currently track your money. Do you know how much you spent last week? Last month? In 3–4 sentences, describe your system and how confident you are in its accuracy.
Question 2 Credit
[2 marks]
Without looking it up: do you currently have a credit card? Do you know your credit score? Have you ever seen a credit report? In 3–4 sentences, describe what you understand about how credit scores are built in Canada.
Question 3 Renting
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what you know about Ontario tenant rights. If a landlord asked you for a $500 "damage deposit," would you pay? Why or why not?
Question 4 Taxes
[3 marks]
Have you ever filed a personal income tax return (T1)? In 3–4 sentences, describe what you understand about Canadian taxes: deductions, credits, refunds, and the TFSA or RRSP.
Question 5 Banking
[2 marks]
Which bank or banks do you currently use? Do you pay any monthly fees? In 3–4 sentences, describe your current banking setup — chequing, savings, investing.
Question 6 OHIP and Transit
[2 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe what you know about (a) what OHIP covers and doesn't cover, and (b) how you currently get around — bus, car, bike, walk, mix.
Question 7 Self-Reflection
[ungraded]
Which of these Unit 4 topics do you feel least confident about: (a) the 50/30/20 budget; (b) credit scores and OSAP; (c) Ontario tenant rights and leases; (d) insurance; (e) taxes, TFSA, RRSP; (f) banking, compound interest, OHIP, transportation costs? Explain what specifically is unclear.

📖 Complete Answer Key — Instructor Reference

Q1 (3 marks): Look for tracking-system maturity. Students who can name last week's spending are starting from a strong place. Students who say "I have no idea" need the 15-minute Sunday review as a habit-installation target. Spreadsheets, apps (KOHO, Mint Canadian alternative, Wealthsimple Cash), and envelope-method all qualify.

Q2 (2 marks): Awareness baseline. Students with no credit history will need direct instruction on the first-card path (low-limit student card, one small recurring purchase, autopay in full). Students who think credit scores are damaged by "checking too often" should be corrected — soft checks via Borrowell, Credit Karma, or Mogo do not damage the score.

Q3 (3 marks): The "damage deposit" question diagnoses Residential Tenancies Act awareness. The correct answer is: do not pay, it is not legal; the RTA limits to one month's rent as last-month's-rent deposit. Students who would pay are highest-priority for tenancy-rights instruction.

Q4 (3 marks): Tax awareness varies widely. Students who have filed (even a $0 return) are at a stronger starting point. Students who confuse TFSA with RRSP need careful instruction on the young-adult investing hierarchy. Students unaware of CVITP / Wealthsimple Tax need direct guidance.

Q5 (2 marks): Banking-setup baseline. Students paying monthly chequing fees while they hold a student card should be coached on switching to a no-fee student or digital chequing. Students with no savings/investing account need the basic setup: one chequing + one high-interest savings + one investing account.

Q6 (2 marks): OHIP gaps and transportation defaults. Students who think OHIP covers dental or vision need correction; gap awareness matters for student-plan and employer-plan negotiation. Transit/car defaults shape budget calculations significantly.

Q7 (ungraded): Use to plan differentiation. Common gaps: credit-score mechanics; landlord-tenant law specifics (deposits, eviction process); the federal vs provincial OSAP interest distinction; the TFSA-vs-RRSP-vs-FHSA hierarchy; compound-interest intuition.

Marking guidance: Diagnostic, not graded. Two strengths + two priorities per student.
