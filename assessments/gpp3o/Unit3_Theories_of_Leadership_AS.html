Topics 3.1–3.2 — Trait and Behavioural Theories
Question 1
Carlyle's 1840 "Great Man" theory proposed that:
Solution: Carlyle (1840) argued history is driven by exceptional individuals. Modern research finds this view too narrow — leadership is partly trait-stable (~30%) but largely learnable (~70%); traits predict emergence better than effectiveness.
Question 2
The Ohio State and Michigan studies identified two dimensions of leader behaviour:
Solution: Two independent dimensions — task and relationship. A leader can be high on both, low on both, or high on one and low on the other. Blake-Mouton's 1964 Managerial Grid plotted this as 9×9.
Question 3
Maria's team likes her — she remembers birthdays, listens carefully, hosts pizza nights. But three tournaments have gone poorly: paperwork late, practice sessions unfocused, two members dropped out citing "no direction."
In behavioural terms, Maria is:
Solution: Maria's strengths are relationship-oriented; her gap is task-oriented. The fix is to add initiating structure (agendas, deadlines, practice goals) without losing the consideration she already provides. The target is 9,9.
Topic 3.3 — Situational Leadership
Question 4
In Hersey-Blanchard's situational leadership model, a "D1 — Enthusiastic Beginner" needs:
Solution: D1 (high commitment, low competence) needs S1 — Directing. The new-to-task person wants to do well but doesn't yet know how; delegating would be abandonment. Match the style to the developmental level.
Question 5
The developmental level in Hersey-Blanchard is:
Solution: Task-specific. A Grade 12 returner is D4 at the routine but may be D1 at a new responsibility. Effective leaders read the level for the task at hand and adjust style accordingly.
Topic 3.4 — Transactional and Transformational
Question 6
Bass's "Four I's" of transformational leadership include all EXCEPT:
Solution: The Four I's are: idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration. Intimidation is not transformational — it is, at best, transactional fear-based management.
Question 7
Transactional and transformational leadership are best understood as:
Solution: Bass-Avolio's Full Range Leadership Model treats them as layered. Pure transactional produces compliance without ownership; pure transformational without operational reliability produces inspiration without delivery. The combination is the target.
Topics 3.5–3.6 — Servant, Ethical, and Cultural Leadership
Question 8
Greenleaf's central question about servant leadership is:
Solution: Greenleaf's test question is about whether the people being served are growing. The test is behavioural and longitudinal — over time, are the served becoming healthier, wiser, freer?
Question 9
Brown, Treviño, and Harrison (2005) define ethical leadership as four specific things. Which of the following is one of them?
Solution: Ethical leadership = modelling the standard + communicating it + reinforcing it + decision-making consistent with it. The four ethical traps to avoid: conflict of interest, abuse of position, coercion, dishonesty.
Question 10
The seven-generations principle, drawn from many Indigenous teachings, asks leaders to:
Solution: The seven-generations principle — present in Haudenosaunee, Anishinaabe, and other Indigenous traditions — asks decision-makers to consider impacts seven generations ahead. It contrasts with short-term, electoral, or quarterly-financial framings.
Question 11
Alice Eagly's research on women in leadership describes a "double bind" — women leaders are often required to be:
Solution: The double bind: warm-and-friendly women are perceived as not-competent, competent women are perceived as not-warm; the "and" is structurally blocked. Intersectional analyses (Crenshaw and others) show additional pressures on racialised, Indigenous, queer, and disabled women.
Question 12
Short answer: In 2–3 sentences, identify which leadership theory from Unit 3 has been most useful to you in understanding a leadership situation you've witnessed (at school, in your community, in the news), and explain why.
Sample: Situational leadership has helped me read my volleyball captain. She was great with returning players (D4 → Delegating worked perfectly) but the new Grade 9s often felt lost (she was delegating when they needed Directing). Once I had the vocabulary, I could see what was going wrong without making it a personality issue. The next captain might benefit from style-flex training. Strong responses name a specific theory, a specific situation, and explain what the theory helped them see.
