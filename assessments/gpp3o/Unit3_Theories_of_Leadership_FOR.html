Unit 3: Theories of Leadership — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prior knowledge of leadership · Strand C
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Help your teacher see what frameworks, examples, and assumptions you bring to the study of leadership theory.
Question 1 Concept of Leadership
[3 marks]
In your own words, what is leadership? Try to write a one-paragraph definition (3–4 sentences) that captures what leadership is — not what makes someone "look like a leader."
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: (1) recognition that leadership involves moving a group toward a goal, (2) some sense of relationship between leader and followers, (3) sophistication beyond "being in charge" or "being charismatic." Strong responses recognise leadership as relational, situational, and service-oriented. Common weak responses centre charisma, position, or trait essentialism. Use the answer to identify each student's starting framework — what they walk in believing.

Question 2 Examples
[3 marks]
Name three Canadian leaders — from any field (politics, sport, arts, science, social-movement organising, business, Indigenous community work) — whose leadership you respect. Briefly explain why for each (one sentence).
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Pay attention to demographic diversity (or lack thereof) in students' examples. Many students will name only white male politicians — Trudeau, Harper, Ford, Singh. Use this to set up Lesson 3.6's expansion of frameworks. Strong responses include leaders from across gender, racial, Indigenous, queer, and field categories — Cindy Blackstock, Roberta Bondar, Murray Sinclair, Tanya Talaga, Jagmeet Singh, Catherine McKenna, Olivia Chow, etc. Marks: 1 per example with reason.

Question 3 Trait Assumption
[2 marks]
"He just has natural leadership presence."
When people say something like this, what do they usually mean? Do you agree that "natural leadership presence" exists? In 2–3 sentences.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This surfaces trait-theory assumptions. Look for: (1) honest engagement with the concept, (2) some critical examination ("does it exist or do we just pick people who look like our cultural script of a leader?"). Use answers to set up Lesson 3.1's discussion of emergence vs effectiveness and the cultural-coding critique of trait lists.

Question 4 Style-Flexibility
[3 marks]
Think of a person who has led you (coach, teacher, captain, employer, older sibling). Describe in 3–4 sentences one situation where their style worked well and one where it didn't. What was different about the two situations?
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This sets up situational leadership. Look for: (1) specific situations, (2) recognition that style-context fit varies, (3) some implicit awareness of follower readiness. Strong responses identify what differed about the two situations without personalising it ("with the experienced players she could just trust them; with the new players she needed to give more direction"). Marks: 1 per situation, 1 for analysis.

Question 5 Indigenous Perspectives
[2 marks]
What, if anything, do you already know about Indigenous understandings of leadership in Canada? (Specific teachings, decision-making models, leaders, traditions.) Be honest — "I don't know anything" is a fine answer; this is a baseline.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Most Grade-11 students will have some elementary-school exposure (Seven Grandfather Teachings, basic awareness of treaty relationships, perhaps residential schools through media coverage) but limited depth. A small number will have deeper exposure (family, community, prior reading). Use answers to calibrate Lesson 3.6's depth and to identify students who might enrich the conversation with lived knowledge.

Question 6 Ethical Pressure
[3 marks]
Have you ever been in a leadership role (formal or informal) where you felt pressured to do something that didn't feel right? Or have you observed someone in a leadership role facing that pressure? Describe in 3–4 sentences (without naming specific people if it would harm them).
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This is sensitive. Use it to surface the ethical-leadership stakes. Some students will report direct experience (peer pressure to cover for a teammate; pressure to bend rules for a friend; pressure to ignore an exclusionary practice). Others will report observed examples. The diagnostic is whether they can articulate the situation; the work of Lesson 3.5 is to provide frameworks for navigating it. Marks: 1 specific situation, 1 named pressure, 1 honest reflection.

Question 7 Self-Goal
[ungraded]
Of the leadership theories you've encountered in this diagnostic (trait, behavioural, situational, transactional/transformational, servant, ethical, culturally-grounded), which sounds most relevant to where you are in your own leadership growth? One sentence.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
