Unit 4: Peer Support and Mentoring — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Readiness for peer-helper role · Strand D
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Before training as a peer-helper, take stock honestly of your readiness. Some students will already have informal experience; others won't. Both starting points are fine.
Question 1 Helping Experience
[3 marks]
Describe one time you helped a friend or peer through something difficult. What did you do? Looking back, what worked and what didn't? (4–5 sentences.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: (1) specific situation, (2) honest evaluation of own response, (3) recognition that something didn't fully work (very few helping moments are perfect; honesty about this is a strength). Strong responses identify a small concrete moment ("I listened for about 90 seconds and then started suggesting things they should do") and recognise both the impulse and its limits. Use the diagnostic to identify which students already have a feel for active listening vs. which will need explicit retraining.

Question 2 Confidentiality
[2 marks]
Have you ever had to keep something a friend told you in confidence? Have you ever struggled with the question of whether to keep a confidence or tell someone? Briefly describe (no names).
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This surfaces students' lived experience of confidentiality dilemmas. Some students will report explicit situations involving risk-of-harm questions. Be ready to follow up privately. Use the diagnostic to set up Lesson 4.3's framing of confidentiality with limits — the three exceptions framework gives students permission and structure.

Question 3 Knowing the Resources
[3 marks]
List as many of these as you can: (a) the names of the guidance counsellor(s) at your school; (b) any school staff who specifically support mental-health (social worker, child-and-youth worker, on-site nurse); (c) the phone numbers of any community crisis or support lines you know. If you don't know — write "I don't know," that's honest data.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Most students will know one or two guidance counsellors but not the social worker. Few will know phone numbers. Use this to set up Lesson 4.4's referral pathways. Provide a school-specific referral card based on the gaps surfaced here. Marks: 1 for any in-school adults named, 1 for community lines named, 1 for honest acknowledgment of gaps.

Question 4 Mentoring Experience
[3 marks]
Have you ever had a mentor? (Older sibling, coach, employer, teacher, religious leader, family friend, online community member, club leader.) Describe what made it work or not, in 3–4 sentences.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Most students have had informal mentors; few have had formal ones. Look for descriptions that match mentor practices (listening first, treating mentee as capable, setting expectations, maintaining boundaries, introducing networks). Strong responses identify what specifically made the relationship work. Use the diagnostic to identify which students might want to mentor younger peers themselves; many do, but they need to see the work as a structured practice, not a vague intention.

Question 5 Boundaries Self-Read
[2 marks]
Of these boundary categories, which would be hardest for you to maintain if you were a peer-helper: (a) time (saying "I'll talk tomorrow, not at midnight"), (b) information (not bringing it up with other friends, even obliquely), (c) scope (not trying to fix everything yourself), (d) social media (not moving into private DMs)? In 2–3 sentences, identify the hardest one for you and why.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Self-knowledge diagnostic. Most students will identify either time or scope. Look for honesty about why. Use the answer to surface specific boundary practices in Lesson 4.6 that match each student's vulnerability.

Question 6 Active Listening Self-Read
[2 marks]
When you listen to someone share something hard, what's going on in your head? Are you usually planning your reply, comparing it to your own experience, feeling their emotion, or something else? Be honest. (2–3 sentences.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This is the heart of active-listening readiness. Most students will report some mix of planning-the-reply and comparing-to-own-experience. Both are normal but limit the conversation. Strong responses are honest about the mix. Use the answer to set up explicit practice in Lesson 2.2 (already done) and reinforce in Lesson 4.2.

Question 7 Self-Identification
[ungraded]
Of the four forms of peer support (peer tutoring, peer mentoring, peer mediation, peer support listening), which would you be most interested in being trained for? Why? One sentence.
