Unit 2: Interpersonal Skills — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Baseline communication check · Strand B
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Help your teacher and yourself see your baseline interpersonal patterns before Unit 2's skill-building. Honest answers produce useful feedback.
Question 1 Communication Style
[3 marks]
In a recent disagreement (with a friend, family member, or teammate), what was your typical pattern of communication? Describe in 3–4 sentences. Try to identify whether your response was closer to passive, aggressive, passive-aggressive, or assertive.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: (1) specific situation, (2) honest self-classification, (3) recognition of impact. Strong students name the style without defensiveness ("I usually go passive — say I'm fine when I'm not — and then snap two weeks later"). Use this to calibrate where each student starts. Many students will report passive or passive-aggressive defaults; some will report aggressive. The point is the diagnostic, not the verdict.

Question 2 Listening
[2 marks]
When a friend tells you something hard they're going through, what is your most common response — give advice, share a similar story of your own, ask a question, or just listen? Be honest, no wrong answer.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Most students will default to advice or "share a similar story." Both are well-intentioned but limit the conversation. Students who default to just-listening have a head-start on Unit 2. Use the answer to identify which students need explicit work on active-listening techniques in Lesson 2.2.

Question 3 Feedback Receiving
[2 marks]
"The hardest feedback I've ever received was..."
Complete the sentence (2–3 sentences). What was the feedback, and what did you do with it?
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: honesty about the difficulty of receiving feedback, some evidence of reflection on response. Students who can name a hard piece of feedback they eventually integrated are showing strong feedback-receiving capacity. Students who report dismissing all hard feedback may need explicit work on Heen & Stone's three triggers in Lesson 2.4.

Question 4 Conflict Patterns
[3 marks]
Think about how you typically handle conflict — at home, at school, in friendships. Identify (a) one situation in which you tend to avoid; (b) one situation in which you tend to push hard for your own way; (c) one situation in which you've collaborated effectively to find a third option. (One sentence each.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This is a mini Thomas-Kilmann self-portrait. Strong responses show flexibility (different styles in different contexts). Responses showing only one style across all three contexts may indicate a default-style trap. Marks: 1 per pairing.

Question 5 Channel Awareness
[2 marks]
Have you ever sent a text or message that landed badly — got read in a way you didn't intend? Describe the situation in 2–3 sentences. What do you think went wrong?
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for awareness of channel limitations. Text strips tone and context; emotional content frequently mis-fires. Strong students recognise this and link to channel choice ("I should have just called"). Use the answer to identify students whose communication-channel awareness needs explicit development in Lesson 2.1.

Question 6 Restorative Thinking
[2 marks]
In your experience, when someone has caused harm to others at your school, what is the typical school response — punishment, suspension, restorative meeting, or something else? In 2–3 sentences, describe what you have observed and what you think works best.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Diagnostic of student exposure to restorative practices. Many Ontario schools have introduced restorative practices since the 2010s; some students will know them, others won't. Use the answer to calibrate Lesson 2.6's depth — for students with no exposure, more foundational; for students with experience, more critical engagement.

Question 7 Self-Identification
[ungraded]
Of Unit 2's six lessons, which one do you most want to grow in? Why? (One sentence.)

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
