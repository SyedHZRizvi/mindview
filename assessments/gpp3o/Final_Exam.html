Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[2]A · Self
Goleman's five domains of emotional intelligence are:
Answer Key

B. Goleman's framework — first three are intrapersonal, last two interpersonal.

2
[3]A · Self
Identify each: (a) affect labelling; (b) Carol Dweck's growth mindset; (c) the SMART goal framework.
Answer Key

(a) Affect labelling — naming an emotion to reduce its automatic intensity (Lieberman, UCLA). (b) Growth mindset — Dweck's term for the belief that abilities are developable through effort; contrast with fixed mindset. (c) SMART goal — Doran 1981: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

3
[2]A · Self
Ann Masten's research describes resilience as:
Answer Key

B. Masten's term "ordinary magic" emphasises that resilience is built through relationships, routines, meaning, and supportive systems — accessible to most people.

4
[2]B · Interpersonal
In Shannon-Weaver's communication model, "noise" refers to:
Answer Key

B. Noise is multi-source; effective communicators check understanding rather than assume.

5
[3]B · Interpersonal
Identify each: (a) active listening (Rogers); (b) the BIS feedback model; (c) restorative practices roots.
Answer Key

(a) Active listening: Rogers's 1957 term for purposeful listening to understand the speaker's experience — attending behaviours, open questions, paraphrasing, reflecting feeling, silence. (b) BIS: Behaviour, Impact, Suggestion — structured feedback model that avoids both vague praise and personal attack. (c) Restorative practices: rooted in Indigenous justice traditions (including Aotearoa-New Zealand Māori and several Indigenous nations in Canada); adopted in Canadian schools and criminal-justice settings from the 1990s on.

6
[2]B · Interpersonal
Thomas-Kilmann's "Collaborating" conflict style is characterised by:
Answer Key

A. Collaborating is high on both axes; it differs from compromising (moderate on both) by aiming at a "both win" third option rather than splitting the difference.

7
[2]C · Leadership Theory
In Hersey-Blanchard situational leadership, a "D2 — Disillusioned Learner" (low commitment, some competence) needs:
Answer Key

B. D2 has lost early commitment; they need both direction (still some competence gap) and emotional support (rebuilding commitment). Coaching is the match.

8
[3]C · Leadership Theory
Identify three of Bass's "Four I's" of transformational leadership.
Answer Key

Any three of: Idealised influence (leader models the values), Inspirational motivation (compelling vision), Intellectual stimulation (invites questioning), Individualised consideration (treats each follower as unique). Last is most-skipped in practice.

9
[2]C · Leadership Theory
Greenleaf's "servant-leader" central test question is:
Answer Key

B. Greenleaf's behavioural, longitudinal test for servant leadership.

10
[2]C · Leadership Theory
The Seven Grandfather Teachings, from Anishinaabe traditions, name seven values:
Answer Key

A. Wisdom, love, respect, bravery, honesty, humility, truth — describing the disposition of a person fit to lead. TRC Call to Action 62 calls on Canadian K-12 systems to integrate Indigenous teachings.

11
[2]D · Peer Support
The five-step helping conversation framework, in order, is:
Answer Key

A. Engage (set frame), Listen (active listening), Clarify (summary check), Options (offer pathways), Next Steps (specific commitment + follow-up).

12
[2]D · Peer Support
Ontario's Child, Youth and Family Services Act (2017) duty to report applies to:
Answer Key

B. CYFSA section 125 imposes a personal duty on every person with reasonable grounds to suspect child abuse of a child under 16. Direct report to a Children's Aid Society; cannot be fully delegated.

Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
13
[5]A · Synthesis
A teammate confides that she is taking five Grade-11 university-track courses, working 12 hours on weekends, training for cross-country five days a week, and is not sleeping well — but she's getting 90s and doesn't want to change anything.
In 5 sentences, apply the 4 Cs of resilience (connection, competence, character, contribution) and Goleman's EI framework to your response. Identify two specific concerns and one specific suggestion. Use a peer-leader voice, not a parent voice.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) named concern about sleep — adolescent need is 7-9 hours; chronic deficit affects cognition, mood, immune function. (2) Named concern about restorative time — high competence/contribution, no restorative practice; 4Cs out of balance. (3) Non-preachy peer-leader response — open question or reflection rather than prescription. EI application: self-awareness moment in your friend; empathic listening from you. (4) Respect for autonomy — you can name observations, but the friend chooses. Marks: 2 concerns, 1 suggestion, 1 EI application, 1 autonomy-respect.

14
[5]B · Synthesis
Mateo texts his friend Lucia at 11 p.m. on a Thursday during exam-prep week: "We need to talk." Lucia spirals into anxiety for two days, sure she has done something wrong. They meet on Saturday; Mateo wanted to ask whether she'd co-host a charity event.
Diagnose using Shannon-Weaver. In 5 sentences, identify the encoding problem, the channel problem, the noise, the feedback gap, and suggest a better encoding.
Answer Key

Encoding: "We need to talk" triggers relationship-threat schemas; mismatched the actual content. Channel: text strips tone and context. Noise: Lucia in exam-prep stress amplifies threat reading. Feedback: two-day delay. Better encoding: "Hey — want to co-host the Spring Drive with me? No pressure, just throwing the idea — chat at lunch tomorrow?" Marks: 1 per element.

15
[5]C · Synthesis
Maria's volleyball team likes her — she remembers birthdays, listens carefully, hosts pizza nights. But three tournaments have gone poorly: paperwork late, practice unfocused, two members dropped out citing "no direction."
Apply the behavioural-theory two-dimension framework and Hersey-Blanchard situational leadership. In 5 sentences, diagnose Maria's strengths and gap, identify how she might be misreading her players' developmental levels, and suggest two specific behaviour changes she could try.
Answer Key

Maria is high on consideration (relationship), low on initiating structure (task). She likely defaults to S3 Supporting or S4 Delegating across all players regardless of their developmental level. The fix: add task-focused leadership (agendas, deadlines, practice goals) without losing the consideration; and read each player's developmental level for the task. Two specific changes: (1) introduce a written agenda + accountabilities for each practice; (2) hold a 15-minute weekly 1:1 with each player to read their development and adjust style — for D1 newbies, more direction; for D4 returners, more delegation. Marks: 1 strengths-gap diagnosis, 1 misread of developmental levels, 2 specific changes, 1 framework synthesis.

16
[5]D · Synthesis
A peer in a listening conversation says: "I've been thinking it would be easier if I weren't here." You ask: "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" They hesitate, say yes, and share that they have looked up methods.
In 5 sentences, describe your next moves — what you say, what you do, and which limit of confidentiality you invoke. Reference safeTALK principles and at least one specific Ontario referral resource.
Answer Key

(1) "I'm really glad you told me. I'm not going anywhere, and I have to bring in an adult to help us keep you safe — that's what I told you at the start." (2) Identify the safest adult with the peer — guidance counsellor, social worker, safe family member. (3) Walk together to that adult; do not leave the peer alone. (4) If after-hours, call 9-8-8 (suicide crisis) or Kids Help Phone 1-800-668-6868 with the peer; stay on the line. (5) Invoke confidentiality limit #1 — risk of harm to self. Document briefly within 24 hours; check on the peer within 48 hours. Marks: 1 affirmation, 1 invoke limit, 1 specific resource, 1 stay-present, 1 follow-up.

17
[5]Synthesis · All Strands
Three weeks before the launch of a Mental Health Awareness Week project, the team discovers their external speaker has cancelled.
In 5 sentences, describe a structured pivot drawing on Unit 5 frameworks. Identify the stand-up move, three options, the decision criteria, what gets documented, and how the change is communicated.
Answer Key

(1) Surface the blocker at the next stand-up: "We have a problem — the speaker is out. Here are three options." (2) Three options: find new speaker by next Wednesday; replace slot with two peer-helpers + school counsellor panel; cut the slot, lengthen activity stations. (3) Decision criteria: alignment with charter purpose; team capacity; resources; consistency with risk register. (4) Document in project log; principal informed. (5) Communicate revised plan to partners and team. Marks: 1 stand-up, 2 options, 1 criteria, 1 documentation/comms.

[8]C and D

Identify both what each leader did and what theoretical lens helps you see it.
Answer Key

Look for: 2 specific leaders; 2 leadership frameworks (trait/behavioural/situational/transformational/servant/ethical/cultural) applied; 1 peer-support/mentoring framework (mentoring practices; helping conversation; boundaries; referral) applied where appropriate; genuine analytic comparison, not parallel description. [6]A · Self
Apply the SMART framework, the Covey time matrix, and a stress-management plan. Convert the goal "I want to be a stronger leader next year" into: (a) a SMART goal; (b) one specific Covey-quadrant-2 weekly practice; (c) one specific stress-management practice across two of the four categories (physical/cognitive/relational/restorative).
Answer Key

Sample: (a) SMART — "By June 20, I will: complete the school's peer-mediator training; co-mediate at least two conflicts under supervision; deliver a 5-minute reflection at our June showcase; and journal 200 words per major experience." (b) Covey-quadrant-2 practice — Sunday 4-5 PM weekly review and back-plan. (c) Stress-management — Physical: bedtime by 11 PM five nights a week; Relational: one phone call with a non-school friend per week. Marks: 2 SMART specificity, 2 quadrant-2 practice, 2 stress-management.

22
[6]B · Interpersonal
Apply assertive communication, BIS feedback, and a restorative question. A teammate consistently arrives late and offers no explanation. Write: (a) one assertive I-statement; (b) BIS feedback in three sentences; (c) one restorative question you could ask to open a conversation.
Answer Key

(a) "I feel frustrated when you arrive 20+ minutes late to our team meetings, because we can't make decisions without quorum and I end up rescheduling. I'd like us to start within 5 minutes of the scheduled time, or shift the meeting to a time that works for you. Which would you prefer?" (b) BIS — "Behaviour: You've arrived 20+ minutes late to four of the last five meetings. Impact: We've had to delay decisions, and two teammates have stopped showing up because they feel the meeting isn't real. Suggestion: Either commit to on-time arrival, or let's reschedule to a window that works." (c) Restorative — "What's been happening that's made it hard to arrive on time?" or "Who's been affected by the late starts, and how?" Marks: 2 assertive, 2 BIS, 2 restorative question.

23
[6]C · Leadership Theory
Apply Hersey-Blanchard situational leadership. Design a three-person team for a bake-sale project. For each role (Liam = Grade 9 first-time volunteer; Aanya = Grade 11 who ran the booth last year; Noah = Grade 12 former student-council treasurer), identify the task you'd assign, the developmental level for that task, the matching style, and one specific leadership move you'd make.
Answer Key

Liam: task — count change at the till; D1 (high commitment, low competence); S1 Directing; specific move — give written instructions, demonstrate, watch first 30 minutes, hand over with check-in every 20 minutes. Aanya: task — lead the booth setup; D3 for the lead-role specifically (high competence at booth, variable commitment to leading alone); S3 Supporting; specific move — "What's your plan? I'll be at the next booth if you need a sounding board." Noah: task — handle the cash float; D4 (high commitment, high competence); S4 Delegating; specific move — "Cash is yours; you decide on counts and reconciliation. Two-person rule on cash counts; you choose your partner." Marks: 2 per role identified with developmental level, style, and specific move.

24
[7]D · Peer Support & Pathways
Apply peer-support frameworks AND pathways planning. (a) Design a personal sustainability plan for yourself as a hypothetical peer-helper: caseload, hours, after-hours protocol, supervision, restorative time, termination criteria. (b) Identify one specific Canadian postsecondary destination (university program, college diploma, apprenticeship, or workplace pathway) where the competencies you've built in GPP3O would be directly valued, and in 2–3 sentences, describe how you would tell that story in a supplementary essay or interview.
Answer Key

(a) Sustainability plan: caseload max 2; hours specific lunch periods M-W-F not at home/night; after-hours referred to Kids Help Phone / 9-8-8; supervision biweekly + on-request; restorative two lunches/week protected; termination criteria — sleep disruption / dread / detachment for 2 weeks → break. (b) Sample pathway: BSc Nursing Toronto Metropolitan University — peer-helper, safeTALK, helping-conversation, boundaries, referral pathways content speaks directly to the relational core of nursing. Story-telling: name a specific moment ("the time I supported a peer through a crisis disclosure"), invoke the framework that guided you, describe the outcome, connect to the program's professional formation aims. Marks: 4 for sustainability plan completeness, 3 for pathway connection.

📝 Communication Category Rubric (instructor-graded)

Per Ontario's Growing Success (2010) Achievement Chart, Communication is one of the four equally-weighted categories (25% of the course mark). This rubric is applied to every student-written response (short-answer, extended-response, essay, lab report, deputation, etc.) on this assessment.

Criterion Level 1 — Limited (50–59%) Level 2 — Some (60–69%) Level 3 — Considerable (70–79%) Level 4 — Thorough (80–100%)
Expression and organisation of ideas Ideas expressed with limited clarity; little organisation. Ideas expressed with some clarity; some organisation. Ideas expressed clearly and organised effectively. Ideas expressed with clarity, precision, and a high degree of organisation.
Communication for different audiences and purposes Limited awareness of audience/purpose; tone often inappropriate. Some awareness of audience/purpose; tone occasionally inappropriate. Consistently appropriate tone, register, and address to audience. Tone, register, voice, and address are precisely matched to audience and purpose.
Use of conventions, vocabulary, terminology Frequent errors in grammar, spelling, punctuation; little discipline-specific vocabulary. Some errors that occasionally impede meaning; emerging use of discipline-specific vocabulary. Few errors; consistent use of discipline-specific vocabulary. Virtually error-free; precise and varied use of discipline-specific vocabulary.

Level 3 represents the Provincial Standard. Use the most-recent and most-consistent evidence across multiple Communication tasks (this assessment, prior FOR work, written responses on AS quizzes, oral presentations) when assigning a course-level Communication mark. Growing Success, 2010, p. 17.
