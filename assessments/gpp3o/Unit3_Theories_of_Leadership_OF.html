Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
Identify each: (a) trait theory; (b) Hersey-Blanchard situational leadership; (c) Bass's transformational leadership.
Answer Key

(a) Trait theory: the view that leaders share consistent personal traits (intelligence, dominance, conscientiousness, extraversion). Useful but incomplete; modern research (Day & Antonakis) finds ~30% trait-stable, ~70% learnable. (b) Hersey-Blanchard: situational model in which leadership style (S1 Directing, S2 Coaching, S3 Supporting, S4 Delegating) matches follower's task-specific developmental level (D1 Enthusiastic Beginner, D2 Disillusioned Learner, D3 Capable but Cautious, D4 Self-Reliant Achiever). (c) Bass's transformational leadership: identification-based leadership through the Four I's — idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration; distinguished from transactional (exchange-based) leadership.

2
[2]
Robert Greenleaf's central question for assessing servant leadership is:
Answer Key

B. Greenleaf's test (1970) is whether those served are growing. Behavioural and longitudinal — the test isn't a survey at one moment but a pattern over time.

3
[2]
In 2–3 sentences, explain the distinction between leadership emergence and leadership effectiveness, with one example.
Answer Key

Emergence = who gets picked or elected as leader; effectiveness = how well they actually do once in role. The two are correlated but not identical. Example: a charismatic, extraverted student may win the election (emergence) but a quieter, more conscientious peer may run a tighter, more inclusive council (effectiveness). Research shows trait theory predicts emergence better than effectiveness — recognising this matters because it means we should design systems that recognise effective leaders even when they don't fit the cultural script of "looking like a leader."

4
[3]
Define each: (a) the Seven Grandfather Teachings; (b) the seven-generations principle; (c) Truth and Reconciliation Commission Call to Action 62.
Answer Key

(a) Seven Grandfather Teachings: Anishinaabe values framework — wisdom, love, respect, bravery, honesty, humility, truth — describing the disposition of a person fit to lead. (b) Seven-generations principle: the practice of considering the impact of decisions seven generations ahead; present in Haudenosaunee, Anishinaabe, and other Indigenous traditions; contrasts with short-electoral-cycle or quarterly-financial decision frames. (c) TRC Call to Action 62 (2015): calls on K-12 systems to develop curriculum that incorporates Indigenous knowledge, residential-school history, and treaties; mandatory K-12 curriculum that addresses these is one of the most-specific TRC recommendations.

5
[3]
Identify three of Bass's "Four I's" of transformational leadership and briefly describe each in your own words.
Answer Key

Any three of: (1) Idealised influence — the leader models the values they ask of others; followers identify with the leader. (2) Inspirational motivation — the leader articulates a vision in language followers find meaningful and compelling. (3) Intellectual stimulation — the leader invites questioning, new ideas, and creative problem-solving. (4) Individualised consideration — the leader treats each follower as a unique person with specific needs, strengths, and growth paths. The most-skipped of the four in practice is individualised consideration. Marks: 1 per identified component with description.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]
A peer leadership case: You are leading a bake sale. Three teammates — Liam (Grade 9, never volunteered before), Aanya (Grade 11, ran the booth last year), Noah (Grade 12, former student council treasurer).
Apply Hersey-Blanchard situational leadership. In 5 sentences, name each teammate's likely developmental level for their task, identify the matching style, and justify briefly. What would be the mistake of using one style for all three?
Answer Key

Liam: D1 for most tasks (high commitment, low competence — eager but new). Style S1 Directing — specific role, written instructions, close support. Aanya: variable; for the booth-leading task, likely D3 (high competence, variable commitment — knows how but is nervous to lead alone again). Style S3 Supporting — sit with her for 30 minutes, ask "what's your plan?", be available, then step back. Noah: D4 (high commitment, high competence — owns the cash float). Style S4 Delegating — hand it over, don't hover. Single-style mistake: directing Noah micromanages him into disengagement; delegating Liam abandons him into floundering; supporting an actually-D4 Noah reads as patronising. Marks: 1 per developmental-level + style match, 1 for the single-style critique.

7
[4]
A recycling-campaign design: (Option A) classroom-participation leaderboard + monthly pizza party for top class; (Option B) town-hall on per-capita waste impact + invitation to redesign cafeteria sourcing + previous-Ontario-student-led campaign story.
Apply the transactional/transformational distinction. In 4–5 sentences, identify which approach each option represents, predict the typical outcome of each, and propose a combined design that captures the strengths of both.
Answer Key

Option A is transactional (contingent reward — leaderboard + prize); typical outcome — high compliance during the campaign, sharp drop afterward. Option B is transformational (idealised influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation); typical outcome — strong engagement among the inspired, patchy participation among the unengaged. Combined design: open with transformational frame (why this matters, what we can do), build transactional structure (clear monthly target with shared celebration), individualise (each grade has a specific role with a named champion). Marks: 1 per identification, 1 per outcome prediction, 1 for combined design, 1 for synthesis.

8
[4]
Apply the ethical-leadership framework. In 4 sentences, describe one situation in your school or community where you have seen one of the four ethical traps (conflict of interest, abuse of position, coercion, dishonesty), and analyse what a Brown/Treviño/Harrison ethical leader would have done differently. Do not name specific people if doing so could harm them.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) specific situation with a named ethical trap, (2) recognition of the structural pressure, (3) articulation of the ethical-leader counter-move (modelling the standard, communicating it, reinforcing it, deciding consistently with it). Strong responses identify subtle versions ("a club president quietly arranging the volunteer-hours allocation to favour her own friends") rather than only dramatic ones. Marks: 1 specific situation, 1 trap identified, 1 ethical-leader analysis, 1 communication.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[6]
Write an analytical paragraph (6–8 sentences) on the claim: "Leadership theory developed in Western corporate and military contexts cannot be applied without modification to Indigenous, women's, or movement-based leadership." Engage at least one specific theory and one specific Canadian example.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) thesis (agreeing, disagreeing, or qualifying); (2) engagement with a specific theory's situatedness (trait lists encode their authors' cultural norms; Hersey-Blanchard assumes hierarchical follower relationships; transformational leadership often centres positional leaders); (3) specific Canadian example (Cindy Blackstock's legal advocacy through the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal is movement-based and Indigenous-grounded; the Seven Grandfather Teachings encode a relational ethic distinct from corporate frameworks); (4) nuance — the theories aren't useless, but they need to be one tool among several. Marks: 1 thesis, 2 theory engagement, 2 example, 1 conventions.

10
[6]
Write a coherent 8–10 sentence comparison of two Canadian leaders (one from your own school/community context, one from public life) using at least two theoretical frameworks from Unit 3.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) two specific leaders, (2) two frameworks applied (trait + situational; behavioural + servant; transformational + ethical; cultural + transactional — any combination), (3) genuine comparison (not just two parallel descriptions), (4) recognition that the leaders are situated differently — positional vs movement; high-resource vs constrained; cultural-context differences. Strong responses move beyond praise to genuine analytic comparison. Marks: 2 specific leaders, 2 framework applications, 2 analytical depth.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
11
[5]
You are the elected co-chair of your school's Mental Health Awareness committee. The school board offers you a slot at a public city event — a visibility opportunity. A junior committee member who has done most of the on-the-ground work, and is from a community rarely visible in such events, has not been offered the slot.
Apply servant leadership and ethical leadership frameworks. In 5–6 sentences, identify two possible courses of action, justify which a servant leader would likely choose, and identify what ethical trap the easier path would be slipping into.
Answer Key

Course A: take the slot, frame it as "representing the committee." Course B: ask the board if the junior member can take the slot, or whether the slot can be a co-presentation. A servant leader would likely choose B (Greenleaf's test: do those served grow?). The easier path slips into abuse of position (the co-chair role doesn't entitle you to personal visibility) and possibly conflict of interest (your own benefit vs the committee's work). A defensible nuance: in some situations Course A is right (the junior member doesn't want it; visibility for the committee strategically matters). Strong responses recognise the situation as a real ethical decision, not an automatic answer. Marks: 2 courses, 2 servant-leader justification, 1 ethical-trap naming.

12
[4]
Apply the situational leadership and behavioural-theory frameworks. In 4 sentences, describe a peer or teammate you've worked with recently. Identify their developmental level for a specific task you observed, identify their behavioural strengths (task vs relationship), and suggest one growth direction.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) specific peer + specific task (no general descriptions), (2) developmental-level diagnosis with rationale, (3) behavioural-strength reading (task vs relationship), (4) concrete growth suggestion. Strong responses avoid both personality-essentialism ("they're just lazy") and over-generous reading ("they're great at everything"). Marks: 1 each.

[3]
STSE / Real-World Application: Identify one Canadian Indigenous leader, woman leader, or movement-based leader whose work you would research more deeply if assigned a leadership-biography project. In 3 sentences, explain who they are and what theoretical lens from Unit 3 you would use to study them, and why.
Answer Key

Many possible answers. Strong responses name a specific person (not a general category) and explain the lens choice. Examples: Cindy Blackstock through ethical leadership + movement-based frame; Roberta Jamieson through Indigenous relational + transformational; Murray Sinclair through ethical + truth-telling-leadership; Maud Lewis (artist) through quiet/character-based leadership; Jagmeet Singh through transformational + identity politics; Buffy Sainte-Marie through cultural/movement leadership. Marks: 1 named leader, 1 lens choice, 1 justification.
