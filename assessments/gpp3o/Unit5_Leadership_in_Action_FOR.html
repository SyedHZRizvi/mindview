Unit 5: Leadership in Action — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Project-readiness check · Synthesis
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Before starting your leadership project, identify your current planning, team-working, and reflective habits. This sets the baseline for the project's reflective evaluation.
Question 1 Project Idea
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe an idea for your leadership project — something you'd like to change, improve, or address in your school or community. (No need to commit; this is a brainstorm.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Use this as the starting point for project-design conversations. Look for: (1) specific scope (not "save the world"), (2) some connection to a real population (students, a community group), (3) some sense of what success looks like. Strong responses are local, specific, and modest in ambition. Coach down grand-ambition projects and coach up under-specified ones.

Question 2 Past Project Experience
[3 marks]
Describe one project (school, sport, club, community, family) you've worked on in the past year. What worked? What didn't? (3–4 sentences.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: (1) specific project, (2) honest evaluation, (3) recognition of patterns (typically: optimistic timeline, late surprises, unclear roles). Use to surface common student-project failure modes that Unit 5 directly addresses — back-planning, weekly stand-ups, change protocols. Mark 1 each.

3 Planning Style
[2 marks]
When you have a big assignment with a deadline four weeks out, what's your typical approach — start immediately and plan back from the deadline, work on it as inspiration strikes, or start in the final week? Be honest.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This surfaces the planning-vs-procrastination tension. Most students will report some version of last-week-cramming or inspiration-based work. Strong responses recognise the cost. Use the diagnostic to set up Lesson 5.1's argument for charters and Lesson 5.2's argument for back-planning.

4 Team Comfort
[3 marks]
On a team, what's your typical role — leader, ideas-person, executor, peacekeeper, observer, something else? In 3–4 sentences, identify your default and one situation where you'd want to push yourself toward a different role.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for self-knowledge about team-role default and growth orientation. Most students have one or two default roles. The goal of GPP3O is to expand the repertoire. Use the answer to identify which students might benefit from leading vs. being the steady executor on the project team. Marks: 1 default, 1 growth direction, 1 reasoning.

5 Reflective Habit
[2 marks]
When was the last time you reflected — wrote about, discussed, or thought carefully about — something you did, with the goal of doing it better next time? Describe briefly.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This sets baseline for Gibbs Reflective Cycle work in Lesson 5.5. Many students will report reflection only when forced (post-game debriefs, school journal assignments). Some will report a personal reflective habit. Strong responses include the next-time-commitment element naturally. Use to identify students who will need structured scaffolding.

6 Pathways Awareness
[2 marks]
What postsecondary destinations are you considering at this point — university, college, apprenticeship, workplace, gap year, undecided? How might leadership experience connect to your direction? (2–3 sentences.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Diagnostic of pathway-thinking. "Undecided" is honest and fine; many Grade-11 students are. The point is to identify which students might benefit from specific pathway-to-leadership conversations (university supplementary essays; college program interviews; trades supervisor preparation; community service positioning). Use to plan Lesson 5.6's depth.

7 Self-Goal
[ungraded]
If your leadership project at the end of this semester turns out really well — what would you want to be able to say about your own growth? One sentence.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
