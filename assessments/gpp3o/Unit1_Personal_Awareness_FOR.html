Unit 1: Personal Awareness — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prior-knowledge and self-readiness check · Strand A
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Help your teacher understand what you already know about self-awareness, emotional intelligence, and goal-setting — and where you'd like to grow. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Be honest; this is your starting point.
Question 1 Self-Reading
[3 marks]
In 3–4 sentences, describe one situation in the past year where you noticed your own emotional response (good or bad) shaping what happened. What did you feel, and what did you do?
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: (1) a specific situation (not generic), (2) a named emotion, (3) an observed action or behaviour, (4) some awareness of the link between feeling and action. Strong responses include a specific moment ("during the science group project last term I felt left out of the WhatsApp planning, got resentful, and contributed less than I could have"). Marks: 1 for specific situation, 1 for named feeling, 1 for action and link. Use feedback to flag whether the student already has language for inner states — students who do will move faster through Lesson 1.1; students who don't may need extra practice with emotion vocabulary.

Question 2 Prior Knowledge
[2 marks]
Have you heard of "emotional intelligence" before? If yes, what do you understand it to mean? If no, what do you think it might mean? (2–3 sentences either way.)
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This is a no-wrong-answer diagnostic. Look for: (1) honesty (some students will know Goleman by name, most won't), (2) intuitive grasp of the construct (recognising emotions, managing them). Note the entry point. Students with prior exposure can be pushed earlier into the empirical-debate territory of Lesson 1.2; students new to the term may need the foundational vocabulary first.

Question 3 Learning Self-Knowledge
[2 marks]
"Most students can describe how a friend learns better than they can describe how they themselves learn." — common observation from classroom teachers.
When you have to learn something new and difficult (a skill in sport, a topic for a test, a video-game mechanic), what is your typical approach? Try to describe it in 2–3 sentences.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for self-knowledge about learning preferences and habits. Strong responses describe specific mode preferences ("I watch videos to see it done, then I try it slowly while talking myself through it") or specific strategies ("I take photo-notes of the board and rewrite them at home"). Use this to identify VARK-style modalities the student leans on. Flag students whose answer is very generic ("I just study") — they may benefit from explicit modality-mapping in Lesson 1.3.

Question 4 Values
[3 marks]
List three things that are important to you. For each, describe a recent moment where you chose for that thing even though it was costly or inconvenient (one sentence each).
Sample Response & Marking Guide

This is a mini values card-sort. Look for: (1) three things named, (2) at least one concrete example of choosing-against-easier-path, (3) coherence between the stated value and the example. Strong response: "Family — I skipped a Saturday tournament to help my mum after her surgery; Honesty — I told my teacher I'd accidentally seen the test draft; Growth — I took the harder math option even though it was a marks risk." Weaker response gives three abstract values with no examples — flag this student for support in Lesson 1.4. Marks: 1 per pairing.

Question 5 Stress & Resilience
[3 marks]
When you are stressed (deadlines, conflict, fear), what do you typically do? Identify (a) one healthy response and (b) one unhealthy response you've noticed in yourself. No judgment — just observation.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Look for: (1) one healthy coping behaviour (going for a walk, talking to a friend, journaling, exercise, deep breathing), (2) one unhealthy coping behaviour (scrolling, isolating, snapping at family, skipping meals or sleep, substance use). Students who can name an unhealthy response without defensiveness are showing strong self-awareness. Students who cannot identify any unhealthy response may need a private follow-up — sometimes a sign of high masking. Marks: 1 healthy, 1 unhealthy, 1 honest observation.

Question 6 Goal-Setting
[2 marks]
Set one goal for this GPP3O course. In 2–3 sentences, describe what you'd like to be able to do or be at the end of the semester that you can't quite do now.
Sample Response & Marking Guide

Use this as a baseline to compare with the student's revised SMART goal at the end of Unit 1. Many initial goals will be vague ("be a better leader," "communicate well"). That's fine — the diagnostic is the start of the conversation. Mark for honesty and specificity. Strong response names a specific competency ("be able to mediate a small conflict between two friends without making it worse").

Question 7 Reflection & Readiness
[ungraded]
Of Unit 1's six lessons — self-awareness, emotional intelligence, learning styles, values, resilience, SMART goals — which one feels most relevant to where you are right now? Which feels most unfamiliar? (One sentence each is fine.)

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
