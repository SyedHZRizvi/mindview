Topic 4.1 — Internal Control & COSO
Question 1
Which is NOT one of the five components of the COSO framework?
Solution:
The COSO components: Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information & Communication, Monitoring. Insurance is not a COSO component.
Question 2
"Tone at the top" refers to which COSO component?
Solution:
Control environment includes the ethical climate ("tone at the top"), integrity, and competence values set by senior management.
Topic 4.2 — Segregation of Duties
Question 3
Which combination of duties should NEVER be performed by the same person?
Solution:
Custody and recordkeeping are two of the four "incompatible" duties. A person who has the money AND records the money can steal and cover it up.
Question 4
Which compensating control is most useful when full segregation is not possible in a small business?
Solution:
Owner-performed reconciliation is the classic compensating control: it provides an independent check by someone who does not record transactions.
Topic 4.3 — Control Procedures
Question 5
Why are pre-numbered cheques and pre-numbered receipts important?
Solution:
Pre-numbered documents close the loop: every number must be accounted for, so a missing one is investigated rather than ignored.
Topic 4.4 — Auditors
Question 6
An "unqualified" (clean) audit opinion means:
Solution:
"Unqualified" means there are no qualifications — the statements present fairly in all material respects. This is the cleanest, best opinion.
Topic 4.5 — Fraud Triangle
Question 7
The three legs of the fraud triangle are:
Solution:
Cressey's fraud triangle: pressure (motivation), opportunity (control weakness), rationalization (mental story).
Question 8
"Lapping" is best described as:
Solution:
Lapping is a slow-motion Ponzi-style scheme inside the AR ledger — each customer's payment is applied to cover the previous customer's stolen payment.
Topic 4.6 — SOX & Bill 198
Question 9
Canada's parallel to U.S. Sarbanes-Oxley for TSX-listed companies is:
Solution:
Ontario's Bill 198 led to NI 52-109, which requires CEO/CFO certification of disclosure and internal controls for Canadian public companies.
