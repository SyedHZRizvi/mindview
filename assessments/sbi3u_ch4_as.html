Topic 4.1–4.2 — Tissues, Homeostasis & Digestion
Question 1
Which is an example of negative feedback?
Solution: Insulin release after a meal reduces blood glucose back toward the set point — negative feedback. Blood clotting and childbirth are positive feedback (the response amplifies the stimulus).
Question 2
The villi and microvilli of the small intestine are important because they:
Solution: Folds + villi + microvilli together create about 250 m² of intestinal surface — vital for rapid absorption of digested nutrients into blood and lymph.
Question 3
Bile, produced by the liver and stored in the gall bladder, acts in the small intestine to:
Solution: Bile is not an enzyme. It emulsifies large fat globules into small droplets, vastly increasing the surface area for pancreatic lipase to digest fats chemically.
Topic 4.3–4.4 — Circulation & Respiration
Question 4
Which heart chamber pumps oxygenated blood into the systemic circuit?
Solution: The left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood through the aorta into the systemic circuit. Its wall is the thickest because of the high pressure required.
Question 5
Capillaries are well-suited to gas and nutrient exchange because they:
Solution: Capillary walls are a single layer of endothelial cells (~1 µm thick), and the body has billions of capillaries giving a huge total surface area. Both features maximise the rate of diffusive exchange.
Question 6
During inhalation, the diaphragm:
Solution: The diaphragm contracts and flattens during inhalation; combined with rib elevation (external intercostals), this increases thoracic volume, lowering pressure below atmospheric, and air flows in.
Question 7
The primary stimulus that drives breathing rate is:
Solution: Central chemoreceptors in the medulla detect rising CO2/falling pH; this is the dominant breathing stimulus in healthy people. Low O2 only becomes a major drive at altitude or in disease.
Topic 4.5–4.6 — Excretion & Nervous System
Question 8
The functional unit of the kidney is the:
Solution: Each kidney contains ~1 million nephrons, each carrying out filtration, reabsorption, and secretion to form urine.
Question 9
ADH (antidiuretic hormone) acts on the collecting duct to:
Solution: ADH from the posterior pituitary inserts aquaporins into collecting-duct cell membranes, increasing water reabsorption — small volume of concentrated urine. Aldosterone handles Na+/water in the distal tubule.
Question 10
A neuron at rest has a membrane potential of about:
Solution: Typical resting potential ≈ −70 mV, maintained by the Na+/K+ ATPase pumping 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in per ATP.
Question 11
The simplest reflex arc consists of:
Solution: A monosynaptic (knee-jerk) reflex: receptor → sensory neuron → motor neuron → effector. Most reflexes also include interneurons in the spinal cord, but the simplest example (the patellar reflex) has no interneuron.
Question 12
Which best describes saltatory conduction?
Solution: Myelin insulates most of the axon; the action potential only regenerates at the bare nodes of Ranvier. Signal effectively "jumps" from node to node — much faster than in unmyelinated axons.
