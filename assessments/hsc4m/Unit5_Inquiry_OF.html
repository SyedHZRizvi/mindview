Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
List the phases of the social-science inquiry process. Why is it called "recursive" rather than linear?
Answer Key

Phases: exploring topics → formulating research question → gathering evidence → interpreting and analyzing → evaluating and drawing conclusions → communicating with proper citation. Recursive because researchers often loop back — a finding may force question refinement; a source gap may force scope adjustment; ethical concerns may force method revision. Strong researchers zigzag and then present the result as coherent. 1 mark for phases, 1 for recursive concept, 1 for clear explanation.

2
[3]
Define and contrast THREE question types: descriptive, interpretive, causal. Match each to its best-suited primary method.
Answer Key

Descriptive (What is X? How many?) → survey, content analysis, descriptive ethnography. Interpretive (What does X mean to participants?) → ethnographic and interview methods. Causal (What produces X?) → experiment, quasi-experiment, process-tracing across cases. 1 mark per type with appropriate method.

3
[2]
The CRAAP test for source evaluation stands for:
Answer Key

A. Currency (when published?), Relevance (does it address your question?), Authority (credentials?), Accuracy (claims supported?), Purpose (why created?).

4
[3]
Define OCAP and identify each of the four elements. Why does it matter for research with Indigenous communities?
Answer Key

OCAP (First Nations Information Governance Centre): Ownership — First Nations collectively own information about themselves. Control — over how information is collected, used, disclosed. Access — to information about themselves regardless of where it is held. Possession — physical possession of data. Matters because research has historically extracted information from Indigenous communities without consent, control, or benefit — OCAP shifts the power dynamic to community partnership. 1 mark for each element, with names of all four = 2 marks total; 1 for "why matters."

5
[2]
Write the correct APA 7th edition reference list entry for a book: Clifford Geertz, "The Interpretation of Cultures," published in 1973 by Basic Books.
Answer Key

Geertz, C. (1973). The interpretation of cultures. Basic Books.

Note: Author surname, comma, initial. (Year). Title in sentence case italics. Publisher. Place is no longer required in APA 7. Article titles use sentence case (first word capitalised, rest lowercase except proper nouns).

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[6]
Topic: "Asian-Canadian high school students."
Working through the inquiry process, narrow this topic into a strong research question for a Grade 12 ISP. Show at least three iterations (broad topic → narrower topic → focused research question). Then justify why your final question is well-formed (specific, focused, open, researchable). Aim for 7–10 sentences.
Answer Key

Strong answer shows iterations like: (1) "Asian-Canadian high school students" — entire field. (2) Narrower: "Korean-Canadian high school students in Toronto" — narrower but still topic-broad. (3) Narrower still: "How do Korean-Canadian high school students in Toronto's east-end navigate their parents' expectations about academic achievement?" — getting close. (4) Final: "How do second-generation Korean-Canadian Grade 11–12 students in Scarborough's TDSB schools experience and respond to parental academic expectations, and how does this experience compare to their first-generation peers?" — focused, specific, comparative, researchable through 8–12 interviews + secondary literature. Justification: specific population, specific location, specific time frame, specific comparison structure, answerable with feasible methods. Marks: 3 for iterations; 3 for justification with each criterion (specific, focused, open, researchable) addressed.

7
[4]
Imagine you find the following four potential sources for an ISP on the impact of the residential school system on contemporary Indigenous communities: (i) TRC Final Report Volume 1 (2015); (ii) a Wikipedia article on residential schools; (iii) a peer-reviewed article in the Canadian Journal of Native Studies; (iv) a YouTube commentary claiming the TRC was politically motivated.
Evaluate each source using the CRAAP test. Identify primary vs secondary status. Indicate which would be cited and how.
Answer Key

(i) TRC Final Report: primary source. CRAAP — current (2015), highly relevant, authoritative (official commission), accurate (extensive evidence base), purpose explicit (truth-telling). Cite. (ii) Wikipedia: not citable; use for orientation; follow its footnotes to underlying scholarly sources. (iii) Peer-reviewed article: secondary source. CRAAP — likely current and relevant, high authority (peer-reviewed Indigenous-studies journal), accurate, scholarly purpose. Cite. (iv) YouTube commentary: primary source of contemporary discourse but not authoritative; needs scrutiny for accuracy and purpose; could be cited as evidence of "discourse" but never as authoritative source. Use selectively with framing. 1 mark per evaluation.

8
[3]
An ISP student wants to do brief interviews with members of a specific Toronto religious community. Identify THREE ethical considerations from TCPS 2 (or related frameworks) that must be addressed, and one specific protective action for each.
Answer Key

Three considerations: (1) Respect for persons / informed consent — protective action: written consent form explaining project, voluntary participation, right to withdraw, contact details. (2) Concern for welfare — protective action: anonymise findings, especially in a small community where individuals could be identified; offer to skip sensitive questions; debrief and offer support resources. (3) Justice / equity — protective action: ensure the research benefits or at least does not exploit the community; consult with community organisations; offer to share findings. 1 mark each.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[6]
Write a well-structured paragraph (7–9 sentences) modelling Grade 12 social-science writing. Topic: explain why both primary and secondary sources are essential in cultural inquiry. Use at least one APA-style in-text citation (invented if needed) and link to a specific cultural topic.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) clear topic sentence asserting both source types are essential; (2) explanation of what each type does (primary = direct evidence; secondary = analytical framework + context); (3) at least one APA in-text citation in proper format, e.g., (Boroditsky, 2011) or (TRC, 2015); (4) link to a specific cultural topic; (5) cohesive prose. Marks: 1 topic sentence, 2 each for primary and secondary explanations with example, 1 APA citation correctly formatted, 1 communication/conventions.

10
[3]
Convert this informal sentence into proper Grade 12 social-science academic register: "A lot of people think Indigenous languages are dying, but that's not really true because some are coming back."
Answer Key

Sample: "While UNESCO classifies thousands of Indigenous languages globally as endangered (UNESCO, 2010), the success of revitalization programs such as the Māori Kohanga Reo (since 1982) and the Mohawk Tsi Tyónnheht Onkwawén:na (since 1985) demonstrates that endangered status need not be terminal — sustained community effort, dedicated funding, and political recognition can produce significant intergenerational language renewal." Marks: 1 academic register; 1 specific evidence; 1 nuanced claim with citation.

11
[3]
Identify three common forms of plagiarism in student research. For each, identify how to avoid it.
Answer Key

Three forms: (1) Direct copying without quotation marks and citation — use quotation marks for any direct words from a source; include in-text citation with page. (2) Paraphrase without citation — substantively rewrite in your own words AND cite the source. (3) Using AI-generated text without acknowledgement — explicitly do not generate text via AI; if any AI tool is used, follow your school's policy (most now require acknowledgement, and using AI to write for you is academic misconduct). Other forms: false citation, self-plagiarism, ghostwriting. 1 mark each.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[6]
Design a complete ISP methods plan for the following research question: "How do recent Syrian-Canadian newcomer adolescents in Toronto experience and respond to their parents' expectations about preserving Arabic language and Islamic religious practice?"
Your methods plan should address: (a) primary method, (b) sample, (c) at least 2 secondary sources (real or plausible), (d) one major ethical consideration with protective action, (e) what a finding would look like.
Answer Key

Strong methods plan: (a) Primary method: semi-structured interviews — interpretive question requires participant perspective. (b) Sample: 6–8 Syrian-Canadian adolescents (ages 15–18) who arrived in Canada 2015–2024, recruited through Settlement Services and Toronto District School Board ESL programs; possible to include a small comparison sample (1.5-generation vs second-generation). (c) Secondary sources: Brettell & Hollifield's Migration Theory (2014); Hassan, Kanaan, & Khoury (2018) on Syrian refugee adolescent adjustment; CCR (Canadian Council for Refugees) reports on the Syrian-Canadian community; relevant TRC and Multiculturalism Act references for Canadian-context framing. (d) Ethical consideration: participants are adolescents — parental/guardian consent needed in addition to participant assent; topics (religion, family expectations) are sensitive; anonymise and offer support resources. (e) Sample finding: "Many participants describe a generational tension: Arabic is treated by parents as marker of identity to preserve at home, while at school it is largely absent; religious practice is similarly framed differently in home and school spheres." Marks: 1 each for five parts; 1 for plausibility/specificity.

13
[3]
STSE / Real-world application: A Toronto-based researcher proposes a study of "cultural integration among Indigenous urban youth." Apply OCAP principles and TCPS 2 Chapter 9 to identify what consultations and partnerships must be in place before this research begins. Identify at least three specific actions.
Answer Key

(1) Connect with relevant Indigenous community organisations — Native Canadian Centre of Toronto, Ontario Federation of Indigenous Friendship Centres (OFIFC), an Elder, Indigenous Education Council. (2) Co-design the research — community helps shape research questions, methods, recruitment, interpretation; OCAP requires this. (3) Establish community ownership of data — agreements about who owns interview transcripts, what is published, when and how findings are shared. (4) Plan for benefits to community — findings shared back, publication and presentation involve community members. (5) Use of "cultural integration" framing itself requires scrutiny — what assumptions does it embed? Marks: 1 each for three specific actions, properly grounded in OCAP/TCPS 2.

14
[3]
Write a strong abstract (4–6 sentences) for a hypothetical ISP titled: "K-pop Fandom and Tamil-Canadian Identity Formation in Scarborough." Include research question, methods, sample, and a hint of findings.
Answer Key

Sample abstract: "This study explores how participation in K-pop fan communities shapes the cultural identity of second-generation Tamil-Canadian adolescents in Scarborough. Drawing on semi-structured interviews with eight participants aged 14–17, supplemented by content analysis of their public social-media activity, the research traces how engagement with a globally-dispersed fandom contributes to participants' sense of pan-Asian identity, ethnic pride, and diasporic connection. Findings suggest that K-pop fandom functions as a third cultural space — neither Tamil nor mainstream Canadian — through which participants negotiate the tensions of growing up in a multicultural city. The research contributes to broader social-science conversations about transnational media fandom and second-generation identity formation in Canada." Marks: 1 research question; 1 methods/sample; 1 hint of findings; communication/style.
