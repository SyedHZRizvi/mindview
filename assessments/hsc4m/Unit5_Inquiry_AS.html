Topic 5.1 — Inquiry Process
Question 1
Which of the following is a well-formed RESEARCH QUESTION (not a topic, not a yes-no question)?
Solution: A good research question is specific, focused, open (not yes/no), and researchable with available evidence. Option (c) names: the specific program, the specific community, the specific time period, and asks BOTH descriptive (how has it contributed?) AND analytical (what conditions shaped it?) — making it answerable through interview, observation, and document review.
Question 2
A research project that asks "What does this experience MEAN to participants?" calls for which question-type and method?
Solution: Questions about meaning are interpretive — they require depth, context, and access to participants' own framing. Ethnography and semi-structured interviews are well-suited to interpretive inquiry. Surveys can complement but cannot deeply answer meaning-questions. Experiments are typically for causal claims about discrete variables.
Topic 5.2 — Methods
Question 3
Ethnographic fieldwork's signature technique is:
Solution: Participant observation — taking part in community life while systematically observing and taking field notes — is the signature ethnographic method. Malinowski's Argonauts of the Western Pacific (1922), based on two years in the Trobriand Islands, is the founding example. The method produces what Geertz called "thick description."
Question 4
"Triangulation" in social-science methods means:
Solution: Triangulation in research = combining multiple methods (interview + content analysis + secondary-source review) or multiple sources (multiple participants, multiple settings) to converge on a finding. If three different approaches all suggest the same pattern, confidence in the finding is higher than if only one method had been used.
Topic 5.3 — Sources
Question 5
A primary source for research on residential schools would include:
Solution: The TRC Final Report is a primary source — created by the official commission, containing survivor testimony, archival research, and the 94 Calls to Action. Wikipedia and textbook summaries are secondary sources. The YouTube commentary is a primary source of contemporary discourse but not an authoritative analytical source — its claims would need verification against the actual record.
Question 6
The CRAAP test for evaluating sources stands for:
Solution: CRAAP (developed at CSU Chico's Meriam Library): Currency (when published?), Relevance (does it address your specific question?), Authority (who is the author? credentials?), Accuracy (claims supported by evidence?), Purpose (why was it created — inform, persuade, sell?). A quick rubric for evaluating any source.
Topic 5.4 — Research Ethics
Question 7
"OCAP" in research with Indigenous communities stands for:
Solution: OCAP (First Nations Information Governance Centre): Ownership (First Nations collectively own information about themselves), Control (over how information is collected, used, disclosed), Access (to information about themselves), Possession (physical possession of data). The framework guides ethical research with Indigenous communities; TCPS 2 Chapter 9 builds on it.
Question 8
The three core principles of the Canadian Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS 2) for research ethics are:
Solution: TCPS 2's three core principles: Respect for persons (informed consent, voluntary participation), Concern for welfare (minimising harm), Justice (fair selection of participants, equitable distribution of benefits and burdens). These principles also map to the U.S. Belmont Report and most international research-ethics frameworks. They emerged from reflection on past research harms (Tuskegee, Nuremberg, etc.).
Topic 5.5 — APA Citation
Question 9
In APA 7th edition, an in-text citation for a direct quotation from page 65 of Boroditsky's 2011 article would be:
Solution: APA 7th: (Author, Year, p. Page) for direct quotation. For paraphrase, page is optional but recommended: (Boroditsky, 2011). Three+ authors use et al.: (Markus & Kitayama, 1991) for two authors, (Hofstede et al., 2010) for three or more. The reference list at the end provides the full bibliographic detail.
Question 10
A correct APA 7th reference list entry for a journal article would look like:
Solution: APA 7th reference list format for a journal article: Author Surname, Initial. (Year). Article title in sentence case. Journal Title in Title Case Italics, Volume(Issue), Page–Page. Note: only the journal title is italicised; article title is plain; first word and proper nouns are capitalised in article title (sentence case). The Purdue OWL and the Scribbr APA guide give complete formatting rules.
