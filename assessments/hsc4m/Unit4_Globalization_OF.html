Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [14 marks]
1
[3]
Define and contrast THREE theories of cultural globalization: homogenization, hybridization, and heterogenization. Give one example illustration of each.
Answer Key

Homogenization (Ritzer): increasing global uniformity along Western/American lines — McDonald's, Hollywood, English as default. Hybridization (Kraidy, Pieterse): global forms get locally reshaped — K-pop combines American R&B + Korean cultural codes; Indian McAloo Tikki burger. Heterogenization (Appadurai): globalization can increase variety, especially via diasporic communities maintaining and proliferating distinct identities through new media. 1 mark per term + example.

2
[2]
Marshall McLuhan's claim "the medium is the message" means:
Answer Key

B. McLuhan's Understanding Media (1964): the structural properties of a medium (linearity of print, synchronicity of broadcast, fragmentation of smartphone) shape thought independently of any specific content.

3
[3]
Define cultural appropriation precisely. Distinguish it from cultural exchange and cultural appreciation.
Answer Key

Cultural appropriation — use of marginalised cultural elements by dominant culture, ignoring meaning, with no credit/compensation, often distorting sacred or significant practices (Scafidi, Keene). Cultural exchange — two-way borrowing between cultures of roughly equal power with consensual participation. Cultural appreciation — informed, contextualised engagement with credit, respect, and support of source-culture practitioners. The key dimension is POWER ASYMMETRY. 1 mark each.

4
[3]
Define each: (a) diaspora, (b) transnationalism, (c) remittances. Give a brief contemporary example of each.
Answer Key

(a) Diaspora — dispersed community maintaining ties to homeland (Tamil-Canadians, c. 300,000, supporting Tamil-language media, family in Sri Lanka, political mobilisation). (b) Transnationalism — sustained social/economic/political ties across borders (Polish workers commuting Warsaw–London during EU free-movement years; Indian engineers shuttling Bangalore–Toronto). (c) Remittances — money sent home by migrants ($647 billion globally in 2022; major source for Philippines, Mexico, Bangladesh; supports families and shapes home-country economies). 1 mark each.

5
[3]
Identify the TRC, its central legal finding, and at least TWO of its 94 Calls to Action that relate to education or culture.
Answer Key

TRC = Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada (2008–2015). Finding: cultural genocide for the residential school system. Calls to Action: 62 (mandatory Indigenous content in K-12 curriculum), 63 (teacher training on Indigenous topics), 80 (federal statutory holiday for Truth and Reconciliation), 83 (cultural support including arts), 13–17 (language and culture). 1 mark for TRC, 1 for finding, 0.5 each for two Calls.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [14 marks]
6
[6]
"McDonald's entered India in 1996 — and immediately had to remake itself. The Big Mac was impossible because most Hindus do not eat beef. McDonald's India developed the Maharaja Mac (chicken/vegetarian patties), the McAloo Tikki burger (potato-and-pea cutlet rooted in Indian street food), and separate vegetarian and non-vegetarian kitchens and staff."
Analyse this scenario through ALL THREE globalization theories (homogenization, hybridization, heterogenization). For each, what does the case support? Aim for 6–8 sentences total.
Answer Key

Strong answers: (1) Homogenization: the McDonald's brand, logo, drive-through format, fast-food rhythm, and labour management are spreading the global form into India, potentially displacing traditional dhabas and street vendors. (2) Hybridization: the McAloo Tikki is genuinely Indian fast food in McDonald's drag — a new hybrid that did not exist before; the menu and kitchen-segregation are McDonald's adapting to India. (3) Heterogenization: the McDonald's brand has been inflected by Indian dietary norms; the company has had to learn vegetarianism at scale; the corporation changed more than the local culture did in some respects. (4) Synthesis: all three are correct at once — globalization is rarely a single uniform process. A Level 4 answer names all three and weighs which is most explanatory for which aspect. Marks: 2 each for accurate use of three theories with the case.

7
[4]
A 15-year-old in Scarborough scrolls TikTok and within weeks her feed is overwhelmingly Tamil-Canadian content. Analyse this scenario through Shoshana Zuboff's surveillance capitalism, Eli Pariser's filter bubble, and Arjun Appadurai's heterogenization. What is the user gaining? What is at stake?
Answer Key

Strong answers: (1) Gaining: she finds Tamil-Canadian content, community, language reinforcement her school did not provide — heterogenization in action; diasporic identity strengthened by platform-mediated content. (2) Stakes — filter bubble: the algorithm progressively narrows her exposure rather than broadens it; she gets more of what she has already clicked. (3) Stakes — surveillance capitalism: her growing identity is being shaped by an algorithm optimising for engagement (and ad revenue); she is also generating behavioural surplus that refines the algorithm and the targeted ads she sees. (4) The platform has made diasporic community more accessible AND more thoroughly commercial. Both readings simultaneously true. Marks: 1 each for accurate use of the three concepts; 1 for the dual-ledger synthesis.

8
[4]
Compare and contrast Māori language revitalization (Aotearoa New Zealand) and Mohawk language revitalization (Tyendinaga, Kahnawà:ke, Six Nations). What conditions support success? What conditions remain challenges?
Answer Key

Strong answers: (1) Both rely on intergenerational immersion from fluent elders to children/adults. (2) Māori advantages: single politically-cohesive nation, single government, 1987 Māori Language Act, Māori TV (2004), institutional scale (Kohanga Reo, Kura Kaupapa, Wharekura, university programs). (3) Mohawk challenges: nation split across multiple communities and jurisdictions (Ontario, Quebec, NY State, two federal governments); more recent and intense language disruption; more fragmented funding. (4) Mohawk strengths: strong community commitment, adult-immersion programs, dedicated language schools (Tsi Tyónnheht Onkwawén:na, Kahnawà:ke language school). (5) Conditions for success generally: political recognition, dedicated funding, institutional scale, intergenerational continuity, media presence. Marks: 1 each for: parallels, Māori-specific advantages, Mohawk-specific challenges, conditions-for-success synthesis.

Part C: Communication [13 marks]
9
[6]
Write a well-structured paragraph (7–9 sentences) responding to: "Cultural appropriation is a useful analytical concept, but it is sometimes applied too loosely. How do we use it well?" Include a thesis, two specific case examples (one clear appropriation, one borderline), and a nuanced conclusion.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) clear THESIS that appropriation is a precise concept (Scafidi, Keene) with four elements: power asymmetry, ignored meaning, no credit/compensation, distortion of sacred practice. (2) Clear case: Halloween "headdress costume" — all four elements present. (3) Borderline case: yoga in North America — power asymmetry exists, but the borderline is whether specific studios credit, employ South Asian teachers, contribute to Indian organisations, or whether they water down the practice for commercial purposes; the answer depends on the specifics. (4) Nuanced conclusion: appropriation is best used as a specific analytical tool, not a blanket charge; it asks specific empirical questions about a specific case. Marks: 1 thesis, 2 each for two examples, 1 nuance, 1 conventions.

10
[3]
Convert this weak claim into a strong analytical thesis: "Globalization affects culture."
Answer Key

Sample: "Globalization simultaneously homogenises cultural forms (McDonald's, Hollywood, English), hybridises them through localisation (McAloo Tikki, K-pop), and heterogenises through diasporic and revitalization counter-flows (Māori Kohanga Reo, Tamil-Canadian platforms) — such that analysing any specific case requires distinguishing which flow predominates and at whose expense." Marks: 1 specificity, 1 debatability, 1 theoretical grounding.

[4]
In one or two sentences each, distinguish: refugee, economic migrant, international student, family reunification immigrant. Identify one Canadian policy that affects each category.
Answer Key

Refugee — fleeing persecution; UN Convention 1951; Canadian programs include Government-Assisted Refugees, Privately Sponsored Refugees, Syrian and Ukrainian initiatives. Economic migrant — seeking work; Canadian programs include Express Entry, Provincial Nominee, Temporary Foreign Worker. International student — pursuing education; study permits, post-graduation work permits (PGWP). Family reunification immigrant — joining established family; Canadian sponsorship programs (spouse, parent, dependent child). 1 mark each.

Part D: Application [14 marks]
12
[6]
"A Toronto yoga studio teaches Vinyasa flow under a Sanskrit-named teacher with limited formal training in the Hindu philosophical context. The studio's marketing emphasizes 'self-care' and 'mindfulness'; the language of yoga (asana names, the eight limbs) appears in classes but its tradition (yamas, niyamas, the Bhagavad Gita context) is not taught. The studio's profits go primarily to its non-Indian-Canadian owners."
Apply Unit 4 concepts (cultural appreciation, exchange, appropriation, commodification, power asymmetry) to analyse this scenario. Identify what is happening and propose how the studio could move toward more respectful practice.
Answer Key

Strong answers: (1) Power asymmetry exists — yoga comes from a South Asian tradition; the studio is non-South-Asian-owned and -profiting. (2) Meaning ignored / commodification — yoga is reduced to body practice and self-care, with its spiritual / ethical framework (yamas, niyamas) stripped. (3) Borderline appropriation, not clear exchange — the studio acknowledges Sanskrit names but does not credit, employ, or financially support Indian-origin yoga organisations or teachers. (4) Path to appreciation: credit the tradition explicitly in classes; employ South Asian teachers with deep training; teach the philosophical and historical context; financially support Indian organisations preserving the practice (e.g., Krishnamacharya Yoga Mandiram, Iyengar Institute); resist further watering-down. (5) The point is not to ban non-South-Asian yoga teaching but to insist on respectful, contextualised, credited, supported practice. Marks: 1 each for: power asymmetry, commodification analysis, appropriation vs exchange distinction, proposal specificity, communication, synthesis.

13
[4]
STSE / Real-world application: The 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine produced one of the largest refugee movements in Europe since WWII. Canada has welcomed over 200,000 Ukrainians by 2026. Apply migration concepts (push/pull, refugee, diaspora, transnationalism, remittances) to analyse this migration's impact on (a) Ukraine, (b) Canada, and (c) the Ukrainian-Canadian community.
Answer Key

Strong answers: (a) Ukraine: demographic loss (especially of women and children, since military-age men cannot leave); remittances flowing back partially compensate; potential brain drain; longer-term: returning population may bring skills and capital. (b) Canada: demographic boost and language/skill mix; pressure on housing, schools, settlement services; long-term integration challenges and opportunities. (c) Ukrainian-Canadian community: pre-existing diaspora of 1.4 million (largely descendants of late-19th/early-20th-century settlement) is mobilising — providing settlement support, raising funds, lobbying government, hosting families. Transnationalism: many families now live across two countries with active back-and-forth communication and identity formation. The Toronto Ukrainian Festival, the Plast Ukrainian-Canadian youth organisation, and the Ukrainian Canadian Congress are key institutional supports. Marks: 1 each for three regions/levels of analysis + 1 for migration-concept use.

[4]
Identify a contemporary cultural-revitalization movement (Māori, Mohawk, Welsh, Hawaiian, Sámi, etc.) other than the Tamil-Canadian or Indigenous-Canadian cases discussed in class. In 4–6 sentences, identify: (a) what is being revitalized, (b) what historical events suppressed it, (c) what current strategies are being used, (d) what success criteria you would use to evaluate progress.
Answer Key

Sample on Welsh: (a) The Welsh language has been the subject of revitalization since the 19th-century National Eisteddfod and especially since the 1967 Welsh Language Act and the 1993 Welsh Language Act. (b) British policies banned Welsh in school (the 1847 "Treachery of the Blue Books" and the "Welsh Not"), suppressed in courts (Acts of Union 1535–1542); industrial revolution displaced rural Welsh-speaking populations. (c) Current: Welsh-medium schools, S4C broadcasting (1982), Welsh-Assembly bilingual proceedings, university programs, signage requirements. (d) Success criteria: speaker numbers (currently ~600,000 fluent, increasing); intergenerational transmission; presence in workplace and public life; cultural production (literature, music, film). Marks: 1 each for four parts.
