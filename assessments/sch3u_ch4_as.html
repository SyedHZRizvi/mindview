Solutions & Solubility
Question 1
A solution containing more solute than the saturation amount at a given temperature is called:
Solution:
Supersaturated — unstable; precipitates readily when disturbed.
Question 2
As temperature rises, the solubility of MOST ionic solids in water:
Solution:
Most ionic solids: solubility increases with T. (Exception: a few, like Ca(OH)₂.) Gases dissolve LESS at higher T.
Question 3
Hexane (non-polar) will dissolve:
Solution:
"Like dissolves like." Non-polar hexane dissolves non-polar octane. NaCl, sugar, water all require polar solvent.
Concentration & Dilution
Question 4
Concentration of a solution of 0.10 mol NaOH in 500 mL of solution (mol/L):
Solution:
c = n/V = 0.10/0.500 = 0.20 mol/L.
Question 5
Volume of 6.0 mol/L HCl needed to prepare 250 mL of 0.20 mol/L HCl:
Solution:
\(V_1 = c_2V_2/c_1 = (0.20)(250)/6.0 = \mathbf{8.33}\) mL.
Question 6
A drinking-water lead limit is 5 μg/L. Express this in ppb (assume density ~1 g/mL).
Solution:
ppb = μg solute / kg solvent. 5 μg/L water with density 1 kg/L = 5 ppb.
Acids, Bases & pH
Question 7
[H⁺] of a 0.010 mol/L HCl solution. pH = ?
Solution:
HCl is strong acid → [H⁺] = 0.010 = 10⁻² mol/L. pH = −log(10⁻²) = 2.0.
Question 8
Which is a strong base?
Solution:
NaOH is a strong base (Group 1 hydroxide, fully dissociates). NH₃ is weak; CH₃COOH and H₂CO₃ are weak acids.
Question 9
Solution X has pH 3; solution Y has pH 5. Solution X has how many times more [H⁺] than Y?
Solution:
pH 3 → [H⁺] = 10⁻³; pH 5 → [H⁺] = 10⁻⁵. Ratio = 10²/1 = 100×.
Titration
Question 10
25.0 mL of HCl is fully neutralized by 22.50 mL of 0.100 mol/L NaOH. Concentration of HCl:
Solution:
c_a V_a = c_b V_b → c_a = (0.100)(22.50)/25.0 = 0.0900 mol/L.
Question 11
For a strong-acid-strong-base titration, the equivalence point pH is approximately:
Solution:
Strong acid + strong base → neutral salt + water → pH ≈ 7.
Question 12
Which indicator's colour change best matches the equivalence point of a strong-acid-strong-base titration?
Solution:
Equivalence pH ≈ 7 → bromothymol blue (range straddles 7). Phenolphthalein is often used in practice because the equivalence is sharp; methyl red/orange are better for weak-base titrations.
