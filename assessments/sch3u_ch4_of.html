Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]

Classify each as soluble or insoluble in water at room temperature (use solubility rules): (a) NaCl; (b) AgCl; (c) BaSO₄; (d) KNO₃; (e) PbI₂; (f) CaCO₃.
Question 2 [2 marks]
Concentration of a solution prepared by dissolving 4.00 g of NaOH (M=40.00) in water to make 500.0 mL of solution.
Solution:
n = 4.00/40.00 = 0.100 mol; c = 0.100/0.500 = 0.200 mol/L.

Question 5 [2 marks]
Why does opening a warm bottle of soda fizz more than opening a cold one?
Solution:
(c) — both: (a) gas solubility drops with T; (b) gas pressure rises with T.

Complete Answer Key

Q1. Soluble: NaCl, KNO₃ (Group 1 / nitrates). Insoluble: AgCl, BaSO₄, PbI₂, CaCO₃.

Q2. 0.200 mol/L.

Q3. [H⁺] = 0.025 mol/L; pH = −log(0.025) = 1.60.

Q4. %m/v: 8.00/250 × 100 = 3.20%. Molar: n = 8.00/110.98 = 0.0721 mol; c = 0.0721/0.250 = 0.288 mol/L.

Q5. (c).

Q6. (a) Saturated = solution at maximum solubility for given T; in dynamic equilibrium with undissolved solid. (b) ppm = mg solute / kg solvent (or mg/L for dilute aq.).

Q7. n(NaOH) = 0.1500 × 0.01785 = 2.678×10⁻³ mol = n(HCl); c(HCl) = 2.678×10⁻³/0.02000 = 0.1339 mol/L; pH = −log(0.1339) = 0.873.

Q8. Mass NaOH = 0.250 L × 0.100 mol/L × 40.00 g/mol = 1.00 g. Weigh 1.00 g NaOH on analytical balance (wear gloves — corrosive). Transfer to 250.0 mL volumetric flask; add ~150 mL distilled water; swirl until dissolved; dilute to the etched mark; invert to mix. Safety: NaOH is caustic — goggles + gloves; never inhale dust.

Q9. V₁ = c₂V₂/c₁ = (1.00)(0.500)/18.0 = 0.0278 L = 27.8 mL. Procedure: Place ~300 mL distilled water in 500-mL volumetric flask. Wear goggles, gloves, lab coat. Slowly pipette 27.8 mL of 18.0 mol/L H₂SO₄ into the water (releases heat; never reverse). Swirl while adding. Cool, then dilute to mark. Safety: concentrated H₂SO₄ is highly corrosive and exothermic in water.

Q10. Concentrated = high solute concentration; saturated = at solubility limit. A 1 mol/L HCl is concentrated but unsaturated (HCl is very soluble). A 36 g/100 g NaCl is saturated but not very concentrated.

Q11. Curve slopes upward; KNO₃ ~32 g at 20 °C, ~110 g at 60 °C. Unsaturated: point BELOW curve. Saturated: point ON curve. Supersaturated: point ABOVE curve (prepared by cooling carefully).

Q12. SA+SB: resulting salt has neither H⁺ nor OH⁻ tendency → pH ≈ 7. WA+SB: salt of weak acid hydrolyzes (e.g., CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻) → solution slightly basic.

Q13. Acid dilution releases substantial heat; if water is added to acid, the dense acid pools below splashing droplets that flash-boil and spray. Adding acid to a larger volume of water dissipates heat safely.

Q14. (a) 0.010 ppm = 0.010 mg/L = 10 μg/L. (b) c = 0.010×10⁻³ g/L ÷ 207.2 g/mol = 4.8 × 10⁻⁸ mol/L.

Q15. Dose = 0.5 × 65 = 32.5 mg per dose. Daily = 65 mg. Each dose = 32.5 mg/0.250 L = 130 mg/L.

Q16. n(HCl) = 0.250 × 0.01240 = 3.10×10⁻³ mol = n(KOH). c(KOH) = 3.10×10⁻³/0.02500 = 0.124 mol/L. Mass dissolved = 3.10×10⁻³ × 56.11 = 0.174 g (in the 25.00 mL sample).

Q17. Acid rain [H⁺] = 10⁻⁴ = 1.0×10⁻⁴ mol/L. Normal rain [H⁺] = 10⁻⁵·⁶ ≈ 2.5×10⁻⁶ mol/L. Ratio ≈ 40×. Consequence: acidifies lakes (kills fish), leaches Al³⁺ from soil, damages forests, corrodes statues/buildings.
