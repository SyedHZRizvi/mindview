Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [14 marks]
1
[3]
Describe the 2019 Canada's Food Guide plate model and the three healthy-eating pillars.
Answer Key

Plate: ½ vegetables & fruit, ¼ whole grain foods, ¼ protein foods (plant-based "more often"). Water is the beverage of choice. Three pillars: (1) be mindful of eating habits — eat regularly and in a positive setting; (2) cook more often, plan, prepare, and share meals; (3) limit highly processed foods.

2
[2]
A Nutrition Facts table shows fibre 5 g (20% DV). According to the 5%–15% rule, this is:
Answer Key

B. 15% DV or more = "a lot". Fibre is a nutrient to encourage, so high %DV is desirable.

3
[3]
Define and distinguish: EAR, RDA, AI, UL. Which is the best target for an individual?
Answer Key

EAR — Estimated Average Requirement; meets 50% of group; for population assessment. RDA — Recommended Dietary Allowance; meets 97–98% of healthy individuals; best individual target. AI — Adequate Intake; used when data are too sparse to set RDA. UL — Tolerable Upper Intake Level; maximum safe daily intake. RDA is the standard individual target.

4
[3]
List four nutrients of concern in a Canadian vegan diet and a strategy to address each.
Answer Key

Vitamin B12 — fortified plant milks, nutritional yeast, or supplement. Vitamin D — fortified beverages and supplement (especially winter). Iron — pair plant iron with vitamin C; consider iron-fortified cereal. Calcium — fortified plant milks, calcium-set tofu, dark leafy greens. (Also: omega-3 — flax, chia, walnuts, algae-derived DHA; iodine — iodised salt, sea vegetables.)

5
[3]
List five of Health Canada's 11 priority allergens.
Answer Key

Any 5 of: peanuts, tree nuts, sesame, milk, eggs, fish, shellfish (crustaceans + molluscs), soy, wheat, sulphites, mustard.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
6
[4]
Cereal A: 30 g, 14% DV sugar, 4% DV fibre, 11% DV sodium. Cereal B: 30 g, 5% DV sugar, 20% DV fibre, 3% DV sodium. Both 110 kcal. Use the 5%–15% rule to recommend which to buy and justify.
Answer Key

Sugar: A 14% (close to "a lot" — limit), B 5% ("a little" — good). Fibre: A 4% ("a little" — bad), B 20% ("a lot" — good). Sodium: A 11% (moderate), B 3% ("a little" — good). Recommend B: better on every nutrient axis; identical calories. The 5%–15% rule says limit-nutrients should be low and encourage-nutrients high — B wins on both.

7
[4]
Design a 7-day food log experiment to assess whether the Canadian Community Health Survey under-reports added-sugar intake. Identify the population, methodology, controls, and the dependent variable.
Answer Key

Population: random Ontario adults n = 100. Methodology: 7-day weighed food record (gold standard) + simultaneous CCHS-style 24-h recall interview. Controls: same week, same researcher protocol, validated dietary-analysis software (Canadian Nutrient File). DV: total added sugar intake (g/day) from each method. Hypothesis: 24-h recall (used in CCHS) under-reports by ~15–25% vs weighed record, because participants forget snacks and beverages. Compare means with paired t-test. This kind of study has been published showing systematic under-reporting in self-reported dietary surveys.

8
[4]
A pregnant woman in her second trimester weighing 65 kg needs 1.1 g protein/kg + 25 g/day. Calculate total protein needs. If she eats: 1 cup yogurt (10 g), 2 eggs (12 g), 1 chicken breast 100 g (30 g), 1 cup lentils (18 g), 1 cup milk (8 g), is she meeting her need?
Answer Key

Need: 1.1 × 65 + 25 = 71.5 + 25 ≈ 97 g/day. Intake: 10 + 12 + 30 + 18 + 8 = 78 g/day. Shortfall of ~19 g — she is below her target. Add another protein-rich snack or larger serving (e.g., 30 g cheese ≈ 7 g + 30 g almonds ≈ 6 g + 100 g tofu ≈ 8 g) to close the gap.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[4]
Explain the difference between the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet. State the strongest piece of evidence supporting each.
Answer Key

Mediterranean — olive oil, fish, legumes, whole grains, nuts, vegetables, moderate wine. PREDIMED trial (2013): ~30% reduction in major cardiovascular events vs control. DASH — vegetables, fruit, whole grains, low-fat dairy, lean protein, sodium 1 500–2 300 mg. Reduces systolic blood pressure ~6–11 mmHg in 2 weeks (Appel et al., NEJM 1997). Both share whole-food, plant-forward emphasis but DASH is more sodium-restrictive.

10
[4]
Construct a one-day meal plan for a 16-year-old vegetarian athlete (60 kg, 3 200 kcal/day, 1.3 g/kg protein). Include three meals plus snacks; estimate protein and iron per meal.
Answer Key

Target ≈ 78 g protein, 15 mg iron. Breakfast: 1 cup oatmeal + 1 cup fortified soy milk + ¼ cup almonds + ½ cup berries (~15 g prot, 4 mg Fe + vitamin C). Snack: 1 cup yogurt + banana (~12 g, 0 mg Fe). Lunch: 1 cup lentil curry + 1 cup brown rice + spinach salad with lemon (~22 g, 7 mg Fe + vitamin C from spinach/lemon). Snack: 30 g peanuts + apple (~7 g, 1 mg Fe). Dinner: 1 cup tofu stir-fry with broccoli, peppers, and 1 cup quinoa (~25 g, 4 mg Fe). Total: ~81 g protein, ~16 mg iron — both met.

11
[4]
Compare and contrast nutrient needs across the lifespan in a 4-row table: infant (6 mo), school-age child (8 y), teenager (15 y), pregnant adult.
Answer Key

Infant: ~600 kcal; protein 1.5 g/kg; vitamin D supplement 400 IU; iron-fortified complementary foods after 6 months. Child (8 y): ~1 600 kcal; protein ~1.1 g/kg; calcium 1 000 mg; iron 10 mg. Teen (15 y): 2 200–3 000 kcal; protein 1.0 g/kg; calcium 1 300 mg; iron 11 mg (M) / 15 mg (F). Pregnant adult: +340 kcal T2, +452 T3; protein +25 g; folate 600 µg (+ 400 µg supplement); iron 27 mg; calcium 1 000–1 300 mg; avoid raw fish, high-mercury fish, alcohol, unpasteurised dairy.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[4]
Case Study — School lunch program. A school cafeteria proposes pizza and chocolate milk daily. Critique using the 2019 Food Guide and suggest two upgrades that keep cost low.
Answer Key

Critique: pizza is highly processed (refined dough, processed cheese, processed meat); a single slice covers minimal vegetables. Chocolate milk has ≈25 g added sugar per 250 mL. The plate is dominated by refined grain and protein with no vegetable section. Upgrades: (1) replace half the pizza with a bean & vegetable chili that uses pantry staples (lentils, tomato, onion, peppers — ~$0.50/serving); offer plain milk and water rather than chocolate milk by default; (2) offer raw vegetable sticks and fruit on the side, included in the price. Net cost change is minimal; nutrition improves substantially.

13
[4]
Case Study — Allergic anaphylaxis at school. A peer eats a granola bar at lunch and within five minutes develops hives, lip swelling, wheezing, and dizziness. He has a known peanut allergy. Describe the emergency response, including specifics of epinephrine use.
Answer Key

This is anaphylaxis. Steps: (1) Recognise — hives + airway swelling + wheezing + cardiovascular signs = anaphylaxis. (2) Use his EpiPen — remove the safety cap and inject IM into the outer thigh (through clothing if needed), hold 3 seconds. (3) Call 911. (4) Lay the student down (or sit up if breathing is the priority) with legs elevated. (5) If symptoms persist 5–15 min and a second EpiPen is available, give it. (6) Stay with him; monitor airway and consciousness. Ontario's Sabrina's Law (2005) mandates school anaphylaxis policies, staff training, accessible epinephrine, and reporting.

14
[4]
STSE — Folic-acid fortification of flour. Health Canada has mandated folic-acid fortification of white flour since 1998. Explain the public-health rationale, the intended outcome, and the measured impact.
Answer Key

Rationale: low folate intake during the first 28 days of gestation greatly elevates risk of neural-tube defects (NTDs) — spina bifida and anencephaly. Most pregnancies are unplanned, so by the time many women learn they are pregnant the neural tube has already closed. Population-wide fortification raises baseline folate intake in all women of childbearing age, before pregnancy is recognised. Impact: NTD prevalence in Canada fell by ~50% in the 5 years after fortification (De Wals et al., NEJM 2007). Voluntary supplementation with 400 µg/day folic acid pre-conception is still recommended on top of fortification.
