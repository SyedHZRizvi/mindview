Unit 6: Media Literacy — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand D
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify what you already know (and what you do not yet know) about media analysis. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Knowledge · Appeals
[3 marks]
Define Aristotle's three rhetorical appeals — logos, ethos, pathos — and give one short example of each (from real or invented media).
Question 2 Skills · Sources
[3 marks]
A friend shares an article with a striking headline. List three specific questions you should ask before deciding whether to trust the article.
Question 3 Prereq · Categories
[3 marks]
Distinguish among news reporting, opinion / editorial, and sponsored / native content. Why does the distinction matter?
Question 4 Skills · Headline Analysis
[3 marks]
Consider these two headlines for the same story: (A) "Federal government commits $4 billion to transit." (B) "Liberals push through $4 billion transit spending." Identify two specific differences in framing and explain what work each framing does on the reader.
Question 5 Skills · Ad Reading
[3 marks]
Think of any TV or online advertisement you have seen recently. Briefly describe it in 2 sentences, then identify one rhetorical technique (appeal, identification, glittering generality, testimonial, etc.) it uses and what effect that technique produces.
Question 6 Knowledge · Media Forms
[2 marks]
In one sentence each, name two conventions of each medium: (a) a TV news broadcast, (b) an Instagram post, (c) a podcast episode.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
How much do you trust the news and media you currently consume? Briefly explain what shapes your level of trust (or distrust) — and what you would need to know to be more confident in your judgment.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
