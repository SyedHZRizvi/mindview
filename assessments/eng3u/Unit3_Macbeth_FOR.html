Unit 3: Macbeth — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand B + A
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify what you already know (or do not yet know) about Shakespearean drama before starting Unit 3. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Prereq · Shakespeare
[3 marks]
What do you already know about Shakespeare's England — the world in which the plays were written and first performed? Write 2–3 sentences. (It is fine if you know little; be honest.)
Question 2 Prereq · Drama
[3 marks]
Define soliloquy, monologue, and aside. Give one short example sentence for any one of them.
Question 3 Prereq · Tragedy
[3 marks]
What makes a literary work a "tragedy"? Write 2–3 sentences. If you can name three features of tragedy (e.g., a tragic hero, a flaw, a fall), do.
Question 4 Skills · Iambic Pentameter
[3 marks]
"Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?" — Shakespeare, Sonnet 18
Mark the stressed and unstressed syllables in this line (use uppercase for stressed, lowercase for unstressed). What is the name of this meter?
Question 5 Skills · Inference
[3 marks]
"Fair is foul, and foul is fair: / Hover through the fog and filthy air." — the witches, opening of Macbeth (1.1.10–11)
Make one inference about the play's moral world based on these two lines. Identify one detail that supports the inference.
Question 6 Knowledge
[2 marks]
In Shakespeare's time, kings and nobles speak in blank verse (unrhymed iambic pentameter), while low-status characters and characters in moments of madness often speak in prose. Why might this difference be analytically important when reading a Shakespeare play?
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Have you read or watched Macbeth before? If yes, what do you remember finding interesting or confusing? If no, what about a 400-year-old play in Early Modern English do you most expect to find challenging?

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
