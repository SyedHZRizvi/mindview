Component 1: Annotated Bibliography (15%) [15 marks]
Submission 1
[15]
Submit your final annotated bibliography of at least five sources. Each entry includes: (a) full MLA-9 citation, (b) 2–3-sentence summary of the source's argument, (c) one sentence on how the source functions in your essay. At least three sources must be peer-reviewed scholarly material (journal article, university-press book); the others may include reputable journalism or primary documents.

Component 2: Research Paper / Sustained Essay (60%) [60 marks]
Submission 2
[60]
Submit your final research paper or sustained essay (1200–1800 words). The paper must: (a) answer your approved research question; (b) develop a clear, debatable thesis; (c) organise body sections around sub-claims, each supported by analysis of evidence (textual or research); (d) engage at least one counter-reading; (e) integrate at least 8 quotations or paraphrases with proper MLA-9 citation; (f) include a Works Cited list.

Component 3: Oral Presentation (20%) [20 marks]
Submission 3
[20]
Deliver a 10-minute oral presentation summarising your ISU thesis, sub-claims, and key evidence, followed by 5 minutes of Q&A. Slides may support the talk but should not replace it (six words per slide is a reasonable upper bound). Submit a copy of your slides or speaker notes.

Component 4: Self-Reflection (5%) [5 marks]
Submission 4
[5]
Write a 250–400-word self-reflection. Address: (a) what surprised you in the research; (b) which sub-claim you would strengthen if you had another two weeks; (c) which research skill you most need to develop before Grade 12; (d) one specific concession or counter-reading you took seriously and why.
