Unit 4: Short Stories & Poetry — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand B
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Identify what you already know about short fiction and poetry before starting Unit 4. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade.
Question 1 Prereq · POV
[3 marks]
Define and distinguish first-person, third-person limited, and third-person omniscient narration. Give one example story (any story you know) for any one of them.
Question 2 Skills · Inference
[3 marks]
"It was a queer, sultry summer, the summer they electrocuted the Rosenbergs, and I didn't know what I was doing in New York." — Sylvia Plath, opening of The Bell Jar
Make two inferences about the narrator based on this opening sentence. Identify the textual detail that supports each.
Question 3 Skills · Form
[3 marks]
What is the difference between a sonnet, free verse, and a haiku? Write one sentence on each.
Question 4 Skills · Theme
[3 marks]
Convert each of the following topics into a defensible theme statement (a debatable sentence): (a) "courage" (b) "memory" (c) "friendship". Use any story or poem you know.
Question 5 Knowledge · Devices
[2 marks]
Define enjambment and caesura. Use any one line of poetry you know as an example for either term.
Question 6 Skills · Compare
[3 marks]
Choose two short stories or poems you have read (in any class, at any time). Write one sentence stating the strongest single difference between them, and one sentence stating what that difference reveals about how each writer approaches storytelling or poetry.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which Unit 4 skill do you feel least confident about: identifying narrative point of view, reading poetic form (metre, rhyme, line breaks), producing theme statements from motifs, or writing comparative paragraphs? Explain what is unclear.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
