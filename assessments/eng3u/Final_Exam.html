Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[3]B · Reading
Define and give a one-line example of each: (a) verbal irony, (b) dramatic irony, (c) situational irony.
Answer Key

(a) Saying the opposite of what is meant ("Beautiful weather!" in a storm). (b) Audience knows what character does not (audience knows Macbeth's witches are dangerous; Macbeth does not yet). (c) Outcome opposite of expectation (a fire station burns down).

2
[3]B · Drama
Define soliloquy, identify Shakespeare's standard verse metre, and name two of Macbeth's soliloquies (with brief reference to the speech's opening words).
Answer Key

Soliloquy = speech delivered alone on stage, voicing inner thought to the audience. Shakespeare's verse = blank verse (unrhymed iambic pentameter). Examples: "If it were done when 'tis done" (1.7); "Is this a dagger which I see before me?" (2.1); "To be thus is nothing, but to be safely thus" (3.1); "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow" (5.5).

3
[3]B · Drama
Define hamartia, peripeteia, and anagnorisis. Briefly locate one example of each in Macbeth.
Answer Key

Hamartia — Macbeth names his own as "vaulting ambition" (1.7.27). Peripeteia — the killing of Macduff's family in 4.2 turns hunter into hunted. Anagnorisis — Macbeth's "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow" (5.5.19–28) or his recognition that Macduff was "from his mother's womb / Untimely ripp'd" (5.8.15–16).

4
[3]C · Writing
Name the four parts of a PEEL paragraph and the function of each. Then write one sentence on what distinguishes a strong analytical thesis from a topic statement.
Answer Key

PEEL: Point (topic sentence with debatable claim), Evidence (embedded quotation with citation), Explain (analysis of how the evidence supports the point), Link (closing sentence connecting to thesis or next paragraph). A thesis names what you will argue and at least gestures at how; a topic only names what the essay is about.

5
[3]C · Citation
Write the correct MLA-9 in-text citation for: (a) line 1.7.27 of Macbeth; (b) line 13 of a Shakespeare sonnet (citation form only); (c) page 102 of George Orwell's 1984. Then write the Works Cited entry for Orwell's 1984 (Secker and Warburg, 1949).
Answer Key

(a) (Shakespeare 1.7.27) or (Macbeth 1.7.27). (b) (Shakespeare 13) — line number for poetry. (c) (Orwell 102) — page number for prose. Works Cited entry: Orwell, George. 1984. Secker and Warburg, 1949.

6
[2]B · Poetry
A Shakespearean sonnet's rhyme scheme is:
Answer Key

B. Three quatrains in alternating rhyme + closing couplet.

7
[2]D · Media
"SIFT" (for source evaluation) stands for:
Answer Key

A. Caulfield's SIFT method.

8
[3]A · Oral / D
Identify Aristotle's three rhetorical appeals and define each in one sentence.
Answer Key

Ethos — appeal through speaker credibility/character. Pathos — appeal to audience emotion. Logos — appeal to logic, reason, evidence.

9
[3]D · Media
Define and give one short example each: bandwagon, testimonial, glittering generality.
Answer Key

Bandwagon: appeal to popularity ("Everyone is switching to this brand"). Testimonial: endorsement by a celebrity or authority. Glittering generality: vague positive language without specifics ("Vote for change," "Open happiness").

Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
10
[8]B · Comparative Passage Analysis
Passage A: "Two roads diverged in a yellow wood, / And sorry I could not travel both / And be one traveller, long I stood / And looked down one as far as I could / To where it bent in the undergrowth." — Robert Frost, "The Road Not Taken"
Passage B: "The fog comes / on little cat feet. // It sits looking / over harbor and city / on silent haunches / and then moves on." — Carl Sandburg, "Fog"
Compare and contrast these two short poems in one paragraph. Address: (i) the dominant figure of speech in each, (ii) the speaker's stance toward the natural world, (iii) the function of form (rhyme/metre or free verse). Aim for 8–10 sentences and end with a synthesis sentence neither poem could produce alone.
Answer Key

Sample synthesis sentence: "Frost's metered, rhymed quatrain stages choice as deliberation across time, while Sandburg's free-verse miniature stages the natural world as something that pauses, watches, and disappears without choosing — what the comparison reveals is that form decides what a poem can even ask about." Marks: 2 figures of speech, 2 stances, 2 form functions, 2 synthesis.

11
[8]B · Drama Analysis
"If it were done when 'tis done, then 'twere well / It were done quickly. If th' assassination / Could trammel up the consequence and catch / With his surcease, success: that but this blow / Might be the be-all and the end-all here…" — Macbeth (1.7.1–5)
Analyse this soliloquy opening. Refer to repetition, conditional grammar, diction, and what the syntax exposes about Macbeth's state of mind. Aim for 6–8 sentences.
Answer Key

Notice: (1) repetition of "done" four times — Macbeth tries to think of the murder as a single completed act; the repetition exposes the effort; (2) the "If… If…" conditionals carry the load — Macbeth has not committed, only imagined; (3) "assassination" is a Latinate, clinical noun (first recorded use in English) that distances Macbeth from the act; (4) the sibilance of "surcease, success" binds Duncan's death to Macbeth's rise; (5) "be-all and the end-all" treats the murder as if it could close itself — the play spends three acts proving this is impossible. Marks: 3 textual moves named, 3 interpretations, 2 communication.

12
[9]D · Media Analysis
A 30-second cola advertisement features a diverse group of friends laughing at a beach at sunset; one bottle of the brand is centred in the frame. Voiceover: "Open happiness." Analyse three rhetorical techniques the ad uses and explain how each produces its effect. Then explain why the product itself is small in the frame. Aim for 7–9 sentences.
Answer Key

Likely techniques: (1) identification (Burke) — the viewer is invited to recognise herself as the kind of person whose afternoons could look like this; (2) ethos — a 130-year-old brand name carries automatic credibility; (3) glittering generality — "Open happiness" is vague enough to deny falsification but specific enough to imply causation; (4) minimal logos — no claims about flavour or nutrition. The product is small in frame because the real product is identity. Marks: 3 techniques + 3 explanations + 2 "product small" reading + 1 communication.

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
13
[12]C · Literary Analysis Essay
Write one complete literary-analysis essay (4–5 paragraphs, ~500 words) on the following thesis: "In Macbeth, the motif of blood migrates from external violence to internal guilt without ever leaving the same word, refusing the boundary between moral and bodily contamination." Use PEEL structure throughout. Embed at least three quotations from across the play with correct act.scene.line citation, drawn from at least two different acts.
Answer Key

Look for: introduction with hook + thesis (2); 2–3 PEEL body paragraphs each with a sub-claim (6); 3 embedded quotations from at least two different acts, e.g., "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood / Clean from my hand?" (2.2.78–9), "A little water clears us of this deed" (2.2.86–7), "Out, damned spot!" (5.1.39); conclusion that widens the implication (2); conventions and MLA (2). Total 12.

14
[8]C · Argumentative Paragraph
Write one analytical PEEL paragraph (6–8 sentences) on a topic of your choice from current Ontario or Canadian public life (e.g., AI in classrooms, social media regulation, cellphone bans, voting age, healthcare). Use at least one piece of named evidence (real or invented), engage one counter-reading honestly with concession-and-refutation, and end with a sentence that re-establishes your claim.
Answer Key

Look for: clear topic sentence asserting a debatable claim (2); evidence integrated with attribution (2); honest counterargument engaged with concession + refutation (3); closing sentence that re-establishes the claim (1).

15
[5]C · Personal Voice
Write the opening of a personal essay (4–6 sentences) on the topic: "Something small that changed how I pay attention." Begin with a specific concrete moment; end with a sentence that gestures toward the larger reflection the rest of the essay would develop.
Answer Key

Look for: a specific concrete starting moment (not vague generalisation); sensory or precise detail; a clear narrative voice; a pivot from concrete to reflective in the final 1–2 sentences. Marks: 2 specificity, 1 voice, 1 pivot, 1 conventions.

Part D — Application [25 marks]
16
[8]B · Inference / Application
"My father was a fox farmer. That is, he raised silver foxes, in pens; and in the fall and early winter, when their fur was prime, he killed them and skinned them and sold their pelts." — Alice Munro, opening of "Boys and Girls"
Apply what you have learned about close reading. Make three inferences about the narrator and her family based on this single opening sentence. For each inference, identify the textual detail that supports it and explain the reasoning. Aim for 6–8 sentences.
Answer Key

Likely inferences: (1) the narrator already identifies herself by reference to her father — supported by the sentence's first words "My father was…", in which her self-introduction is via him; (2) the narrator reports from between two worlds — supported by the corrective "That is", which assumes a reader unfamiliar with farm life; (3) violence has been normalised as ordinary labour — supported by the matter-of-fact verb sequence "killed them and skinned them and sold." Marks: 3 inferences × 2 (inference + supporting detail), 2 communication.

17
[8]A / B · Performance Analysis
Apply what you have learned about performance reading. Describe how a director could stage Lady Macbeth's "unsex me here" speech (1.5.40–55) to read either as (a) a declaration of cruelty or (b) a confession of need. For each, name two specific staging choices (lighting, blocking, gesture, voice, pacing) and explain what those choices make the speech mean. Aim for 7–9 sentences total.
Answer Key

Strong responses give two specific choices per reading. (a) Confident reading: high-angle lighting, hard side-light, steady voice, expansive gestures, brisk pacing — reads as a queen taking command. (b) Vulnerable reading: low single light, kneeling blocking, voice that drops in volume on "fill," held silence after "unsex me here" — reads as a prayer of someone without what she is asking for. Marks: 2 × 2 = 4 staging choices, 2 readings × 2 = 4 connecting choices to meaning.

18
[9]D · Real-World Media Literacy
Real-World Application: A friend forwards you a viral social-media post claiming a Canadian celebrity has died, linking to a website you have never heard of. In 6–8 sentences, walk through the steps you would take to evaluate the claim before sharing. Reference at least three specific techniques from Unit 6 (SIFT, lateral reading, source labels, framing, news vs. opinion vs. sponsored, etc.).
Answer Key

Strong responses apply SIFT and lateral reading: (1) Stop — notice the urge to share; (2) Investigate — open new tabs and check the site's reputation; (3) Find better coverage — search the celebrity's name on CBC, Reuters, BBC; if no reputable outlet reports it, the claim is unverified; (4) Trace claims — find the original source of any quote or screenshot. Notice framing devices ("Just in!"), check whether the post distinguishes news from opinion. Marks: 3 techniques named and applied (6); a clear procedural sequence (2); communication (1).
