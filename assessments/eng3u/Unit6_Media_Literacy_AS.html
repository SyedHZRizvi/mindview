Topic 6.1 — Media Conventions
Question 1
Marshall McLuhan's slogan "the medium is the message" argues that:
Solution: McLuhan's claim is that the form is content. A news story rendered as a 30-second TikTok and a 4,000-word feature article are not the same story in different containers; they are different stories. Strong media analysis takes the form itself as content.
Question 2
A typical newscast frames the anchor at eye level, in centre frame, with soft three-point lighting. These choices primarily code:
Solution: The convention is the credibility appeal. "Objectivity" in television news is, technically, a particular set of visual codes. Becoming aware of those codes is the first step toward asking what they hide.
Question 3
An affordance in media studies is:
Solution: Affordances are what a medium makes possible or impossible. Naming the affordances of a platform is the move that turns a media essay from description ("the post is short") into analysis ("the post is short because TikTok's affordances reward density of attention and punish complexity").
Topic 6.2 — News and Bias
Question 4
"Bias" in news reporting is best understood as:
Solution: All news is selective; there is no neutral choice about which stories to cover. The Grade 11 media-literate reader does not look for sources without bias — she looks for sources whose biases she can identify, whose factual claims she can verify, and whose method she can hold to a standard.
Question 5
The most reliable evaluation technique is:
Solution: Lateral reading shifts the question from "does this site look trustworthy?" — a design question that is cheap to fake — to "what do independent sources say about this site?" — an evidence question that is much harder to fake.
Question 6
"False balance" is:
Solution: False balance treats unequal claims as if they were equally well supported. A news story that presents "scientists who study climate change" and "people who deny climate change" as two equivalent sides has done false balance. Recognising it is part of media literacy.
Topic 6.3 — Rhetorical Appeals
Question 7
Aristotle's three classical rhetorical appeals are:
Solution: Logos, ethos, pathos — Aristotle's three appeals, still the standard vocabulary for rhetorical analysis. Every persuasive media text combines at least two of these; identifying which appeals are doing the work is the first analytical move.
Question 8
Kenneth Burke's concept of identification in persuasion claims that:
Solution: Burke's identification — modern advertising's most powerful technique — sidesteps scepticism because it never asks the viewer to evaluate a claim, only to recognise herself in a scene. An ad rarely says "buy this"; it stages a scene the viewer wants to be inside and offers the product as the entry ticket.
Question 9
A "glittering generality" is:
Solution: Glittering generalities are vague positive abstractions used in propaganda and advertising. They feel meaningful but resist being tested against evidence — exactly the function the speaker wants.
Topic 6.4 — Media Production
Question 10
Strong media production begins with:
Solution: The brief is the production document. Every later choice — music, framing, length, captions — should be answerable back to the brief. Starting with the camera before the brief is the most common reason student media projects miss their purpose.
Question 11
Captions on a media production are:
Solution: Captions are accessibility, not afterthought. They also catch transcription errors no other editing pass catches — "captioning is editorial proofreading by other means." High-contrast typography is part of the same accessibility commitment.
Question 12
Under Canadian fair dealing, a student can use short excerpts of copyrighted material for educational purposes, but those rights end the moment:
Solution: Canadian fair dealing covers limited educational and review use. Publishing a student project on a public platform in a way that could compete with the original (e.g., uploading a copyrighted song under your own video) is outside fair dealing. Use royalty-free libraries or seek explicit permission for any borrowed clip.
