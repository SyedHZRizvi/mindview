Topic 4.1–4.2 — Supply Chain & Processing
Question 1
Which Canadian agency enforces the Safe Food for Canadians Act?
Solution: The CFIA enforces the Safe Food for Canadians Act (2012) and Regulations (2019). Health Canada sets the food-safety standards and nutrition policy; CFIA enforces them.
Question 2
In the NOVA classification system, ultra-processed foods are which group?
Solution: Group 4. Industrial formulations with substances rarely used in home kitchens.
Question 3
Which is an example of mandatory food fortification in Canada?
Solution: Mandatory: iodine in salt (1949), vitamin D in milk and margarine (1970s), folic acid + thiamin + riboflavin in white flour (1998). Calcium in OJ is voluntary fortification.
Topic 4.3 — Cooking Methods
Question 4
The Maillard reaction happens at approximately what temperature?
Solution: ~140 °C is the threshold for the browning reactions between amino acids and reducing sugars — characteristic of dry-heat cooking (baking, roasting, searing).
Question 5
Which cooking method retains the most vitamin C in broccoli?
Solution: Steaming retains ~85% of vitamin C vs ~45% for boiling. Microwaving is comparable to steaming. The water-soluble vitamin leaches into discarded cooking water during boiling.
Topic 4.4–4.5 — Foodborne Illness & the Four Cs
Question 6
The "temperature danger zone" is:
Solution: 4–60 °C — pathogenic bacteria multiply most rapidly. Refrigerate ≤ 4 °C; cook to safe internal temperature ≥ 60 °C (and usually well above).
Question 7
The safe internal cooking temperature for ground beef (in °C) is:
Solution: 71 °C for ground beef, pork, lamb. Chicken/turkey + ground poultry + leftovers/casseroles 74 °C. Whole cuts of beef/pork 63 °C with 3-minute rest.
Question 8
Which pathogen grows even at refrigerator temperature (4 °C) and is particularly dangerous in pregnancy?
Solution: Listeria can grow at 4 °C. It contaminates deli meats and soft cheeses; in pregnancy can cause miscarriage and stillbirth. Maple Leaf 2008 outbreak: 23 deaths.
Question 9
A meal sits at room temperature for 3 hours after cooking. According to the two-hour rule:
Solution: Discard. Bacteria multiply rapidly in 4–60 °C; some (e.g., Staphylococcus aureus) produce toxins that survive reheating. Refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour if > 32 °C).
Topic 4.6 — Food Preservation
Question 10
Low-acid vegetables like green beans must be home-preserved using:
Solution: Pressure canning at ~116 °C destroys Clostridium botulinum spores. Water-bath canning at 100 °C is only sufficient for high-acid foods (pH < 4.6). Home-canning low-acid foods without a pressure canner is the leading cause of botulism in Canada.
Question 11
Freezing food at −18 °C does which of the following?
Solution: Freezing pauses bacterial growth but does not kill bacteria. Once thawed, bacteria resume multiplying — so thaw in fridge (not on counter) and use promptly.
