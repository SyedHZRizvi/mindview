Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [14 marks]
1
[3]
List the organs of the GI tract from mouth to anus, in order. Identify the three accessory organs that contribute digestive secretions.
Answer Key

Order: mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine (cecum, colon, rectum) → anus. Accessory organs: salivary glands, liver (with gallbladder), pancreas.

2
[2]
The pancreas secretes:
Answer Key

C. Pancreatic juice contains pancreatic amylase, lipase, trypsin/chymotrypsin (as zymogens), and bicarbonate ions to neutralise gastric acid in the duodenum.

3
[3]
Describe the structure of villi and microvilli. How do they enhance absorption?
Answer Key

Villi are finger-like projections (~1 mm tall) lining the small intestine; microvilli are smaller hair-like extensions on the apical (gut-facing) surface of each villus epithelial cell, forming the "brush border". Together they multiply absorptive surface area to ~250 m². Each villus contains a blood capillary (for water-soluble nutrients) and a lymphatic lacteal (for lipids), allowing direct uptake into circulation.

4
[3]
Trace the three stages of cellular respiration. State the location and key product of each.
Answer Key

(1) Glycolysis — cytoplasm — glucose → 2 pyruvate + 2 ATP + 2 NADH. (2) Pyruvate oxidation + citric-acid cycle — mitochondrial matrix — 6 CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂ per glucose. (3) Electron transport chain + chemiosmosis — inner mitochondrial membrane — ~26 ATP using H+ gradient; O₂ is final electron acceptor → H₂O.

5
[3]
Define basal metabolic rate (BMR), thermic effect of food (TEF), and physical-activity energy expenditure (PAEE). Express their typical contribution to total daily energy expenditure (TDEE).
Answer Key

BMR — energy used by a fasting, resting body at thermal neutrality; 60–75% of TDEE in sedentary adults. TEF — energy cost of digestion/absorption/storage; ~10% of intake. PAEE — energy for movement (exercise + NEAT); 15–40% depending on activity. TDEE = BMR + TEF + PAEE.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [12 marks]
6
[4]
Calculate BMR for a 72 kg, 178 cm, 17-year-old male using Mifflin–St Jeor, then estimate his TDEE assuming a 1.55 activity factor (moderately active).
Answer Key

Male BMR = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5 = 10(72) + 6.25(178) − 5(17) + 5 = 720 + 1 112.5 − 85 + 5 = 1 752.5 kcal/day. TDEE = 1 752.5 × 1.55 ≈ 2 716 kcal/day.

7
[4]
A student tests how cooking temperature affects starch digestion by salivary amylase. She has cooked potato (gelatinised starch) and raw potato (crystalline starch). Predict which digests faster and explain.
Answer Key

Cooked potato will be digested faster because heat gelatinises starch granules — water enters the granules, they swell and rupture, exposing many α-1,4 glycosidic bonds to amylase. Raw potato's tightly-packed crystalline structure resists enzyme access (it is, in part, "resistant starch"). She can test by adding amylase to equal portions of each and observing the time until glucose is detectable with a Benedict's test or a glucometer.

8
[4]
A patient with a non-functional gallbladder has bile dripping continuously into the duodenum rather than being released in pulses. Predict the effect on (a) digestion of a small low-fat meal and (b) digestion of a single high-fat meal. Suggest dietary adjustments.
Answer Key

(a) Small low-fat meal: bile trickling is enough; digestion is essentially normal. (b) Large high-fat meal: bile trickle cannot match the sudden lipid load → incomplete emulsification → undigested fat reaches colon → steatorrhoea (fatty diarrhoea). Adjustments: spread fat across several smaller meals; prefer moderate-fat sources; allow weeks for the enterohepatic system to adapt.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[4]
Explain the role of the gut microbiome in human physiology. Reference at least three functions and define dysbiosis.
Answer Key

~\(10^{14}\) bacteria in the colon. Functions: (1) ferment dietary fibre into short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate) that supply ~10% of daily energy and feed colonocytes; (2) synthesise vitamin K, biotin, and small amounts of B12; (3) outcompete pathogens; (4) modulate immune function. Dysbiosis = disturbed microbial composition associated with IBS, IBD, and (in mouse models) obesity and depression.

10
[4]
Construct a labeled diagram (description acceptable) of a villus showing epithelial cells with microvilli, the central capillary network, the lacteal, goblet cells, and the lamina propria. Indicate the destination of absorbed glucose, amino acids, and long-chain fatty acids.
Answer Key

Villus: finger-like fold of mucosa lined by columnar epithelial cells whose apical surface bears microvilli (brush border). Goblet cells secrete protective mucus. Lamina propria fills the core. A capillary network drains into the hepatic-portal vein (carrying absorbed glucose and amino acids to the liver). A central lacteal (lymphatic vessel) collects chylomicrons containing long-chain fatty acids, joining the thoracic duct and entering venous blood near the heart.

11
[4]
Compare and contrast aerobic respiration and anaerobic (lactic-acid) fermentation in a 3-row table covering: oxygen requirement, ATP yield per glucose, by-products, and biological situation in human muscle.
Answer Key

Aerobic: requires O₂; ~30 ATP/glucose; CO₂ + H₂O; sustained activity. Anaerobic fermentation: no O₂; 2 ATP/glucose; lactate; intense short bursts when O₂ delivery cannot keep up. Both share the glycolysis step; aerobic adds pyruvate oxidation + Krebs + ETC. Lactate accumulation contributes to muscle fatigue and the burning sensation; it is cleared by the liver (Cori cycle).

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[4]
Case Study — Probiotic claim. A yogurt label promises "billions of probiotics for digestive health". Critically evaluate this claim using Strand A research-and-inquiry skills.
Answer Key

Probiotics are defined as live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host. "Billions" sounds impressive but probiotic effects are strain-specific and dose-specific. Health Canada permits the claim "contributes to healthy gut flora" for products with specified strains. Critical questions: (1) Are the strains listed (e.g., Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG)? (2) Was the dose tested in clinical trials? (3) Is the bacteria still alive by the best-before date? (4) Is the labelled benefit ("digestive health") supported by RCTs in healthy people, or only in those with antibiotic-associated diarrhoea? Anecdotal "feels better" is not RCT-level evidence.

13
[4]
Case Study — Weight maintenance. A 17-year-old male athlete has BMR 1 800 kcal/day and an activity factor of 1.7 (vigorous training). He currently eats 2 800 kcal/day. Is he in caloric surplus, deficit, or maintenance? Predict the rate of weight change in g/week (use 7 700 kcal = 1 kg fat).
Answer Key

TDEE = 1 800 × 1.7 = 3 060 kcal/day. He eats 2 800 → deficit of 260 kcal/day. Weekly deficit = 260 × 7 = 1 820 kcal/week. Predicted weight loss = 1 820 / 7 700 ≈ 0.24 kg/week (≈ 240 g/week). If muscle gain is the goal, he should add ~500 kcal/day (with adequate protein) to reach modest surplus instead.

14
[4]
STSE — Lactose intolerance. Explain the molecular cause of lactose intolerance, what happens in the gut when undigested lactose reaches the colon, and the role of commercial lactase supplements.
Answer Key

Lactase is a brush-border enzyme that hydrolyses lactose (glucose + galactose) for absorption in the small intestine. Lactase non-persistence (the genetic norm in much of the world) reduces lactase expression after weaning; undigested lactose passes to the colon, where bacteria ferment it producing gas (CO₂, H₂, methane) and lactic acid; lactose's osmotic load also draws water into the colon, producing diarrhoea. Symptoms: bloating, gas, cramps, diarrhoea 30 min–2 h after dairy. Lactase enzyme supplements (drops or tablets) hydrolyse lactose ahead of bacterial fermentation, allowing dairy consumption. Lactose-free dairy products are pre-treated with lactase.
