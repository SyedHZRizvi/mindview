Topic 2.1 — Active Citizenship Beyond Voting
Question 1
A "deputation" to a municipal council is best described as:
Solution: A deputation is a procedural address delivered to a council, committee, or board. In most Ontario municipalities you register in advance, get a fixed time slot (usually 5 minutes), and may be asked questions by council members.
Question 2
"Civil disobedience" is best defined as:
Solution: Civil disobedience requires public, non-violent action, willingness to accept legal consequence, targeting of an unjust law or policy, and (usually) exhaustion of ordinary channels first. Examples: Thoreau's tax refusal; Martin Luther King Jr.'s Birmingham campaign; sit-ins during the Civil Rights movement.
Topic 2.2 — Movements that Changed Canada
Question 3
The 1929 "Persons Case" ruled that:
Solution: The Famous Five (Murphy, McClung, Parlby, Edwards, McKinney) won at the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London, which articulated the "living tree" doctrine that the Constitution can grow within natural limits.
Question 4
Tommy Douglas is most associated with:
Solution: Douglas, as Premier of Saskatchewan from 1944 to 1961, introduced universal hospital insurance and then medicare. He was voted "The Greatest Canadian" in a 2004 CBC nationwide poll. The federal Medical Care Act of 1966 extended medicare nationally.
Topic 2.3 — Media Literacy
Question 5
The SIFT mnemonic for evaluating sources stands for:
Solution: Caulfield's SIFT method emphasises lateral reading — leaving a suspect source to check it against other sources, rather than only analysing it in isolation.
Question 6
The difference between misinformation and disinformation is:
Solution: The distinction is about intent. A parent forwarding a sincerely believed rumour is spreading misinformation; a state-backed influence campaign is spreading disinformation. Propaganda is a third category — agenda-driven information, possibly true but selectively framed.
Topic 2.4 — Indigenous-Crown Relations
Question 7
Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982 does which of the following?
Solution: Section 35 explicitly names First Nations, Métis, and Inuit as the Aboriginal peoples of Canada and protects their existing rights — including treaty rights. It sits outside the Charter (which is Part I); section 35 is Part II of the 1982 Act.
Question 8
The Truth and Reconciliation Commission produced how many Calls to Action in 2015?
Solution: The TRC's 94 Calls to Action cover child welfare, education, language and culture, health, justice, reconciliation, museums, missing children, media, business, sports, and newcomer-to-Canada orientation.
Topic 2.5 — Community Partnership
Question 9
"Nothing about us, without us" is best understood as:
Solution: Originating in the disability-rights movement and widely adopted as a community-partnership rule, the slogan insists that lived experience cannot be replaced by good intentions; affected people must be at the design table from the start.
Question 10
A "stakeholder map" in civic-action planning is:
Solution: Stakeholder mapping forces the planner to ask who is affected, who has formal decision power, who has informal influence, and who has lived experience — before designing actions.
Topic 2.6 — Civic-Action Planning
Question 11
A "specific ask" in a civic-action plan should:
Solution: Decision-makers can act on specific asks ("approve $5,000 in the parks budget for 100 new trees"). They cannot act on vague calls ("do better"). The discipline of writing a specific ask is the difference between a serious civic-action plan and a wish list.
Question 12
"Reflective practice" in civic action means:
Solution: Civic engagement is a habit, not an event. Reflection turns a one-time project into a sustainable practice — without it, the same mistakes recur and the learning does not transfer.
