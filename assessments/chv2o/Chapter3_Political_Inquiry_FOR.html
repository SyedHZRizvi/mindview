Chapter 3: Political Inquiry Skills — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand A
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Map your current research and source-evaluation skills before Chapter 3. Teacher feedback only — no grade. Be honest about what is unclear.
Question 1 Inquiry
[3 marks]
Look at this weak inquiry question: "Is fast food bad?" Without doing research, rewrite it as a stronger civic-inquiry question that is open-ended, bounded, contestable, and significant.
Question 2 Sources
[3 marks]
You're researching how many Canadians voted in the most recent federal election. Name three Canadian sources you would consult and explain (briefly) why each is credible.
Question 3 Primary/Secondary
[2 marks]
In your own words, what is the difference between a primary source and a secondary source? Give one example of each, related to any historical or current event you choose.
Question 4 Poll Analysis
[3 marks]
"A new poll shows 64% of Ontarians support the policy."
List four questions you would ask before treating this number as reliable evidence.
Question 5 Argument
[3 marks]
Define a "logical fallacy" in your own words and name two common fallacies (e.g., ad hominem, strawman, false equivalence) with a one-sentence example of each.
Question 6 Communication
[3 marks]
What is the difference between an op-ed and a news article? Give one civic situation where each would be the right form.
Question 7 Reflection
[ungraded]
Of these skills, which do you most want to improve in Chapter 3: formulating questions, finding sources, judging source credibility, analysing evidence, drawing conclusions, or communicating conclusions? Be specific so your teacher can target instruction.

Teacher Feedback

Your teacher will provide descriptive feedback after reviewing your responses.
