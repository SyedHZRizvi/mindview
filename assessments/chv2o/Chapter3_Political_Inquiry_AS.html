Topic 3.1 — Inquiry Questions
Question 1
Which of the following is the strongest inquiry question?
Solution: Strong inquiry questions are open-ended, bounded (Ontario, municipal, voting age 16), contestable (informed people disagree), and significant (real policy debate). (a) is yes/no with an obvious answer; (b) is fact-recall; (d) is personal preference.
Question 2
The political inquiry process recommended by the Ontario curriculum has five stages, in order:
Solution: The five-stage process is iterative — new evidence can require reformulating the question, which is why the process is often drawn as a circle, not a straight line.
Topic 3.2 — Source Credibility
Question 3
A primary source for the 1982 patriation of the Canadian Constitution would be:
Solution: A primary source is created at the time and place of the event. The other choices are secondary — interpretations after the fact.
Question 4
"CRAAP" stands for:
Solution: CRAAP is a librarian's mnemonic for source evaluation: when was it published, does it address my question, who wrote it, are the claims supported, and what is the apparent purpose?
Topic 3.3 — The Four Concepts of Political Thinking
Question 5
The four concepts of political thinking in the Ontario curriculum are:
Solution: These are the disciplinary thinking tools civics students use to analyse any issue. The four categories in choice (d) are the achievement-chart categories — a different framework, applied across all subjects.
Question 6
Distinguishing "objectives" from "results" is the discipline of:
Solution: Intended consequences and actual consequences diverge often. A policy can fail to achieve its declared objective and still be defended on other grounds; a citizen who can distinguish the two is harder to mislead.
Topic 3.4 — Drawing Conclusions
Question 7
"Steel-manning" an argument means:
Solution: Steel-manning is the opposite of strawmanning. It produces conclusions that opponents find more persuasive because the inquirer engaged the best counterargument honestly.
Question 8
Including a falsifying condition in a conclusion means:
Solution: Naming your own falsifying conditions protects against motivated reasoning. It is what informed citizens do that ideologues do not.
Topic 3.5 — Communicating Conclusions
Question 9
A typical op-ed is approximately:
Solution: The op-ed standard is 700–1000 words. A deputation is about 5 minutes spoken (700–900 words). An infographic must communicate in under 30 seconds of viewing.
Question 10
A formal council deputation should:
Solution: The deputation is not a debate — it is a structured presentation. The 5-minute time limit is strict in most Ontario municipalities; practice with a stopwatch before you deliver.
