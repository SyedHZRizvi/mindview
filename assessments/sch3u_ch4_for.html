🔍 Chapter 4: Solutions and Solubility

Assessment FOR Learning — Pre-Chapter Diagnostic
📋 Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Check what you remember about polarity, IMFs (Ch 1), and stoichiometry / mole conversions (Ch 3) before tackling Ch 4.
Prerequisite Skills (Ch 1 + Ch 3)
Question 1 [2 marks]
What is the meaning of "(aq)" in a chemical equation?
Reveal expected answer
"Dissolved in water" / aqueous solution. The substance is broken up into solvated ions or molecules surrounded by water.
Question 2 [2 marks]
Predict whether each will dissolve well in water: (a) salt (NaCl); (b) sugar (C₆H₁₂O₆); (c) vegetable oil; (d) octane (C₈H₁₈). Justify briefly.
Reveal expected answer
(a) yes — ionic, hydrates. (b) yes — polar/H-bonding. (c) no — non-polar. (d) no — non-polar.
Question 3 [2 marks]
If 100 mL of solvent contains 5.0 g of solute, what is the % m/v concentration?
Reveal expected answer
5.0%. (5.0 g per 100 mL = 5.0% m/v by definition.)
Question 4 [2 marks]
Convert 250 mL to L; convert 0.0200 L to mL.
Reveal expected answer
0.250 L; 20.0 mL.
Question 5 [2 marks]
How many moles in 5.85 g of NaCl? (M=58.44 g/mol.)
Reveal expected answer
n = 5.85/58.44 = 0.100 mol.
Question 6 [3 marks]
What is an acid and what is a base in everyday language? Give one example of each from the kitchen or bathroom.
Reveal expected answer
Acids taste sour, turn blue litmus red — e.g., vinegar (acetic acid), lemon juice (citric acid). Bases feel slippery, turn red litmus blue — e.g., baking soda, household ammonia, soap.
Question 7 [2 marks]
If pH = 4, is the solution acidic, basic, or neutral?
Reveal expected answer
Acidic (pH < 7).
