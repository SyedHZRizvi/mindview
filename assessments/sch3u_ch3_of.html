Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [15 marks]
Question 1 [2 marks]
Calculate the molar mass of Mg(NO₃)₂. (M: Mg = 24.31, N = 14.01, O = 16.00.)
Solution:
24.31 + 2(14.01 + 3·16.00) = 24.31 + 2(62.01) = 148.33 g/mol.

Question 4 [4 marks]

Part B: Thinking & Inquiry [15 marks]
Question 6 [5 marks]

Question 7 [5 marks]
5.00 g of zinc reacts with 25.0 mL of 1.50 mol/L HCl: \(\text{Zn} + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\). (a) Identify the limiting reactant. (b) Calculate the mass of H₂ produced.
Question 8 [5 marks]
In a lab, 10.0 g of Mg burns in excess O₂ to give MgO. A student isolates 14.5 g of MgO. Calculate the theoretical yield and the percent yield. Suggest one source of error if percent yield is below 100%.

Question 14 [4 marks]
Iron is produced industrially by reducing iron(III) oxide with carbon monoxide: \(\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \rightarrow 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2\). What mass of iron can be produced from 1000 kg of Fe₂O₃ (M=159.69), assuming 92% yield?
Question 15 [4 marks]

Question 16 [3 marks]
A chemist analyzes a sample of an iron-oxide ore and finds it contains 52.0% Fe by mass. Predict whether the empirical formula is FeO (77.7% Fe) or Fe₂O₃ (69.9% Fe), and explain the discrepancy from the measured value.

Complete Answer Key

Q1. 148.33 g/mol.

Q2. n = 36.0/18.02 = 1.998 mol; N = n × N_A = 1.998 × 6.022×10²³ = 1.20 × 10²⁴ molecules.

Q3. M = 132.15 g/mol. %N = 28.02/132.15 × 100 = 21.20%; %H = 8.08/132.15 × 100 = 6.11%; %S = 32.07/132.15 × 100 = 24.27%; %O = 64.00/132.15 × 100 = 48.43%.

Q4. In 100 g: K = 26.6/39.10 = 0.680 mol; Cr = 35.4/52.00 = 0.681 mol; O = 38.0/16.00 = 2.375 mol. Divide by smallest (0.680): K = 1.00; Cr = 1.00; O = 3.49 ≈ 3.5. Multiply by 2: K₂Cr₂O₇ → empirical formula K₂Cr₂O₇ (potassium dichromate).

Q5. Empirical mass CH = 13.02 g/mol. 78.0/13.02 ≈ 6. Molecular formula = C₆H₆ (benzene).

Q6. n(CH₄) = 8.00/16.05 = 0.4984 mol → n(CO₂) = 0.4984 mol → m(CO₂) = 0.4984 × 44.01 = 21.9 g.

Q7. n(Zn) = 5.00/65.38 = 0.0765 mol; n(HCl) = 0.0250 × 1.50 = 0.0375 mol. Zn needs 2× HCl = 0.153 mol but only 0.0375 mol HCl available → HCl is limiting. n(H₂) = 0.0375/2 = 0.01875 mol; m(H₂) = 0.01875 × 2.016 = 0.0378 g.

Q8. n(Mg) = 10.0/24.31 = 0.4114 mol → n(MgO) = 0.4114 mol → theoretical = 0.4114 × 40.31 = 16.58 g. Percent yield = 14.5/16.58 × 100 = 87.5%. Loss possible during transfer; incomplete combustion; not all Mg oxidized.

Q9. Standard definitions; see lesson notes.

Q10. Mass is conserved → product mass ≤ reactant mass. Causes: (a) water/solvent not fully removed from product; (b) impurities/by-products counted as product.

Q11. Mole ratio = ratio of coefficients in balanced equation. Mass ratios fail because atoms of different elements have different masses (1 mol of H ≠ 1 mol of Cl in grams).

Q12. Final answer's precision limited by least precise input. E.g., 5.0 g / 18.02 g/mol = 0.28 mol (only 2 sig figs, not 0.27746).

Q13. (a) %N = 8.45/25.0 × 100 = 33.8%. (b) %N in NH₄NO₃ = 28.02/80.05 × 100 = 35.0%. Mass NH₄NO₃ providing 8.45 g N = 8.45/0.350 = 24.1 g.

Q14. n(Fe₂O₃) = 1.000×10⁶/159.69 = 6262 mol → n(Fe) = 2 × 6262 = 12 524 mol → m(Fe) theoretical = 12 524 × 55.85 = 6.99×10⁵ g = 699 kg. Actual = 0.92 × 699 = 643 kg.

Q15. n(N₂) = 80.0/22.4 = 3.571 mol. From 2NaN₃ → 3N₂, mole ratio: n(NaN₃) = (2/3)(3.571) = 2.381 mol. Mass = 2.381 × 65.01 = 155 g.

Q16. 52.0% is between FeO (77.7%) and Fe₂O₃ (69.9%). Both are too high — sample likely contains Fe₃O₄ (72.4% Fe) plus non-iron mineral impurities (silica, etc.) that dilute the iron content below either pure-oxide value.
