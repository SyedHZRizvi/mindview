🔍 Unit 2: Control Flow and Logic — Pre-Unit Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning — Teacher Feedback
📋 Not Graded — Descriptive Feedback Only
Purpose: Your teacher uses this to check your readiness for Unit 2 — whether you remember Unit 1 essentials and can reason about logic and repetition before we add formal Python control structures.
Question 1 — Unit 1 review (variables and I/O)
In one sentence each, what does input() return, and why must we usually wrap it with int() or float()?
Question 2 — Truth table
Fill in the truth table. Use T or F.
A | B | (A and B) | (A or B) | (not A) T | T | ? | ? | ? T | F | ? | ? | ? F | T | ? | ? | ? F | F | ? | ? | ?
Question 3 — English to logic
A driver may take their full G licence test if (a) they are at least 18 years old, AND (b) they have held a G2 for at least 12 months OR have completed a Ministry-approved driving course. Write this rule in compact logical form using the variables age, g2_months, and course.
Question 4 — Predict a repetition
Without writing code, predict the result.
"Start with the number 1. Repeatedly double it. When does it first reach 1000?" State the number of doublings and the final value.
Question 5 — Decision tree
A grocery checkout: 10% discount for orders over $100; 5% discount for orders $50–100; otherwise no discount. List the conditions in the order you would check them and explain why the order matters.
Question 6 — Sketch a loop in English
In plain English, describe a procedure that finds the largest of three numbers entered by the user. (No code yet.)
Question 7 — Spot the missing check
A program asks for a "test mark" and uses it directly. What input values should be rejected before the program does any arithmetic? List as many edge cases as you can think of.
Question 8 — Confidence check
Rate your confidence (1=none, 5=expert) on each:
  • Writing an if/else with the right comparison
  • Combining conditions with and / or / not
  • Tracing a loop on paper
  • Choosing between for and while
  • Spotting a bug in someone else's code
