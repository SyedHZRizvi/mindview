Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[3]A · RIASEC
Define and give one career example for each of the six Holland (RIASEC) interest types.
Answer Key: R-Realistic — hands-on (electrician); I-Investigative — analytical (biologist); A-Artistic — creative (graphic designer); S-Social — helping (nurse); E-Enterprising — leading/persuading (entrepreneur); C-Conventional — structured (accountant). 0.5 mark per type + example.
2
[2]A · Mindset
Carol Dweck's "growth mindset" is the belief that:
Answer Key: Option 2. Growth mindset = ability is developed. It contrasts with fixed mindset. It is a method, not a slogan.
3
[2]A · Time
In Covey's Time Management Matrix, the quadrant most determinative of life quality is:
Answer Key: Q2. Planning, deep practice, exercise, sleep, relationships. Time spent in Q2 prevents Q1 crises.
4
[3]A · Stress
Briefly describe the Lazarus & Folkman transactional model of stress, and name two interventions that follow from it.
Answer Key: Stress = demand × appraisal × resources. Interventions: (1) change appraisal (cognitive reframing — "this test is data, not a verdict"); (2) build resources (study earlier, ask for help, restore sleep, find a tutor, get a study partner). 1 mark each element + 0.5 each intervention.
5
[3]B · Communication
Distinguish synchronous from asynchronous workplace communication. Give one workplace scenario each is preferable for.
Answer Key: Synchronous (call, video, in-person) — nuance, sensitive disagreement, decisions needing alignment. Asynchronous (email, chat, doc) — records, status updates, instructions to refer back to. 1 mark per definition + 0.5 per example.
6
[2]B · Teamwork
Tuckman's stages of group development in order are:
Answer Key: Option 1. Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.
7
[2]B · Law
The three worker rights guaranteed under the Ontario Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) are:
Answer Key: Option 2. OHSA three rights: know, participate, refuse.
8
[3]C · Pathways
Name and briefly describe the five Ontario postsecondary destinations.
Answer Key: Apprenticeship (paid on-the-job + in-class, Certificate of Qualification); College (1–4 year applied programs); University (3–4 year academic + graduate); Workplace (direct employment); Community living (supported community participation). 0.5 per destination + 0.5 description × 5 = 3 marks weighted.
9
[2]C · Goals
SMART goals stand for:
Answer Key: Option 2. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
10
[3]C · Money
Define each: (a) OSAP, (b) RESP + CESG, (c) the "debt ≤ first-year salary" rule.
Answer Key: (a) OSAP — Ontario Student Assistance Program; mixed loan + grant based on income. (b) RESP + CESG — Registered Education Savings Plan; gains tax-sheltered; CESG matches 20% on first $2,500/year of contributions ($500/yr, $7,200 lifetime). (c) Rule of thumb: total student-loan debt at graduation ideally ≤ expected first-year-after-graduation salary. 1 mark each.
Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
11
[5]A · Self-Assessment Critique
A classmate says: "I took one online RIASEC quiz and it told me I should be a graphic designer. So that's my career."
Critique this reasoning. Identify two flaws and recommend a better approach.
Answer Key: Flaws: single-source result (no triangulation); treating output as a verdict rather than a working hypothesis. Better: multiple assessments (RIASEC, skills, values, learning preferences) + external feedback + informational interviews + low-stakes tests (club, job-shadow). The result is a hypothesis to refine, not a label. 1 per flaw + 3 marks for substantive recommendation.
12
[5]B · Team Diagnosis
A four-person Grade-11 project team has met five times. Members are friendly. Almost no work has been done. Deadlines approach.
Using Tuckman and Belbin, diagnose the situation and recommend three specific actions to move the team forward.
Answer Key: Diagnosis: stuck in Forming, never reached Norming; possibly homogeneous in Belbin roles (e.g., all Teamworkers — friendly but no Coordinator/Shaper driving). Actions: (1) Explicit Norming meeting — deliverable scope, individual tasks, deadlines, communication norms; (2) Volunteer/assign a Coordinator role to drive the next two weeks; (3) Use a shared task tracker so commitments are visible; (4) Schedule weekly check-ins with one thing going well and one stuck point. 2 marks diagnosis + 1 each action × 3.
13
[5]B · Ethics
Tomás's restaurant supervisor tells him to claim falsely that the chicken is locally sourced — customers tip better.
Apply the four-way ethical test (legal? policy-consistent? fair? publicly defensible?) and recommend what Tomás should actually do, including the words he could use.
Answer Key: Three of four tests fail (potentially violates consumer-protection law; not policy-consistent; unfair to customers; not publicly defensible). Tomás should decline politely; ask for written confirmation; consider escalating; not jeopardise his reputation for short-term tips. Sample words: "I'd rather stick to what the menu says — could you put the change in writing or have the owner confirm?" 1 mark per test element (× 4) + 1 mark for thoughtful wording.
14
[5]C · Pathway Matching
Sienna is Investigative-Artistic, drawn to research and writing, comfortable in academic settings, values learning and meaning, wants graduate-level training available later.
Recommend her strongest postsecondary destination and one specific Ontario option within it. Justify with three references to her profile.
Answer Key: Strong fit: university, undergraduate in humanities, social science, or English/journalism at a research-intensive Ontario university (e.g., U of T, McMaster, Western, Queen's, Carleton). Justifications: (1) I-A profile aligns with research and writing; (2) academic setting fits her comfort and learning preferences; (3) university opens graduate study (MA, PhD, professional school) which matches her stated preference; could combine with a college journalism diploma later for industry skills. 1 mark pathway + 1 specific destination + 1 per justification × 3.
15
[5]C · Financial Analysis
Aanya's Year-1 university residence costs: tuition $7,000 + residence/meals $14,000 + books $1,200 + personal/transport $2,000 + phone/internet $400 = $24,600. Year-1 funding: OSAP $12,000 (about half loan, half grant), summer earnings $7,500, scholarships $1,000, family contribution $4,100.
Calculate Aanya's Year-1 loan portion and estimated four-year debt if Year 1 is typical. Briefly comment on what graduate salary would make this debt manageable using the "debt ≤ first-year salary" rule.
Answer Key: Year-1 loan portion ≈ $6,000 (half of $12,000 OSAP). 4-year cumulative ≈ $24,000 (excluding accrued interest). Per the rule, this is manageable on starting salaries $30K+, very manageable on $45K+, painful on $25K. Aanya should match her program choice to its likely starting salary range. 1 mark loan calc + 1 4-year calc + 2 marks salary reasoning + 1 mark application of rule.
Part C — Communication [25 marks]
16
[6]B · Workplace Email
Write a 5–7 sentence workplace email to your supervisor at a part-time job requesting a shift swap on a specific date because of a school commitment. Include an action-oriented subject line, BLUF-structured body, a proposed alternative, and a courteous closing.
Answer Key: Look for: subject line names action + date; first sentence states request (BLUF); brief reason; specific proposed swap (named replacement or proposed alternative date); courteous closing with deadline for response. 1 mark subject + 1 BLUF + 1 specifics + 1 proposed alternative + 1 closing + 1 conventions.
17
[5]C · STAR Answer
Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to answer the behavioural question: "Tell me about a time you handled a conflict with a teammate." 6–8 sentences. Include all four parts and one brief reflection.
Answer Key: Strong response has clear: Situation (specific setting + time); Task (what had to be done); Action (specific behaviour — initiative, listening, redistribution); Result (outcome with a number or piece of evidence + brief reflection like "I learned that conflict often signals a hidden constraint, not bad faith"). 1 each STAR element + 1 reflection.
18
[4]C · Résumé
Take the weak bullet "Worked at a coffee shop" and rewrite it as a strong, evidence-rich résumé bullet (title, location, dates, scale, accomplishment).
Answer Key: Sample: "Crew Member, Tim Hortons, Mississauga ON (May–Aug 2025). Served 60+ customers per shift on cash and drive-thru; trained one new hire on POS after three weeks; cross-trained on espresso bar; food-handler safety certified June 2025; recognised by manager for perfect attendance." Include numbers, scale, certifications, and at least one corroboration or accomplishment. 1 mark each: scale/numbers; action verbs; specifics (dates, certs); accomplishment.
19
[5]A · Personal Profile
Write a 5–7 sentence personal profile of yourself for your IPP. Include one interest, one skill (with brief evidence), one value, and one learning preference. Use specific language, no slogans.
Answer Key: Look for: at least one interest named specifically; one skill with brief evidence (not just a claim); one value; one learning preference; cohesive paragraph; no vague slogans ("I'm a people person"). Strong profiles read as honest hypotheses rather than performances. 1 mark per element + 1 mark for tone/conventions.
20
[5]C · Plain-Language Explanation
Explain to a Grade 9 audience, in plain language, why understanding the difference between values and interests matters for career planning. Include one concrete example. 3–5 sentences.
Answer Key: Look for: clear plain-language distinction (interests = what you like; values = what matters about how you live); explanation that a job matching interests but violating values produces value conflict; one concrete example (someone who loves the theatre but values stability ending up unhappy as a touring performer); clear for Gr 9 audience. 1 each: definitions, value-conflict concept, example, conventions, audience-appropriateness.
Part D — Application [25 marks]
21
[6]C · IPP Synthesis
Apply the four IPP questions to yourself. Write 2–3 sentences under each: (1) Who am I? (2) What are my opportunities? (3) Who do I want to become? (4) What is my plan for achieving my goals?
Answer Key: Look for: synthesised personal profile (1); 2–3 specific career hypotheses (2); integrated goal connecting self and opportunity (3); concrete action items — courses, placements, certifications, financial moves (4); cohesion across the four sections. Honesty matters more than ambition. 1.5 marks per question.
22
[5]C · Transition Plan
Apply Schlossberg's 4-S model (Situation, Self, Support, Strategies) to plan your upcoming Grade 11 → 12 transition. Name two concrete actions or resources under each S.
Answer Key: Situation — accurate description (academic year, applications, more demanding courses); two factors specific to the transition. Self — one strength and one risk factor honestly named. Support — two specific people/resources (named counsellor, peer study group, mentor, Kids Help Phone, family doctor). Strategies — two concrete actions (sleep window, weekly review, exercise schedule, application timeline, scholarship search). 0.5 mark per element × 8 = 4 marks + 1 mark for cohesion.
23
[5]C · Career Research Applied
Apply career research to one occupation that interests you. Give: (a) occupation, (b) a credible Canadian source (NOC code or organisation), (c) one fact about wages or training pathway, (d) one strong informational-interview question, (e) one Grade-11 action you could take this term to test the fit.
Answer Key: Look for: specific named occupation; credible Canadian source (Job Bank NOC entry, professional association page, college program page); a defensible fact about wage or pathway; a strong question (typical day / surprises / advice for Gr 11) that couldn't be looked up; a Grade-11-feasible action (volunteer placement, club, summer job, job-shadow, informational interview). 1 mark per element.
24
[5]A · Growth Mindset Applied
Apply growth mindset to a real Grade-11 setback. Describe one setback (academic, social, or personal), the inner monologue you would use to respond with growth mindset (use the word "yet"), and the concrete next step you would take.
Answer Key: Look for: specific, plausible setback; inner monologue that names what specifically went wrong (concept or conditions); the word "yet" or equivalent reframe; one concrete next step (talking to teacher, changing study strategy, asking for feedback). Growth mindset is a method, not a slogan. 1 mark setback + 2 mindset reframing + 1 "yet" + 1 next step.
25
[4]STSE · Capstone
Capstone STSE reflection: Imagine yourself five years from now. In 5–7 sentences, describe what you hope your life looks like — postsecondary destination, work, community involvement, financial habits, one strength you have developed and one habit you have built. Tie the description back to at least two specific elements of your GWL3O learning (Strand A, B, or C).
Answer Key: Look for: plausible, specific 5-year picture; connection to at least one Strand-A self-knowledge element (interest, value, skill, time/stress habit); at least one Strand-B interpersonal element (communication, teamwork, feedback, ethics); at least one Strand-C goal/transition/finance element; coherence between self-knowledge and the picture (no random "I'll be a billionaire" without grounded reasoning). 1 mark plausibility + 1 specificity + 1 Strand connection (× 2 explicit) + 1 conventions/cohesion.
