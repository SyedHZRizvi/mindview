Chapter 1: Self-Knowledge & Personal Management — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand A — A1.1, A1.2, A1.3, A2.1, A2.4
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Help your teacher (and you) identify what you already know about self-awareness, learning preferences, values, time management, stress, and mindset before Chapter 1 instruction begins. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Be honest — this works only if you write what you genuinely think.
Question 1 Self-Awareness
[3 marks]
List five words or short phrases that describe you as a learner and as a person. (For example: "patient," "quick to bore," "good with people," "needs structure.") There are no wrong answers — first instinct is fine.
Question 2 Interests
[3 marks]
Name three activities (in school, after school, online, or at home) where you lose track of time because you're engaged. What do those three activities have in common, if anything?
Question 3 Learning Preferences
[3 marks]
When you have to learn something new and complicated, what works best for you — reading about it, watching someone demonstrate, hearing it explained out loud, or trying it hands-on? Give one example.
Question 4 Values
[3 marks]
What three things matter most to you in a future job? (Examples: helping others, making good money, time with family, doing creative work, being respected, having stable hours, learning constantly.) Order them from most to least important.
Question 5 Time & Stress
[3 marks]
Describe one week from your last term that felt out of control (too busy, not enough sleep, behind on work). What specifically went wrong — was it overload, procrastination, lack of sleep, distraction, all of the above? One paragraph.
Question 6 Mindset
[3 marks]
A student writes after failing a Grade 11 chemistry test: "I'm just not a science person. Why bother trying — I'll never get this."
In two or three sentences, rewrite the student's thought as a growth-mindset response. What would the new thought sound like, and what concrete action would the student take next?
Question 7 Career Awareness
[3 marks]
Name two careers you are currently curious about. For each, write one sentence about why it interests you.
Question 8 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which part of Chapter 1 are you most uncertain about going in: self-awareness, learning preferences/MI, RIASEC interests, values, time management, stress, or growth mindset? One sentence is enough.
📖 Complete Answer Key & Marking Rubric

Q1 — 3 marks. Look for five distinct, plausible self-descriptors. Award full marks for honest specificity; partial marks for fewer than five or for vague entries like "good." No "wrong" answers — score honesty + clarity.

Q2 — 3 marks. 1 mark per activity, with a fourth mark for a thoughtful common-thread observation. Look for honest engagement signals (debate, organising events, drawing, fixing things, gaming, helping siblings). Common threads might be: working with people; pattern-finding; physical/spatial; creative expression.

Q3 — 3 marks. Award full marks for a clear modal preference (read/write, visual, kinesthetic, or auditory) with a concrete example demonstrating self-knowledge. Note: this is a hypothesis, not a verdict — students may discover during the course that they were wrong.

Q4 — 3 marks. 1 mark per ranked value. Look for evidence of thought (some students will list socially desirable answers; nudge in feedback toward honesty). Use later inventories to test these.

Q5 — 3 marks. 1 mark for honest scenario; 1 for diagnosis of cause(s); 1 for self-awareness in tone (no scapegoating). This question diagnoses both stress patterns and willingness to be honest in writing.

Q6 — 3 marks. Strong responses use "yet" or equivalent reframe (1); ask a diagnostic question — what specifically did I miss? (1); and name a concrete next step — talk to teacher, change study strategy, ask for help (1). Look for the method, not the slogan.

Q7 — 3 marks. 1 mark per career named + 1 for thoughtful reasoning across both. No marks lost for unusual or modest choices; flag for follow-up only if reasoning is missing.

Q8 — Ungraded. Use answers to target instruction. Common starting points of uncertainty: distinguishing values from interests; "doing" RIASEC and MI properly.

Feedback rubric: Provide descriptive feedback rather than a grade. Focus on one strength (specific) and one next step (actionable for the next two weeks).
