Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]B · Channels
Distinguish synchronous from asynchronous communication. Give one example of each, and one workplace scenario where each is preferable.
Answer Key: Synchronous = real-time (phone, video, in-person); preferable for nuance, sensitive disagreements, decisions needing alignment. Asynchronous = at-receiver's-leisure (email, chat, shared docs); preferable for records, status updates, anything needing to be referred back. 1 mark per definition + 0.5 example each.
2
[2]B · Listening
Name three of the four active-listening micro-skills Carl Rogers identified.
Answer Key: Option 2. The four are paraphrasing, open-ended questions, reflection of feeling, and summarising. Together they signal full attention and force accuracy.
3
[2]B · Teamwork
In Tuckman's model, which stage is characterised by visible conflict as members stake out roles and disagree about direction?
Answer Key: Storming. Conflict in this stage is normal and useful — it leads to Norming once the team negotiates how to work together. Avoiding Storming usually delays effective work.
4
[3]B · Feedback
Define the SBI feedback framework. Give one example of feedback delivered using SBI.
Answer Key: SBI = Situation, Behaviour, Impact. Name when/where (S), describe specific observable behaviour (B), explain the consequence on you or the team (I). Example: "In yesterday's meeting (S), when you presented our team's data without naming Maya's contribution (B), it felt like our shared work was being claimed and I worried Maya was being overlooked (I)." 1 mark per element of definition + 1 mark example using all three.
5
[3]B · Law
Name the three Ontario workplace-law frameworks every employee should know, and one thing each protects.
Answer Key: (a) Employment Standards Act (ESA) — minimum wage, hours, breaks, vacation, notice/termination. (b) Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) — three worker rights (know, participate, refuse unsafe work). (c) Ontario Human Rights Code — protection against workplace discrimination on protected grounds (race, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, family status, etc.). 1 mark per Act + 0.5 per protection.
Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]B · Channel + BLUF
Your supervisor has asked you to communicate to the team that the Friday team meeting is moved to Monday because of a client visit. You want the team to read it quickly and confirm attendance.
Draft the message you would send. Include: appropriate channel choice, an effective subject line, BLUF-structured body, and a clear next step. (You may use the email format.)
Answer Key: Look for: channel = email or team chat (asynchronous, needs record, low emotion); subject line = action-oriented ("Action: confirm new meeting time — Mon Oct 28 at 10am"); body opens with the change ("The Friday team meeting is moved to Monday Oct 28, 10am, due to a client visit."); context follows; closing names next step ("Please reply by Thu 4pm if you can't attend"). 1 mark channel + 1 subject + 2 body structure + 1 closing.
7
[4]B · Team Diagnosis
A project team has met four times. Members are friendly. Almost no work has been done. Meetings drift; deadlines approach.
Using Tuckman's stages, diagnose what is happening. Propose two concrete actions someone could take to move the team forward.
Answer Key: Diagnosis: stuck in Forming — friendly but never explicitly negotiated Norming (roles, deadlines, communication norms). Friendliness has been confused with effectiveness. Actions: (1) Run an explicit Norming meeting: deliverable scope, individual tasks, deadlines, weekly check-in time, how to flag delays. (2) Volunteer someone (perhaps the student answering) to play a Coordinator role until the team reaches Performing. (3) Use a shared task tracker (Google Doc/sheet) so commitments are visible. 1 mark diagnosis + 1.5 per action × 2.
8
[4]B · Conflict
A co-worker has been arriving late to your morning shift handover for the past four days. The brief gets cut short and you're worried something will be missed. You want to address it without damaging the relationship.
Using SBI and a collaborative conflict approach, write the conversation opening (3–5 sentences) and name the Thomas-Kilmann style you're aiming for.
Answer Key: Strong response approximates: "Hey Mateo, can I grab you for two minutes? Situation: the last four mornings, you've come in around 8:45 instead of 8:30. Behaviour: we've been squeezing the handover into ten minutes. Impact: I'm worried something will be missed and the rest of the team gets stressed. What's going on at your end, and how can we fix it for next week?" Style = Collaborating (high concern for own + other; surfaces underlying interest). 1 mark per SBI element + 1 mark for Collaborating identification and tone (open question, no character attack).
Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[5]B · Workplace Email
Write a 5–7 sentence workplace email to your supervisor at a part-time job (your invention) asking to switch your shift on a specific date because of a school commitment. Include a clear subject line, your proposed alternative, and a courteous tone.
Answer Key: Strong response includes: action subject line (e.g., "Shift swap request: Sat Nov 8 evening for Mon Nov 10 evening"); first sentence stating the ask; brief reason; proposed solution (named replacement coworker if applicable); closing with deadline for response and thanks. 1 mark per element: subject; opening request; specifics; proposed alternative; courteous closing.
10
[3]B · Rewrite
Rewrite the following weak résumé bullet into a strong, evidence-rich one suitable for a Grade 11 / 12 résumé: "Worked at a coffee shop."
Answer Key: Sample strong rewrite: "Crew Member, Tim Hortons, Mississauga ON (May–Aug 2025). Served 60+ customers per shift on cash and drive-thru; trained one new hire on the POS system after three weeks; cross-trained on espresso bar; food-handler safety certified June 2025; recognised by manager for perfect attendance." 1 mark numbers/scale + 1 mark action verbs + 1 mark specifics (cert, dates, accomplishment).
11
[4]B · Plain-Language Explanation
In 3–4 sentences, explain to a Grade 9 student why "active listening" is one of the most undervalued workplace skills. Use one concrete example.
Answer Key: Look for: (1) clear definition of active listening in plain language; (2) explanation that most people listen to respond, not to understand; (3) concrete example of how active listening changes an outcome (tutoring a student, supervisor giving feedback, customer complaint); (4) clarity for a Gr 9 audience. 1 mark each.
Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[5]B · Ethics Application
You work part-time at a small retail store. Your supervisor tells you that when customers ask about return policy, you should say "no returns" — even on items you know are returnable under store policy — because returns hurt the supervisor's numbers.
Apply the four-way ethical test (legal? policy-consistent? fair? publicly defensible?) to this situation. State your conclusion and describe what you would actually do, including the words you would use with the supervisor.
Answer Key: Test: probably-illegal (potentially violates consumer-protection law and store policy that customers reasonably rely on); contradicts store policy; unfair to customers; not publicly defensible. Action: decline politely; ask for written confirmation of the new policy; offer to follow whatever the company-wide written policy is; if pressure continues, raise with the store owner or escalate. Words: "I'd rather follow the written store policy — could you put the change in writing or have the manager confirm it? I don't want to tell a customer something we'd need to walk back." 1 mark per test element (× 4) + 1 mark for thoughtful action wording.
13
[4]B · Team Roles Application
Apply Belbin's roles to a real team you've been part of (school project, sports team, club, work). Identify which role(s) you tend to play, which role was missing from the team, and what the consequence was.
Answer Key: Look for: (1) specific team named; (2) self-identification of role with reasoning (e.g., "I tend to be a Coordinator — I clarify goals and assign tasks"); (3) one missing role + observed consequence (e.g., "no Completer-Finisher — we missed details in our final submission"); (4) reflection on what to do differently. 1 mark per element.
14
[3]B · STSE / Real-World
STSE Application: Why might a 60-second thank-you email after a job interview be more valuable to an applicant than a longer, more elaborate follow-up? 2–3 sentences.
Answer Key: Brief is more likely to be read by a busy interviewer; signals professionalism, attention, and follow-through; gives one chance to reference something specific from the conversation (which jogs memory of the candidate). Long follow-ups can feel anxious or performative. The key skill is doing the small thing well, not the big thing fancy. 1 mark per insight, max 3.
