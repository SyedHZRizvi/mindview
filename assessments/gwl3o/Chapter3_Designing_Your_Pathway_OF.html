Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]C · Pathways
Name and briefly describe the five Ontario postsecondary destinations.
Answer Key: Apprenticeship — paid on-the-job + in-class training, 2–5 years, Certificate of Qualification. College — 1–4 year applied programs; certificates, diplomas, advanced diplomas. University — 3–4 year undergraduate degrees with graduate study above. Workplace — direct employment after Grade 12. Community living — supported community participation for some students. 0.5 each + 0.5 description × 5.
2
[2]C · OYAP
The Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program (OYAP) lets eligible Grade 11–12 students:
Answer Key: Option 2. OYAP allows Grade 11–12 students to begin counting apprenticeship hours toward a Certificate of Qualification while still in high school. It is one of the most powerful Strand-C tools for students whose pathway is apprenticeship.
3
[2]C · NOC
The federal Canadian system that codes every occupation by a five-digit number is the:
Answer Key: Option 3 — NOC. Federal classification linked to Job Bank wage and outlook data. The 2021 update introduced the TEER structure (Training, Education, Experience, Responsibilities).
4
[3]C · Goals
Define a SMART goal. Take the wish "I want to do better in chemistry" and convert it to a SMART goal.
Answer Key: SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Sample conversion: "Raise my Grade 11 chemistry term mark from 68% to 78% by the end of this term, by completing two weekly problem sets, biweekly self-tests, and a 30-minute Sunday review block — chemistry is a prerequisite for the kinesiology program I'm exploring." 1 mark definition + 1 specific number/outcome + 1 deadline/time-bound.
5
[3]C · Money
Define each: (a) OSAP, (b) RESP, (c) CESG. For each, write one sentence about what it does for a student or family.
Answer Key: (a) OSAP (Ontario Student Assistance Program) — mixed loan + grant package based on income; grants don't repay, loans are interest-free during studies. (b) RESP (Registered Education Savings Plan) — tax-sheltered account for postsecondary savings; gains grow tax-free until withdrawn. (c) CESG (Canada Education Savings Grant) — 20% federal match on first $2,500/year of RESP contributions, up to $7,200 lifetime. 1 mark each definition + description.
Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]C · Pathway Matching
Diego scores Social-Investigative on RIASEC, wants to be in the workforce within two or three years rather than four-plus, values balance and helping, has solid Grade-11 math and biology marks, and dislikes long lectures.
Recommend Diego's strongest pathway and one specific Ontario destination within it. Justify with three references to his profile.
Answer Key: Strong fit: college nursing or paramedic program (e.g., Mohawk, Centennial, Conestoga, Algonquin, Fanshawe). Justifications: (1) Social-Investigative aligns with health-helping work with assessment/analysis; (2) 2–3 year college program fits his time-to-workforce preference; (3) paid clinical placements suit his dislike of long lectures and reinforce hands-on learning; (4) balance is more achievable on a typical hospital schedule than in many longer-credentialed pathways. Bridge to BScN is available if he wants more later. 1 mark for pathway + 1 for specific destination + 1 per justification × 3.
7
[5]C · Financial Analysis
Anya is comparing 4-year university journalism (residence) at $24,900/year and 3-year college journalism (residence) at $19,800/year. Both pathways exit to expected starting salaries of $42,000–$48,000. Available funding per year: OSAP $10,000 (half grant, half loan), summer work $7,000, family contribution $4,000, scholarships ≈ $1,000.
For each pathway, calculate the annual gap, total debt at graduation (assume the loan half of OSAP is the only debt accumulated), and recommend which pathway better fits the "debt ≤ first-year salary" rule. Briefly note one non-financial factor that should also be considered.
Answer Key:

University: annual cost $24,900, funding total $22,000, annual gap $2,900. Loan portion of OSAP = $5,000/year × 4 = $20,000 over 4 years (plus accrued interest). Cumulative debt ≈ $20K+.
College: annual cost $19,800, funding total $22,000, annual gap $0 (surplus ≈ $2,200/year if family contribution holds). Loan portion = $5,000 × 3 = $15,000 over 3 years (plus interest).
Both fit the "debt ≤ starting salary $42K–$48K" rule, but college leaves roughly $5,000 less debt and one year sooner in the workforce — more financially advantageous on these numbers.
Non-financial factors: program quality/reputation in chosen niche; specific instructors; networks; preferred city; whether degree opens future graduate options Anya may want.

1 mark each calculation (× 2) + 1 mark rule application + 1 mark recommendation + 1 mark non-financial factor.

8
[3]C · Critique
A peer says: "I watched a few medical shows on TV and now I'm sure I want to be a forensic pathologist."
Critique your peer's career-research approach. Identify two flaws and recommend two specific research steps from Chapter 3.
Answer Key: Flaws: (1) TV/social-media portrayals of careers (forensics especially) are dramatised and miss daily realities; (2) single-source impression rather than multi-source research. Recommendations: (a) Look up the NOC entry for pathologist on jobbank.gc.ca for actual duties/training/wages/outlook; (b) do an informational interview with a working pathologist, asking "what does your typical day actually look like?" "what surprised you about this work?" (c) check the educational pathway (medical school → residency in pathology) and timeline. 1 mark per flaw + 0.5 per recommendation.
Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[6]C · Cover Letter
Write a short cover-letter body (5–7 sentences) for a Grade 11 summer position at a community recreation centre as a Junior Camp Leader. Include why this role, why you (with one concrete example/evidence), and what you bring. Use a professional opening and closing line.
Answer Key: Look for: (1) clear opening naming role and source ("I'm writing to apply for the Junior Camp Leader position at..."); (2) qualifications connected to job — specific evidence (volunteered with kids, sports leadership, certifications like first aid/CPR/HIGH-FIVE); (3) "why this employer" — specific reason for choosing this organisation; (4) professional closing. 1 mark opening + 2 marks qualifications with specific evidence + 1 mark "why this employer" + 1 mark closing + 1 mark conventions/tone.
10
[5]C · STAR Answer
Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to answer the behavioural question: "Tell me about a time you missed a deadline or fell behind on a commitment, and how you handled it." 6–8 sentences. Include all four parts and one brief reflection at the end.
Answer Key: Look for clear: Situation (specific setting + time); Task (what you owed and to whom); Action (specific behaviour — communicating early, redistributing, asking for help); Result (outcome — what was salvaged, what was learned); brief reflection. 1 mark each STAR element + 1 mark reflection. Penalise vague generalities ("I had some trouble but it worked out").
11
[1]C · Reference
Name one specific question a Grade 11 student should ask in an informational interview about a career they are considering. (Not "how much does it pay?" — assume that can be looked up.)
Answer Key: Strong examples: "What does your typical day actually look like?" "What surprised you about this work that you wouldn't have known from school?" "What do you wish you had done differently in your training?" "What did you not see coming?" "What advice would you give a Grade 11 student today specifically?" 1 mark for any thoughtful question that couldn't easily be looked up.
Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[6]C · IPP Synthesis
Apply the four IPP questions to yourself. Write 2–3 sentences under each: (1) Who am I? (2) What are my opportunities? (3) Who do I want to become? (4) What is my plan?
Answer Key: Look for: synthesised personal profile (1); 2–3 specific career hypotheses with reasoning (2); an integrated goal connecting self and opportunity (3); concrete action items — courses, placements, certifications, financial moves (4); cohesion across the four sections. Honesty matters more than ambition — strong responses are realistic, specific, and tied to evidence about the student rather than slogans. 1.5 mark per IPP question.
13
[6]C · Transition Plan
Apply Schlossberg's 4-S model (Situation, Self, Support, Strategies) to your own upcoming Grade 11 → 12 transition. Write 2 sentences under each S, naming at least one specific resource or action under each.
Answer Key: Situation — describe the nature/timing/duration accurately (academic year, applications, more demanding courses). Self — name one strength and one risk factor honestly. Support — name specific people (named guidance counsellor, mentor, study partner) and one formal resource (school counsellor, Kids Help Phone, doctor). Strategies — concrete actions (sleep window, weekly review, exercise schedule, application timeline). 1.5 marks per S; reward specificity over abstraction.

📝 Communication Category Rubric (instructor-graded)

Per Ontario's Growing Success (2010) Achievement Chart, Communication is one of the four equally-weighted categories (25% of the course mark). This rubric is applied to every student-written response (short-answer, extended-response, essay, lab report, deputation, etc.) on this assessment.

Criterion Level 1 — Limited (50–59%) Level 2 — Some (60–69%) Level 3 — Considerable (70–79%) Level 4 — Thorough (80–100%)
Expression and organisation of ideas Ideas expressed with limited clarity; little organisation. Ideas expressed with some clarity; some organisation. Ideas expressed clearly and organised effectively. Ideas expressed with clarity, precision, and a high degree of organisation.
Communication for different audiences and purposes Limited awareness of audience/purpose; tone often inappropriate. Some awareness of audience/purpose; tone occasionally inappropriate. Consistently appropriate tone, register, and address to audience. Tone, register, voice, and address are precisely matched to audience and purpose.
Use of conventions, vocabulary, terminology Frequent errors in grammar, spelling, punctuation; little discipline-specific vocabulary. Some errors that occasionally impede meaning; emerging use of discipline-specific vocabulary. Few errors; consistent use of discipline-specific vocabulary. Virtually error-free; precise and varied use of discipline-specific vocabulary.

Level 3 represents the Provincial Standard. Use the most-recent and most-consistent evidence across multiple Communication tasks (this assessment, prior FOR work, written responses on AS quizzes, oral presentations) when assigning a course-level Communication mark. Growing Success, 2010, p. 17.
