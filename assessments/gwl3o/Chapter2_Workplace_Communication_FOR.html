Chapter 2: Workplace Communication & Teamwork — Diagnostic

Assessment FOR Learning · Prerequisite check · Strand B — B1.1, B1.2, B1.3, B2.1, B2.4, B3.2
Not Graded — Teacher Feedback Only
Purpose: Help your teacher (and you) identify what you already know about workplace communication, listening, teamwork, feedback, and ethics before Chapter 2 instruction begins. You will receive descriptive feedback, not a grade. Be honest — this works only if you write what you genuinely think.
Question 1 Channels
[3 marks]
You need to (a) confirm receipt of a project file, (b) tell a teammate you disagree with their proposed plan, and (c) ask HR about a workplace accommodation. For each, name the channel you would use (email, chat, call, in-person, formal letter) and one short reason.
Question 2 Listening
[3 marks]
Describe a recent moment where someone (a friend, family member, teacher, supervisor) really listened to you. What specifically did they do that made you feel heard? Two or three sentences.
Question 3 Writing
[3 marks]
Rewrite this weak email opening into something a busy supervisor could action quickly:
"Hey, I had a question about the thing we talked about yesterday. Not sure what you meant exactly. Let me know whenever."
Question 4 Teamwork
[3 marks]
Describe one group project (school, sports, club, work) that went well and one that went poorly. In one or two sentences each, name what made the difference.
Question 5 Feedback
[3 marks]
A teammate has been arriving 15 minutes late to your group's morning meeting for the past four days, and it's compressing the time available for the daily plan. You want to address it without damaging the relationship.
Write what you would actually say (or text) to the teammate. Three to five sentences.
Question 6 Conflict
[3 marks]
Describe how you most often respond when you disagree with someone in a school or workplace setting — do you tend to push back, avoid the conflict, accommodate, compromise, or try to find a both-sides solution? One or two honest sentences.
Question 7 Ethics
[3 marks]
If a supervisor at a part-time job asked you to do something you thought was wrong (mislead a customer, skip a safety step, work off-the-clock), what would you do? Be honest — there is no single right answer here, but the chapter will give you frameworks for handling these situations.
Question 8 Reflection
[ungraded]
Which part of Chapter 2 are you most uncertain about going in: communication channels, active listening, workplace writing, teamwork stages/roles, feedback & conflict, or workplace ethics & law? One sentence is enough.
📖 Complete Answer Key & Marking Rubric

Q1 — 3 marks. Expected reasoning: (a) email or chat — asynchronous, low-stakes, written record; (b) in-person or call — synchronous, tone matters for disagreement; (c) formal email or written request — needs HR record, may need policy reference. 1 mark per appropriate channel + brief reason.

Q2 — 3 marks. Look for awareness of active-listening micro-skills (eye contact, paraphrasing, open questions, not interrupting, presence). 1 mark for plausible scenario + 2 for specifics demonstrating awareness of what made listening feel real.

Q3 — 3 marks. Strong rewrites use BLUF + specific subject line + named deadline + concrete question. Example: Subject "Clarification needed by Wed 4pm — Q1 budget assumptions"; Body: "Hi Priya — could you confirm whether we should use the updated supplier price ($14.20/unit) or the original quote ($12.80) for cells C12–C18? Happy to update once I hear." 1 mark subject; 1 specificity; 1 deadline/closing.

Q4 — 3 marks. Look for awareness of teamwork dynamics — Norming agreements, distributed leadership, communication frequency, roles. 1 mark good case + 1 bad case + 1 plausible insight about what differed.

Q5 — 3 marks. Strong responses approximate SBI (Situation/Behaviour/Impact) without being told the framework: specific times mentioned, observable behaviour described (not character), impact stated as own experience or team consequence, an open question or proposed solution at the end. 1 mark specificity + 1 non-attacking tone + 1 next-step move.

Q6 — 3 marks. Honest self-knowledge scores higher than performance. Tendencies map roughly to Thomas-Kilmann styles (Competing, Accommodating, Avoiding, Compromising, Collaborating). Diagnostic only.

Q7 — 3 marks. Look for awareness that the answer is not "always do what the boss says" and not "publicly explode." Strong responses include a path: clarify, ask for written confirmation, decline politely, escalate if needed, know OHSA/ESA rights. 1 mark per element.

Q8 — Ungraded. Use answers to target instruction. Common starting points: subject-line specificity; channel choice; SBI framework.

Feedback rubric: Provide descriptive feedback, not a grade. Focus on one strength + one next step.
