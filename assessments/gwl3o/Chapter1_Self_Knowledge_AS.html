Topics 1.1–1.2 — Self-Awareness & Learning Styles
Question 1
According to Tasha Eurich's research, the two kinds of self-awareness are:
Solution: Eurich distinguishes internal self-awareness (how clearly you see your own values, reactions, and impact) from external self-awareness (how clearly you understand how others see you). Most people overestimate both — which is why feedback-seeking matters.
Question 2
Which study technique has the strongest research support across decades of educational research?
Solution: Self-testing (closing the book and recalling), spaced practice (sessions distributed over days), and elaboration (explaining concepts in your own words) consistently outperform passive re-reading and highlighting. Matching teaching to a single VARK style alone does not reliably boost grades.
Question 3
Aaliyah reflects in her journal: "I lose track of time when I'm debating, organising family meal plans, or coding short interactive stories." She is using which self-awareness practice?
This is best described as:
Solution: Naming the activities where time disappears is classic internal self-awareness through reflective journalling. The results are a starting hypothesis, not a verdict — Aaliyah's next step is to test the pattern with low-stakes experiments (debate club, junior coding contest) and external feedback.
Topic 1.3 — Holland Codes (RIASEC) & Values
Question 4
In Holland's RIASEC framework, a person whose top code is SIE (Social–Investigative–Enterprising) would most likely thrive in:
Solution: SIE combines Social (helping), Investigative (analysing), and Enterprising (leading). Health professions with leadership and analytic components (nurse practitioner, school principal, clinic director, public-health manager) fit naturally. Options (a) is RRC, (b) is C-dominant, (d) is I-only.
Question 5
The three families of work values commonly used in career counselling are:
Solution: Intrinsic values are about the work itself (challenge, meaning, helping); extrinsic are about rewards (salary, status, security); lifestyle are about how the work fits into life (hours, location, flexibility). All three matter; no mix is "more idealistic" than another.
Question 6
Diego's top five values are helping others, learning, autonomy, balance, and integrity. He is offered a high-paying pharmaceutical-sales position that requires 70-hour weeks and pressuring physicians to prescribe drugs he believes are over-marketed.
A career counsellor would describe this situation as:
Solution: Value conflict is the technical term for a job whose daily demands violate a person's deeper values. Diego's top five are all violated — the 70-hour week kills balance and autonomy, the pressure tactics violate integrity, the marketing focus crowds out helping and learning. Research finds value conflict reliably predicts burnout regardless of pay.
Topic 1.4 — Time Management
Question 7
In Covey's Time Management Matrix, life quality is most determined by how much time you spend in:
Solution: Q2 is the highest-leverage quadrant — planning, deep practice, exercise, sleep, relationships, long-term project work. Students stuck in Q1 (crisis mode) usually got there by under-investing in Q2. The weekly habit: book Q2 anchors into your calendar before Q1 and Q3 swallow the week.
Question 8
The Pomodoro technique is best described as:
Solution: Pomodoro = 25 minutes focused + 5-minute break, repeated 4× before a longer break. It respects how attention actually works (short bursts with recovery) and is gentler than "I'll study for six hours straight" plans that rarely survive contact with phone notifications.
Topic 1.5 — Stress & Wellness
Question 9
The transactional model of stress (Lazarus & Folkman) says that stress depends on:
Solution: The transactional model explains why two students under the same workload experience different stress levels — appraisal (how threatening they judge it) and resources (study time, sleep, support, prior preparation) differ. Intervention paths: change the appraisal (cognitive reframing) or build resources (study earlier, ask for help, restore sleep).
Question 10
Which single physical-wellness lever has the strongest evidence for protecting teenagers' learning, mood, and reaction time?
Solution: Sleep is the single biggest physical-wellness lever for teens. Chronic shortfall (anything under 8 hours regularly) degrades memory consolidation, mood regulation, and reaction time. Caffeine masks but doesn't replace sleep; all-nighters worsen test performance.
Topic 1.6 — Growth Mindset & Character
Question 11
Sienna gets her first Grade 11 chemistry test back: 54%. She thinks: "I haven't mastered moles and stoichiometry yet. Let me figure out what I missed, book a help session with my teacher, and try spaced practice this week."
Sienna's response illustrates:
Solution: The word "yet," the diagnostic posture (what specifically did I miss?), and the new strategy (help session, spaced practice) are textbook growth-mindset moves. Growth mindset is a method — turn a setback into a plan — not a slogan about positive thinking.
Question 12
Ontario's Learning Skills and Work Habits report names six habits associated with success. The list is:
Solution: The six Ontario Learning Skills are responsibility, organisation, independent work, collaboration, initiative, and self-regulation. These are the same habits employers prize and universities cite for first-year success. They are reported separately on the report card because they predict outcomes as strongly as marks do.
