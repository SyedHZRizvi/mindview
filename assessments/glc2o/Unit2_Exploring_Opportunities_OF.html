Part A: Knowledge & Understanding [13 marks]
1
[3]
Name the five Ontario postsecondary destinations as defined by the Ministry. For each, give one sentence describing what it leads to.
Answer Key

Apprenticeship — paid on-the-job + in-class training → Certificate of Qualification in a recognised trade. College — 1–4 year applied programs at public colleges → certificate, diploma, advanced diploma, or applied degree. University — 3–4 year academic programs → undergraduate degree (BA, BSc, etc.). Workplace — direct entry to employment after Grade 12. Community living — supported community participation for some students with significant intellectual disabilities. 0.6 marks each.

2
[2]
The federal system that gives every Canadian occupation a code and standardised description is called:
Answer Key

National Occupational Classification (NOC). Used by Statistics Canada and Job Bank to label every Canadian occupation. Knowing NOC is the one-step lookup that makes career research efficient.

3
[3]
Describe three current trends in the Canadian labour market and explain a potential implication of each for Grade 10 students planning their career.
Answer Key

Acceptable trends include: demographic ageing → retirement waves create openings (especially trades, public service, health care); automation/AI → tasks involving judgment, dexterity, care, persuasion harder to automate; climate transition → new green-economy occupations (EV electricians, energy auditors, climate-adaptation planners); chronic shortages in skilled trades, health care, and ECE; hybrid/remote work in knowledge sectors. Marks: 1 per trend + valid implication.

4
[2]
The Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program (OYAP) is:
Answer Key

B. OYAP students bank apprenticeship hours during high school. Combined with SHSM-Construction (or similar), students can graduate Grade 12 with ~2,000 trade hours already on the books and a sponsoring employer.

5
[3]
Define each: (a) Red Seal trade, (b) Specialist High Skills Major (SHSM), (c) Business Model Canvas.
Answer Key

(a) Red Seal — interprovincial standard certifying trade competence; lets a tradesperson work across Canada without re-certifying provincially. (b) SHSM — Ministry-approved bundle of credits and sector certifications taken in Grade 11–12 that produces an OSSD with a red sector seal, signalling industry readiness. (c) Business Model Canvas — Osterwalder's one-page tool that sketches a business across nine blocks (customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure). 1 mark each.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]
A Grade 10 student wants to research becoming a Registered Nurse in Ontario.
Outline a structured five-step research plan they should follow. For each step, name one specific source or action they should take.
Answer Key

Sample plan: (1) Identify focus — researchable question: "What does an RN do in Ontario, what training is required, and is demand growing?" (2) Gather from multiple sources — Job Bank NOC 31301 Registered Nurses for duties/wages/outlook; College of Nurses of Ontario for licensure; one Ontario nursing school program page for prerequisites. (3) Triangulate — confirm wage range and demand across at least 3 sources; check for regional variation. (4) Informational interview — talk to a working RN about a typical shift, surprises, advice for a Grade 10. (5) Synthesise & cite — one-page summary with sources listed for re-checking. 1 mark per step + 1 for explicit source naming.

7
[4]
Two profiles: Mateo wants to be an electrician (NOC 72200). Anya wants to be a journalist (NOC 51113). Both want to know how durable their pathway is.
Using what you know about COPS labour-market projections, explain how durable each pathway is and what each student should plan to do differently to manage the risk.
Answer Key

Mateo (electrician): Construction electrician is flagged as in shortage across most provinces — driven by demographic retirements, EV build-out, residential construction. Implication: strong demand, median wages $80–100K+ for journeypersons, training paths via OYAP/apprenticeship lead to durable employment. Anya (journalist): Many regions show surplus — advertising shifted to social platforms, layoffs at traditional outlets, rising freelance share. Implication: still a real career path but requires specialisation (data, video, niche expertise) and tolerance for income variability. Risk management: Mateo should test fit via co-op or summer trade work; Anya should specialise early and plan to freelance, possibly stack a complementary skill (data analytics, photography). 2 marks per pathway analysis.

8
[4]
A Grade 10 student wants to start a tutoring business for Grade 7–8 math students. Using the Business Model Canvas, identify three blocks they must think through and what each requires.
Answer Key

Acceptable answers (any three): Customer segments — who exactly will pay (Grade 7–8 families in the local neighbourhood; willing to pay $20–$30/hr). Value proposition — why choose this tutor over a tutoring centre (cheaper, more flexible, peer-to-peer relatable). Channels — how to reach customers (flyers, neighbourhood Facebook, word-of-mouth). Revenue streams — pricing model (hourly $25, bundle 4 for $90). Cost structure — printing, transit, materials. Key activities — session prep, marketing, scheduling. 1 mark per identified block + brief justification × 3, plus 1 mark for plausibility.

Part C: Communication [12 marks]
9
[5]
In one paragraph (6–8 sentences), explain to a Grade 9 student the differences between an apprenticeship, a college diploma, and a university degree. Use plain language and one concrete example for each pathway.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) clear definition of each pathway; (2) accurate facts about duration and structure (apprenticeship: paid, ~80% on-the-job, 2–5 years → Certificate; college: 1–4 years applied → certificate/diploma/applied degree; university: 3–4 years academic → BA/BSc); (3) one concrete example each (electrician/PSW/teacher etc.); (4) plain, age-appropriate language for a Grade 9 reader. Marks: 1 each pathway definition + 1 plain language + 1 conventions.

10
[3]
Write one strong résumé-quality bullet point describing a community-involvement placement (real or hypothetical). Include duration, role, audience/scale, and one concrete outcome or feedback.
Answer Key

Sample strong bullet: "Volunteer reading tutor, Toronto Public Library Bloor/Gladstone Branch (Sept 2024 – June 2025, 60 hours): supported four Grade 2 students in weekly one-on-one reading sessions; coordinated with branch librarian to track progress; recognised by branch supervisor as "consistently prepared, patient, and supportive of new readers." Marks: 1 specific role/location, 1 duration/hours, 1 outcome/feedback.

11
[4]
A friend says: "Trades are for students who can't do school." Write a 3–4 sentence response that corrects the stereotype using evidence about what modern Ontario trades actually require.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) acknowledgement that the stereotype is outdated; (2) specific evidence that modern trades require strong literacy, numeracy (including trig/algebra), digital skills, ongoing certification; (3) labour-market data — chronic shortages, journeyperson wages often $80–100K+; (4) clear, respectful tone. Marks: 1 each element.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[5]
Apply the career-research method to one occupation that interests you. Provide: (a) the occupation name; (b) one source you would use; (c) one fact you already know (or could find) about wage or training; (d) one question you would ask in an informational interview.
Answer Key

Look for: a specific occupation; a credible source (Job Bank NOC, professional association, college program page, Statistics Canada); a defensible fact (median wage, training duration, prerequisites); a thoughtful interview question — preferably one of the three best ("walk me through a typical day," "what surprised you about this work," "what would you tell a Grade 10 student considering this field"). Marks: 1 each + 1 for substantive question.

13
[4]
Apply the 40-hour community-involvement requirement strategically. Sketch a plan that uses three different placements over Grades 9–11 to test three different career hypotheses. Name each placement, the hours, and what career interest it would test.
Answer Key

Strong plans pair each placement to a Holland code or career hypothesis. Example: (1) Long-term-care home recreation programs (12 h) — tests health/Social interest; (2) Library reading tutor (12 h) — tests teaching/Social-Artistic; (3) Habitat for Humanity build day (10 h) — tests trades/Realistic. Marks: 1 per placement (× 3) + 1 for strategic alignment to career hypotheses.

14
[3]
STSE / Real-World Application: The federal government invests in OYAP and SHSM partly because Ontario faces a structural shortage of skilled trades. In 2–3 sentences, explain how this public-policy choice connects to Canadian labour-market needs and to your own career planning.
Answer Key

Strong response: government invests in OYAP and SHSM because skilled-trade retirements outpace replacements; the policy aims to channel more students into apprenticeship before the labour gap widens. From a Grade 10 perspective, this means OYAP is a real, well-supported pathway worth considering — not a "fallback." A student weighing university against trades has evidence that one of those pathways is being actively encouraged by public-policy investment, which interacts with personal fit and values. 1 mark per insight.
