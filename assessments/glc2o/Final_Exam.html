Part A — Knowledge & Understanding [25 marks]
1
[3]A · Self
Define and give one career example for each of the six Holland (RIASEC) interest types.
Answer Key

R-Realistic — hands-on (electrician); I-Investigative — analytical (biologist); A-Artistic — creative (graphic designer); S-Social — helping (nurse); E-Enterprising — leading/persuading (entrepreneur); C-Conventional — structured (accountant).

2
[2]A
In the Big Five (OCEAN) model, which trait is most strongly linked to job performance?
Answer Key

Conscientiousness — planning, organisation, persistence. Decades of meta-analyses confirm this is the strongest single predictor across nearly every field.

3
[3]A · Mindset
Define growth mindset (Dweck) and grit (Duckworth). How are they related?
Answer Key

Growth mindset = belief that ability is developed through effort, strategy, and feedback. Grit = sustained passion and perseverance toward long-term goals. Growth mindset is a precondition for grit; grit extends growth mindset across years.

4
[3]B · Pathways
Name and briefly describe the five Ontario postsecondary destinations.
Answer Key

Apprenticeship — paid on-the-job + in-class training → Certificate of Qualification. College — 1–4 year applied programs → certificate/diploma/applied degree. University — 3–4 year academic programs → undergraduate degree. Workplace — direct employment after Grade 12. Community living — supported community participation for some students.

5
[2]B
The federal system that classifies every Canadian occupation by code is called:
Answer Key

NOC. Federal classification linked to Job Bank data on duties, training, wage, and outlook.

6
[3]B · OYAP
Define each: (a) OYAP, (b) Red Seal, (c) Specialist High Skills Major (SHSM).
Answer Key

(a) Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program — start apprenticeship hours in high school. (b) Red Seal — interprovincial trade standard letting tradespeople work across Canada. (c) SHSM — Ministry-approved Grade 11–12 sector-focused bundle that ends with a red sector seal on the OSSD.

7
[2]B · Volunteering
The Ontario OSSD community-involvement requirement is:
Answer Key

40 hours. Completable any time from summer after Grade 8 to graduation; not during instructional school hours; not paid work or court-ordered service.

8
[2]C · Decisions
A "type-1" decision is best described as:
Answer Key

Roughly irreversible. Type-1 — slow careful. Type-2 — fast experimentation.

9
[2]C · Goals
SMART goals stand for:
Answer Key

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

10
[3]D · Money
Define each: (a) TFSA, (b) compound interest, (c) credit score.
Answer Key

(a) TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account; investment gains/withdrawals not taxed. (b) Compound interest — interest earned on previously-earned interest as well as principal. (c) Credit score — 300–900 in Canada (Equifax/TransUnion); driven by payment history, utilisation, history length, mix, applications.

Part B — Thinking & Investigation [25 marks]
11
[5]A · Self-Assessment
A classmate says: "I took one online career quiz and it told me to be a graphic designer. So that's my career."
Critique this reasoning. Identify two flaws and recommend a better approach.
Answer Key

Flaws: single-source result (no triangulation); treating output as a verdict rather than a working hypothesis. Better: multiple assessments (RIASEC, skills, values, learning preferences) + external feedback + informational interviews + low-stakes tests (club, job-shadow). The result is a hypothesis to refine, not a label.

12
[5]B · Labour Market
Anya wants to become a journalist; Mateo wants to become an electrician.
Using what you know about COPS labour-market projections and the differences between these fields, explain how durable each pathway is and what each should plan differently to manage the risk.
Answer Key

Anya/journalist: many regions classify the field as surplus due to advertising shifts; she should specialise (data/video/niche) and plan for income variability. Mateo/electrician: chronic shortage across most provinces (retirements + EV build-out); OYAP and SHSM-Construction are strongly supported pathways with median journeyperson wages $80K+. Each pathway is legitimate; planning differs.

13
[5]C · Decision Matrix
Mateo is comparing 2-year college (Mech Eng Tech), 4-year university (Mech Eng), and OYAP electrician. He uses five criteria: Fit(3), Time-to-Income(2), Cost(2), Demand(3), Reversibility(2), rating each option 1–5.
Construct his decision matrix, compute the totals, and recommend the strongest option. Then identify one limitation of decision matrices.
Answer Key

Reasonable scoring: College 4·3+4·2+4·2+4·3+4·2 = 48; University 4·3+2·2+2·2+4·3+3·2 = 38; Apprenticeship 5·3+5·2+5·2+5·3+3·2 = 56. Recommend apprenticeship for his R-strong, hands-on, math-comfortable, lower-debt profile. Limitation: matrices reflect only the chosen criteria and weights; they can't capture qualitative factors (passion, family pressure, geography). Clarifies thinking, doesn't replace judgement.

14
[5]D · Money
Aanya's Year-1 university costs: tuition $7,000 + residence/meals $14,000 + books $1,200 + personal/transport $2,000 + phone/internet $400 = $24,600. Funding: OSAP $12,000 (about half loan, half grant), summer earnings $7,500, scholarships $1,000, family contribution $4,100.
Calculate her Year-1 loan portion and estimate four-year debt if Year 1 is typical. Briefly comment on what graduate salary would make this debt manageable.
Answer Key

Year-1 loan ≈ $6,000 (half of OSAP). 4-year ≈ $24,000 (excluding interest). Manageable on graduate salary of $50K+; difficult at $35K; painful at $25K. Choice of program (and its likely starting salary) interacts with the borrowing decision.

15
[5]C · Bias
Name three cognitive biases that distort career decisions and give a one-sentence example of each in action.
Answer Key

(1) Sunk-cost — "I've already studied biology two years, I have to finish" — ignores future fit. (2) Anchoring — first option seen becomes the standard. (3) Availability bias — overweighting the careers shown on TV / social media. (4) Confirmation bias — only consuming info that supports the option already preferred. Any three with valid examples.

Part C — Communication [25 marks]
16
[6]D · Cover Letter
Write a short cover-letter paragraph (5–7 sentences) for a Grade 11 summer position at a community library as a Reading Buddy. Include why this role, why you are qualified (with one concrete example), and what you bring.
Answer Key

Look for: clear opening naming role and source; qualifications connected to job requirements with one specific example (volunteering with kids, tutoring siblings, OSP oral communication evidence); the "why this employer" piece (interest in literacy / public library mission); professional conventions.

17
[5]D · STAR Answer
Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to answer the behavioural question: "Tell me about a time you handled a conflict with a teammate." 6–8 sentences. Include all four parts.
Answer Key

Look for clear Situation (specific setting), Task (what had to be done), Action (specific behaviour — initiative, listening, redistribution), Result (outcome with a number or piece of evidence + brief reflection: "I learned that conflict often signals a hidden constraint, not bad faith").

18
[4]D · Résumé
Take the weak bullet "Worked at a coffee shop" and rewrite it as a strong, evidence-rich résumé bullet (title, location, dates, scale, accomplishment).
Answer Key

Sample: "Crew Member, Tim Hortons, Mississauga ON (May–Aug 2024). Served 60+ customers per shift on cash and drive-thru; trained one new hire on POS after three weeks; cross-trained on espresso bar; food-handler safety certified June 2024; recognised by manager for perfect attendance." Include numbers, scale, and at least one corroboration.

19
[5]A · Personal Profile
Write a 5–7 sentence personal profile of yourself for your IPP. Include one interest, one skill (with brief evidence), one value, and one learning preference. Use specific language, no slogans.
Answer Key

Look for: at least one interest named specifically; one skill with brief evidence; one value; one learning preference; cohesive paragraph; no vague slogans ("I'm a people person"). Strong profiles read as honest hypotheses rather than performances.

20
[5]D · Plain-Language Explanation
Explain to a Grade 9 audience, in plain language, why paying a credit-card statement balance in full each month is a powerful habit. Include the rough interest rate and one consequence of carrying a balance. 3–5 sentences.
Answer Key

Pay-in-full = no interest. Carry balance = ~20–22% APR compounds; a $2,000 balance at minimum payments can take 15+ years and cost over $4,000 total. If you can't afford the spending, that's a signal to spend less rather than borrow more.

Part D — Application [25 marks]
21
[6]D · IPP
Apply the four IPP questions to yourself. Write 2–3 sentences under each: (1) Who am I? (2) What are my opportunities? (3) Who do I want to become? (4) What is my plan for achieving my goals?
Answer Key

Look for: synthesised personal profile (1); 2–3 specific career hypotheses (2); an integrated goal connecting self and opportunity (3); concrete action items — courses, placements, certifications, financial moves (4); cohesion across the four sections. Honesty matters more than ambition.

22
[5]D · Transitions
Apply Bridges's three-phase transition model (Ending, Neutral Zone, New Beginning) to plan your own Grade 10 → 11 transition. Name two concrete actions per phase.
Answer Key

Sample: Ending — finishing Grade 10 with exemplars saved, identifying referees. Neutral Zone — reading first chapters of next year's textbooks, attending Grade 11 orientation, interviewing a Grade 11 student, restoring sleep schedule. New Beginning — daily study block, joining one club, weekly Sunday review, October check-in with guidance counsellor. 2 per phase × 3 phases.

23
[5]B · Pathway Planning
Apply career research to one occupation that interests you. Give: (a) occupation, (b) credible Canadian source, (c) one fact about wage or training pathway, (d) one informational-interview question, (e) one Grade-10 action you could take this term to test the fit.
Answer Key

Look for: a specific named occupation; credible Canadian source (Job Bank NOC, professional association, college program page); a defensible fact; a strong interview question (typical day / surprises / advice for Grade 10); and a Grade-10-feasible action (volunteer placement, club, summer job, job-shadow).

24
[5]A/C · Growth Mindset Applied
Apply growth mindset to a real Grade-10 setback. Describe one setback (academic, social, or personal), the inner monologue you'd use to respond with growth mindset (use the word "yet"), and the concrete next step you'd take.
Answer Key

Look for: specific, plausible setback; inner monologue that names what went wrong specifically; the word "yet" (or equivalent reframing); a concrete next step (talking to teacher, changing study strategy, asking for feedback). Growth mindset is a method, not a slogan.

25
[4]STSE · Capstone
Capstone STSE reflection: Imagine yourself five years from now. In 5–7 sentences, describe what you hope your life looks like — postsecondary destination, work, community involvement, financial habits, one strength you have developed and one habit you have built. Tie the description back to at least two specific elements of your Career Studies learning.
Answer Key

Look for: plausible, specific 5-year picture; connection to at least one Strand-A self-knowledge element (interest, value, skill); at least one Strand-B opportunity (postsecondary or work); at least one Strand-C goal/decision artefact; at least one Strand-D habit (financial, professional, transition); coherence between self-knowledge and the picture (no random "I'll be a billionaire" without grounded reasoning). Marks: 1 plausibility, 1 specificity, 1 connection to course learning, 1 conventions.

📝 Communication Category Rubric (instructor-graded)

Per Ontario's Growing Success (2010) Achievement Chart, Communication is one of the four equally-weighted categories (25% of the course mark). This rubric is applied to every student-written response (short-answer, extended-response, essay, lab report, deputation, etc.) on this assessment.

Criterion Level 1 — Limited (50–59%) Level 2 — Some (60–69%) Level 3 — Considerable (70–79%) Level 4 — Thorough (80–100%)
Expression and organisation of ideas Ideas expressed with limited clarity; little organisation. Ideas expressed with some clarity; some organisation. Ideas expressed clearly and organised effectively. Ideas expressed with clarity, precision, and a high degree of organisation.
Communication for different audiences and purposes Limited awareness of audience/purpose; tone often inappropriate. Some awareness of audience/purpose; tone occasionally inappropriate. Consistently appropriate tone, register, and address to audience. Tone, register, voice, and address are precisely matched to audience and purpose.
Use of conventions, vocabulary, terminology Frequent errors in grammar, spelling, punctuation; little discipline-specific vocabulary. Some errors that occasionally impede meaning; emerging use of discipline-specific vocabulary. Few errors; consistent use of discipline-specific vocabulary. Virtually error-free; precise and varied use of discipline-specific vocabulary.

Level 3 represents the Provincial Standard. Use the most-recent and most-consistent evidence across multiple Communication tasks (this assessment, prior FOR work, written responses on AS quizzes, oral presentations) when assigning a course-level Communication mark. Growing Success, 2010, p. 17.
