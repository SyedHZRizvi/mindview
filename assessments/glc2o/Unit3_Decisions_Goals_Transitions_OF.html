Identify each letter in the SMART goal framework and provide a one-sentence example of how that criterion improves a goal.
Answer Key

Specific — names what exactly will change. Measurable — attaches a number you can track. Achievable — realistic given starting point and time. Relevant — connects to a larger purpose. Time-bound — has a deadline. Example application: "Raise MPM2D mark from 67% to 75% by end of Term 2 via weekly 30-min reviews + 10 problems/Wed." 0.6 marks each criterion + 1 application.

Answer Key

A. STAR structures a behavioural-interview answer. Situation = context; Task = what needed to be done; Action = what you did; Result = what happened (with numbers and reflection when possible).

3
[3]
Define each financial term: (a) TFSA, (b) compound interest, (c) credit score.
Answer Key

(a) TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account; gains and withdrawals are tax-free; eligible at age 18 in Canada; annual contribution limit. (b) Compound interest — interest earned on previously-earned interest as well as the principal; works powerfully both for saving (helpful) and debt (harmful). (c) Credit score — 300–900 in Canada via Equifax/TransUnion; summarises borrower trustworthiness; driven by payment history, credit-utilisation rate, history length, mix, recent applications. 1 mark each.

Part B: Thinking & Investigation [13 marks]
6
[5]
Mateo is choosing between three postsecondary options: a 2-year college diploma in Mechanical Engineering Technician, a 4-year university degree in Mechanical Engineering, or an OYAP electrician apprenticeship. He values fit with his interests (R, hands-on), reasonable time-to-income, low debt, strong job demand, and the ability to revise if he changes his mind.
Build a decision matrix that scores each option against the criteria (use a 1–5 scale). Briefly justify which option the matrix favours and explain one limitation of the matrix.
Answer Key

Sample weighted matrix (criteria weighted 1–3): Fit(3), Time-to-Income(2), Cost(2), Demand(3), Reversibility(2). College: 4·3+4·2+4·2+4·3+4·2 = 48. University: 4·3+2·2+2·2+4·3+3·2 = 38. Apprenticeship: 5·3+5·2+5·2+5·3+3·2 = 56. Matrix favours apprenticeship for Mateo's profile. Limitation: the matrix only reflects the criteria and weights chosen; it can't capture qualitative factors (passion for design, family pressure, geographic constraints). It clarifies thinking, doesn't replace judgement. Marks: 2 matrix structure + 1 calculation + 1 justification + 1 limitation.

7
[4]
Vague wish: "I want to get into a good university."
Rewrite this wish as a SMART long-term goal, then write three short-term goals (this term) and a one-week action plan that would support it.
Answer Key

Sample long-term SMART: "By end of Grade 12, have a Top-6 average of at least 82% with all prerequisites for Engineering programs at three target universities." Sample short-term goals: (1) "Maintain 80%+ in each Grade 10 academic course this term." (2) "Establish 30-min/night homework habit by Friday week 3." (3) "Build a study group of 3 students by end of October." Sample action plan: Mon math review, Tue physics problems, Wed English, Thu chem, Fri self-test, Sun 10-min Sunday review. Marks: 1 long-term SMART (with all criteria), 1 each short-term (× 3 = 3), 1 actionable weekly plan. Total slightly compressed; max 4.

8
[4]
Aanya plans to attend a 4-year Ontario university with tuition $7,000, residence/meals $14,000, books $1,200, personal/transport $2,000, phone/internet $400 — for total Year 1 cost about $24,600. She expects OSAP of about $12,000 (half loan, half grant), $7,500 net summer earnings, $1,000 scholarships, and $4,100 family contribution. How much OSAP debt does she carry forward from Year 1, and what would the four-year debt total be if Year 1 is typical?
Answer Key

OSAP $12,000 split roughly half loan/half grant → Year 1 loan ≈ $6,000 (the grant portion does not need to be repaid). Total funding = 12,000 + 7,500 + 1,000 + 4,100 = $24,600 = exactly Year 1 cost. Four-year loan total ≈ 4 × $6,000 = $24,000 (excluding interest and assuming Year 1 is typical). Comment: a $24K debt is manageable on a graduate salary of $50K+, hard at $35K, painful at $25K. The numbers should drive the choice of program and back-up plan. Marks: 2 correct Year 1 loan, 1 four-year estimate, 1 comment on affordability.

9
[6]
Write a short cover-letter paragraph (5–7 sentences) for a Grade 11 summer job at a local public library as a Reading Buddy. You may invent reasonable details. Address: why this job, why you are qualified, and what you would bring to the role.
Answer Key

Look for: (1) clear identification of the role and where the candidate saw it; (2) specific qualifications connected to the role (volunteering with children, tutoring siblings, OSP skills like oral communication and working with others); (3) a concrete example demonstrating one of those skills; (4) why this employer (interest in literacy, public library mission); (5) professional, error-free conventions; (6) closing courtesy. 10
[3]
Use the STAR framework to draft a 90-second behavioural-interview answer to: "Tell me about a time you handled a conflict with a teammate." Include all four STAR elements.
Answer Key

Look for: Situation — specific group project/team setting; Task — what needed to be done; Action — what the candidate did (showed initiative, listened, redistributed work, escalated appropriately); Result — outcome with a number or piece of evidence (mark, completion, feedback) and a brief reflection ("I learned that conflict often signals a hidden constraint, not bad faith"). Marks: 0.75 each element + 1 final reflection.

11
[3]
In 3–4 sentences, explain to a Grade 9 student why paying a credit-card statement balance in full each month is a powerful financial habit. Use plain language and include the approximate interest rate that accrues if you don't.
Answer Key

Strong response: paying the statement balance in full means no interest charges; carrying a balance triggers interest around 20–22% APR, which compounds rapidly. A $2,000 balance at minimum payments can take 15+ years and cost over $4,000 total. The habit costs nothing if you can afford the spending; if you can't afford it, that's a signal to spend less rather than borrow more. Marks: 1 the rule, 1 the rate, 1 the consequence/example, 1 plain-language framing.

Part D: Application [12 marks]
12
[5]
Apply Bridges's three-phase transition model to plan your own Grade 10 → 11 transition. For each phase (Ending, Neutral Zone, New Beginning), name two concrete actions you will take.
Answer Key

Look for: Ending (June): finishing Grade 10 cleanly — collecting exemplars, identifying referees, noting study habits that worked. Neutral Zone (July–Aug): reading first chapter of next year's textbooks, attending Grade 11 orientation, interview a current Grade 11 student, restoring sleep schedule. New Beginning (Sept–Oct): committing to a daily study block, joining one club, weekly Sunday IPP review, October check-in with guidance counsellor. Marks: 2 actions × 3 phases (× 0.8 = 5 total). Look for specificity and plausibility.

13
[4]
Apply the IPP framework to yourself by sketching a one-page IPP outline. Provide a brief response (1–2 lines) under each of the four questions: Who am I? What are my opportunities? Who do I want to become? What is my plan for achieving my goals?
Answer Key

Look for: (1) Who am I? — synthesised personal profile (Holland code, OSP strengths, values, learning preferences). (2) Opportunities? — two or three specific career hypotheses or postsecondary destinations being considered. (3) Who do I want to become? — a goal statement that integrates self and opportunity. (4) Plan? — concrete action items (specific courses, placements, certifications, financial steps). Marks: 1 each section + 1 for cohesion across the four answers.

14
[3]
STSE / Real-World Application: Why does the Ontario Ministry of Education require GLC2O as a compulsory half-credit and tie it to the Individual Pathways Plan? Explain in 3–4 sentences, connecting career-development competence to lifelong public-policy outcomes.
Answer Key

Strong response: GLC2O is compulsory because career-development competence — self-knowledge, opportunity awareness, decision-making, transition management — is a lifelong skill set that underpins economic participation, social mobility, and personal wellbeing. The IPP is the document that operationalises that skill set across K-12, ensuring no student finishes Grade 12 without having structured time to think about their pathway. From a public-policy view, students with grounded plans use postsecondary funding more effectively, have higher labour-market participation, and are more able to revise plans as the economy shifts. The Ministry treats this as a foundational diploma requirement, not an elective. 1 mark per insight, max 3.
