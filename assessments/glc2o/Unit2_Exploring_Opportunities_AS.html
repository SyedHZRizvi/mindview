Topic 2.1 — Researching Careers
Question 1
"Triangulation" in career research means:
Solution: Triangulation = using at least three independent sources to verify a finding. For careers, that usually means combining Job Bank/NOC data, a professional association's website, and an informational interview with someone in the field.
Question 2
The National Occupational Classification (NOC) is:
Solution: NOC = Canada's federal occupational classification. Each NOC code links to standardised duties, education pathways, wages, and outlooks via Job Bank — the one-step lookup that makes career research efficient.
Topic 2.2 — Labour Market Trends
Question 3
The Canadian Occupational Projection System (COPS) classifies each occupation as:
Solution: COPS produces a 10-year forecast labelling each NOC occupation as shortage, balanced, or surplus — a basic input to a career decision (alongside fit, cost, and personal goals).
Question 4
A Grade 10 student says: "My older cousin says journalism is dying, so no one should ever go into it."
The most accurate response is:
Solution: Trends inform planning probabilistically, not deterministically. Journalism has shifted, not disappeared; the strategic response is to specialise and freelance, not to write off the field entirely.
Topic 2.3 — Postsecondary Destinations
Question 5
An Ontario apprenticeship typically combines:
Solution: Apprenticeships in Ontario are paid from day one; about 80–90% on-the-job with a sponsor employer plus 10–20% in-class technical training. They lead to a Certificate of Qualification, often Red Seal.
Question 6
Which Ontario credential is most commonly two years of applied study at a public college?
Solution: Ontario college diplomas are typically 2 years. Certificates are ~1 year; advanced diplomas 3 years; applied degrees 4 years. University undergraduate degrees are 3–4 years.
Topic 2.4 — OYAP & Trades
Question 7
OYAP — the Ontario Youth Apprenticeship Program — allows students to:
Solution: OYAP lets you stack apprenticeship hours during high school. Combined with SHSM-Construction or similar, students can graduate Grade 12 with ~2,000 trade hours already on the books and a sponsoring employer.
Question 8
Lucas, a Grade 10 student, is interest-coded R, strong in math, enjoys hands-on work, and lives in a town with several construction firms.
The strongest match from the options below is:
Solution: Lucas's profile (R, math, hands-on, local construction market) maps directly to a trade pathway. OYAP + SHSM-Construction lets him bank apprenticeship hours and gain industry certifications inside secondary school — a head-start of 1–2 years on the Certificate of Qualification.
Topic 2.5 — Entrepreneurship
Question 9
In a Business Model Canvas, the "value proposition" answers the question:
Solution: Value proposition = the specific problem your business solves and the reason customers would choose you over the alternative. A weak value proposition is the most common reason a small business fails to find customers.
Topic 2.6 — Community Involvement
Question 10
The Ontario OSSD community-involvement requirement is:
Solution: 40 hours, completable any time between summer after Grade 8 and graduation. Each hour must be documented on the Ministry form, signed by the supervising organisation.
Question 11
Which of the following activities would NOT count toward the 40-hour community-involvement requirement?
Solution: Paid work that displaces a paid worker (especially in a for-profit business) doesn't count. Approved activities are unpaid, benefit a community organisation, and don't occur during instructional school hours.
